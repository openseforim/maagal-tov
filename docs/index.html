Ḥida, Ma'agal Tov

The travel diary of Ḥayyim Yosef David Azulai, with an English translation paragraph by paragraph · מעגל טוב

Ḥayyim Yosef David Azulai (the Ḥida, 1724–1806) travelled twice as an emissary of the Jews of Hebron and the Land of Israel, in the 1750s and the 1770s, through Egypt, Italy, Germany, Holland, France, England and Tunisia. His diary, Ma'agal Tov ("the good circuit"), records the money he raised, the scholars he argued with, the libraries and courts he saw, and his own grief and melancholy. It was not printed in his lifetime.

The full diary

Both journeys, 1753–58 and 1772–78, from A. Freimann's edition, Ma'agal Tov ha-Shalem (Berlin 1921, Jerusalem 1934), in its order. The 1885 printing below has only parts of the second journey, rearranged by its editors; rows of the full diary link to the matching 1885 paragraph and back.

The Hebrew text

  • Base text: the printing of Przemyśl, 1885 (Sefer Ma'agal Tov), which reprints the Livorno printing of 1879 (Eliyahu Ben Amozegh). Its editor, Joseph Shabbetai Farḥi, says in his preface that R. Abraham Ḥai Musafia had seen the autograph in the hands of the Ḥida's grandson in Ancona, and came to Livorno with his extracts in Tammuz 5627 (1867). It holds the second journey, 1773–1778 (Egypt, Tunis, Italy, France, Holland, Paris again, Turin, and the return to Livorno), a few later entries to 1793, and the editors' additions: family memories of the Ḥida (Shem Tov), his private notes (Sefer ha-Zikkaron) and stories about him. The first journey is not in it. Scan: archive.org, maagaltob00azul (University of Illinois, 650 ppi).
  • Order of the leaves. The leaves of the scanned copy are bound out of order. Scans 34–39 (viewer numbers) are the title page, the prefaces and the opening of the diary; some leaves after them are lost, and the text takes up again in Tunis at scan 7. Scan 33 goes on directly to scan 40. The reader follows the order of the text, and each part lists its scans.
  • OCR: Kraken with the PP-OCRv6 model, line by line, then checked against the scan by eye. Spelling, abbreviations and punctuation are as printed.
  • Complete text: A. Freimann's edition, Ma'agal Tov ha-Shalem (Mekize Nirdamim, Berlin 1921 and Jerusalem 1934), holds both journeys; it is the text of the full diary above. Its pages 1–180 (the diary) were read by OCR from the copy at the National Library of Israel (shelfmark S 32 V 929), checked against the scan line by line and translated paragraph by paragraph. Freimann's notes and introduction are not reproduced. In that copy the lower corner of the leaf with pages 119–120 is torn away; the lost words are marked [...]. The autograph (Jewish Theological Seminary, MS 5388–5389) was not consulted.

Method

  • Rows. One row is one paragraph of the print. The print ends each entry with a colon.
  • English. Plain and close to the Hebrew. Dates by the weekly Torah portion are kept ("Tuesday of the week of Shemini").
  • Uncertain renderings have the mark ⚠ and a note at the end of the page; unresolved abbreviations are left as uncertain.
  • Translator. The translation is by Claude (Anthropic), directed and reviewed by Ezra Brand.
