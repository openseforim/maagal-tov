126.1

17 Tevet, the night of Friday [the evening of Thursday 15 January 1778]: we came to the village of Soignies. The people of the inn were good and humble ⚠1, and did us honor at the beginning of the night; but in the morning, when we set out, they mistreated us, broke their terms and took a livre and a half more. We traveled on in peace. In truth, in France the innkeepers were good: even if some of them ask much, nevertheless they do not change their word. But the Germans ⚠2, their mind is more on the penny [b. Kiddushin 12a], and everything is built on stinginess. Already in the towns of Flanders we saw small carts with five or six dogs pulling them. On Friday at midday [16 January 1778] we came to the city of Brussels. At the gate I showed the passport; they did us honor and did not open the clothes. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. As we passed through the city in a carriage we saw that the city is beautiful, built with fine buildings and fine streets. There is a great square (plaza), and in it the palace of the Emperor's uncle ⚠3; at the door of the palace is a horse and its rider of bronze, gilded, very beautiful indeed; and all round the square are palaces (palacios), very fine buildings. They said that there are great factories (fábricas) of every skilled craft: camlets, cloths (telas) and stuffs (estofas), all of it fine and beautiful. We came to the house of a Jew, Lipman Nathan Roi Botish ⚠4, who has lived there with his wife for some years, and I brought him a recommendation from Signor Solomon Perpignan. He told me there was no room. We went to a gentile inn, the Hôtel Royal, and spent the Sabbath there in a fine room, blessed be the Lord.

טבת 17. אור הששי בשבת באנו לכפר שוניי ובני האושפיזא טובים ועניים ועשו לנו כבוד בתחילת הלילה אבל בבקר כשנסענו התעללו בנו והפרו תנאם ולקחו ליטרא וחצי יותר. ונסענו לש' ובאמת שבצרפת היו בני האושפיזות טובים ואף אם יש מהם שואלים הרבה מ"מ דיבורם אינם מחליפים אבל האשכנזים דעתייהו אפרוטה טפי והכל מבונה על הקמצנות. וכבר ראינו בערי פיינדרא עגלות קטנות ה' או ששה כלבים מושכין אותה. ויום הששי בחצות באנו עיר ברוסילים ובפתח הראיתי הפ"פ וכיבדונו ולא פתחו הבגדים הודו לה' כי טוב כל"ח. ומידי עוברנו בעיר בקארוסה ראינו שהעיר יפה ובנויה בנינים יפים ומבואות יפות, ויש פלאסה גדולה ויש בה פלטין דוד האינפיראדור ועל פתח הפלטין סוס ורוכבו נחשת ומוזהב הרבה יפה מאד ובכל סביבות הפלאסה פלאסיוס בנינים יפים מאד ואמרו שיש פאבריקאס גדולות מכל מלאכת מחשבת גאמילוטים וטילאס ואיסטופאס והכל חשוב ויפה. ובאנו לבית יאודי א' ליפמאן נתן רואי בוטיש שדר שם עם אשתו זה כמה שנים והבאתי לו ריקו' מסי' שלמה פירפינייאן ואמר לי לא יש מקום הלכנו לאש"ג אוטיל רוייאל ושבתנו שם בחדר יפה ברוך ה'.

126.2

19 Tevet, Sunday of Va'era [18 January 1778]: I went to the house of the said Ashkenazi. He said that he had gone to some expense for me for the Sabbath, and why had I not come to eat? I said to him: why did you not tell me to come? He answered that I should have gone of my own accord; and I answered: that is not done in our parts. I was worried about the road, and there was an Ashkenazi there, a scholar, R. Elḥanan son of Mattathias, who is also going to Holland. I told him to come with us, for there is no one with us who knows the language, neither French nor German nor Dutch, and we have several roads before us. May it be His will that it be for good and for peace for us; so may it be His will.

טבת 19. א' בשבת וארא הלכתי לבית האשכנזי הנז' ואמר שהוציא כמה הוצאות בשבילי לשבת ולמה לא הלכתי לאכול. וא"ל למה לא אמרת לי שאבא. והשיב שאני הי"ל ללכת מעצמי, והשבתי לא יעשה כן במקומנו. והייתי בצער על הדרך ושם נמצא אשכנזי ת"ח ר' אלחנן בר מתתיה שגם הוא הולך להולאנדה ואמרתי לו שיבא עמנו כי אין אתנו יודע לשון לא צרפת ולא אשכנז ולא הולאנדיו ויש לפנינו כמה דרכים יה"ר שיהיה לנו לטובה ולש' כי"ר.

126.3

On Sunday morning the innkeeper fell upon us and broke his word, what we had agreed with him, and took fifteen livres against our will. We rode in a wide carriage. I told the servant to come and sit with us, and he swore that he would not come, and I bore his stubbornness. He also left two pairs of shoes and stockings in the inn, and they are not to be found in these parts. This too is for the good.

ויום א' בבקר בעל האושפיזא התגולל עלינו וחילל דברו שהתנינו עמו ולקח בע"כ ט"ו ליטרין. ורכבנו בקארוסה רחבה ואמרתי למשרת שיבא לשבת עמנו ונשבע שלא יבא וסבלתי סרבנותו גם הניח שני זוגות מנעלים ובתי רגלים בית האושפיז' ואינם נמצאים במקומות אלו גם זו לטוב.

126.4

20 Tevet, the night of Monday [the evening of Sunday 18 January 1778]: we came to the great city of Antwerp. On Sunday we had our meal in a beautiful town, Mechelen ⚠5. There about three hundred people gathered in the street to see us; Abraham talked with them and they laughed at him, and he did not care. In the city of Antwerp the innkeeper's wife was one of the pious of the nations of the world, and treated us with honor and compassion. We sought to hire a wagon to Moerdijk, and they asked a great deal of money, for the road in Holland in winter is very bad, earth and deep mud ⚠6, and six horses are needed. I pressed them to take off from what they asked, and the wagoner would not listen and went away. Afterwards I weighed the matter, I considered my ways [Ps 119:59], and I was forced to send after him; they went four times and he did not come, and I was distressed. Afterwards he came, one hour before midday [Monday 19 January], and we came to terms. In truth we saw that the road was bad and dangerous; with six horses we came to Moerdijk at three hours into the night, by a miracle, for near the town the wagon sank in the mud and we were almost in danger that the coach would overturn with the clothes on top of us, and it was in the dead of night and darkness [Prov 7:9]. And He in His mercy lit up our darkness [Ps 18:29], and we arrived in peace. Also, three hours after we left Moerdijk ⚠7 is the end of the towns of Flanders, and in German this province is called Brabant. There imperial guards came out and stood to ask about the passport and the clothes, and, blessed be the Name, we got away in peace and they did not look at the clothes. Blessed is He who bestows good things on the undeserving, who has bestowed every good on us. I am too small for all the kindnesses [Gen 32:11]. Blessed be the name of the Lord from now and for ever [Ps 113:2], for His kindness has prevailed over us, and the truth of the Lord is for ever, hallelujah [Ps 117:2]. The Lord will accomplish it for me; Lord, Your kindness is for ever; do not forsake the work of Your hands [Ps 138:8].

טבת 20. ליל ב' בשבת באנו בעיר הגדולה אנוירים. ויום א' סעדנו בעיר יפה מילכה. ושם נקבצו כש' נפשות ברחוב לראותנו ואברהם מדבר עמם וצוחקים עליו ולא איכפת ליה. ובעיר אנוירים בעלת האושפיזא היתה מחסידי א"ה ונהגה כבוד ורחמנות. ובקשנו לשכור עגלה למורדיק ושאלו ממון רב כי הדרך בהולאנדה בחורף רע מאד עפר וטיט הנרוק וצריך ששה סוסים והפצרתי לנכות p. 127ממה ששאלו ולא אבה העגלון שמוע והלך. ואח"ך שקלתי הדבר חשבתי דרכי והייתי מוכרח לשלוח אחריו והלכו ד' פעמים ולא בא והייתי בצער. ואח"ך בא שעה אחת קודם חצות ונתפשרנו. ובאמת ראינו כי הדרך רע ומסוכן ובששה סוסים באנו למורדיק לג' שעות בלילה בנס כי סמוך לעיר העגלה נטבעה בטיט והיינו כמעט בסכנה שיהפוך הקרון ובגדים עלינו והוא באישון לילה ואפלה. והוא ברחמיו הגיה חשכנו ובאנו לש' גם אחר שלש שעות שיצאנו ממורדיק הוא סוף ערי פילנדרא ובאשכנז קורין זה המחוז בירואנה ושם יצאו נצבים שמירות אינפירייאל לשאול על פ"פ והבגדים וברוך השם נפטרנו לש' ולא ראו הבגדים. ברוך הגומל לחייבים טובות שגמלנו כל טוב קטנתי מכל החסדים יהי שם ד' מבורך מעתה ועד עולם כי גבר עלינו חסדו ואמת ה' לעולם הללויה ה' יגמור בעדי ה' חסדך לעולם מעשה ידיך אל תרף.

127.1

21 Tevet, the night of Tuesday [the evening of Monday 19 January 1778]: we came in peace to the town of Moerdijk in Holland. We had to cross a river in a sailing ship. In the morning [Tuesday 20 January] the wind was very strong, and they waited until two hours before midday. We crossed in the ship in half an hour; but as we came near the town there was terrible ice and the ship could not get through. We went into a small boat (kaik), and the sailors broke the ice with poles shod with iron, leaning on them and pushing the boat; we sat in it more than a quarter of an hour. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, who saved us. We came in peace to a village and wanted to hire a calash. They said that the road was bad and four horses were needed, and that the rule of the prince (principe) is to pay twelve florins for three parasangs, to the last detail. I pressed and pleaded, and it did not help. When I came to ride I found an open cart (carretta). I said that I wanted a calash, and no calash was to be found; in the end they covered the cart. In truth the road was bad, and in two hours we came to the lodging.

טבת 21. אור ג' בשבת באנו לש' לעיר מורדיק מהולאנדה והיינו צריכים לעבור נהר בספינה ברוח. ובבקר הרוח היה חזק מאד והמתינו עד ב' שעות קודם חצות ועברנו בספינה בחצי שעה אך כשקרבנו לעיר היה קרח נורא ולא יכלה הספינה לעבור ונכנסנו בקאיק קטן והיו המלחים שוברים הקרח בכח עצים שחודן ברזל ונסמכים עליהם ודוחפים הקאיק וישבנו בזה יותר מרביע שעה הודו לה' כי טוב שהצילנו ובאנו לש' לכפר א' ורצינו להשכיר קאליסו ואמרו שהדרך רע וצריך ד' סוסים ודת הפרינסיפי לשלם על ג' פרסאות י"ב פלורין ופרוטרוט והפצרתי והתחננתי ולא הועלתי וכשבאתי לרכוב מצאתי קאריטה גלויה ואמרתי שאני רוצה קאליסו ולא נמצא קאליסו וסוף דבר כיסו הקאריטה ובאמת שהיה דרך רע ובב' שעות באנו למלון.

127.2

22 Tevet, the night of Wednesday [the evening of Tuesday 20 January 1778]: we came to the village of Puttershoek and went to a gentile inn. They gave us a fine room, clean and well kept; but the innkeeper asked four and a half florins, although we neither eat nor drink [there]. We wore ourselves out with him, and he kept saying that such is the law of Holland, nothing less. I am grieved for the bag of dinars that went on the roads without enjoyment and with pain. May the Omnipresent have mercy on us in His great mercy, say "Enough" to our troubles, and pour out blessing for us [Mal 3:10] with honor, for His service and His fear, in the joy of the festival, with His help; so may it be His will. In the morning we crossed the river by ferry; at its edge there was ice, and they were cutting it, and blessed be the Lord, we crossed in peace. There was an open cart there, and they asked four florins to go near Rotterdam, as far as the river. I wore myself out pressing, speaking harshly and pleading, and it did not help, for they were wicked village gentiles. In the morning, when we got up, there was rain and a strong wind, and my heart melted to think of this road, for I knew already since yesterday that not even a covered wagon was to be found. We rode in the wagon. In the first hour the sun shone; in the second hour the wind was strong and there were some drops of rain, and the Lord saw our affliction and the rain was held back [Gen 8:2]; and in the third and fourth hours the wind calmed somewhat. We came to the river, took a ferry and reached Rotterdam in peace. At once we found R. Eizik Goldschmidt, who received us in an inn. R. Eizik and his wife remembered me from twenty-two years ago, when I was there. My soul revived, and I began: Blessed is God who saves from troubles upon troubles on this road. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. Before the afternoon prayer I went to the rabbi, and he told me: R. Abraham Ze'evi, the emissary from Hebron, was already here five months ago. I told him: I am an emissary to the cities of Italy ⚠8 and am going to Amsterdam; if you see fit, pay the cost of the inn. He spoke harshly to me: nothing need be given, for I am an emissary to the Sephardim and they will pay; and just as I came from Paris to here, I will pay the inn here ⚠9, and the like. I got up and went to the synagogue, and after the afternoon prayer I came to terms with R. Eizik mentioned above for the lodging; the food I will pay out of my own pocket, until after the Sabbath, for now the river is frozen and no ship goes. May the Lord lead me in paths of righteousness for His name's sake [Ps 23:3], for His mercies have not ended [Lam 3:22], and may He in His mercy help us for the glory of His name; amen, so may it be His will. 1885 printing: 66, 67

טבת 22. אור ד' בשבת באנו לכפר פיטארסוק ובאנו לאש"ג ונתנו לנו חדר יפה ונקי ומהודר אך בעל האושפיזא שאל ד' פלורין וחצי אף שאין אנו אוכלים ושותים ונלאינו עמו והיה אומר שכך משפט הולנדה אין נגרע. וצר לי על תרקבא דדינרי כי אזלת בדרכים בלי הנאה ובצער המקום ירחמנו ברחמיו הרבים ויאמר די לצרותנו ויריק לנו ברכה בכבוד לעבודתו וליראתו בשמחת הרגל בעזרתו כי"ר. ובבקר עברנו מעבורת הנהר ובקצהו היה קרח והיו חותכים אותו וברוך ה' עברנו לש'. ושם קאריטה בלי כיסוי ושאלו ד' פלורינים ללכת סמוך לרוטרדם עד הנהר ונלאיתי להפציר ולדבר קשות ולהתחנן ולא הועלתי כי היו גויים כפריים רשעים. ובבקר כשקמנו היו גשמים ורוח חזק ונמס לבי לחשוב בדרך הזה כי ידעתי גם מתמול אשר לא נמצא אף עגלה מכוסה. ורכבנו בעגלה ושעה א' זרחה השמש ושעה ב' היה הרוח חזק והיו קצת טיפות גשמים וה' ראה בענינו ויכלא הגשם וגם שעה ג' וד' נח מקצת הרוח ובאנו עד הנהר ולקחנו מעבורת והגענו לרוטרדם לש' ותכף מצאנו לר' איזק גולדשמיט שקבלנו בבית אושפיזא ור' איזק ואשתו זכרו ממני מזה כ"ב שנה כשהייתי שם ותשב נפשי ופתחתי ברוך האל המושיע מצרות צרורות בדרך הזה הודו לה' כי טוב כל"ח. וקודם המנחה הלכתי אצל הרב וא"ל כבר היה ר' אברהם זאבי משולח מחברון זה ה' p. 128חדשים א"ל אני שליח לערי איטליא והולך לאמשט' אם טוב בעיניכם לשלם שכר האושפיזא ודיבר לי קשות שאין ליתן כלום שאני שליח לספרדים והם ישלמו וכאשר באתי מפאריץ עד כאן אני אשלם האושפיזא כאן וכיוצא וקמתי והלכתי לבה"כ ואחר מנחה נתפשרתי עם ר' איזק הנו' שכר דירה והמזונות אוציא מכיסי עד אחר השבת כי עתה הנהר מוקר"ח ואין ספינה הולכת ה' ינחנו במעגלי צדק למען שמו כי לא כלו רחמיו והוא ברחמיו יעזרנו עדכ"ש אכי"ר.

128.1

24 Tevet, Friday [23 January 1778]: in the morning Isaiah Karanfil came from Amsterdam; Signor D. K. Azevedo [David ha-Kohen Azevedo] had sent him for me. He brought me a letter from my dear Raphael ⚠10, full of great longing, and I had joy of it; and a letter from my friend Signor Aaron; and a letter from our teacher R. Abraham Ze'evi, may God keep him, the emissary to Germany, who went to Amsterdam and has stayed a year, and is still there. In this matter I had grief for several reasons, as we will write below.

טבת 24. הששי בשבת בבקר בא ישעיה קאראנפיל מאמשט' ששלחו סי' ד"ך אזיוידו בשבילי והביא לי כתב מחמודי רפאל בגעגעין גדולים והי"ל נ"ר וכתב ידידי סי' אהרן וכתב מהר"א זאבי נר"ו שליח אשכנז שהלך לאמשט' וישב שנה ועודנו עומד שם. והי"ל בענין זה צער מכמה צדדין כמו שנכתוב לקמן.

128.2

25 Tevet, the holy Sabbath [24 January 1778]: they called me up to the Torah fourth, after the rabbi. After the afternoon prayer I visited the new warden and had the third meal there with Signor Ariani and Isaiah mentioned above, and he honored me greatly. The teacher R. Eizik Kohen was there, and he gave me a small book that he had printed.

טבת 25. ש"ק עלו אותי לס"ת רביעי אחר הרב ואחר מנחה בקרתי לפרנס החדש ועשיתי שם סעודה ג' עם סי' ארייאני וישעיה הנז' וכיבדני מאד. ונמצא שם המלמד ר' אייזק כהן ונתן לי ס' קטן שהדפיס.

128.3

26 Tevet, Sunday of Bo [25 January 1778]: after prayers I went to the new warden, R. Moses Springer mentioned above, and he did me great honor. He called the beadle and trustee, R. Hirz, who is a scholar, and sent word to R. Eizik Goldschmidt, the innkeeper. He had asked twelve florins for four days' lodging, and [the warden] sent to tell him to deduct it from what he owes the community, and he accepted. Afterwards he gave me provisions for the road and a gift for the servants, and he gave me a gift from his own money. May the Lord repay their deeds. At midday I sent my two trunks (baúles) and the clothes of Signor Ariani with Abraham to the ship for Amsterdam. Three hours later I, with Signor Ḥ. R. A. ⚠11 and Isaiah, went by carriage to the village of Dragaho ⚠12, to go from there in the ship that travels to Amsterdam, with the help of God.

For such is the custom of many gevirim, who have a Torah scroll kept in honor, with a very splendid ark and silver ornaments, and on Friday night they read the portion from it. On the Sabbath day [7 February], at the afternoon prayer, Rabbi Shalem preached on "If you will go with me" [Judg 4:8] to the song, and so on: that the meaning is that they should sing while they are still in the midst of the battle, on the strength of the promise; and she answered that the song was not handed to him alone to sing, for Israel too must sing, and that is when the miracle is done. He brought in the matter of Jonathan and Saul's ban [1 Sam 14], and explained that the Lord did not answer Saul because he had transgressed the words of Samuel, in that he did not wait [1 Sam 13:8]. And as for the rule that a king's honor cannot be waived [b. Ketubbot 17a], that is for the sake of Israel, and if Israel waive it, it can be waived; and this is why they said, "As the Lord lives, not a hair of his head shall fall" [1 Sam 14:45], and so on. Further, that he did not know [of the oath]; and the salvation came through him and not through Saul because [Saul] had transgressed the words of a prophet. This is the gist of the whole sermon, and in my humble opinion none of it is true. But in the course of his words he said one thing that is good: that the astonished question "Shall Jonathan die, who has wrought this great salvation?" [1 Sam 14:45] is because [Saul] himself had said, "Today no man shall be put to death, for today the Lord has wrought salvation" [1 Sam 11:13]; so he held that on a day of salvation one does not insist on honor. And here Jonathan had not heard the ban, and nothing remained except that he had transgressed his decree, and one should not put him to death [on] a good [day] (?) ⚠25; and this is "Shall Jonathan die?" He spent half an hour of the sermon saying this at length.

18 Shevat, Sunday, the night after the holy Sabbath [the evening of Saturday 14 February 1778]: I was at the house of Rabbi Shalem, and he was praising himself for his sermons and his studies and the like; and how, when he began to reign in Amsterdam ⚠30, he preached on the passage of the kingship of Saul and David, and the like. Also his moral teachings: "Arise, my love, my fair one" [Song 2:10], that the man speaks to his wife, that she should go to pasture alone in the gardens. Out of great modesty she answered nothing but "O my dove, in the clefts of the rock" etc. [Song 2:14], and her husband says "let me see your countenance" etc., and she answers: how shall I go? "Take us the foxes" [Song 2:15]: young men and lads with the sweetness of their lips, like the bite of a fox, whose beginning is not felt and whose end is very bad; so the young men spoil the vineyard of the Lord of hosts [Isa 5:7]. He explained further: "our sons as plants" [Ps 144:12]: from seedlings one must remove certain bad seeds that lessen the strength of the soil, and also because the good ones suck in the bad from the bad ones; so the sons are like plants, that they should have no bad company. "Our daughters as corner pillars": they do not go out of the house, and from modesty they are like walls. Through this there is wealth: "our garners are full" etc., "our sheep" etc., that the beasts do not die; "no breach", that the waters do not break the fences; "and no going forth", that they do not go out over the fence; and through this "no outcry" [Ps 144:13–14]. He went on at length and explained "but of the matter of the kingdom he told him not" [1 Sam 10:16]: and if you say it was because it came from a man who is not trustworthy, and he feared that the thing would not be fulfilled, to this the verse says "whereof Samuel spoke", and Samuel is trustworthy; rather it was out of humility. This was a hint at the gevir Samuel Ximenes, who conspired over the rabbinate, and it remained closed for forty days ⚠31. He showed me a very old quire, and in it a lament that the Ran made in the year [5]130 [1370] on the destruction, and Don Isaac Levi answered after him in the same manner. The Ran wrote to him that the poets of the city had praised his lament more, and he answered that he did not understand his words, since he knew of no poets there, except that the poets who had died had come to life ⚠32. At his table he boasted that if he knew Torah as he knew poetry, he would have excellence over the Ran in everything; and his wife answered him: do not say so, for the worth of the Ran is greater than yours in everything. The teller came to the Ran's city, Barcelona, and told it all, and the Ran answered this in fine rhetoric, and in his mouth were rebukes for his "greatness from the earth" ⚠33, because he knows a chapter of poetry, and also for having belittled the poets of Barcelona; and he gave thanks to the house of Isaac, "a help against him" [Gen 2:18]. After that the brother of the Rivash took up the inkhorn in poems to rebuke him; the matter went on long, and there was almost a quarrel: one fanned it, and another came and fanned it. I also saw in Amsterdam the Yalkut ha-Makhiri in manuscript on Proverbs, composed by R. Makhir bar Abba Mari; the Siftei Yeshenim mentions it, and it is almost like the Yalkut Shimoni. I also saw the court house, which they call the Casa de la Villa [the town hall]. In it are figures of fruits and subjects and stories (historias), shown as with a finger in the building ⚠34, among them the story of the two harlots before Solomon. It is a large hall (sala), with houses and rooms on this side and on that for several matters: for the bank (banco), for money and for various judges; and in the hall the floor has the science of astronomy drawn on the ground. All is well made and beautiful.

132.3

21 Shevat [Wednesday 18 February 1778]: in Holland it is a day of atonement and fasting and many sermons of reproof, and they call it the day of prayer (biddag) ⚠36. The Jews too hold a sermon, the Ashkenazim and the Sephardim, and Rabbi Shalem preached thus. "Let your garments be always white" etc. [Eccl 9:8]; "a little city" [Eccl 9:14]: this is the body, etc. A man who sees that his expenses are greater than his income is tested by this, whether he is wise or a fool. If he is wise, his eyes will foresee the straits ⚠37: if every year he takes from the capital, the capital is used up and he comes to need other people; so the counsel is to cut expenses until the income is greater than the expense. If he does not take it to heart, he will suddenly be broken without remedy [Prov 29:1]. So it is with the soul that commits sins and does not feel them, because the evil inclination makes things smooth for it, as it is written "a little city" etc. They were forced to expound it of the evil inclination, for according to its plain sense, if the city is little and there are few men in it, why should a great king need to come and send his legions and destroy it? And further, how could a poor man save the city? And if it refers to the affair of Sheba son of Bichri, was not Abel "a city and a mother in Israel" [2 Sam 20:19], while here it is a little city? Therefore they expounded it of the evil inclination. "And built great bulwarks against it": on the dressing of the hair that they do even on the Sabbath, which is also smoothing and smearing, and loses the prayer, and the like. On the Sabbath in the synagogue they talk, etc., and one must beware of the evil inclination. This is "let your garments be always white": the garment of the rabbis. Although "oil", which is the evil inclination that soils the garments, "shall not be lacking" [Eccl 9:8], even so beware that the oil does not fall and soil the white garments. As its plain sense teaches about oil, that it should not fall on the white garments, so its midrashic sense teaches about the evil inclination, that its counsel should not fall and soil the garments of the soul. This is "there is precious treasure and oil in the dwelling of the wise, but a foolish man swallows it up" [Prov 21:20]. Its plain sense: there is a treasure of precious garments, and jars of oil are there; if it is in the dwelling of a wise man, all is in order, for he takes care in coming in and going out, so that the oil does not fall and soil all the precious things of the treasure; but "a foolish man swallows it up", he spoils the precious treasure, for the oil falls and all is spoiled. Its midrashic sense: the precious treasure is the body, within which is the soul, and the oil is the evil inclination, which soils like oil. This holds "in the dwelling of the wise", who knows how to beware of the evil inclination, and the treasure remains precious and the oil in its place; but the foolish man does not take care with the oil, and it falls and spoils the treasure. This is "the wise man's eyes are in his head" [Eccl 2:14]: he takes care with the oil, which is the evil inclination that is on his head, so that it does not make him sin. And blessings for the authorities of the city, the Prince (principe) and his wife and their children, and all the heads. This is the gist of the sermon; it took three quarters of an hour. The weather was cold, and not half as many people came as come on the Sabbath.

133.2

23 Shevat, the night of Friday [the evening of Thursday 19 February 1778]: the Ashkenazi sage came to my house to praise what I had said on the Mordekhai. He told things and exaggerations about the rabbi of Riznitz ⚠39, that he almost had the holy spirit; he went on at length and said that he knew what was in the world in detail, even far off, many parasangs away. In the end a Jewish lad lay with a gentile maidservant, and his father asked him, for he wanted to send him away in flight, and he told him there was nothing to fear, for he was a lad. A short time after, they seized the lad and burned him; and that same day he said that they should prepare his shrouds, for the lad had summoned him to judgment; and at once he died. This Ashkenazi sage is named R. Ẓevi, and he is a disciple of the rabbi R. Jonathan [Eybeschütz], and for this reason the Ashkenazim here pay no attention to him. He was rabbi in Bolechów, and another came and took the rabbinate by the power of the authorities, and he fled here, and he wanders for bread [Job 15:23]. May God have mercy on him.

It is a wonder, above all at this time, when several houses have gone down, and they are worried because of the London funds (rentes), which have fallen. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. May He in His mercy complete it for good and great success, may it be His will.

135.3

8 Adar, the holy Sabbath; at night, the Sabbath of Zakhor [the evening of Friday 6 March 1778], we were at the house of the gevir Signor Isaac Gutierrez, with the sage R. Abraham Ze'evi and the sage R. A. K. ⚠52. Signor D. H. A. ⚠53 did not want to come until I appeased him greatly. The said Signor Isaac gave me a small prayer book of the Attias press as a gift, and we were glad. In the day [Sabbath 7 March] Rabbi Shalem preached before the reading of the Torah, and this is the gist of his sermon, three quarters of an hour: "Remember what Amalek did to you" [Deut 25:17]. The Yalkut: when David remembered that converts are not accepted from Amalek, etc. The trait of hatred is bad, and baseless hatred all the more; and this is the trait of Amalek, who came to fight Israel right after the going out of Egypt. This is the root of Haman, and it is learned from the counsel about Vashti. He explained the verses of Vashti, with the vernacular, points of grammar and introductions, at length. The explanation: if the king had sent a message (recado) to the princess (princesa) Vashti, asking her pardon and that she come, she would have come; also if by the hand of nobles, the princes. But he erred twice: he sent the eunuchs to bring Vashti without any word or appeasement, only that they should take her and bring her. This is what it says: "he commanded Mehuman" etc. "to bring Vashti" etc. [Esth 1:10–11], like one of the vain fellows [2 Sam 6:20]. "But the queen Vashti refused to come at the king's commandment by the eunuchs" [Esth 1:12], for it was a disgrace to her to come without a greeting of peace, and with eunuchs at that. "Therefore was the king very wroth" [ibid.] that he had sent such a mission, "and his anger burned in him", for he was compelled to judge her, and they would condemn her: for all the people are judged by the judges appointed to uphold the laws of Persia and Media, but the king has power to bring her before judges of his own choosing. Therefore "the king said to the wise men" [Esth 1:13], the rabbis, "who knew the times", who judge by a ruling for the hour; and here too, although she was liable to death, they would judge her to life by a ruling for the hour; and this is "who knew the times". And if you ask what reason there was to bring his case to the sages of Israel, to this it says "for so was the king's manner toward all that knew law and judgment" [ibid.]: the king may bring his case to any court he wishes. "What shall we do unto the queen according to law" [Esth 1:15]: he revealed that they should not apply the law in full, only "according to law", to lighten it from her; and also because "the queen Vashti" is of the royal seed of Nebuchadnezzar; "because she has not performed" etc. "by the eunuchs" [ibid.], for she has some claim. "And Memucan answered: not to the king only" etc. [Esth 1:16]: the choice is yours, to bring her before whom you wish; for "all the princes" etc.; if so, it is a matter that touches them all, and you are compelled to judge it like a commoner's case. "If it please the king, let there go forth a royal commandment" [Esth 1:19], that is, that law of the king, that he has leave to judge with any court whatever, let it go forth now "and let it be written among the laws of the Persians" etc., for it touches everyone. From this the baseless hatred of Haman for Vashti is seen. This is the verse "Esther had not shown" etc. [Esth 2:10]: so that he would not kill her and seek a pretext as with Vashti, since he was evil and a hater, and all the more of Israel and of Saul. Also from the Amalekite stranger who came to David his hatred is shown, for he said that he had killed Saul and brought the news of his killing to King David, may he rest in peace [2 Sam 1]. Therefore: "Remember what Amalek did", for he is a hater.

10 Adar, Monday [9 March 1778]: an hour before midday Moses and Aaron, the Signori padrinos, came in a painted and decorated carriage belonging to Signor Capadose, with a carpet on its floor worth several florins. The coachman was dressed in fine clothes with a good new fur coat ⚠55, and the said Signor's servant, William ⚠56, was dressed up, with a new galloon hat, on foot ⚠55; and Solomon Levi Maduro went before him, going round the doors. I was dressed in almost all my Sabbath clothes. Signor Suasso and I sat in the best place, for the carriage was Signor Capadose's. We went to the house of Signor Crasto, the parnas, and he signed with love and honor for seven florins. After him we went to Signor Jacob Teixeira, son-in-law of Signor Ximenes ⚠57, who last year inherited four tons ⚠58, each ton a hundred thousand florins. When he saw us, trembling seized his bones [Job 4:14], and he began to put forward complaints: that Signor Crasto had acted improperly, since he is a parnas, and it was never heard that the parnasim should be the first, for then they oblige the people; and more of the branches and dreams of politics; and further, that he ought to have consulted the second and third about the signing, and this was a slight to his honor; and further, how could he sign seven? Therefore he would not sign, for he has no means; and the like, all with a sad face and full of anger. Signor Aaron, a man among men, scattered and refuted his shaky claims, born of weakness, the discussions of the great chapter of the Miser, and pretexts against "counsel" and against "the churl, and churlishness is with him" [1 Sam 25:25] ⚠59. But with great appeasing, affection and honors he begged him to sign, and he signed with trembling, sweat and shuddering. For indeed the doubts of men that are like a handful of beans, and no secret of stinginess is too hard for him [Dan 4:6]; the laws of miserliness are fluent and in order in his mouth; thirteen camel-loads of doubts about terefot [b. Ḥullin 95b] he decides leniently, and against them he has earned the right to add laws of "covering" [y. Shabbat 1:4], thirteen camel-loads of general rules and particulars, extensions and limitations of stinginess ⚠60. But in the end he was forced to sign against his will, for in summer he lives in Maarssen ⚠61 in the houses of Signor Aaron Capadose, and he asks him to repair and build, and Signor Aaron did not want to; so he did his will, so as to have a mouth with him ⚠62. Even so it was a miracle, for one of the branches of miserliness is that when he sees the future he is obliged to give, judging "as he is there" [Gen 21:17]; and when his eyes look far ahead, it is a law of indirect damage not to give, or he tells everyone that he is a wise man who sees what is coming [b. Tamid 32a] ⚠63. So this is a wonder, that he forgot his learning, and the chapter of "less" slipped his mind ⚠64. May my mouth speak thanks and praise to the Lord according to all that the Lord has bestowed on me [Isa 63:7]. For before I came, gevirim were raising their voices ⚠65, and an old gevir, Telles da Costa, began and said: "The emissary so-and-so who is coming, I do not hate him; but since we sent for him to be rabbi of our congregation and he refused, no person of honor among the elders of the gate should become his padrino." And when I came, the Lord gave me favor, and they did me great honor, and without any trouble the padrinos came forward of themselves, from the greatest of the rich and important men of the holy congregation and of the lineage of the Nation ⚠66. Moreover, all their business is in shares, and Signor Capadose is their head in importance, and he deals in thirty tons of his own; and the orphans of Niso ⚠67, Signor Elisha Pereira, of whom all the people without exception say there is none like him in this matter; and Signor Suasso too is of a great family, and his father-in-law Signor Fonseca is rich, one of the great shareholders. With all the emissaries, the matter of the padrinos is completed only after some time, in which they themselves, intermediaries and the rabbi take trouble, and they are not of the important ones. Moreover the time is treacherous and all of them are in distress, for all their business is in shares and all their capital is in London, and everything is at the lowest fall, for London has been turned into terrors because of the war of the Americans who rebelled. But these are His mighty deeds, these are His awesome deeds: from the Lord the matter came [Gen 24:50], twenty-five days after I came here, that Signor Aaron agreed to be padrino without any trouble at all. On the forty-second day after I came I went with them to sign; give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. Afterwards we went to the house of Signor Isaac Teixeira; although he had been ill on his sickbed [Ps 41:4] for more than ten days, he received us, and his son-in-law took the register into the bedroom and he signed. After that we went round all the men of the Mahamad, and not one was at home, or some were and did not want to receive us, and the cards were left in each one's house.

He said to him: "Do you want me to send for the drugs in your presence?" He said yes; he went and bought them, and gold came out, the amount of several dinars. His eyes lit up [1 Sam 14:27], and he did it again twice. He was grateful to him and taught him the secret of the mirror, while the other kept saying he had no need of it, and almost against his will he taught it to him; and at once he left.

ניסן 1. א' ליל מש"ק ויקרא הייתי אצל ה' שלם וסיפר לנו כמה סיפורי גוזמות. שגוי הכה שור והמיתו ופתחה הארץ ובלעו. וגוי שראה שבגורן היו הרבה עכברים מאד וציוה לשרוף הגורן עם העכברים. ותכף נסתמא. וכפרי א' מצא בקעה מלח עפר בקמח והיו נזונים ממנו כמה חדשים כל העיר בשנת בצורת. וקבוץ עופות חסידה והמיתו נקבה שישבה על ביצי אווז. וערמת גוי א' עני שלקח מהמלך אלף דינרי זהב ושחקם ועירב עם כמה סמים ועפר דק והלך לעיר א' אצל מי שידע סוד המראה להטיחו בטיח מאחוריו ובתחילה הלך לבעלי חניות הבשמים והסמים ואביזרייהו ומכר להם מעפר זה ואמר שהוא סם מעירו וקרא לו שם ומכרו בזול ולא נשאר חנות מהם שלא מכר. ואח"ך הלך כמה פעמים אצל האומן הנז' וא"ל יש לי סוד ולא מצאתי אדם צנוע ובעל סוד כמוך לגלות סתרי שאני יודע לעשות כימיה שתקח חצי ליטרא מדבר פלוני (כשם שקרא לו) ותערב עם סם פ' ופ' ותן באש ותכף יצא איזה זהב. וא"ל רצונך שאשלח להסמים בפניך וא' כן והלך וקנאם ויצא זהב שיעור כמה דינרין. ויאורו עיניו ושוב עשה כן ב' פעמים. והחזיק לו טובה ולמדו סוד המראה והוא היה או' שאין לו צורך וכמעט ע"כ למדו ותכף נסע.

140.4

In the day [Sunday 29 March 1778] we went round with the padrinos for three hours, and most of the householders did not receive us; may the Lord bring it to a good end, may it be His will. First we went to Rabbi Shalem, and the padrinos told him that they ought to have gone to him on the first day with the elders of the gate. He answered: "I do not suspect that you meant to slight me." Afterwards we went to the overseers of the town ⚠88, and from there to the other individuals; and with whoever did not receive us they left a card, and they left it as a q. ʿa. ⚠89

וביום הלכנו עם הפאדרינוס ג' שעות ורוב הבעה"ב לא קבלונו ה' יגמור לטובה כי"ר ובתחילה הלכנו אצל ה' שלם וא"ל הפאדרינוס שהיה ראוי ללכת אצלו ביום הראשון עם זקני שער והשיב איני חושד שלבזותני כיונתם ואח"ך הלכנו לחזני מתא ומשם לשאר יחידים וכל מי שלא קבל היו מניחים אצלו קארטה והניחו בתורת ק"ע.

Notes and uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 טובים ועניים — ועניים may be "and poor" or "and humble" (ענוים); read as humble, since they did him honor.
  • ⚠2 האשכנזים — "Ashkenazim" here means the people of the German-speaking and imperial lands he was now entering (the Austrian Netherlands), not Jews.
  • ⚠3 פלטין דוד האינפיראדור — Literally "the palace of the uncle of the Emperor": probably Charles of Lorraine, governor of the Austrian Netherlands, uncle of Emperor Joseph II, whose palace and square were then new in Brussels.
  • ⚠4 ליפמאן נתן רואי בוטיש — Name as printed; רואי בוטיש is not explained (a surname or a street?). Not identified.
  • ⚠5 בעיר יפה מילכה — The first letter may also be read ט (טילכה). Probably Mechelen (Malines), between Brussels and Antwerp.
  • ⚠6 עפר וטיט הנרוק — הנרוק is not a known word; perhaps a misprint (for הגרוע, "the worst"?). Rendered by the sense, "deep mud".
  • ⚠7 אחר שלש שעות שיצאנו ממורדיק — So printed. By the route, the border of Brabant was passed three hours after leaving Antwerp, on the way to Moerdijk; ממורדיק may be a slip.
  • ⚠8 אני שליח לערי איטליא — So printed. He was the emissary of Hebron; perhaps he means that his mission was not to Holland's Ashkenazim, or that he was only passing through. Translated literally.
  • ⚠9 וכאשר באתי מפאריץ עד כאן אני אשלם האושפיזא כאן — The rabbi's words reported in the first person of the Ḥida: as he paid his own way from Paris, he should pay the inn here too.
  • ⚠10 מחמודי רפאל — "My darling Raphael": probably his son Raphael Isaiah Azulai. Not certain.
  • ⚠11 סי' חר"א — Initials, not resolved; elsewhere חר"א is "the sage R. Abraham" (Ze'evi), but Ze'evi was then in Amsterdam. Perhaps Signor Ariani, who travelled with him.
  • ⚠12 לכפר דראגאהו — A village on the boat route from Rotterdam to Amsterdam; not identified.
  • ⚠13 בשוק בריט היסטראם — Printed בריט היסטראם; probably the Breestraat (Jodenbreestraat) in the Jewish quarter. Not certain.
  • ⚠14 בסאליאה — A vehicle; perhaps from Spanish or Portuguese silla, a sedan chair, or the horse-drawn sledge used in the streets of Amsterdam. Translated "carriage", as in the 1885 parts.
  • ⚠15 הג' ר' צבי אבר' ברלין — אבר' read as av beit din: Ẓevi Hirsch Levin (Hart Lyon), rabbi of Berlin from 1772, brother of Saul Löwenstamm; both were grandsons of Ḥakham Ẓevi Ashkenazi.
  • ⚠16 תלמיד הרב יהונתן — Probably Jonathan Eybeschütz. Not certain.
  • ⚠17 בענין חקה ותורה — Not explained; probably a Talmudic discussion of the terms ḥukkah and torah (Freimann points to b. Kiddushin).
  • ⚠18 סי' לוב'ו — Read as the family name Lobo; not identified.
  • ⚠19 ואח"כ עבר לפני ממש מאחוריו — Unclear: "right in front of me, from behind him" (perhaps behind the bridegroom or the cantor). Translated literally.
  • ⚠20 בורנו טוטון — Turkish burun tütünü, "nose tobacco", snuff; abbreviated בב"ט below.
  • ⚠21 שהוא יועץ ועצל ונעשה יארא"ן — Isaiah is probably Isaiah Karanfil, who had brought him from Rotterdam. יועץ may be a misprint; יארא"ן is perhaps Turkish yaran, a jesting companion. The sense is not certain.
  • ⚠22 ות"ח שניו — So printed; read שנים, "two scholars", as Freimann suggests.
  • ⚠23 משה גומייראס — Family name as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠24 וחר"א הכהן — Initials, read as "the sage R. A. ha-Kohen"; the same man as חרא"ך at 135:17, named both times beside Ze'evi. Full name not known.
  • ⚠25 ואין להמיתו מטובה (?) — Freimann's question mark is in the text. מטובה is unclear; perhaps "on account of the good (the salvation)", that is on the day of salvation.
  • ⚠26 מהרימ"ט ח"א — מהרימ"ט = R. Joseph di Trani (Maharit), first part of his responsa. The 1885 printing has a different reading here (R. Moses di Trani).
  • ⚠27 הירניקא אוף גוולט — Name of a (probably non-Jewish) merchant house, as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠28 סי' קאראסטו — Read as Crasto (de Crasto), a Portuguese family of Amsterdam. Not certain.
  • ⚠29 סי' ואלזידו — Name as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠30 וכשמלך באמשט' — Literally "when he reigned in Amsterdam": read as when he took up the rabbinate there.
  • ⚠31 שמואל שימינים קשר על הרבנות ונשאר סתום מ' יום — Read as Samuel Ximenes; "it remained closed forty days" is unclear, perhaps the rabbinate stayed vacant forty days.
  • ⚠32 אך אף שחיו המשוררים שמתו — Obscure as printed; the rendering is a guess (a sarcastic reply).
  • ⚠33 גדול"ו מן האר"ש — A play on a phrase ("his growth from the earth"), marked with quotation signs in the print; the sense is his arrogance.
  • ⚠34 מראים באצבע מבנין — Unclear; perhaps the reliefs are worked into the building itself.
  • ⚠35 ומשה למד — Printed ומשה ("and Moses"); read as ומשם, "and from there", which the sense needs.
  • ⚠36 יום בידאה — Dutch biddag, the national day of prayer and fasting.
  • ⚠37 עיניו תחינה מצרי"ם — So printed, with the quotation sign on מצרים; read as "his eyes will see (תחזינה) the straits", a play on "the wise man's eyes are in his head" (Eccl 2:14).
  • ⚠38 סי' דכ"א — Initials as printed; Freimann identifies him as David Kohen Azevedo.
  • ⚠39 רב ריזניץ — Place name as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠40 ביום בערב סי' הרופא — The sentence has no verb; "there came" is supplied.
  • ⚠41 א' חצוה — Printed חצוה; read תצוה, the week of Tetzaveh, in which 2 Adar 5538 (Sunday) falls.
  • ⚠42 והייתי מחרים נגדו — The print seems to read מחרים (the OCR gave מתרים); read as מתריס, "arguing against".
  • ⚠43 הסופייאלים — Apparently a family or a pair of travellers from Bordeaux; not identified.
  • ⚠44 אברהם גומים — Printed with ג or נ (גונים/גומים); read as the name Gomes. Not identified.
  • ⚠45 וציום לדרוש ש' — ש' is unresolved (perhaps שם, "there", or a name); the reference is probably to an earlier emissary.
  • ⚠46 מסו' ק"י ושבועות — ק"י read as Kiddushin; uncertain.
  • ⚠47 סי' פראדום — Read as the name Prado; uncertain.
  • ⚠48 שניים בן סי' יאודה — שניים is unclear: a name (Senior?) or a misprint.
  • ⚠49 סי' אדו — So printed (אדו); not identified, perhaps a short form of a family name.
  • ⚠50 בסאליאה — A conveyance, probably a light carriage or sedan chair; the word is not identified.
  • ⚠51 וע"כ דבר בבת א' — Unclear; perhaps "so that everything was served at one time", or ע"כ = על כל.
  • ⚠52 וחרא"ך — Initials as printed; the same man as "the sage R. A. ha-Kohen" at 130:7. Full name not known.
  • ⚠53 סי' דה"א — Initials as printed; probably the same man as סי' דכ"א of 22 Shevat (David ha-Kohen Azevedo).
  • ⚠54 דברי הר"ם בהרמת הדשן — So printed (הר"ם, הרמת). Probably a ruling of R. M[eir] or of the Rambam as quoted in Terumat ha-Deshen (Israel Isserlein); not identified.
  • ⚠55 ופליסה ... וקאפילו גאלון חדש ברגליו — פליסה read as Italian pelliccia, a fur coat; קאפילו גאלון as a hat (cappello) trimmed with galloon (gold braid). ברגליו read as "on foot"; it could also mean "at his feet".
  • ⚠56 אילייאם — Read as the servant's name, William; not certain.
  • ⚠57 טיקשיירה ... שימינים — The OCR had מיקשיירה; the print reads טיקשיירה, Teixeira, as with Isaac Teixeira below. שימינים read as Ximenes.
  • ⚠58 4 טונילים — Dutch ton, a sum of 100,000 florins, as the text itself explains. The figure 4 is printed as a digit.
  • ⚠59 ילידי הרפ"ה וסוגיות פרק הקומ"ץ רבא ותואנות עלי עצו"ר ועלי נב"ל ונבלה עמו — Satire on the miser with abbreviation marks used for puns: הרפ"ה ("the weak one", 2 Sam 21:16), a mock tractate chapter "the great Miser" (קומץ), עצו"ר and נב"ל (1 Sam 25:25, Nabal the churl). The exact sense of each pun is uncertain.
  • ⚠60 ספיקי דגברי דדמו לקומצ"י גריס וכל רז בנבלות לא אניס ליה ... הלכות עממות — Mock-Talmudic language. Read by sense: גריס, רז and אניס (Dan 4:6) where the print looks like גרים, רו, אנים. Freimann refers to b. Ḥullin 95b (the thirteen camel-loads) and y. Shabbat 1:4 (laws of covering food, עימום), punning on עמום/קמצנות. The rendering is approximate.
  • ⚠61 במארשא — Read as Maarssen on the Vecht near Utrecht, where rich Amsterdam Jews had summer houses; not certain.
  • ⚠62 להיות לו פה — Literally "to have a mouth with him": to be on speaking terms, or to have a claim on him.
  • ⚠63 מחייב לתת דן באשר הוא שם ... דין גרמא — Satirical rules of the miser: "as he is there" (Gen 21:17, Ishmael judged by his present state) and a play on dina de-garmi (indirect damage). The sense is approximate.
  • ⚠64 פרק בצי"ר — Printed with abbreviation marks; read as a mock chapter "less" (בציר, Aramaic). Not certain.
  • ⚠65 היו גבירים בקוליהו — Read as בקולייהו, "in their voices": raising complaints. Not certain.
  • ⚠66 ומהוייחוס די לא נאסון — Spanish/Portuguese "de la Nación": the Portuguese Jewish Nation of Amsterdam. Reading of the first word not certain.
  • ⚠67 ויתמי ניסו — "The orphans of Niso" (a family name?) is not clear; ניסו occurs again on p. 140. Perhaps Elisha Pereira acts for the orphans' estate. Not identified.
  • ⚠68 וחבלים נפלו לי — A play on Ps 16:6 ("the lines have fallen to me"): here חבלים as "pangs".
  • ⚠69 מינה — The text does not say to what R. Aaron was appointed; probably as R. Abraham's deputy or agent in Amsterdam.
  • ⚠70 דברי יוסף זבח השלמים לרבו הרב המג"ן. שערי ירוש' — Zevaḥ ha-Shelamim is the book of R. Moses Galante (Amsterdam 1698), so המג"ן = Moses Galante. Whether Sha'arei Yerushalayim is another book printed by R. Joseph or a description is not clear.
  • ⚠71 שחמת — Printed as one word; Freimann reads it as שחכם מרדכי תמה, "that the sage Mordecai Tama". Followed here.
  • ⚠72 חרח"ם — Initials of a sage, perhaps ח"ר ח' מ'; not resolved.
  • ⚠73 ח"ר צבי הפולאקו — The print reads הפולאקו, "the Pole" (the OCR had המולאקו). Perhaps Ẓevi (Hirsch) of Bolechów; not certain.
  • ⚠74 בסאמאדה — Perhaps Spanish/Portuguese zamarra/samarra, a sheepskin coat or wrap; not certain.
  • ⚠75 ר' ליב רידיש — So printed; the 1885 translation has Leib Hirsch. Not resolved.
  • ⚠76 לסי' חד"א — Initials; Freimann reads them as Ḥakham David Azevedo. The 1885 translation took them as a name, Ḥada.
  • ⚠77 בפרצופין חגרין — חגרין, "cripples"; Freimann suggests הגרין, Hagarenes (Arabs), which fits the Turks that follow.
  • ⚠78 ירידת הרווחים בלונדרים — The fall of the stocks in London; probably the fall of British funds in March 1778, when France's treaty with the American colonies became known.
  • ⚠79 מהנדזין — So printed; not a known word. From the context, "hesitating" or "grumbling".
  • ⚠80 סי' אלארים — So printed. Perhaps Alvares (אלואריש), the Ḥida's friend Joseph Alvares; not certain.
  • ⚠81 מעשה אורג ניסו — Read as a name, Niso (compare "the orphans of Niso" on p. 137), with the father whose honor was said to be slighted. The sense of the sentence is not certain.
  • ⚠82 נואיטים — Read as annuities (the British government annuity stocks); not certain.
  • ⚠83 קונפאניאה די הידייא — Freimann reads הינדיא, India: the East India Company.
  • ⚠84 לערך כל ק' ר"מ בק"י — The abbreviations ר"מ בק"י are not resolved; rendered as "at a rate of so much per hundred".
  • ⚠85 פיבילזמאן — So printed here (twice); the 1885 printing has Ebelsman, whose form is kept. Not identified.
  • ⚠86 קארלו בירנאנדי — Freimann gives also the reading בירנאנבי. Not identified.
  • ⚠87 בקעה מלח עפר בקמח — So printed (מלח, בקמח); read as מלא עפר כקמח, "full of earth like flour".
  • ⚠88 לחזני מתא — Aramaic "overseers of the town"; probably the wardens or community officers.
  • ⚠89 בתורת ק"ע — Abbreviation not resolved; perhaps the card was left as a formal notice or record of the visit.
