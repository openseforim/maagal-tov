26.8

21 Kislev [Thursday 5 December 1754], week of Vayeshev: from Zons we reached the city of Düsseldorf, and they did not let us enter. We spent the Sabbath outside the city in a gentile inn, hungry and thirsty. On Friday I had already sent a letter by a gentile to the rabbi of the community, entreating him to send us a cup of wine for kiddush and some cooked dish to go with the bread, and he turned me away empty. Although I sent him the register and the Frankfurt letters, which speak up for me with full force and great merit, and the community sent a donation by the beadle, even so he scrawled on a small paper, at the urging of the gentile my messenger: "Impossible." On the Sabbath night I was sleeping on a chair, and the gentile innkeeper came and woke me, saying to me: Get out of here. Sullen and angry [1 Kings 20:43], I was alarmed and went out, and he seated me in another house whose windows were broken. In the end I learned that his wife, that Zeresh, had gone into labor and screamed in her pangs, and gave birth to a daughter for Gehenna. I had grinding sorrow on that Sabbath; this too is for the good.

כסליו 21. וישב יום ה' מצונס והגענו לעיר דיזילדורף ולא הניחונו ליכנס ושבתנו מחוץ לעיר באושפיזת גוים רעבים גם צמאים וכבר שלחתי יום הששי ע"י ערל כתב להרב של הק"ק לחלות פניו ישלח לנו כוס יין לקידוש ואיזה תבשיל ללפת את הפת והשיב פני ריקם. ואף ששלחתי לו הפנקס וכתבי פרנקפורט אשר בכל תוקף מליצים יושר וזכות מאד ושלחו הק"ק נדבה ע"י השמש. מ"מ שרטט וכתב בנייר קטן להפצרת הערל שלוחי: אי אפשר: וליל ש"ק הייתי ישן על הכסא והנה בא בעל האושפיזא הגוי ויעירני באמור אלי צא מכאן. ואני סר וזעף נכהלתי ויצאתי והושיבני בבית אחר ופתחי החלונות שבורים וסוף דבר ידעתי כי זרש אשתו תחיל תזעק בחבליה והמליטה בת לגיהנם והיה לי צע"ר כותש בשבת ההוא גם זו לטוב.

26.13

28 Kislev [Thursday 12 December 1754], week of Mikketz: from Xanten we set out after midday, and we came with the post to the city of Cleve about two hours into the night. We were in distress, for no one would take us in. We went into a Jewish inn, and he did not want to receive us, for he had no room; we threw ourselves at his feet to give us a place to sit. As we sat in distress and in straits, I heard that the custom of the town is that when travelers and wayfarers come in, they must go to the governor and tell him that they wish to lodge there; and if they will stay in the city a day or two or more, they say so to the governor and he gives them a note. Whoever does not follow this custom, there is one law for him [Esth 4:11]: instead of favor, disgrace; he is punished as the governor sees fit. I was very distressed at the news when it came, and at that hour whom could we send, and who would go for us, walking in darkness [Isa 50:10] close to midnight? I pressed hard, to the point of shame, the Jew who stood with us to do us the great kindness of going with the servant Samuel to the authorities. They went and came back, and with them two distinguished men whose looks showed that they were men of mark [Zech 3:8]. They questioned me ⚠3: Where do you come from, and what is your occupation? And David spoke: I am an emissary of the rabbis of the holy city of Hebron, may it be rebuilt and established, and here I have letters from Frankfurt that testify greatly to my mission. When they read the Frankfurt letters, I was called to come to their house, in their courts and their castles [Gen 25:16]. We girded ourselves [Num 31:3] and went to their house. These men are wholehearted [Gen 34:21]; they are the sons of the famous qatzin R. Bendit Gumpel of Nijmegen. I lodged in the house of one of them, the praised qatzin R. Feibelman, and they did me great honor; he put me in the chambers of his imagery [Ezek 8:12], royal rooms. In this city of Cleve I found my mother's sister, Mrs. Gitli, daughter of a king ⚠4, my lord my grandfather, the rabbi, the pious kabbalist, the holy man of God, R. Joseph Bialer of blessed memory, who came to the holy city of Jerusalem in the company of the holy rabbi R. Judah Ḥasid of blessed memory, about sixty years ago, more or less. I asked her what our kinship is with R. Falk of blessed memory, author of the Derishah and the Sema, and she told me: the rabbi R. Judah, son or grandson of the Sema, had a daughter named Hindel. Mrs. Hindel had a daughter named Sarah, and Mrs. Sarah married the pious rabbi R. Nathanel of blessed memory; she was the second wife of R. Nathanel of blessed memory. R. Nathanel had from the said Mrs. Sarah a daughter named Hindel, who was the wife of my grandfather R. Joseph of blessed memory; and his daughter, my lady my mother Mrs. Sarah, may her soul be bound in the bond of life, was named after her grandmother, the wife of R. Nathanel. The said Mrs. Gitli, my mother's sister, is the daughter of my grandfather by another wife. She married the rabbi R. Falk, son of R. Nathanel of blessed memory, who was head of the rabbinical court in Cleve ⚠5; he is from the first wife of R. Nathanel, named Mindeli. After her Mrs. Gitli has a daughter, who is married in Xanten; and my aunt Mrs. Gitli also has a son, the young man R. Judah, who is in Cleve. The pious rabbi R. Nathanel of blessed memory has an approbation in the book Me'il Shemu'el, the indexes and abridgement of the Shelah, and in the Midrash Rabbah. He was head of the yeshivah in the holy community of Noorden near Amsterdam, and his name is great in Israel; may his merit protect us, amen, may it be His will. There I saw the book Meshivat Nefesh of R. Jacob Tzemaḥ of blessed memory, on transmigrations, in manuscript.

27.1

3 Tevet [Tuesday 17 December 1754], week of Vayigash: we set out from Cleve and came to Nijmegen the same day. I lodged with the qatzin, the parnas ⚠6, R. Elia son of R. Bendit, brother of the said R. Feibelman. There I was bowed down at what I heard [Isa 21:3]: a fire broke out in the holy community of Frankfurt am Main on Hanukkah in the house of R. Reuben Scheuer mentioned above. His house and all he had were burned, and his wife and daughter were burned. The young man R. Wolf mentioned above leaped from several storeys, fell and died; and R. Reuben and his son R. Judah threw themselves down, broke their legs and stayed alive. My soul almost left me; my spirit was broken [Job 17:1] and much alarmed at the burning which the Lord has kindled [Lev 10:6]. And if so to those who do His will, etc. A calamity came and cut off the tale of distress, and sorrows clung for this beauty that wastes in the earth [Berakhot 5b]: my friend and beloved R. Wolf, whose love for me was wonderful, more than the love of women [2 Sam 1:26]. I was like a man astonished [Jer 14:9], wrapped in bitterness, and my soul was caught in a thicket [Gen 22:13] of grief and sighing; covered and entangled ⚠7, stricken and stricken again, and blessing: Blessed is the true Judge; He is righteous and His judgment is righteous. My prayer shall return to my bosom [Ps 35:13]: may the soul of R. Wolf mentioned above and the souls of the burned be bound in the bond of life, to rest and to inheritance [Deut 12:9]. May Heaven have mercy on the remnant, R. Reuben and his only son; may He restore their wealth to them, and may healing always be given them ⚠8 in all the land. To return to our matter: great was our distress over our books, which we had deposited with the pious R. Reuben mentioned above; and I, the least, had also deposited with him close to three hundred florins of the mission. We had almost despaired of them, and accepted the judgment upon us.

טבת 3. ויגש ג' נסענו מקליף ובאנו לנאיימיגין בו ביום ונתאכסנתי אצל הקצין פ"ו ר' אליא בן ר' בענדיט אחיו של ר' פייבילמן הנז' ושם נעויתי משמוע כי אש יצאה בק"ק פפד"מ בחנוכה בבית ר' ראובן שאייר הנז"ל ונשרף ביתו וכל אשר לו ואשתו ובתו נשרפו והב"ח ר' וואלף הנז"ל דלג כמה קומות ונפל ומת ור' ראובן ובנו ר' יאודה הפילו עצמם ונשברו רגליהם ונשארו חיים. וכמעט שלא פרחה נשמתי רוחי חובלה ומאד נכהלה על השרפה אשר שרף ה' ואם לעושי רצונו כך וכו' אתא אי"ד ופסיק ספור הצי"ר ודבקו צערי"ם על האי שופרא דבלי בארעא הוא ידידי וחמודי ר' וואלף אשר נפלאת אהבתו לי מאהבת נשים ואהי כאיש נדהם מרור כורך ואנא נפשאי נאחז בסבך יגון ואנחה סכיך וסריך חביט חביט ובריך ברוך דיין האמת צדיק הוא וצדיק דינו ותפלתי על חיכ"י תשוב תהי נפשו של ר' וואלף הנז' ונפשות הנשרפות צרורות בצרור החיים אל המנוחה ואל הנחלה ומן השמים ירחמו על שארי הפליטה ר' ראובן ובנו יחידו וישב עליהם את הונ"ם וארוכ"ת תמיד נתנה להם נכרא"ו בכל הארץ. אתאן לדידן כי גדל צערינו על ספרינו אשר הפקדנו ביד החסיד ר' ראובן הנז' וגם אני הצעיר p. 28 הפקדתי בידו קרוב לשלש מאות פלוריני"ס מן השליחות וכמעט נתיאשנו מהם והצדקנו עלינו את הדין.

טבת 9. ויחי ב' נסענו מנאיימיגין סמוך למנחה ובאנו בלילה לארנאהיים.

28.2

11 Tevet [Wednesday 25 December 1754], week of Vayeḥi: from Arnhem to Amersfoort. There, at Amersfoort, I found the sage R. David Meldola, who had come out to meet me, he and one man with him, from Amsterdam; so he said. But in truth I recognized that he came for his own needs, to sell the books he had printed. I suffered damage through him: I was forced to travel with him to Amsterdam, and the qatzinim, because they were forced to give him a donation for his books, gave less to the mission.

טבת 11. ויחי ד' מארנאהיים לאמספורט ושם לאמספורט מצאתי לח"ר דוד מילדולה שיצא לקראתי הוא וחד דעמיה מאמשטרדם אלו דבריו ולפי האמת הכרתי שבא לצורך עצמו לחלק ספריו אשר הדפיס והיה לי נזק בסיבתו שהוכרחתי ליסע עמו לאמשטרדם והקצינין על ידי שהוכרחו לתת לו נדבה בעבור ספריו מיעטו בנדבת השליחות.

28.3

12 Tevet [Thursday 26 December 1754], week of Vayeḥi: we set out from Amersfoort and came on Thursday night [the evening of 26 December] to Amsterdam, and I lodged in the house of the man mentioned above. My mouth shall be full of praises to the One awesome in deeds, who does great things, who showed us His many kindnesses so wonderfully. We found in Amsterdam the two chests of books that R. Reuben mentioned above had sent from Frankfurt am Main. It seems that on the very day whose night his house was burned, the spirit of the Lord moved him to send them from his house, and it was a miracle. Also the deposit of the money I had with him, as mentioned: he promised me in writing to send it, and within a few days he passed to his eternal home, alas for those who are lost; and the dear son R. Judah, may he live, paid in full and sent it to me through the qatzin R. Johanan Hanover. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. I am unworthy of all the kindnesses and all the truth that He has done for His servant [Gen 32:11]. In the city of Amsterdam the Lord gave me grace and favor in the eyes of the officers and deputies of the Sephardi community, and He put it in the heart of two golden pipes [Zech 4:12], the gevir the sage R. Isaac Teixeira de Mattos and the gevir Signor Moses de Raphael Mendes da Costa, to work for my mission; they did, and they succeeded. May the Lord repay their deeds, amen. On the Sabbath nights, all the time I was in Amsterdam, I was invited by the gevirim; on the Sabbath days I did not want to go, because of the foods cooked on fire and the like. Two Sabbaths I was with Signor Jacob Nunes Henriques, two Sabbaths with Signor Samuel Bueno de Mesquita, one Sabbath with Signor the sage R. Abraham da Veiga, one Sabbath with Signor David Henriques de Castro, one Sabbath with Signor Aaron de Jacob de Prado, one Sabbath with Signor Solomon and Signor Aaron Lombroso, one Sabbath with Signor Samuel Levi Ximenes, one Sabbath with Signor the sage R. Moses de Gandis ⚠9, and one Sabbath with Signor Isaac Teixeira de Mattos mentioned above. The Purim meal I had with Signor Aaron de Prado mentioned above. The gevirim and the sages did me great honor, and I had many friends; above all Signor Jacob Nunes Henriques, Signor Samuel Bueno de Mesquita mentioned above and his brother Signor R. Aaron and his household ⚠10, and Signor the sage R. Moses Raphael Ḥizkiau da Veiga. They did wonderfully in kindness and truth with me; may their reward ⚠11 be good for them all their days. But I passed through bitter waters with the sage R. D. M. ⚠12, the master of the house, for he wanted to swallow me up for nothing: over the books I bought for others, over the books I bought for myself, and over my plan to print my small book there. In my poverty I took great care to escape from his hand with honor and not to come to a quarrel with him. I bore a great deal, and I give thanks and praise [Dan 2:23] that He gave me strength to bear it and good sense to conduct myself properly, and He saved me from his hand. He wanted to accompany me to The Hague and London, meaning to sell his books as he had done at Amersfoort, to harm my mission and the like. This is why I left Amsterdam almost on the eve of Passover, at a time when the master of the house could not take his things out of there. Even so he took from me 14 florins every week; this was by order of the leaders and good men of the city, who arbitrated between us. When I reckon ⚠13 for myself, I find that the total of the florins he took came to 186, and the sign is the word kof ["monkey", 186] before the D. M., "Kum puk" ["get up, go out"] ⚠14. While I was still in Amsterdam I considered my ways [Ps 119:59], to set my fasts day after day in the days of Shovavim, as is our custom; and I saw myself thin and stricken, extremely weak from the weariness of trouble upon trouble that had passed over my head in Germany. I had already fasted three days, and on the third day I could not look at a book from great weakness. Therefore, so that study should not cease from my mouth, Megillat Ta'anit was annulled ⚠15 that year. Besides, the winter was dreadful and strong [Dan 7:7], and the cold was like wood; all the streams froze with the dreadful ice, and their surfaces counted as dry land. People went to and fro on them, and tents were set along the streams; the rivers, all of them, lifted up ⚠16. When a man got up early in the morning holding a jug of water, it was not poured from vessel to vessel [Jer 48:11], and its water ⚠17 turned to frozen ice, as if no water had ever passed through it. The hand writes, and the pen-holder of the inkhorn ⚠18 cannot stand: the inkhorn has no ink; the bows of the mighty are broken [1 Sam 2:4]. This went on for more than seventy days. I will recount the kindnesses of the Lord, according to all that the Lord has bestowed on me [Isa 63:7], and He saved my soul from death. One morning I rose early to go to the splendid and magnificent synagogue of the Sephardi community. It was very far from the inn, for one must cross three bridges and several alleys. As I walked in my innocence, I looked, and behold, desolation [Jer 4:23], and the ground was slippery, for early that morning a little fine rain had fallen, and the rain was on the earth upon the dreadful ice, and the ground was made like natron and soap [Jer 2:22]. I saw people falling ⚠19 on the ground, for all flesh had corrupted its way upon the earth [Gen 6:12]. Even so my heart did not fear, since I was walking on an errand of a commandment, and to the house of God we walk in the throng [Ps 55:15]; only I watched my steps and walked gently and very lightly. As I came to the alley of the synagogue, I lifted my eyes and behold, a carriage was coming toward me at a run. The custom of the city is that everyone is pushed aside before the carriages, for the land is wide [Gen 34:21] and the carter cares for no one, even if he crushes his skull [Judg 9:53]. So my distress was great, for I had to run and turn off the road: woe is me from the carriage, and woe is me for the road was very slippery, and on one side was the river. I remember that I turned off the road. Apparently I fell as I turned, and was struck on the pavement on my temple; some Jew happened to be there, and he lifted me and took me to the synagogue. I felt nothing of all this; only I found myself in the synagogue, very alarmed, agitated and trembling, without knowing what or why. After a short time the servant came, and I asked him what had happened to me, for he had been with me on the way, but far from me. He answered that I had fallen and they had carried me and brought me; he saw it from afar and shouted, but had no strength to come to me because of the bad road. I opened the prayer book to pray, and I looked, and I could not see the letters. From the great confusion and distress at not seeing the letters, no breath was left in me [Dan 10:17]. I began to pray by heart, one up and seven down ⚠20, and after the prayer my reason returned to me [Dan 4:31], and the light of my eyes too was with me [Ps 38:11]. Blessed is God the Savior; I will greatly thank the Lord with my mouth, and among the many I will praise Him [Ps 109:30]. Blessed be the name of the Lord from now and for ever [Ps 113:2]. In Amsterdam I preached on the portion Va'era. There I saw the book Tzitzat Novel Tzevi, printed in secret.

9 Iyar [Sunday 20 April 1755], week of Emor: in the evening we reached the river of Harwich, the beginning of England. All the goods and passengers of the packet boat are brought to the customs house. I took out some silver coins of English money to pay the customs officers for the permits, and put the purse in the belt at my waist. Then I came to the house where the goods were, and they opened my trunk and searched it thoroughly, through all the clothes and between the folds of the turban, very slowly. They took all the letters of the mission and brought them to the governor, for there was already a smell of war from France. They searched the sailors after them, and each man took off his shoe [Ruth 4:7] and his stockings, to look in the hidden places. On one of them they found a Dutch gold coin, and they cut it and threw it away. In my poverty I was very distressed and frightened, for I had about one hundred and eighty Dutch gold coins in the money belt on me. Meanwhile the customs man searched my clothes from outside and heard the sound of coins, glad and laughing ⚠22. I was frightened, and then I remembered that the purse with the English money was in my belt. So I took it and threw it before him; when he saw that it was English coin, he was ashamed and gave it back. I brought a letter of recommendation to the postmaster there, and he spoke well for me, and they returned the letters to me and did not see the gold coins on me. Praises to God, blessed be He; what shall I render to the Lord for all His bounties to me [Ps 116:12]. We stayed there in a gentile inn two days.

31.1

11 Iyar [Tuesday 22 April 1755], the week of Emor: at midday we left Harwich. After we had gone out of the gate of the town, and the calesso was ready, I wished to pass water, and for modesty's sake I climbed a wall of stones. Because I was still in sight of everyone, I walked a little, and I saw in a field on the other side a low place, somewhat deep, and it seemed to me to be solid ground. I jumped over the stone wall that divided them and put one foot down on the ground, and my foot and thigh sank into mud and dung: it seems that place was a slope that collected all the filth and dung of the place. One foot and thigh were sunk, as I said, the other foot hung in the air, and my hand held on to the wall. I saw myself in great danger, "there is but a step between me [and death]" [1 Sam 20:3], and I very nearly sank there, God forbid. I began to cry out bitterly and no one heard me, until a gentile woman who lived some way off heard my voice and also began to cry out. And I, for my sins, had no strength left to stay hanging, and I was afraid that the wall would fall on me and it would be stoning and strangling at once, God forbid. But the God of my father was my help [Ex 18:4], and by the merit of the fathers "they drew near and came" [Isa 41:5], the calessiere and the servant, and saw my distress, "and they drew and lifted up Joseph" [Gen 37:28] ⚠23, caught in ropes of scorn, "and the part that remains over" [Ex 26:12] ⚠23. Most of my clothes, not all alike, but what they had in common was that they were soiled and filthy and stinking ⚠24. In His mercy the Lord put it into the heart of a gentile who came at the noise to take me into his house, where there was a spring of water. I took off my clothes and washed myself and my clothes to remove the filth from them, put the zimarra on my body and got into the calesso, with the clothes soaking beside me. I will thank the Lord with all my heart, who has chastened me sore but has not given me over to death [Ps 118:18]. I am not worthy of all the kindnesses [Gen 32:11]. Blessed is God who saves.

31.2

12 Iyar [Wednesday 23 April 1755], the week of Emor: in the evening, at sunset, we came to London, the great city. On Thursday night [the evening of 23 April] I took trouble until I found lodging in the house of a Sephardi named Signor Aaron ha-Kohen, may God keep him. Though the place was cramped, I agreed to it as a clean lodging, together with the baggage ⚠25; it had been the lodging of the earlier emissaries. In the matter of the mission, wonder upon wonder was done for me. For while I was still [on the way], the three ⚠26 heads of the city had sent to tell me not to come to the city, for I would achieve nothing, and the emissaries of Safed who came before had come twice and left with empty hands. I answered that an obligation lay on me to go and nothing exempted me, and on the day I went ⚠27 the Lord would do what is good in His eyes. When I came, all the gevirim had gone out of the city to feed in the gardens [Song 6:2], and there was not a man at home in his town. I took on myself the quality of patience and said: there is no physician like time. "Hush, for we may not mention" [Amos 6:10] the matter of the mission until the eye sees how to win some friends, and to see "whither the spirit" [Ezek 1:12] among the gevirim I should go. For the gevirim are divided among themselves inwardly, and their inside is not like their outside. Among the sages of the city too I saw a horrible thing: they speak to one another insults and abuse, they burn one another with the rod of their tongue and swallow one another alive [Prov 1:12], and "enough contempt" [Esth 1:18] before the eyes of the gevirim. Woe to the eyes that see such desecration of the Torah and its students! I looked into the letters of the mission and saw one written to a certain sage, and no more. At once I asked: is sage so-and-so the rabbi of the place or a dayyan? They answered and said: this sage is not great in this place. And I said: if so, since there are no letters to the important men and to those greater than him, I would be stirring up jealousy, and damage is likely to come of it ⚠28. So I put [the letter] under guard, and the crooked became straight [Isa 40:4]. But God meant it for good [Gen 50:20], for this certain sage was hated, and the dayyanim and the heads of the gevirim were his enemies. Blessed be His glorious name, who has not withheld His kindness [Gen 24:27] and gave me favor in the eyes of some friends. Their head is the perfect sage, the gevir ⚠29, our teacher R. Isaac del Valle ⚠30, and Signor Phinehas Gomez Serra, may God keep him. There were also the great and mighty our teacher R. Jacob Kimḥi, may God keep him, son of our teacher R. S. Kimḥi of blessed memory of Constantinople, and the perfect sage our teacher R. Ḥayyim Albeli ⚠31, may God keep him; I made a covenant with them and special affection. They all answered and said: for the matter of the mission a great assembly is needed, and the custom of the city is to hold this assembly at the beginning of winter; our teacher R. Mas'ud Bonan, may God keep him, already had to wait several months until the time of the council came, and we do not know what you will do. "If you have understanding, hear this" [Job 34:16]: the gevir Signor Joseph Salvador is one of the wardens, and he has gone to the baths ⚠32; he is clear of mind, and what he decrees will stand [Job 22:28], for he is powerful. When Joseph comes home [Gen 43:26], if the Lord gives you favor in his eyes, the man will not rest until he has finished the matter [Ruth 3:18] for good. When the gevir Signor Joseph Salvador, may God keep him, came, I went to him, and I saw that he was a man of great measure ⚠33, clear without dregs. I set out my pleas about the matter of the mission with soft speech and strong arguments. And thus he answered me: you surely know that in these lands "there is not a man upon the earth" [Dan 2:10] "who does as he pleases" [Dan 5:19]; nothing is done except with the agreement of the majority. Even so, go and see the gevirim Signor Franco and Signor Mendes, whether ⚠34 they will stand at your right hand. I took my leave of him in peace. And the man, a mighty man of valor [1 Sam 9:1], took trouble, and they held a council of the wardens. I went to the council, and the other wardens rose to swallow me up for nothing, but he gave me a hint, as if in passing, to show that he was on my side. I came out of the assembly, and he did not rest or stir [Esth 5:9] until he had managed to gather the great council out of its season, and in the end the matter came out well from the general fund. After that, through Signor Francos, "the thing came from the Lord" [Gen 24:50] that Signor Phinehas Gomez Serra mentioned above and Signor Joseph de Abraham Francos should be in charge of the collection ⚠35 from individuals, and so they did. After the collection from individuals was finished, a letter came from the English ambassador in Constantinople instructing Signor Francos about me. Had this letter come earlier it would have been of great use; even so it was an honor and a glory for me in the eyes of the gevirim. Praise and glory to Him who lives for ever! Among the friends I had was the cantor Signor David Castro, for he has a hand and a good name [Isa 56:5], and his words bear fruit and fruit of fruit with the gevirim. Remember him, O God, for good [Neh 5:19]. In the city of London they took me to the tower that they call the Tower. There I saw lions, an eagle a hundred years old, a cat from India as big as a dog, another cat crossed with a wild beast, and other fearsome animals in chains of iron. I also saw an upper hall perhaps fifty cubits long or more, made into rooms upon rooms, and the dividing walls are guns and all kinds of weapons, arranged most beautifully, as though it were a wall with a door open in the middle of it; so too at the sides, and the ceiling is a wonder for the multitude of weapons, by the thousands and tens of thousands and many tens of thousands, and the beauty of their order and arrangement, in their shapes and heights. I also saw all the kings of England, the likeness of their form in iron, riding on horses of iron, and whoever sees them with his eyes would judge that there is a living spirit in them, a wonder. And how many suits of armor of all kinds, set out, arranged and put in order, each flock by itself; and how many kinds of very great weapons of war, so that I was frightened; drums, tiros ⚠36, so to say, and arrays of arms, shield and buckler, and tiros taken from their enemies since England has existed, and the like, weapons of many and strange kinds, "dreadful and high" [Ezek 1:18]. In a somewhat dark room, an iron partition dividing it, they showed us inside the royal crown and stones of the diadem sparkling, full of brightness, giving out [light], [brightness] ⚠37, they lighted up like lightning, and a cup of gold with which they anoint the king, and the like of precious vessels and the treasure of kings, of precious stones and pearls. All these my eye saw, "peering through the lattices" [Song 2:9], grieving and rejoicing: if so for those who transgress His will, how much more for those who do His will. Behold, days are coming of beauty and glory for the remnant of the house of Israel, and our eyes shall see "the breath of our nostrils, the Lord's anointed" [Lam 4:20], shining and gleaming "as the light of the sun sevenfold" [Isa 30:26], crowned with crowns of crowns, holy of holies to the Lord. So may it be His will. I preached in London on the portion (?).

33.4

12 Av [Sunday 20 July 1755], the week of Ekev: while it was still day we entered the great city of Paris. I went to Signor Jacob Rodrigues Pereira, who lives there, for whom I had brought a letter of recommendation from Amsterdam, and I asked him to find me lodging; he found me a room with a Jew, Signor Léon, may God keep him. Signor Jacob mentioned above told me an astonishing thing of wisdom: by his wisdom and his devices, with great labor, he had succeeded in taking a deaf man of the kind the sages speak of, who neither hears nor speaks from the womb and from conception, and bringing him to speech, so that he answers with his mouth what they ask him in writing, his yes yes and his no no; he also writes with his hand in French and in the holy tongue, and reads aloud from books in both languages and understands what he reads. He is also a man of skill who "guided his hands wittingly" [Gen 48:14]: he gave signs by the joints of his fingers, each joint a sign for one letter, and when he wants to speak to him he very easily takes and moves the joints of his fingers that stand for the letters of the word he wants, and the mute, from long practice, grasps his question at once and answers. My eyes saw in his house a boy who, they said, had been mute, neither hearing nor speaking, and now I saw him speak to me in the holy tongue and in French; but for the lack, his voice was like the beasts of the forest, strange, unlike a human voice. The reason is that he lacks the sense of hearing and hears neither our voice nor his own, to straighten and correct it to the voice of men. I looked at Signor Jacob pointing with his fingers to this mute, and at once he answered his question. And as if in play I too signed to him with my fingers as it happened, for I did not know the signs that Jacob had chosen, and I saw the boy astonished at the sight and distressed that he did not understand. Then his master said to him: do not be distressed, for he "put forth the finger" [Isa 58:9] without intention. Signor Jacob mentioned above also showed me, in the book composed by the scholars of the Académie of the sciences in Paris, which is printed in France, that they speak of this matter: that Signor Jacob mentioned above brought this thing to light by his wisdom and labor, and that they had tested it and inquired deeply into the roots of the matter, and knew clearly that the boy had been mute from birth and from the womb, neither hearing nor speaking, and that through the labor, wisdom and devices of Signor Jacob mentioned above he little by little came to understand, distinguish and speak, until he brought him to the state described. When Signor Jacob read from a book, I, the least, had some discernment and a little understanding of French, and I saw for myself that he read what was printed and written in the book. The matter reached the King of France, and he wanted to see this great sight. Signor Jacob and the boy went before the king, who was much amazed, and he drew up and wrote and sealed with the king's ring that he be given eight hundred francs each year from the king's treasury; I saw this writ and the king's signature. Blessed is He who graciously gives man knowledge. Because I had brought a bill of exchange (lettera di cambio) from London, I had to seek out its drawee, and I walked about three hours in the streets of Paris, streets and squares they are. The land is broad, "whose merchants are princes, whose traders are the honorable of the earth" [Isa 23:8]. In passing, my eye saw in one square the likeness of Louis XIV and Henri IV, and the kings they conquered, all of burnished bronze [Ezek 1:7], the work of a craftsman. Signor Jacob took me to the house of the officer of the Académie whom they call the directeur de l'Académie des Sciences, and he and I were there alone for about five hours. He showed me the books of the sciences that they compose and print every year, with all kinds of outside sciences in them, and he read some matters to me, and for the moment I understood the rough form of things. I saw books of anatomy. Alas, for their sins, they spend their days in sciences and in devising novelties, subtleties and new inventions from now on ⚠39. I went to the bibliothèque and saw there about twenty men reading and copying. I asked the man in charge there whether they had a manuscript of the Rambam. He answered: today the printed books are open and on Friday the manuscripts; come to me on Friday and I will show you all your manuscripts; meanwhile we have the Rambam in print, if you wish, take it. Out of courtesy I took the Rambam with the Migdal Oz, sat there and read two chapters, and then I got up and saw the books of Israel there, of all kinds, but they are a few out of many. Yet there are halls there full of books of all religions and sciences, in all languages, a wonderful thing. I had in hand a recommendation to a nobleman, the director of the Compagnie des Indes, and I went to his palace, where all say "Glory" [Ps 29:9]; I asked him for recommendations to the places I am going, and he gave them to me. In passing I saw the jardins and the king's palaces. But as for the choicest ⚠40 of the cities I passed through, I did not see a city perfect in beauty with the splendor of splendors, beautiful, polished and clean in good order, like Amsterdam. And the holy Sephardi congregation of Amsterdam, its fame has gone out through all the earth [Ps 19:5]. Indeed all perfections were gathered into it: very large, beyond measure, and half of it ⚠41 a palace of kings; the study house, the Talmud Torah and the yeshivot are close by and visible within the street of the synagogue. It stands on the height of the city and in a corner of the light ⚠42, polished and clean, and its size and breadth are seen and open to the eyes, for it has only four pillars and no step, and young and old come into it easily. It was built in the year "In the abundance of Your kindness I will come into Your house" [Ps 5:8; 5435, 1675] ⚠43. May it be established in righteousness [Isa 54:14] until Shiloh comes [Gen 49:10]; may God show him Israel passing through his border.

35.3

28 Av [Tuesday 5 August 1755], the week of Shofetim: we set out in a boat on the river and came in five hours to the city of Bordeaux. I was worried how I would enter the city and where I would go and look, and behold, on the near shore there was a sedan chair. When we arrived they called me, I got out and they seated me in the chair, and behind me some of the notables of the community on foot. I gave thanks to the Merciful One, "who lifts up the needy from the dunghill to seat him with princes" [Ps 113:7–8], "according to the estate of a man of high degree" [1 Chr 17:17], and my mouth is full of praise to Him who is terrible in His doings, who put it into the heart of the heads of the holy congregation to honor me greatly. All the gevirim and the heads of the holy congregation came early to my door, and feet did not cease day and night until midnight all the days I was there. There was confusion in the city, for the moreh of the holy congregation, the aged sage R. Jacob Atias, had disqualified an expert shoḥet; the whole holy congregation had eaten of his slaughtering, and another shoḥet, a kinsman of the sage R. Jacob mentioned above, was slaughtering for him. I told them that on weekdays I do not eat meat, and from Sabbath to Sabbath I would slaughter some fowl for myself; for that reason, while I was in London, I learned in a few days to slaughter fowl. So I did: each week on Friday I slaughtered some fowl for myself, and with me was Signor Benjamin Abraham Ashkenazi, a shoḥet licensed by the rabbi of Amsterdam, and the two of us together examined the knife for about an hour. From the day I came, the wardens and all the gevirim begged me greatly to settle this quarrel of the shoḥatim, and I, for the honor of the old man, held back with excuses, and day after day I told our teacher R. Jacob mentioned above to bring the matter to a good end, for everyone was grumbling after him. But he hardened his heart [Deut 2:30] without understanding. In the end, after eighteen days and their nights, when they had wearied of urging me, they sent me a messenger [to say] that it seemed to them I was not doing well in holding back from the matter while it was in my power. At once I went to the moreh and said: you have seen eye to eye how I have been careful of your honor, but now I am forced to deal with it. He told me that this was his wish too. Then I said to him: if so, I act with permission. He showed me a ruling he had written on it, like one who has never seen light, "iron mixed with miry clay" [Dan 2:41], with as much savor as "the slime of mallows" [Job 6:6] ⚠44. I sent word to the wardens to write me a supplique in their language, so that all the people of the land should know that it was not of my own mind, and I also wrote a deed for the sage R. Jacob mentioned above to sign; and they compelled him against his will to sign, for whatever I said would stand. I chose with me two learned men who were there, the sage R. Abraham Lopes and the sage R. Abraham Monsanto, who also have a feel for examining the knife. At night I sent for a lung, and for an Ashkenazi shoḥet to tell me where the parts called umah and unah ⚠45 are, and the like; afterwards I went over the laws in the Shulḥan Arukh and some of the Beit Yosef. I looked into the case of the shoḥet whom the moreh had disqualified and saw clearly ⚠46 that his words had neither taste nor smell. Afterwards I "took sweet counsel" [Ps 55:15] with the two good men mentioned above, they agreed with me, and we met to examine the shoḥatim. The shoḥet whom the sage R. Jacob mentioned above had disqualified was expert and skilled, while his kinsman the shoḥet could not in five days produce one [fit] knife. So too we examined the other shoḥatim who slaughtered for others, and we removed some of them. I wrote all of it and signed it in the register of the holy congregation. I was grieved over the desecration of God's name, and shed tears ⚠47 over the old moreh; even so, all the people at the gate knew that my deeds were for the sake of Heaven and that I took up the matter against my will. The moreh asked me to tell him the reasons for which I had declared fit the shoḥet he had disqualified, and further asked ⚠48 that I give him back his ruling. I refused, for he has no mind to accept the truth and relies on his son, who never saw the light of study and ruling. So I said: if you keep silent, well and good; if not, I will write to all the dispersions of Israel and send your ruling and mine, and they will decide. I explained the matter before the eyes of the community in such a way, and took great trouble with good manners and tact, until I established the truth. I did not come to a quarrel with the moreh, though his deeds were not proper, and I honored him and mentioned him with great honor in the two sermons I preached there. I give thanks and praise that I did not dine with anyone except Signor Benjamin Gradis, who is known to keep kashrut according to the law of Moses and Israel. They already wanted to invite me to dairy and fish dishes, and I was worried about the vessels and the maidservants, who cook everything; but God brought it about that the fine fish ⚠49 they wanted for my honor and glory did not come, and through this I was saved from eating with anyone except at the inn, whose people were kosher, from the Maghreb. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for all His bounties to me [Ps 116:12]. I did not want to preach a second sermon until they urged me till I was ashamed [2 Kgs 2:17]. My reason was that it is their custom to give some small donation there, and people think it is for the emissary; and to put away perverse lips [Prov 4:24] and the words of a whisperer about the emissaries, which we have heard with our ears, I did not want to preach a second time until the wardens and the good men of that holy congregation had entreated me. It was a great honor, praise to God, blessed be He.

על המורה הזקן ומ"מ כל העם אשר בשער ידעו כי מעשי לשם שמים ועל כרחי נטפלתי והמורה היה שואל לאמר לו הטעמים שהכשרתי לשוחט שפסל ועוד בו שאתן לו הפסק שלו ואני מאנתי כי אין בו דעת לקבל האמת וסומך על בנו אשר לא ראה אור עיון ופסק מעולם. וכך אמרתי אי שתקת שתקת ואם אין אכתוב לכל תפוצות ישראל ואשלח פסקך ופסקי והם יכריעו. והייתי מסביר הדברים לעיני הקהל באופן שטרחתי הרבה בהלכות דרך ארץ וסכלנות עד אשר העמדתי האמת. ולא באתי למחלקת עם המורה אף אם מעשיו שלא כהוגן והייתי מכבדו ומזכירו בכבוד גדול בשני דרושים שדרשתי שם ואהודא ומשבח אנא שלא סעדתי אצל שום אדם כי אם אצל סי' בנימין גראדיץ שהוחזק לקדש כדת משה וישראל וכבר היו רוצים להזמין אותי במאכלי חלב ודגים והיה צר לי על הכלים ועל השפחות שהן הנה מבשלות הכל והאלדים עשה שלא באו דגים מצויינים אשר היה ברצונם לכבוד ולתפארת ועי"ז ניצולתי מלסעוד אצל שום אדם כי אם באושפיזא שהיו כשרים ממערב הודו לה' כי טוב על כל תגמולוהי עלי: ולא רציתי לדרוש דרוש שני עד שהפצירו בי עד בוש וטעמא דידי כי נוהגים להתנדב איזה נדבה קלה שם וסיברין שהיא לשליח וכדי להרחיק לזות שפתים ודברי נרגן על השלוחים אשר באזנינו שמענו לא רציתי לדרוש שנית כי אם אחר שחילו פני הפרנסים וטובי הק"ק ההוא והיה לכבוד גדול תלי"ת.

Uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 מהר"ר ישראל — R. Israel of Bingen; Freimann identifies him as R. Israel Mengeburg, rabbi in Bingen, who died in 1756. Not in the glossary.
  • ⚠2 המ"ץ — Read as מורה צדק, the local rabbinical judge or teacher.
  • ⚠3 ולשאו"ל הגיעו חיי"ם שאול"ה — Wordplay with marked words (Saul, life, question); read as "they asked me". The exact pun is not clear.
  • ⚠4 בת מלך — Literally "daughter of a king" (Ps 45:14), an honorific; the Ḥida then names her father, his grandfather.
  • ⚠5 אשר היה אב"ד בקליף — The clause may refer to R. Falk or to his father R. Nathanel.
  • ⚠6 פ"ו — Print unclear between ס"ו and פ"ו; read פ"ו, probably a title such as parnas (community leader).
  • ⚠7 סכיך וסריך חביט חביט ובריך — Aramaic rhymed phrase; rendered "covered and entangled, stricken and stricken again, and blessing".
  • ⚠8 וישב עליהם את הונ"ם וארוכ"ת תמיד נתנה להם נכרא"ו — The marked words are a pun not resolved here; נכרא"ו is left untranslated.
  • ⚠9 ח"ר משה די גאנדיס — Family name as printed (גאנדיס); Freimann suggests the reading גאדינס. Not identified.
  • ⚠10 ואביזריהו — Literally "and his accessories"; read as his household or associates.
  • ⚠11 שכמ"ה לטוב להם כל הימים — שכמ"ה not resolved; read as a wish for their reward. "Good for them all the days" follows Deut 6:24.
  • ⚠12 החרד"ם — Read as הח"ר ד(וד) מ(ילדולה), the sage R. David Meldola, in whose house he lodged ("the master of the house").
  • ⚠13 וכשאני לעצמי — So printed; a verb seems to be missing. Read as "when I reckon for myself".
  • ⚠14 קו"ף בפני הד"ם. קום פו"ק — קוף = 100+6+80 = 186, and פוק has the same letters. "A monkey before a man" (כקוף בפני אדם, Bava Batra 58a) is played on with הד"ם, the D. M., David Meldola; "get up, go out" also has the value 186. The reading of the pun is likely but not certain.
  • ⚠15 ובטלה מגילת תענית — Talmudic phrase (Rosh Hashanah 18b), used as a pun: his own fasts were cancelled.
  • ⚠16 ונסמכו אהלים לנחלים נטיו נשא"ו נהרות כל"ם — Play on Num 24:6 and Ps 93:3; the marked words are a pun not fully clear. Rendered loosely.
  • ⚠17 וידי לאבן קרח"א מצנינא — וידי is not clear; read as "and it" (its water) turned to stone, frozen ice.
  • ⚠18 ותופ"ס הקס"ת — Literally "the holder of the inkhorn"; קסת (inkhorn) is played on with קשת (bow) of 1 Sam 2:4.
  • ⚠19 טרדי"ן — Marked word, not clear; read from context as people slipping and falling.
  • ⚠20 אחת למעלה ושבע למטה — Not clear; perhaps a proverbial phrase for confused, faltering prayer.
  • ⚠21 באלבו"ד — Name as printed; Freimann identifies the place (אלפיסלוס) as Hellevoetsluis. The form אלבו"ד is not explained.
  • ⚠22 שה ושחק — So printed; read as שש ושחק, "glad and laughing" (the customs man).
  • ⚠23 וימשכו ויעלו את יוסף אחוז בחבלי בוז וסר"ח העודף — Play on Gen 37:28 (Joseph is the Ḥida's own name) and Ex 26:12 (סרח העודף, 'the overhanging remainder', here his dirty clothes); the wording is a joke, rendered close to the words.
  • ⚠24 לא רא"י זה כרא"י זה הצד השוה שבהם ... ומרזח סרוחים — Talmudic formula of the 'common feature' (הצד השוה), and a pun on Amos 6:7 מרזח סרוחים ('the revelry of the sprawlers'), with סרוח read as 'stinking'.
  • ⚠25 ואת הכבודיה — So printed; read as הכבודה, 'the baggage'. Uncertain.
  • ⚠26 שלשת הנפת ראשי העיר — הנפת is clear in the print but makes no sense to me; perhaps a misprint (for הגבאים or the like). Rendered 'the three heads of the city'.
  • ⚠27 וביום לכתי מחנים ה' הטוב בעיניו יעשה — מחנים is unclear (perhaps an allusion to Gen 32:3, or a misprint); rendered by sense, the word left out.
  • ⚠28 ושכיח היזקא ניזקא ופלגו נזקא אהא דסקא — Talmudic-style Aramaic phrase on damages, partly unclear in sense; rendered by the general meaning, 'damage is likely'.
  • ⚠29 ה"ה הן גביר — ה"ה = הלא הוא; הן is unclear (perhaps 'indeed'). Rendered 'the gevir'.
  • ⚠30 יצחק דיל ואלי — Read as 'del Valle'; the family is not identified here.
  • ⚠31 חיים אלבעלי — Name transliterated from the print; not identified.
  • ⚠32 והלך למרחצאות — 'The baths': probably the town of Bath, or a spa; uncertain.
  • ⚠33 וחזיתיה לבעתיה דכיילין ליה בקבא רבא — Aramaic idiom, 'whom they measure with a great kav', i.e. a man of large stature; the first words are unclear.
  • ⚠34 על חלו"ת — Abbreviation not resolved; rendered 'whether' by sense.
  • ⚠35 יעמדו על הבר"כה — Read as בירכה / ברכה, the collection or gift ('blessing'); uncertain.
  • ⚠36 תפים תירו"ם אימרא — תירו"ם probably Spanish/Portuguese 'tiros', shots or guns; אימרא read as Aramaic 'I would say'. Uncertain.
  • ⚠37 מפיקים [נגה] — Freimann's bracketed [נגה] is printed in the text; rendered as a word supplied.
  • ⚠38 ואני שמרתי ארחות פארי"ץ — Pun on Ps 17:4, 'I kept from the paths of the violent (פריץ)', read as 'Paris'.
  • ⚠39 חדשות מעתה — The print reads הדשות; read חדשות. מעתה ('from now on') is rendered as printed; perhaps for מעת לעת.
  • ⚠40 לענין גולתי הערים — Read as גולת, 'the crown, the choicest'; the word is unclear.
  • ⚠41 וחצה לו פלטין של מלכים — So printed; perhaps for וחצר לו ('and it has a court like a royal palace'). Uncertain.
  • ⚠42 ותלוי בקרן האורה — Literally 'hanging in the corner of the light'; sense unclear, perhaps 'standing in full light'.
  • ⚠43 אב'א' ב'י'ת'ך' — Chronogram from Ps 5:8; the year 5435 (1675) is the known date of the Amsterdam Portuguese synagogue.
  • ⚠44 ותכלין יש בו בריר חלמות — Job 6:6, 'is there any taste in the slime of mallows?': i.e. the ruling was tasteless. תכלין rendered 'savor'.
  • ⚠45 אומה ואונה — Technical names of parts of the lung in the laws of terefot; left untranslated.
  • ⚠46 בע"ד — Abbreviation not resolved (perhaps בעליל דבר or בעיני דידי); rendered 'clearly'.
  • ⚠47 ודומי דמעה — So printed; read by sense as 'and I shed tears'; ודומי is unclear.
  • ⚠48 ועוד בו שאתן לו — בו makes no sense; Freimann's footnote suggests בקש, 'he asked', which is followed here.
  • ⚠49 דגים מצויינים — Read as 'fine, choice fish'; uncertain.
