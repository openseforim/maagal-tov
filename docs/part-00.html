About this text

14 paragraphs, scans 34–39 of the 1885 printing (numbered as in the archive.org viewer). One row is one paragraph of the print, which ends each entry with a colon. Boxes marked "scan N" in the Hebrew show where a scan page begins; each links to that page on archive.org, as do the page numbers under the title. References in [gray brackets] are the translator's: identifications of biblical phrases, and the civil dates of the Ḥida's dates. He dates by the weekly Torah reading: "Wednesday of the week of Re'eh" is the Wednesday before the Sabbath on which Re'eh is read (readings of the diaspora). i

The book Ma'agal Tov, that is, the order of the journey, found in the holy handwriting of the rabbi, the gaon, the all-embracing scholar, the kabbalist, the holy man of God, our teacher R. Ḥayyim Joseph David Azulai, may the memory of the righteous and holy be a blessing for the life of the world to come, may his merit protect us and all Israel. In it he wrote the whole order of his journey when he went about on a mission of a commandment from the Holy Land to the lands of Africa and Europe; and the reader will find in it precious and lofty things.

Ḥida, pure Sephardi

חיד"א ס"ט

xiii

In the year 5524 [1763–64], when I came to Egypt, a wonderful book came into my hands, from the manuscript of our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital, of blessed memory, on practical kabbalah and the science of letter combinations, and other matters from the Tannaim. I took this book on loan together with other books, and went and secluded myself with it. Hardly had I begun to understand a little of it, by way of attainment, and I had a great fervor; and while I was studying it I dozed off, and I saw our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital, of blessed memory. I asked him a question, and he answered me; and he gave me a cup of wine to drink, and I was very glad.

scan 38 בשנת תקכ"ד בבואי מצרים בא לידי ספר מופלא מכ"י מוהרח"ו זצ"ל בקבלת מעשית וחכמת הצירוף ועניינים אחרים מהתנאים. ולקחתי בתורת שאלה ספר זה עם ספרים אחרים והלכתי והייתי מתבודד בספר הנז'. וכמעט שהתחלתי להבין קצת בדרך השגה והיה לי התלהבות גדול. ובעוד שהייתי מעיין בו נתנמנמתי וראיתי למוהרח"ו ז"ל והייתי שואל שאלה ממנו והשיב לי. ונתן לי כוס של יין לשתות והייתי שמח הרבה:

xiv

In Sivan 5533 [May to June 1773] the gevir R. Samuel was very ill, and his whole body trembled. I prayed much, and I made redemption-offerings for him, from my own money as well as those they made from his money ⚠28. Many days of distress I passed in this, until the Holy One, blessed be He, had mercy on us: He sent His word and healed him [Ps 107:20]. Meanwhile I was in distress and did not know where to turn. If I thought of going to Livorno, I was afraid of the danger of captivity, for the Muscovite had struck and raised his head ⚠29, and the sea was full of corsairs ⚠30, and my heart melted within me and became like water [Josh 7:5]; until I heard that on the side of Tunis there were no Muscovites. There was a ship going to Tunis and from there to Livorno, and I made up my mind to travel on it. On Tuesday of the week of Matot and Masei [13 July 1773] I set out on the said ship. But the captain was a very wicked man, proud and a fool. He did great injustices to me: he ordered that they give me no water, and that whenever I wanted anything I should ask him. I made him a present of fine tobacco and several flasks of syrup ⚠31, and it availed me nothing [Esth 5:13]; he was wicked from beginning to end. All the week I did not eat the measure of bread that requires the grace after meals, because of the water for washing the hands; and how much distress I had over this at the meals of the holy Sabbath. On Wednesday of the week of Ki Tetze [25 August 1773] we reached the city of Sousse. On Sunday of the week of Ki Tavo [29 August 1773] permission came ⚠32, and we went ashore in peace. On Wednesday of the week of Nitzavim [8 September 1773] we came to Tunis, and the master of the house did me very great honor. Several women came to the gevir's house, women who were pregnant, wishing to see me; they stood at the windows and looked at me from afar, and I did not notice. On the Sabbath I was walking in the courtyard there with the gevir, and I felt that the women were behind me, taking the hems of my garments from behind, gently and lightly, to kiss them; and the women never sat at the table at all. But in the matter of food they have no manners whatever, "for God did not lead them by the way [derekh eretz]" [Exod 13:17] ⚠33; and I used to tell them that manners are a coin not current in their land. There was the rabbi R. Abraham Tayyib, who was absorbed in study even on the road; he had a number of disciples, rabbis and sages. And the rabbi R. Joseph Zarqa, a sage and a scribe, pious and pure and righteous, had about sixty disciples. Among them were weighty students; and among the greatest of his disciples were his son-in-law R. Joshua, R. Abraham Katan ⚠34, the warden, and R. Jacob Ma'arek ⚠35. All of them were dear to me, and I studied with them; and so with very many scholars, perhaps about a hundred scholars, with whom I studied and debated, and I found favor in their eyes. From what I heard, there are today nearly three hundred scholars in Tunis. I also saw boys of fourteen who were studious and keen, who could sling at a hair [Judg 20:16]. But all their occupation is with the Talmud and the Maharsha, and the novellae and methods of the Ashkenazi rabbis, and a little […] ⚠36

Uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 ואך מ / הדפ / לזכות את הר / עד ש — The left edge of this page is cut off in the scanned copy, so the end of each line is lost: "only a f…" (ואך מ, probably "only a few"), "therefore … print…" (הדפ), "to bring merit to the …" (את הר, probably "the public"), and the number of years of the reprint ban (עד ש…). The gaps are marked […]; the missing words are not supplied.
  • ⚠2 אי"ש צעיר — "A young man" is the usual humble self-description. אי"ש is printed with abbreviation marks, so it may also hide the initials of the editor, who signs at the end as ישע"ה (Isaiah); not resolved.
  • ⚠3 ספר אורחות חיים — The Livorno editor opens with "the book of the paths of life" (orḥot ḥayyim), a play on the Ḥida's first name Ḥayyim; the book is named Ma'agal Tov a few lines below. The right edge of this page is torn in the scanned copy, so many lines lose their first word or letters; gaps are marked […].
  • ⚠4 ביד זרע רב ה"ה הח"הש ר' / ה כ"ע — The grandson's name falls at the torn start of a line: only ...ה and the abbreviation כ"ע remain. ה"ה is read as "namely" (הלא הוא) and הח"הש as "the sage" (perhaps החכם השלם, "the perfect sage"); כ"ע is not resolved. He is the son of R. Isaiah Azulai, the Ḥida's son.
  • ⚠5 בהיותו סובב / ימותו — "Going about" ends one line and ימותו ("his days"?) begins the next after the torn edge; the words between are lost, and the reading ימותו is not certain.
  • ⚠6 את כל הקורות דבר בע"טו קש"תו ככס"רת — Rhymed prose with abbreviation marks inside the words, perhaps marking a wordplay or a chronogram. דבר בעטו is read as דבר בעתו, "a word in its season" (Prov 15:23, which goes on "how good it is", tov, the title word). קש"תו and ככס"רת (on the scan perhaps כנס"רת) are not understood and are left untranslated.
  • ⚠7 בלשון הזהב והסדר"ת — The Hebrew paragraph begins in the middle of the editor's sentence. "In order" reads והסדר"ת as a form of סדר (perhaps כסדרת, "in sequence"); the abbreviation marks are not explained.
  • ⚠8 והנו תמתו — תמתו begins a line after the torn edge; letters before it are probably lost. Not translated.
  • ⚠9 על כל / ות סודו לה' כי טוב — The start of the line is lost (…ות, perhaps "the good things"). סודו is read as הודו ("give thanks"), the opening of Ps 136:1; ה and ס look much alike in this type.
  • ⚠10 ובהיותו רב / נאי קדוש יאמר לו — "When he was rabbi" ends a line; the next line begins …נאי after the torn edge, so the words between are lost (perhaps the name of a place or community). The later …ת אנקונא has also lost its start (perhaps "the holy congregation of Ancona").
  • ⚠11 ביד נכדו הר"ת הנז' ז"ל — "His grandson" is the Ḥida's grandson named above. הר"ת is not resolved (perhaps "the rabbi R. T…"). An Abraham Ḥai Musafia of Ancona was the Ḥida's friend in 1776 (part 2); the man who came to Livorno in 1867 must be a later bearer of the name, perhaps a descendant. As printed, it is Musafia who saw the manuscript in Ancona and the editor who met him in Livorno.
  • ⚠12 לא / ב ידו מבלע — The phrase is Lam 2:8 "he did not draw back his hand from swallowing" (לא השיב ידו מבלע); only the last letter of the verb survives the torn edge.
  • ⚠13 ללקט באומרי"ם — Wordplay: Ruth 2:7 has "to glean among the sheaves" (ba-omarim); the editor spells it באומרים, "among the sayings", with abbreviation marks to point to the pun. The next phrase "…טי בתר ליקוטי" (gleanings after gleanings, Aramaic) has lost its first letters; "as bees do" (הדבו"רים, Deut 1:44) plays on devorim "bees" and dibburim "words".
  • ⚠14 וכאשר בא תהר"א ל / פ"ה — Printed תהר"א; read as מהר"א, "our teacher R. A[braham]" (Musafia), with מ misprinted or misread as ת; suspected error. After it, the end of the line (ל…) is lost.
  • ⚠15 להיפים ספרו — Printed להיפים ("to beautify"?); read as להדפיס, "to print", which the context requires (the type makes ה/ס and ם/ס look alike). Suspected misprint or misreading.
  • ⚠16 לה לדויד ע"ם תהלים — The title's first letters are lost at the torn edge; probably Tehillah le-David ("A praise of David", Ps 145:1), a work on Psalms. ע"ם is read as "on the Book of Psalms" (על ספר?). Not checked in bibliographies.
  • ⚠17 בא צריק לבית מלוני — צריק is read as צדיק, "a righteous man" (ד and ר are close in this type).
  • ⚠18 יתי אותו ואת כנהו — The start of the line is lost (…יתי, perhaps "I saw"). כנהו is not clear (perhaps "his stock", cf. Ps 80:16, or כחו "his strength"); left untranslated.
  • ⚠19 ות את בכ"י את יופיו — The start of the line is lost (…ות, perhaps "to show"). בכ"י is read as בני כלל ישראל, "all Israel"; not certain. The line before also lost its first word after חשקה ("[my …] longed").
  • ⚠20 כ"ד הצעיר מקי"ר ע"ה ישע"ה ס"ט — כ"ד = "these are the words of". מקי"ר and ע"ה are not resolved (ע"ה perhaps "servant of the Lord" or "dust and ashes", a humble formula). ישע"ה is the name Isaiah, printed as an abbreviation. ס"ט = ספרדי טהור, "pure Sephardi", a usual signature of Sephardi rabbis, also used by the Ḥida below.
  • ⚠21 ושיכויי דחיקי. ובוקי סריקי. שאין האים מכוקם — Aramaic rhymed prose. שיכויי is read as "thorns" (cf. Num 33:55 שכים); בוקי סריקי ("empty flasks") is a Talmudic phrase for worthless people. שאין האים מכוקם is not understood (perhaps a misprint), and the end of the line is cut off; left untranslated.
  • ⚠22 חכי"כה בצער — Abbreviation not resolved; perhaps a phrase about being saved from distress. Left untranslated.
  • ⚠23 למרחב אות / זאת היתה ל — The left edge of this page is cut off in the scanned copy, so the last word of several lines is lost or broken: אות… ("sign"), היתה ל…, על…, הנ(ס)…, ופי…, כהי…, ברכ…. The gaps are marked […].
  • ⚠24 מפרשת העבו"ר על / ועל צוארי — העבור is read as "what passed" over me (העבור עלי), with abbreviation marks perhaps pointing to a pun (also "intercalation" or "pregnancy"). The word after על is lost at the cut edge.
  • ⚠25 ואת הנ"ס למינהו על חד מאה בדשערוהי — "After its kind" (למינהו) is Genesis wording. בדשערוהי is read as Aramaic "than what he estimated" (שער "to estimate"); not certain.
  • ⚠26 לאל העושה כהור עכני סגיאן מלוהי — כהור עכני is not understood (perhaps a misprint for an Aramaic phrase such as "with me", עמי); left untranslated. סגיאן מלוהי is Aramaic "many are His words (or deeds)".
  • ⚠27 כהי / כאלי חסדי חסדים — The end of the line (כהי…) is cut, and כאלי is not understood; only "kindnesses upon kindnesses" is clear.
  • ⚠28 ועשיתי לו פדיונות וממוני כוסף על אשר עשו מממונו — Pidyonot, gifts of charity given as "redemption" for a sick man. כוסף is read as כסף (money), meaning that he gave from his own money in addition to what was given from the sick man's; the sentence is hard and the reading is not certain.
  • ⚠29 המוסקו"ף הכה ויקף ראש — The Muscovite: the Russian fleet in the Mediterranean during the Russo-Turkish war of 1768–1774. ויקף ראש is read as "raised his head" (ויזקוף ראש), that is, grew bold; not certain.
  • ⚠30 מקורסאלי"ם — Italian corsali, corsairs.
  • ⚠31 טבא"קו חשוב וכמה צלוחיות שארו"פי — טבאקו = tobacco (Italian tabacco); שארופי = syrup (Italian sciroppo, Arabic sharāb).
  • ⚠32 יו"א תבא בא רשות ויצאנו לשלום — Permission from the port to land, probably after a quarantine at Sousse (pratique); read so from the context.
  • ⚠33 כי לא נח"ם אלהים דרך ארץ — Wordplay on Exod 13:17 ("God did not lead them by the way of the land of the Philistines"): derekh eretz, "the way of the land", also means good manners. The abbreviation marks on נחם point to the pun.
  • ⚠34 ור' אברהם קטן הפרנס — Katan is taken as a family name; it could also be read "the young R. Abraham, the warden".
  • ⚠35 ור' יעקב מעארק — Family name as printed; the vowels (Ma'arek, Me'arek) are not certain.
  • ⚠36 וקצת — The paragraph ends in the middle of a sentence: the following leaves are lost in this scanned copy. The text resumes in part 1, in Tunis.
