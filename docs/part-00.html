i

The book Ma'agal Tov, that is, the order of the journey, found in the holy handwriting of the rabbi, the gaon, the all-embracing scholar, the kabbalist, the holy man of God, our teacher R. Ḥayyim Joseph David Azulai, may the memory of the righteous and holy be a blessing for the life of the world to come, may his merit protect us and all Israel. In it he wrote the whole order of his journey when he went about on a mission of a commandment from the Holy Land to the lands of Africa and Europe; and the reader will find in it precious and lofty things.

This holy book was printed in the land of Italy, and only a few of them came to our country, and those were sold at a great price; and they too are used up and gone [Ps 73:19], and none of them is to be found at all. Therefore we have printed it anew, in splendor and beauty and with careful proofreading, to bring merit to the public. And we forbid anyone to print it again for ten years from today.

vi

The book "Paths of Life" ⚠2, found in the holy handwriting of the great rabbi, the gaon of our strength, our teacher R. Ḥida, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, in the hand of a descendant of the rabbi, namely the perfect sage R. Moses ⚠3, son of that righteous man, the rabbi R. Isaiah Azulai, of blessed memory, the son of the rabbi Ḥida, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing. He wrote it along the roads, when he was going about on his mission as emissary of the holy cities, may they be rebuilt, the city of his birth: all that befell him, each thing in its time ⚠4, in golden language and splendor ⚠5. And here it is, in his bag, to be a memorial for him, since the Lord was with him [Gen 39:23]; to give thanks to the Lord for all His benefits: "give thanks to the Lord, for He is good" [Ps 136:1]. Therefore he called its name Ma'agal Tov, "the good path" [Prov 2:9]. And when the merciful rabbi ⚠6, of whom "holy" shall be said [Isa 4:3], our teacher R. Abraham Ḥai Musafia, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, was in the city of Ancona, may its Rock and Redeemer protect it, he saw the said holy manuscript in the hand of his grandson, the aforementioned R. M. ⚠7, of blessed memory. And it was, when he tasted it [Ps 34:1], its words a honeycomb [Prov 16:24] to every palate that tastes them, he did not draw back his hand from swallowing [Lam 2:8]; while it was still in his palm he swallowed it [Isa 28:4]. He put out his hand to glean among the sayings ⚠8 [Ruth 2:7], gleanings after gleanings, as bees do [Deut 1:44]. And when our teacher R. A. ⚠9, mentioned above, came here, to the city of Livorno, may its Rock and Redeemer protect it, in the month of Tammuz 5627 [July 1867], to print his book Tehillah le-David on the Book of Psalms ⚠10, a righteous man came to my lodging, and I set my eyes on him [Gen 44:21]. I saw him and his stock ⚠11; the talk of scholars, he went around it and pondered it [Deut 32:10]. My soul longed to copy it, to make the sound of its praise heard [Ps 66:8], and to strive to bring it up to the press, to show all Israel ⚠12 its beauty, his conduct and his holiness: it teaches man knowledge [Ps 94:10], his ways, the way in which they shall walk [Exod 18:20], to fear the Lord and to love Him, when they go to bring a teaching up to the law as it is decided. From him they will see, and do likewise [Judg 7:17], and walk as he walked; they will come and declare his righteousness [Ps 22:32].

ix

It is good to give thanks to the Lord [Ps 92:2] for the kindnesses and the good things, all His benefits [Ps 116:12]. He was with me on my far-off roads [Gen 35:3], and in many adventures, and pressing thorns, and worthless men, from whom no man comes away clean ⚠14; the light of [my] face was changed [Dan 5:6].

x

And the kindness of the Lord is from everlasting [Ps 103:17]: grace ⚠15 in distress, and He brings me out into a broad place [Ps 18:20]: His signs, how great they are, and His wonders [Dan 3:33]. This has been mine [Ps 119:56]: this slender reed has ruled lines and written one out of ten of the account of what passed ⚠16 over me and over my neck; but only the stump of its roots [Dan 4:12]. Truly they said: the one for whom a miracle is done does not recognize his miracle [b. Niddah 31a], and the miracles of every kind, a hundredfold beyond what was estimated [Onkelos Gen 26:12] ⚠17. And my mouth I will fill with praises to God who does ⚠18; many are His words. Rivers, streams ⚠19 of kindnesses upon kindnesses; may His name be blessed and exalted above all blessing [Neh 9:5]; I kneel on my knees [Dan 6:11]. Thus says the servant: I, the least, the youngest of his brothers.

וחסד ה' מעולם חני"נה בצער ויוצא למרחב אותי אתוהי כמה רברבין ותמהוהי. זאת היתה לי דק קנה זה שרטט וכתב אחד מעשרה מפרשת העבו"ר עלי ועל צוארי ברם עיקר שרשוהי. באמת אמרו אין בעל הנס מכי"ר ואת הנ"ס למינהו על חד מאה בדשערוהי. ופי אמלא תהלות לאל העושה כהור עכני סגיאן מלוהי. נהרי נחלי חסדי חסדים יהי שמו מבורך ומרומם על כל ברכה ברך על ברכוהי. אמור יאמר העבד אנא זעירא זוטר דאחוהי:

חיד"א ס"ט

xii

In the year 5524 [1763–64], when I came to Egypt, a wonderful book came into my hands, from the manuscript of our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital, of blessed memory, on practical kabbalah and the science of letter combinations, and other matters from the Tannaim. I took this book on loan together with other books, and went and secluded myself with it. Hardly had I begun to understand a little of it, by way of attainment, and I had a great fervor; and while I was studying it I dozed off, and I saw our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital, of blessed memory. I asked him a question, and he answered me; and he gave me a cup of wine to drink, and I was very glad.

scan 38 בשנת תקכ"ד בבואי מצרים בא לידי ספר מופלא מכ"י מוהרח"ו זצ"ל בקבלת מעשית וחכמת הצירוף ועניינים אחרים מהתנאים. ולקחתי בתורת שאלה ספר זה עם ספרים אחרים והלכתי והייתי מתבודד בספר הנז'. וכמעט שהתחלתי להבין קצת בדרך השגה והיה לי התלהבות גדול. ובעוד שהייתי מעיין בו נתנמנמתי וראיתי למוהרח"ו ז"ל והייתי שואל שאלה ממנו והשיב לי. ונתן לי כוס של יין לשתות והייתי שמח הרבה:

xiii

In Sivan 5533 [May to June 1773] the gevir R. Samuel was very ill, and his whole body trembled. I prayed much, and I made redemption-offerings for him, from my own money as well as those they made from his money ⚠20. Many days of distress I passed in this, until the Holy One, blessed be He, had mercy on us: He sent His word and healed him [Ps 107:20]. Meanwhile I was in distress and did not know where to turn. If I thought of going to Livorno, I was afraid of the danger of captivity, for the Muscovite had struck and raised his head ⚠21, and the sea was full of corsairs ⚠22, and my heart melted within me and became like water [Josh 7:5]; until I heard that on the side of Tunis there were no Muscovites. There was a ship going to Tunis and from there to Livorno, and I made up my mind to travel on it. On Tuesday of the week of Matot and Masei [13 July 1773] I set out on the said ship. But the captain was a very wicked man, proud and a fool. He did great injustices to me: he ordered that they give me no water, and that whenever I wanted anything I should ask him. I made him a present of fine tobacco and several flasks of syrup ⚠23, and it availed me nothing [Esth 5:13]; he was wicked from beginning to end. All the week I did not eat the measure of bread that requires the grace after meals, because of the water for washing the hands; and how much distress I had over this at the meals of the holy Sabbath. On Wednesday of the week of Ki Tetze [25 August 1773] we reached the city of Sousse. On Sunday of the week of Ki Tavo [29 August 1773] permission came ⚠24, and we went ashore in peace. On Wednesday of the week of Nitzavim [8 September 1773] we came to Tunis, and the master of the house did me very great honor. Several women came to the gevir's house, women who were pregnant, wishing to see me; they stood at the windows and looked at me from afar, and I did not notice. On the Sabbath I was walking in the courtyard there with the gevir, and I felt that the women were behind me, taking the hems of my garments from behind, gently and lightly, to kiss them; and the women never sat at the table at all. But in the matter of food they have no manners whatever, "for God did not lead them by the way [derekh eretz]" [Exod 13:17] ⚠25; and I used to tell them that manners are a coin not current in their land. There was the rabbi R. Abraham Tayyib, who was absorbed in study even on the road; he had a number of disciples, rabbis and sages. And the rabbi R. Joseph Zarqa, a sage and a scribe, pious and pure and righteous, had about sixty disciples. Among them were weighty students; and among the greatest of his disciples were his son-in-law R. Joshua, R. Abraham Katan ⚠26, the warden, and R. Jacob Ma'arek ⚠27. All of them were dear to me, and I studied with them; and so with very many scholars, perhaps about a hundred scholars, with whom I studied and debated, and I found favor in their eyes. From what I heard, there are today nearly three hundred scholars in Tunis. I also saw boys of fourteen who were studious and keen, who could sling at a hair [Judg 20:16]. But all their occupation is with the Talmud and the Maharsha, and the novellae and methods of the Ashkenazi rabbis, and a little with the wording of the Rambam ⚠28. But they are not so well versed in the codes and the responsa, and they have no memory; so much so that I, for my sins, who have no memory, was counted by them a man of great memory. And the gevirim esteem the Torah, and all their desire is to have sons who are scholars; they are very charitable, and honor the Torah and those who study it very, very much.

xiv

On the festival of Sukkot, the whole festival, I went to the synagogue and did the wavings [of the lulav] according to the intentions of the Ari, of blessed memory, and I put in the box ⚠29 a small paper of Names, and they did not notice. When I first came, the circle of kabbalists came to visit me, and I told them that I am not versed in kabbalah; in these words I told them: "there are no kabbalot here" ⚠30.

בחג הסוכות כל החג הייתי הולך לבית הכנסת ועושה הנענועים ע"פ כוונות האר"י ז"ל ומשים בתיבה נייר קטן של שמות ולא היו מרגישים. ובתחלת ביאתי באו כת המקובלים לבקרני ואמרתי להם שאיני בקי בקבלה. ובלשון הזה אמרתי להם אין כאן קבלות:

xv

And there, in the city of Tunis, although there were great and keen scholars there, and I am poor and lightly esteemed [1 Sam 18:23], I found much favor in their eyes, and they held me to be a great man. This is from the kindness of the Most High, for I am more brutish than any man [Prov 30:2]. I had a great love with the sage R. Joseph Zarqa, may his light shine, and with some of his disciples. The daughter of the gevir, the master of the house: one day I was studying, and she came and put a paper before me, and spoke to me as a maidservant to her mistress [cf. Ps 123:2]; I did not know what was in it, only I spoke to her a little, modestly, and at once she went. And I found in the paper ten Venetian [coins] ⚠31, and her husband was very fond of me ⚠32 and never ceased to show me love. I do not remember one in a hundred of their kindnesses and the good things they did me. Behold, their reward: length of days and years of life [Prov 3:2], riches and honor [Prov 3:16].

ושם בעיר תונס הגם שהיו שם חכמים עצומים וחריפים ואנכי רש ונקלה מצאתי חן הרבה בעיניהם והיו מחזיקים אותי לגברא רבא. וזה מחסד עליון כי בער אנכי מאיש. והיה לי אהבה רבה עם החר"י זרקא נר"ו ועם קצת תלמידיו. ובת הגביר הבעה"ב יום אחד הייתי לומד ובאה והניחה נייר לפני ואמרה לי דברים כשפחה לגבירתה ואני לא ידעתי מה בתוכו רק בצניעות דברתי לה קצת ותכף הלכה. ומצאתי בנייר עשרה ויניציאנו"ס ובעלה היה אוהבי מאד ותמיד לא זז מחבבני. ואיני זוכר א' ממאה מחסדיהם והטובות שעשו עמי. הנה שכרם אורך ימים ושנות חיים עושר וכבוד:

Uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 אי"ש צעיר — "A young man" is the usual humble self-description. אי"ש is printed with abbreviation marks, so it may also hide the initials of the editor, Joseph Shabbetai Farḥi, who signs at the end as ישפ"ה; not resolved.
  • ⚠2 ספר אורחות חיים — The Livorno editor opens with "the book of the paths of life" (orḥot ḥayyim), a play on the Ḥida's first name Ḥayyim; the book is named Ma'agal Tov a few lines below. The right edge of this page is torn in the archive.org copy; the lost letters are supplied from the copy of the 1885 printing at the National Library of Israel (S 32 A 1051) and from the earlier Italian printing (NLI, S 32 A 930).
  • ⚠3 ה"ה הח"הש ר' משה כ"ע — The archive.org copy is torn here and keeps only …ה; the NLI copy and the earlier printing read ר' משה, R. Moses: R. Moses Azulai, son of R. Isaiah, whom Shem Tov (part 10) also places in Ancona with the Ḥida's manuscripts. ה"ה is read as "namely" (הלא הוא) and הח"הש as "the perfect sage" (החכם השלם); כ"ע is not resolved.
  • ⚠4 את כל הקורות דבר בע"טו קש"תו ככס"רת — Rhymed prose with abbreviation marks inside the words, perhaps marking a wordplay or a chronogram. דבר בעטו is read as דבר בעתו, "a word in its season" (Prov 15:23, which goes on "how good it is", tov, the title word). קש"תו and ככס"רת (on the scan perhaps כנס"רת) are not understood and are left untranslated.
  • ⚠5 בלשון הזהב וההדר"ת — Read וההדר"ת, "and splendor" (hadderet), as the Piotrków reprint has it; in this type ה and ס look alike. The abbreviation marks are not explained.
  • ⚠6 ובהיותו רב רחומ'אי — Rav Reḥumai, the name of a Talmudic sage, used as a play: "a merciful rabbi". Shem Tov makes the same play on Musafia (part 10). The archive.org copy is torn here; the NLI copy, the earlier printing and the Piotrków reprint have it.
  • ⚠7 ביד נכדו הר"מ הנז' — הר"מ, "R. M[oses]", the grandson named above; so the NLI copy and the earlier printing (the worn archive.org copy seemed to read הר"ת).
  • ⚠8 ללקט באומרי"ם — Wordplay: Ruth 2:7 has "to glean among the sheaves" (ba-omarim); the editor spells it באומרים, "among the sayings", with abbreviation marks to point to the pun. ליקוטי בתר ליקוטי is Aramaic, "gleanings after gleanings"; "as bees do" (הדבו"רים, Deut 1:44) plays on devorim "bees" and dibburim "words".
  • ⚠9 וכאשר בא מהר"א הנז"ל — מהר"א, "our teacher R. A[braham]" (Musafia); so the NLI copy and the earlier printing (the archive.org copy seemed to read תהר"א). Shem Tov (part 10) calls Musafia the editor's dear friend.
  • ⚠10 תהלה לדויד ע"ס תהלים — Tehillah le-David ("A praise of David", Ps 145:1), a work on Psalms; ע"ס = על ספר, "on the book of". The title is torn in the archive.org copy and complete in the NLI copy. Not checked in bibliographies.
  • ⚠11 יתי אותו ואת כנהו — כנהו is not clear (perhaps "his stock", cf. Ps 80:16, or כחו "his strength").
  • ⚠12 ות את בכ"י את יופיו — בכ"י is read as בני כלל ישראל, "all Israel"; not certain.
  • ⚠13 כ"ד הצעיר מקי"ר ע"ה ישפ"ה ס"ט — כ"ד = "these are the words of". ישפ"ה = יוסף שבתי פרחי, Joseph Shabbetai Farḥi (1802–1882) of Livorno: so the National Library of Israel catalogue of the Livorno printing, and both copies of the 1885 printing read פ (not ע, as we first transcribed). The letters also spell yashfeh, "jasper" (Exod 28:20). מקי"ר and ע"ה are not resolved (ע"ה perhaps "servant of the Lord"). ס"ט = ספרדי טהור, "pure Sephardi", a usual signature of Sephardi rabbis. The Livorno printing (Eliyahu Ben Amozegh) is dated by the chronogram ברכת טוב, [5]639 = 1879.
  • ⚠14 ובוקי סריקי. שאין האיש מנוקה — בוקי סריקי ("empty flasks") is a Talmudic phrase for worthless people. שאין האיש מנוקה is read as "from whom no man comes away clean" (cf. Exod 34:7 נקה); in this type ש/ם and נ/כ look alike, and the Piotrków reprint reads האיש מנוקה. Not certain.
  • ⚠15 חני"נה בצער — Read חנינה, "grace", with the Piotrków reprint (in this type נ and כ look alike); the abbreviation marks may point to a pun.
  • ⚠16 מפרשת העבו"ר על / ועל צוארי — העבור is read as "what passed" over me (העבור עלי), with abbreviation marks perhaps pointing to a pun (also "intercalation" or "pregnancy").
  • ⚠17 ואת הנ"ס למינהו על חד מאה בדשערוהי — The whole phrase על חד מאה בדשערוהי quotes Targum Onkelos to Gen 26:12 (Isaac's field yielded "a hundredfold beyond what they had estimated"). ואת הנ"ס למינהו plays on ואת הנץ למינהו, "the hawk after its kind" (Lev 11:16), with נס, "miracle", for נץ, "hawk".
  • ⚠18 לאל העושה כהור עכני סגיאן מלוהי — כהור עכני is not understood; the Piotrków reprint reads העונה נהור ענני, which is not clear either. Left untranslated. סגיאן מלוהי is Aramaic "many are His words (or deeds)".
  • ⚠19 נהרי נחלי חסדי חסדים — Read נהרי נחלי, "rivers, streams" (cf. Job 20:17), with the Piotrków reprint; in this type נ and כ look alike.
  • ⚠20 ועשיתי לו פדיונות וממוני כוסף על אשר עשו מממונו — Pidyonot, gifts of charity given as "redemption" for a sick man. כוסף is read as כסף (money), meaning that he gave from his own money in addition to what was given from the sick man's; the sentence is hard and the reading is not certain.
  • ⚠21 המוסקו"ף הכה ויקף ראש — The Muscovite: the Russian fleet in the Mediterranean during the Russo-Turkish war of 1768–1774. ויקף ראש is read as "raised his head" (ויזקוף ראש), that is, grew bold; not certain.
  • ⚠22 מקורסאלי"ם — Italian corsali, corsairs.
  • ⚠23 טבא"קו חשוב וכמה צלוחיות שארו"פי — טבאקו = tobacco (Italian tabacco); שארופי = syrup (Italian sciroppo, Arabic sharāb).
  • ⚠24 יו"א תבא בא רשות ויצאנו לשלום — Permission from the port to land, probably after a quarantine at Sousse (pratique); read so from the context.
  • ⚠25 כי לא נח"ם אלהים דרך ארץ — Wordplay on Exod 13:17 ("God did not lead them by the way of the land of the Philistines"): derekh eretz, "the way of the land", also means good manners. The abbreviation marks on נחם point to the pun.
  • ⚠26 ור' אברהם קטן הפרנס — Katan is taken as a family name; it could also be read "the young R. Abraham, the warden".
  • ⚠27 ור' יעקב מעארק — Family name as printed; the vowels (Ma'arek, Me'arek) are not certain.
  • ⚠28 וקצת בלשונות הרמב"ם — From here to the end of the front matter, and the first line of part 1, the text is from a page lost in the archive.org copy (printed page ד), read from the copy of the 1885 printing at the National Library of Israel (S 32 A 1051). Letters lost at the cut right edge of that scan are supplied from the Piotrków reprint.
  • ⚠29 ומשים בתיבה נייר קטן של שמות — תיבה is read as a box or case, perhaps the holder of the lulav, or the reading desk (tevah) of the synagogue; a paper with holy Names was a kabbalistic practice.
  • ⚠30 אין כאן קבלות — A pun: kabbalot is "kabbalistic traditions" and also "receipts", so "there is no kabbalah here" is also "there is nothing to receive here".
  • ⚠31 עשרה ויניציאנו"ס — Veneziani, Venetian gold coins (sequins), current in the Mediterranean trade.
  • ⚠32 ובעלה היה אוהבי — The first letters are lost at the cut edge of the NLI scan; the Piotrków reprint reads אהובי, "my beloved".
