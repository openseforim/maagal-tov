141.1

2 Nisan [Monday 30 March 1778]: I was at the house of Signor Isaac ben Dana, and he gathered a large company, about fourteen souls of his relatives and friends. We were there about three hours, smoking ⚠1, with tea and "the miserable bread" [Num 21:5] and its trimmings. I spoke about the affairs of the Land of Israel, the five kinds ⚠2, and various matters, and I said to them: I am grieved for you. If you think that all this is exaggeration and lies, how can you be counted as God-fearing men and scholars? And if you hold that it is true, why is the closed heart in you not torn open [Hos 13:8], and why does your compassion not stir [Isa 63:15]? And if you say that the times are against you, why do you not stint your dough for comedies and your pleasures? In the day we went round with the padrinos ⚠3; most of them did not receive us, and cards ⚠4 were left in plenty. 1885 printing: 83

6 Nisan, Friday [3 April 1778]: three hours after midday I went to the house of the gaon, our teacher R. Saul, may his light shine, to go with him to the dedication of the synagogue that the Ashkenazim had built, for they had invited me. We went with him to their community chamber. R. Shalem was there, and three wardens of the Sephardim, Signor Mendes, Signor Suasso and Signor Alzido ⚠8, and their own wardens. At four o'clock their clerk ⚠9 stood up and read from a paper, calling the two rabbis of the communities to go; after that he called some of their wardens, the three Sephardi wardens and me, and we went to their old synagogue. There they were calling men to take the Torah scrolls, to take them out of the ark and hand them over, and they called me to take a Torah scroll out of the ark. Afterwards the clerk stood and read from the paper: the two rabbis are to go with the bearer of the first scroll, and I with the bearer of the second scroll, and so on up to seven. We went to the new synagogue, and they began to sing the songs that their rabbi had composed and the order he had arranged. They made seven circuits, and the clerk read from the paper which of them was to hold each of the seven scrolls in each circuit; in the first circuits they gave a Torah scroll to a Sephardi warden in each circuit. At the end of the seven circuits they sang the piyyut that the rabbi, our teacher R. Saul, had made; all of it was printed, and they gave out the booklets to everyone seated. After this they called the rabbi, our teacher R. Saul, to bring the first scroll into the ark; the second, R. Shalem; the third, me; the fourth, R. Saul; the fifth, R. Shalem; the sixth, me; the seventh, R. Saul. Then the gaon, our teacher R. Saul, stood there at the ark and said: Our rabbis said on "And Moses blessed them" [Exod 39:43]: with what blessing did he bless them? [Sifra, Shemini] One may ask: what does it matter to him what blessing he blessed them with? Have you taken the blessings out of the world? ⚠10 But it was hard for him, for the Blessed One punished Melchizedek because he put the blessing of Abraham, peace be on him, before the blessing of the Holy One, blessed be He [b. Nedarim 32b]. So what is this, that Moses our master blessed them and not the Holy One, blessed be He? On the contrary, the duty was to bless the Holy One, blessed be He, for He gives strength to do valiantly [Deut 8:18] and everything comes from Him; as it is said: one who has built a new house or bought new vessels says "who has kept us alive" [m. Berakhot 9:3]. To this he said: with what blessing did he bless them, them and not the Holy One, blessed be He? And he answered: that the Shekhinah should dwell [in the work of your hands]. And the Shekhinah among those below is a need of the One on high, as our rabbis said; so this blessing rises to the Lord, for by their blessing He, may He be exalted, is exalted, and great is the Lord and highly praised [Ps 48:2], for the Shekhinah among those below is a need of the One on high. So I too bless: may it be His will that the Shekhinah dwell in your works. And I also, in the name of them all, bless "who has kept us alive"; and he said the blessing "who has kept us alive" with the Name and the kingship. Then R. Shalem left, at six o'clock. The Sephardi wardens told me that I had to pray the afternoon and evening prayers there, and so I did. At first R. Shalem sat at the left of R. Saul, in the rabbi's chair; R. Shalem left, and afterwards R. Saul called me to sit beside him in R. Shalem's place.

7 Nisan, the Sabbath [4 April 1778]. On the night of the holy Sabbath [the evening of Friday 3 April] I prayed the evening prayer there, and they drew out their melodies very long: "for three things the earth trembles" ⚠11 [Prov 30:21], after their custom, in three kinds of voice; and most of all the welcoming of the Sabbath. 142.2

On the holy Sabbath day [4 April 1778] R. Shalem preached in the Sephardi synagogue, and this is a summary of his sermon and the gist of the words. "Is it not to deal your bread to the hungry, and that you bring the poor that are cast out to your house?" [Isa 58:7]. From the first of the month: "This month shall be to you" [Exod 12:2]: the charity is yours, the mercy is yours, the justice is yours, the Land of Israel is yours. One must praise the honorable wardens of the Mahamad, who do justice and charity; for charity needs great consideration of place, time, persons and bounds. That is what is said: "Happy is he who considers the poor" [Ps 41:2], and it does not say "who gives to the poor", for great understanding is needed. The honorable wardens do charity and kindness and judgments; and as for the wardens who now step down and the new ones who come in, their reward is to serve in the holy place: "they shall revive as the corn" [Hos 14:8], etc. The honorable wardens in their righteousness have made a new mantle for the Torah scroll at great cost, and it will be a memorial for them always before the Lord, as the verse says: "And the crowns shall be to Ḥelem" etc. "in the temple of the Lord" [Zech 6:14]. The honorable ones' charities are many, and we will mention a few in our sermon today. Our rabbis said on "As the girdle cleaves to the loins of a man" etc. [Jer 13:11]: a parable of a king who commanded his servant every single day about his shirt: did you keep it? did you fold it? etc. He said to him: my lord king, of all the garments you have, you command only about the shirt? He said to him: I command you about the shirt because it cleaves to my flesh [Lev. Rabbah 2]. So the Holy One, blessed be He, commands about Israel, for of the Congregation of Israel ⚠12 He said: "As the girdle cleaves to the loins of a man" etc. And with this we will understand the reading Vayeshev: "Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, and he made him a coat of many colors; and his brothers saw that their father loved him" etc. [Gen 37:3–4]. For one may ask: why did he not make him an important garment, a covering of honor, in which his affection would show more? Further, why does it say "his brothers saw", since this is not real seeing but understanding? And further, what hatred belongs to a coat for a son of old age, since that was the way, that a son of old age is doted on? But Jacob hinted by the coat that Joseph cleaved to him like a very coat; and the sign came, that he made him a coat, which was not so with his brothers; and this is "they saw", with the eye, that their father loved him more than all. And we learn that commanding about a thing many times shows affection, as in the parable of the king, who daily commands about the shirt, constantly, and about all its parts. So charity is precious before Him, for He always commands with doubled words: "you shall surely give" [Deut 15:10], "you shall surely open" [Deut 15:8], "you shall surely furnish" [Deut 15:14], "you shall not harden ... nor shut" [Deut 15:7]. And one can prove also from other commandments in which there is no mention of charity that their essence is for charity. Go and learn from the Passover offering: He commanded that they be counted ⚠13 while it is still day, that it be eaten in one house, only until midnight, and what remains until morning be burned. All this is for the need of the poor: for a rich man alone, who has no household, must take a lamb for the sake of honor; and what shall he do? He cannot leave it after midnight and cannot send it outside; and there are also the festival offering and the peace-offerings of rejoicing, and there is no time to eat it all. So he is compelled to invite poor men while it is still day, for another rich man would not agree to leave his house. And that is what is said: "Is it not to deal your bread to the hungry" etc. What is "is it not", which means certainty? Further, it should have said "you shall surely deal"; and further, "when you see the naked, that you cover him": it should have said "you shall cover him", for the word "and you cover him" indicates certainty. But it speaks of the Passover offering: "is it not dealt out": certainly a thing that is dealt out among the members of the household, and cannot be eaten [alone]: "your bread to the hungry", where "bread" is a feast, as in "made a great feast" 14 [Dan 5:1]. Also with the Passover offering there are matzot and bitter herbs, and certainly one must invite poor men into the house, for "you shall not carry out of the flesh outside" [Exod 12:46]. One must invite them on the eve of Passover; and if the poor man whom you invite to sit in the house with the lady is naked, you are compelled: "and you cover him". And although this is almost by compulsion, it is reckoned as charity, as they explained: "He has scattered, he has given to the needy" [Ps 112:9]: because he has already scattered, he gives to the needy; even so, "his righteousness endures for ever". So great is charity, and it belongs to Israel, and the men of this Mahamad do much of it. That is "the charity is yours, the mercy is yours": that is, charity in any manner, even if it is a thing already scattered, with no remedy but to throw it away, and one gives it to the poor, that too is yours, to take a reward for it. "The Land of Israel is yours": that you strive to support its inhabitants, for the matter is much needed, as now in Hebron; and they have sent their emissary, the great rabbi, a precious light ⚠15 [Zech 14:6], he who is "and the glory of the Lord filled" [Exod 40:34], "life for evermore" [Ps 133:3], who stands among us. They have already begun to make the donation; may they finish it well, for they are in danger, and for this reward may our holy house be built. This is the sermon, most of it as good as all. And now, at the end of the matter, he woke up to mention me and my mission. Had it been [the Sabbath of] Beshallaḥ ⚠16, it would have done some good. But it seems that he was forced to it outwardly because of a letter that came to him on Thursday from the captains of the hosts, the Officials of Constantinople, who urged him on this matter. 1885 printing: 85

28 Nisan, the holy Sabbath [25 April 1778]: the sage R. David Leon ⚠33 preached for about three quarters of an hour in Portuguese rhetoric, and found grace and favor in the eyes of all [Esth 2:17]. This is the gist of his sermon. "And David danced" etc. [2 Sam 6:14]. [The tractate] Megillah: "And David was the least" [1 Sam 17:14]: he made himself small, etc. [b. Megillah 11a]. The trait of pride is not accepted; therefore, a man of understanding, in what shall he pride himself? If in wealth, Koraḥ and Haman prove [the contrary]. If in wisdom, only the wise man knows that he does not know. If in lineage, behold David, peace be on him, of the tribe of Judah, and Ruth from the king of Moab; and "David was the least", for he made himself small. And if in might, behold Samson. So there is no ground for pride; and for this I have come armed today: I will draw the sword of the Torah to prove the truth. One must inquire: David, peace be on him, slung the stone into Goliath's forehead, and he fell [1 Sam 17:49]; and in Nebuchadnezzar's dream "a stone was cut out without hands", it fell, "and became a great mountain" [Dan 2:34–35]. Since David, peace be on him, did all his affairs in a spirit of wisdom, why did his stone fall and sink into the Philistine's forehead, while the stone of Nebuchadnezzar's dream became a great mountain? But from there you will know the truth of our words. The stone that David took was on the ground, and he lifted it with his sling up and up, and it came to rest in the forehead of the Philistine, whose height was six cubits and a span [1 Sam 17:4]; and that is its lowering: it fell with him to the ground. But in Nebuchadnezzar's dream, where the stone was cut out above and fell downward, it became a great mountain. This hints at the proud man who rises upward: "there he fell, destroyed" [Judg 5:27]. And the matter is: he who lowers himself, the Lord raises him. Therefore David's stone, which went up to a place not its own, came down a step ⚠34 and fell, never to rise again [Amos 5:2]; but the stone of Nebuchadnezzar's dream, which was at the height of the steps and fell at the feet of the image, "iron mixed with miry clay" [Dan 2:41], was raised up and became a great mountain. I shall further compel [assent to] the truth of our words from what is written: "and the spirit of God hovered over the face of the waters" [Gen 1:2]. One must inquire: why did the spirit of God rest on the element of water more than on all the four elements? But the element of fire, all its nature is to rise up and up, and it is like the very essence of pride. The element of earth, though most of it is low, the ground of the world, has nevertheless in some part the high mountains, wondrous in height, "mountains of peaks" [Ps 68:17]. The element of wind comes with force, with tyranny and terror, "rending mountains and breaking rocks" [1 Kgs 19:11]. But the element of water is very low, always going to the lowest places. Also in the creation, He, blessed be He, commanded the earth to bring forth its fruits, and it brought forth kinds of grass and trees fair to the eye of the sun; it showed its powers in beauty and splendor; and so beasts and cattle and man, wonderfully, all in the open. But when He commanded the waters, "Let the waters swarm" [Gen 1:20], they brought forth many, many living creatures and very awesome beings, like the work of the dry land; but they hid them, and they are hidden from the eye in the water, "as the waters cover the sea" [Isa 11:9]. And since lowliness and humility are in the nature of water, it merited that the spirit of God hovers over the face of the waters and not over the other elements, to let us know how precious is the trait of humility. And so you find in Jeremiah: when he entered the court of the kingdom to prophesy evil against the king and the princes, he said: "O land, land, land, hear the word of the Lord" [Jer 22:29]. One must understand why he said "land, land, land". For Jeremiah was great in strength, relying on the Lord who sent him, and with a strong heart he came to the king to prophesy evil; for were it not for His help, blessed be He, "there is one law for him, to put him to death" [Esth 4:11]. If so, why did he speak to the land? He could have spoken to them with a strong hand. Granted that one of the other people, reprovers and preachers, for fear of men of rank lest they crush his skull 35 [Judg 9:53], would put his words as though speaking to the earth and to dumb things, and hint at the great men; but Jeremiah, his hair did not stand on end, and he did not tremble or fear at all the evil prophecies; so why did he say "land, land, land"? But since he was speaking to the king and the princes, he hinted to them that they were created from the earth, and are dust in their lives, and at their death they return to the earth; that is "land" three times. And they have no reason to be proud, for in what shall one take pride who was created from dust, and is dust, and returns to dust? They once asked a certain distinguished man: Wherewith have you prolonged your days? And he answered: in all my days I never met a man lower than my rank 36. His words are good if he walked in darkness, like infants who never saw light [Job 3:16], or if all his life he did not stir from his house; but if he goes out and comes in, in the way of men, is not his saying an absolute lie? For lowly and inferior men go out and stand about in multitudes. But surely you will think that this man speaks princely things [Prov 8:6]: for when he goes out to the gate by the city [Job 29:7], if he meets a man wiser or richer or stronger than he, then he spoke truth, that he is indeed less than the man who meets him. And if he is wise and meets a simple man or a fool, he would say: his simplicity or his foolishness is greater than mine, for the sin that he sinned was not with understanding, and he is nearer to atonement than I, who know and understand the essence of the sin. And if he happens to meet a poor man and a wanderer, then he will think that he is righteous, for by his poverty and suffering he is atoned, as it is said: "He delivers the poor by his affliction" [Job 36:15]. And if he is crushed and broken, weak and in suffering, then he will say: all his good deeds are stored up for the world to come, for in this world he has seen no good; and the like. And so was David, peace be on him: in his youth he was lowly, and in his youth even his enemy [Doeg, b. Sanh 93b] spoke in his praise: "skilful in playing, a mighty man of valor, a man of war" etc. "and the Lord is with him" [1 Sam 16:18]. "Look at the little fellow at the back!" ⚠37: he made himself small, even when he became king; and therefore he danced with all his might before the Lord, and did not regard his own honor nor the honor of the kingship, for he was before the Lord, and before Him all is as nothing; and for the honor of the Lord he did not regard the ceremonies and the manners of the kingship. Indeed, therefore the first redeemer is the last redeemer, and David is prince for ever [Ezek 37:25]. May He in His mercy send him and gather our dispersed, and "he whose is the house shall come" 38 [Lev 14:35], speedily and soon.

147.6

On the Sabbath day [2 May 1778] R. Isaac Kohen Belinfante preached, and this is the gist of his sermon. "When a man has the plague of leprosy, he shall be brought to the priest" [Lev 13:9]. Chapter 2 of Avot: "Know what is above you" and so on [Avot 2:1]. A man trembles and is startled to see the changes of fortune: yesterday an awesome king and today a slave of slaves; yesterday dressed in dyed embroidery and today in a torn, trailing cloak; yesterday reclining at royal dainties and today dust is his bread; yesterday every precious stone was his covering [Ezek 28:13] and today thick darkness is his swaddling band [Job 38:9]. And it was not a descent step by step, but from the heights of heaven to the depth of the great abyss, a mighty fall and a fresh wound. All these befell the work of the hands of the Holy One, blessed be He: Adam, king over all the earth, reclining in the Garden of Eden, the ministering angels roasting meat for him [b. Sanh 59b], his servants a flaming fire [Ps 104:4]; and as in a moment he was driven out and was trampled, and put on garments of skin, and by the sweat of his brow he would eat thorns and thistles [Gen 3:18–19]. This is a wonder. But its root was the envy of the serpent, and the desire for the tree of knowledge, and honor: "and you shall be like God" [Gen 3:5] [envy, desire and honor: m. Avot 4:21]. Instead of these three, terrors were turned upon him [Job 30:15]; and about these will our words be today. Envy is the father of impurity. Four opened with "af" [Gen. Rabbah 19:2]: the serpent, "af ki amar Elohim" [Gen 3:1]; the baker, "af ani ba-ḥalomi" [Gen 40:16]; Koraḥ, "af lo el eretz" [Num 16:14]; Haman, "af lo heviah Esther" [Esth 5:12]; all of them from the greatness of their envy. The serpent, as is known, envied the greatness of Adam; the baker envied his fellow, when he saw his fellow's interpretation; Koraḥ and Haman are known. And it is the initials: קנאה, envy = Koraḥ, Naḥash (the serpent), Ofeh (the baker), Haman. And we find Cain, who envied Abel and killed him. And we saw the twelve princes, who were equal in their offerings, and none had any precedence over another. Therefore Scripture spells out each offering of each one, though they were equal, and Scripture could have included them as one; but it revealed their righteousness, that they all intended to be equal in everything, so that none would exceed his fellow. And of Judah, who was first, it is written "ve-korbano" with a vav [Num 7:13], to teach that although he was first, he counted himself as if another had gone before him. And Judah earned being first, as it is said "Judah became His sanctuary, Israel His dominion; the sea saw and fled" [Ps 114:2–3], for Naḥshon leapt in [Sotah 37a], and so on; and even so he did not act with lordship at all. That is what it says: "How good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell" [Ps 133:1]: that they do not envy like Cain and Abel; when they love one another without envy, how good and how pleasant! And it says "also together", to say that it is good also when they are not brothers but are together partners in their authority, wardens and leaders, and they have no envy: good and pleasant. And about desire it says: "Many are the pains of the wicked, but he who trusts in the Lord, kindness surrounds him" [Ps 32:10]. One may explain that the rich, when they see some loss in trade and profits and the like, and want to manage their affairs, first cut off the charity: if they used to give some sum, they stop it. And when an honored poor man comes to them, and they feel that he comes about money matters, they begin to tell their sorrow, that they have come down from their wealth, the trade goes on dwindling, the profits too have collapsed, and the capital of the householders goes to ruin [b. Suk 29b]; so that the poor man has no mouth to speak and goes away disappointed. That is "many are the pains of the wicked": he tells that his troubles and pains are many, and his intention is that the poor man should not ask of him. "But he who trusts in the Lord", who says: I will do my part to revive the heart of the crushed, "kindness surrounds him", and He pours out a blessing for him without end [Mal 3:10]. Or else it speaks of the wicked who all night eat and drink and feast and dance and sing and drink wine and strong drink; and if a man goes to visit one of them, he will see him fallen on the bed, his head like Carmel [Song 7:6] from much drinking, his tongue cleaving to his palate from much shouting, his throat dried and burning with fire from the wine that burned it, his belly aching from gluttony, and his legs aching from the dancing, from the sole of the foot to the head, and so on. And that is "many are the pains of the wicked". Against this, the scholar who studies Torah all night: a thread of kindness is drawn over him by day [Ḥag 12b]. And that is "he who trusts in the Lord", that He will give him strength to study His Torah, not slumbering or sleeping: kindness, a thread of kindness, surrounds him, and he is healthy and strong. Therefore if all gather for the pleasures of the throat and to rejoice in songs of grapes, evil will come upon them; but at meals for a commandment, for the sake of Heaven, with words of Torah at the table and eating and drinking as is fitting, that is the service of the Lord. And that is "for not by bread alone" [Deut 8:3], meaning a feast, as in "Belshazzar made a great feast (lit. bread)" [Dan 5:1]: if it is alone, without Torah and charity to the poor, "does man live"; for on the contrary, the table (shulḥan) turns into a serpent (naḥash), the feast (mishteh) into a mockery (meshatteh) 52, the filling of the belly (keres) into destruction (qeretz) [Jer 46:20], the belly (beten) of the wicked into a viper (peten), joy (sasson) into darkness (ishon), laughter (tseḥikah) into crying out (tse'akah), and the Song of Songs (shirim) into pangs (tsirim); upon the ten-stringed instrument and upon the lute [Ps 92:4], upon want and upon vanity. Therefore it hints: "not by bread alone does man live, but by everything", that is, by what is here, "that comes out of the mouth of the Lord", words of Torah: if it is in all his affairs, at his table and in the company of his friends, then "man lives". And honor: we shall speak of it in particular. "Who is honored? He who honors people" [Avot 4:1]; and today they despise him who is fit for honor, such as the scholar and the like, and honor the brutish among the people. As we find with Ahab: Ben-hadad sent to him to ask for his wives and his silver, and he held nothing back from him; and in the end, when he would ask of him the delight of his eyes [1 Kings 20:6], that is the Torah scroll [b. Sanh 102b], he spoke to the elders, and they did not agree; for though they were idolaters they knew the honor of the Torah. And the prophet sent to him that he should not fear, and should take care to kill Ben-hadad; and he, when Ben-hadad's servants came, said to them "Is he still alive? He is my brother" [1 Kings 20:32], and took him into the chariot, and paid no heed to the prophet's words, for he said: the prophet and the sage know only their Torah and not the conduct of the kingdom, what they call politica di stato; and he said of a man who was an adversary and an enemy, "he is my brother". And against that, he said to Elijah the prophet, "Is it you, troubler of Israel?" [1 Kings 18:17]. So he despised the prophet of the Lord, who is fit for all honor, and honored the enemy of the Lord and His people. And that is the way of many simple ones of our people: they despise the scholars and honor those hated by the Lord. To these three the Mishnah hints: "Know what is above you: an eye that sees" — against envy: do you envy what the Holy One, blessed be He, wanted? For an eye sees, and everything is from Him. "And an ear that hears" your desire, your dainties, your songs and your dancing; and be ashamed and confounded. "And all your deeds" — against honor, that you honor the transgressors and despise the scholars — "are written in a book". But the remedy for this is: "When a man has the plague of leprosy, he shall be brought to the priest." For there are three kinds: a rising (se'et), a scab (sappaḥat) or a bright spot (baheret) [Lev 13:2]. Se'et is smoke and fire, as in "and the column of smoke (mas'et) began to rise" [Judg 20:40], for envy is in him like fire in his bones; and the remedy is "he shall be brought to the priest", to the scholar, to teach him knowledge, that this world is vanity, and no man touches what is prepared for his fellow [Yoma 38b]. And it is hinted in the Mishnah: "Know what is above you: an eye that sees"; by this knowledge you will keep far from envy, for He sees the chambers of your heart, your envy and your causeless hatred. And if the leprosy is sappaḥat, it hints at desire, as it is said "Attach me (sefaḥeni), I pray, to one of the priestly offices, that I may eat bread" [1 Sam 2:36]; then the remedy is "he shall be brought to the priest", the scholar will teach him: "the righteous eats to the satisfying of his soul, but the belly of the wicked shall want" [Prov 13:25]. And that is hinted in the Mishnah: "and an ear that hears" words of Torah, the food of the soul. And if it is baheret, seeking honor, and honoring the wicked and despising the righteous: "he shall be brought to the priest", he will open his eyes: "he honors those who fear the Lord" [Ps 15:4]. And it is hinted in the Mishnah: "and all your deeds are written in a book". "And the living will lay it to heart" [Eccl 7:2], and turn back and repent; and by this we shall merit the redemption, speedily in our days, amen. That is the summary of his sermon; he went on at length, with rhetoric and with pleasant words.

19 Iyyar, Saturday [16 May 1778]: On the holy Sabbath R. David Leon preached, and this is the gist of his sermon in brief. "Whoever guards his mouth and his tongue guards his soul from troubles" [Prov 21:23]. The Yalkut: the tongue kills, the tongue gives life, as it is said, "Death and life are in the power of the tongue" [Prov 18:21]. It is a wonder that a man can do harm with his tongue. He is like the locust, which in a short moment darkens the land, covers the sun and consumes everything. So the slanderer, in all his aspects, covers the truth that is clear as the sun, covers over things that are not so, darkens a man's face with many evils, and consumes and harms everything. Among the plagues, Pharaoh was struck with the frogs, which cried out and croaked, and it is written, "that He may take away from me only this death" [Exod 10:17] ⚠70. See that in all the plagues he did not call them death, only with the frogs, to hint that it is the tongue that kills. And when He brought the locust, which is like the slanderer, it is written, "and drove them into the Sea of Reeds" [Exod 10:19], measure for measure, for the Sea of Reeds is called a tongue, as it is written, "And the Lord shall utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea" [Isa 11:15]; the locust is a master of the tongue, therefore it was drowned in the tongue of the sea. The cause of evil speech is that a man does not set his heart to look into his own faults, but sees the faults of others; and if he set his heart, it would appear that he who speaks evil has many evils himself. We see this in Absalom, who said to Hushai, "Is this your kindness to your friend?" [2 Sam 16:17], rebuking him: since he was David's friend, how could he leave him in his trouble? And he did not see himself, that he was his son, whom [David] had brought into the world and raised, and he sought to kill him. And this is the reason 71 that in the Temple there were "windows broad within and narrow without" [1 Kings 6:4] (written in the notebook of the journeys, section 246). Another explanation: "whoever guards his mouth and his tongue": for sometimes one indulges in much mockery and slander in order to eat in the houses of the rich; and he who guards his mouth, so that he does not go after his belly, thereby guards his tongue, and guards his soul from troubles. Or: whoever guards his mouth and his tongue from forbidden food ⚠72 guards his soul from troubles, from being defiled by the unclean spirit that is in forbidden foods. And he spoke at much greater length.

26 Iyyar, Saturday, the holy Sabbath of the portion Behar [23 May 1778]: At night [the evening of Friday 22 May] I was at the house of Signor Moses Enriques Medina, and I was in joy, blessed be the Lord. In the day R. Isaac Cohen Belinfante preached, and this is the gist of his sermon in brief. "And I will turn to you and multiply you" etc. [Lev 26:9]. Judah ben Tema says: Be bold as a leopard, etc. [m. Avot 5:20]. For ever and ever this is the rule, that a man learns from what he sees others do; therefore fathers must take care to walk in good ways, so that the sons learn from them. One must learn qualities for His service even from the beasts; this is what it says, "Be bold as a leopard." For although boldness is a disgraceful quality, if it is for the sake of Heaven it is praiseworthy. The proof is from Ruth: it seems that she was bold-faced in coming to Boaz, but because it was for the sake of Heaven, Obed came out of it. "Light as an eagle": it is merciful to its young and flies higher and higher, and one must learn mercy from it, as it says, "As an eagle stirs up her nest" etc. [Deut 32:11]. And Solomon said, "There are three things which are too wonderful for me, and four which I know not: the way of an eagle in the air, the way of a serpent upon a rock, the way of a ship in the midst of the sea, and the way of a man with a maid" [Prov 30:18–19]. The plain sense: the eagle flies up to the heavens and is burned, falls into the sea and is renewed, as it says, "your youth is renewed like the eagle" [Ps 103:5]; this happens to it ten times, as the naturalists say, and in the end it falls into the sea and there is its burial. The wonder is: after it was once burned and then renewed with difficulty, how does it go up again to be burned? That is "the way of an eagle in the air." "The way of a serpent": it feeds on dust, so how can it be "upon a rock," since it has no enjoyment or use from the rock? "The way of a ship in the midst of the sea": there is a dangerous place, and many mishaps have happened in that place, and even so they go that way. "The way of a man with a maid": evil diseases come upon a man through intercourse 74, and after he is cured with difficulty and suffering he returns again to his former habit, and that is a wonder. The homiletic sense: the carcass of a clean bird defiles in the gullet, and an unclean bird does not defile [Sifra, Aḥarei Mot 11]; that is "the way of an eagle," meaning an unclean bird, and he took the eagle because it is the king of birds; and that is "in the air (ba-shamayim)": only in heaven do they know the reason. "The way of a serpent upon a rock": only the eight creeping things defile, and others do not [b. Ḥul 122a]; that is "the way of a serpent," which is different. "Upon a rock (tzur)": as it were, He alone knows the reason of the thing. "The way of a ship": it is clean even if it is of earthenware, as stated in the chapter Rabbi Akiva [b. Shabbat 83b], and that is a wonder. "The way of a man with a maid": if another man betrothed his divorced wife, she is forbidden to him even though the other did not come to her, and if she whored she is permitted to him [b. Yev 11b], and that is a wonder. And the moral: a man toils greatly to grow rich. If his intention is to multiply charity and to have mercy on the poor, that is "the way of the eagle," which is merciful; this one stores up "in the heavens," as it says, "Monobaz: I have stored up above" [b. Bava Batra 11a]. "The way of a serpent": if his intention is to fill his desire with eating and drinking, that is the way of the serpent, and everything goes to loss, and the thing is hard as a rock; that is "upon a rock." "The way of a ship": his intention is to send ships and merchandise; in the end the ships will sink and the wealth is lost "in the midst of the sea," where the ships sink. "The way of a man with a maid": if his intention is to whore, to hire singing women, "there he fell down despoiled" [Judg 5:27], and that wealth is lost in an evil matter. "Swift as a deer and strong as a lion": his intention should be for the sake of Heaven, for if not, he will be a mockery and a derision. Whoever takes care in this, to have in his hand these four things, bold, light, swift and strong, "behold, his reward is with him" [Isa 40:10]. And to this He alluded: "And I will turn to you, and make you fruitful, and multiply you" [Lev 26:9], "and I will walk among you … and make you go upright" [Lev 26:12–13]: these are four things, each one a reward against the four things mentioned. After prayers most of the wardens and several men of standing came to visit me, because they knew that I was setting out in peace. 1885 printing: 98

All his occupation is in the woods and on the seashore, in solitude. He used to say that he busies himself for the good of the world, that he is of the tribe of Judah, that no man has gone so deep in his works these four hundred years, and that he is king over the whole world, since he has gone through the paths of the sea. And that Joseph della Reina and R. Judah the Pious occupied themselves with his works ⚠91; and other foolishness of this kind, that he walks in a wood that dates from the six days of creation. What is plain to see is that he has much money.

סיון 12. א' בהעלותך בבקר בא ר' אברהם בועז ור' משה ור"ש הנז' בקארוסה לביתי והלכתי אני וסי' דוד אתם לכפר שיולינג ששם הוא חוף הים וכבוד גדול עשו לי והיינו שם כמו ד' שעות. וסיפרו על ב"ש מלונדרים שמואל פולאק בן הרב יהושע פלאק נפלאות ולובש בגד זהב ואותיות חקוקות בו מעשה ידיו ויש לו חרב שפורחת. וכל עסקו ביערים ובשפת הים בהתבודדות והוא היה אומר שמתעסק לטובת העולם והוא משבט יאודה ובמעשיו לא נתעמק אדם זה ת' שנה, והוא מלך על ארץ תבל שהלך הוא בשבילי הים. ויוסף די לה ריינא ור' יהודה החסיד נתעסקו במעשיו וכיוצא בזה שאר שטיות שהולך ביער שהוא מששת ימי בראשית. והנגלה הוא שיש לו ממון הרבה.

155.2

We dined with the ḥazzan, Signor R. S. Saruk, and he did wonders in honoring us. I went up to his room and saw the book Ḥesheq Shelomo, by him, in manuscript, [written] when he became ḥazzan in the Sephardi congregation of The Hague called Ḥonen Dal. In it are written the plain customs of the prayers, some gleanings ⚠92 from the early authorities, and at the end some opinions, responsa of his, on smallpox, an error in a Torah scroll, salting, and reading with the stress on the last syllable: four or five, and no more.

וסעדנו אצל החזן סי' ר"ש סרוקו והפליא לכבדנו ועליתי לחדרו וראיתי ס' חשק שלמה ממנו כ"י בעת שנעשה חזן בק"ק ספרדים של האייא הנקרא חונן דל וכתוב בו פשוטי מנהגי התפלות ואיזה גרגירים מהראשונים ובסופו איזה דעות תשובות ממנו על חולי האבעבועות וטעות ס"ת ומליחה, וקריאת מלרע, ד' או ה'. ותו לא.

155.3

After the afternoon prayer we went to the warden Moses de Pinto, son-in-law of Moses de Pinto; his little son is called Moses de Pinto. His mother-in-law has an income of sixty thousand florins a year, the fruits of her capital in London. He showed us "the riches of his glory" [Esth 1:4]: twelve chairs, each worth a hundred sequins, all of them needlework, with embroidered pictures, wonderful; a canapé of the same, three hundred sequins; a shining bureau ⚠93, a wonder, whose value the notable, the prince, his father-in-law, did not want to reveal; a spinet (espineta), a musical instrument, wonderfully large; a crystal chandelier with a crown at its top, London work, wonderful; his garden (jardín), and the like. They treated us to fruit and sweets, in cleanliness. All his brothers-in-law were there, the sons of Signor Moses de Pinto, and Signor Moses Suasso and Signor Isaac Suasso, and the precious ladies; all of them honored us greatly with a pleasant countenance. We sat with the ḥazzans until one hour before midnight, and they sent us home by carriage.

אחר מנחה הלכנו לפרנס משה די פינטו חתן משה די פינטו ובנו הקטן נק' משה די פינטו וחמותו יש לה הכנסה ששים אלף פלורינים לשנה פירות קרן שלה בלונדרים. והראנו את עושר כבודו י"ב כסאות כל א' שוה ק' סיקיני שהם כלם מעשה מחט וציורים מעשה רוקם להפליא. וקאנאפי מזה ש' סיקיני. ובורו מבהיק דבר פלא שלא רצה הקצין השר חמיו לגלות ערכו. ואיספיניתא שהוא כלי זמר גדול להפליא. ומנורת קריס' עם עטרה בראשה מעשה לונדרים להפליא. והג'ארדין שלו וכיוצא וכיבדנו בפירות וממתקים בנקיות והיו שם כל גיסיו בני סי' משה די פינטו וסי' משה סואסו וסי' יצחק סואסו והנשים יקרות הכל כיבדונו מאד בסבר אשר סוברים פנים יפות וישבנו עם החזנים עד שעה א' קודם חצות לילה ושלחנו בקארוסה לבית.

155.4

13 Sivan, Monday [8 June 1778]: We visited the notable R. Eliezer, son of R. Baer Kann 94, the great notable of Frankfurt am Main. Two hours before the afternoon prayer we went with the ḥazzan and his son, in a carriage that Signor Benjamin Teixeira sent, to go to the garden (jardín). He received us with a pleasant countenance, and we went round the garden. It is large, and in it is a pool with fish, and figures of statues (estatuas), boys and girls, this one holding his aching head and that one carrying his weapons, and the like. In it are strawberries (Italian fragole) and melons, and also the fruit ananas of America, and grapes, and all vegetables and pulses for the table, and roses and beautiful, lovely flowers, and strange plants from America, and the like; the eye is not satisfied with seeing [Eccl 1:8]. We went to the house, and there was a table of all delicacies, and we said the blessing she-heḥeyanu over a melon. He paid us great honor, and we went with the carriage to the synagogue for the afternoon prayer.

סיון 13. ב' בקרנו להקצין ר' אליעזר בן ר' בער קאן הקצין הגדול מפפד"ם וקודם מנחה שתי שעות הלכנו עם החזן ובנו בקארוסה ששלח סי' בנימין טיקשיירה ללכת לחארדין וקבלנו בסבר פנים יפות וסבבנו החארדין והוא גדול ובו בריכה עם דגים וצורות היסטאטואס ילדים וילדות זה חש בראשו וזה נושא כלי זיינו וכיוצא ובו פראוולאס ומילונים וגם פרי אנאנאס של מריקה וענבים וכל ירקות וקטניות לסעודה ושושנים ופרחים יפים ונאים ועשבים משונים מהמיריקא p. 156 וכיוצא לא תשבע עין לראות והלכנו לבית ושם שלחן מכל מגדים ובירכנו שהחיינו על מילון וכיבדנו כבוד גדול והלכנו עם הקארוסה לבה"כ למנחה.

Notes and uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 מעלי עשן — Literally "raising smoke": read as smoking tobacco.
  • ⚠2 וחמשת המינים — Probably the five kinds of funds or collections for the Land of Israel that the emissary explained; not the five species of grain. Sense uncertain.
  • ⚠3 הפאדרינוס — Spanish/Portuguese padrinos, "patrons": the members of the Portuguese community appointed to sponsor the emissary's collection (Aaron Capadose and Moses de Isaac Suasso).
  • ⚠4 קארטאס — Spanish cartas: visiting cards left where the host was not received or not at home.
  • ⚠5 מהר"ש — R. Saul Löwenstamm, chief rabbi of the Ashkenazi community of Amsterdam.
  • ⚠6 יעקב מליקמאן — Family name given as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠7 ר' ליב גורבין ופריגיר — The scan has gimel in both names (the first pass read נורבין ופריניר). פריגיר is probably Preger, that is Prager, a known Ashkenazi family of Amsterdam; Gorbin is not identified.
  • ⚠8 סי' ואלזידו — So printed (checked on the scan); not identified. Perhaps a misprint for אזיוידו (Azevedo), a family that appears in the next pages.
  • ⚠9 הנאמן — Literally "the trusted one": read as the community's secretary or clerk.
  • ⚠10 אטו ברכות שקלת מעלמא — Printed so; an Aramaic rhetorical question, literally "have you taken blessings from the world?", that is: why should the midrash care which blessing it was? Rendering uncertain.
  • ⚠11 תחת שלש רגזה ארש — Prov 30:21 reads רגזה ארץ; the print has ארש. A joke on the three kinds of voice (perhaps cantor, choir and congregation) that "shook the earth".
  • ⚠12 כי הוא כ"י אמר — כ"י read as כנסת ישראל, "the Congregation of Israel"; the phrase is awkward and the reading is uncertain.
  • ⚠13 שיתענו מבע"י — So printed (checked on the scan): "that they fast". The law of the Passover offering is that the eaters are counted on it in advance (נמנין עליו), so שיתמנו, "be counted", is probably meant.
  • 14 כמו עבד לחם רב — Dan 5:1, Belshazzar "made a great feast" (עבד לחם רב): לחם is a feast, so "your bread" is the Passover meal, which must go to the hungry. R. Isaac Belinfante uses the same proof in his sermon of 5 Iyar.
  • ⚠15 המאה"ג אור יקרות ה"ה וכבוד ה' מלא חיים עד העולם — Honorific of the Ḥida himself, with a play on his name Ḥayyim (חיים); the joining of the verses is uncertain.
  • ⚠16 ואלמלא היה פ' בשלח — That is, had he said this some two months earlier (the Sabbath of Beshallaḥ was 7 February 1778), near the start of the Ḥida's stay.
  • ⚠17 אכלתי מצה בלתי בלילה וביום עשירה — The eve of Passover fell on the Sabbath. מצה בלתי is short for "matzah that is not [enriched]": plain matzah at the Friday-night meal, and "enriched matzah" (מצה עשירה, made with fruit juice or eggs) at the day meal, since plain matzah is not eaten on the day before Passover.
  • ⚠18 אבל לא דברתי הלילה — Perhaps he did not expound the Haggadah at length as usual; sense uncertain.
  • ⚠19 סאליאה — Probably Spanish silla (de manos) or Italian sedia, a sedan chair.
  • ⚠20 בב"י סי' תמ"ג — Printed תמ"ג (443). The custom of Hallel in the synagogue on Passover night is in the Tur and the Beit Yosef, Oraḥ Ḥayyim 473 (תע"ג), and the translation follows that.
  • 21 ככתוב בברכי יוסף — Freimann: Birkei Yosef, Oraḥ Ḥayyim 487; the proof that was brought is from b. Arakhin 10.
  • ⚠22 אברהם — The Ḥida's attendant on the journey, Abraham Gabbai.
  • ⚠23 ייא פאתאח ייא ראזאק ייא עאלים — Arabic: "O Opener, O Provider, O All-Knowing", names of God.
  • ⚠24 סירמוניא ... וינגא אין בונורה — Spanish ceremonia, and venga en buena hora, "welcome", a greeting of Sephardi women.
  • 25 מהר"ר העשיל — R. Abraham Joshua Heschel of Kraków (about 1595–1663), who left almost no written work. Freimann refers to Zunz, Ir ha-Tzedek, p. 104, and to the Ḥida's Shem ha-Gedolim, where these stories are told again under R. Heschel and under Siftei Kohen.
  • ⚠26 מהר"ש בעל ווי הע' — R. Shabbetai Sheftel Horowitz, son of the author of the Shelah, author of Vavei ha-Amudim; he became rabbi of Vienna only in 1655, so the year does not fit. The story is as told. Freimann refers to Kaufmann, Die letzte Vertreibung der Juden aus Wien, p. 63.
  • 27 וכהא"ג — כהאי גוונא, Aramaic for "in the same way".
  • 28 הרב הירש מבאלחוב — Freimann: the same man as R. Ẓevi of Bolechów on 7 Iyar (Hirsch is the Yiddish for Ẓevi); he refers back to p. 133 and takes him to be the author of a book printed in 5528 (1768).
  • ⚠29 הסי' אזיוידוס — Printed אזיוידום; read as the plural אזיוידוס, "the Azevedos" (ם for ס).
  • 30 ספר שהוא עושה — Freimann: the book is Siaḥ Yitzḥak; a sample of it was printed by Gabriel Polak in Ha-Maggid, 1869, nos. 10–11.
  • ⚠31 וירנאנדי — Printed so; probably the gentile merchant Carlo Birnandi (בירנאנדי) of the earlier entries in Amsterdam.
  • 32 אור הששי — אור is the evening before the day, as in אור לארבעה עשר [m. Pes 1:1]: Thursday evening, when 27 Nisan began.
  • ⚠33 ח"ר דוד ליאון — David de Isaac de Leon, a preacher of the Portuguese community of Amsterdam.
  • ⚠34 אחרי דרגא — Printed אחרי דרגא, "after a step", which gives no sense. Probably the Aramaic נחית דרגא or אחות דרגא, "go down a step" [b. Yev 63a]; the translation follows that.
  • 35 פן יריצו את גולגלתו — After Judg 9:53, ותרץ את גלגלתו, "and crushed his skull".
  • 36 לא פגעתי אדם קטון מערכי — The answer is the saying of the pious man in Ḥovot ha-Levavot, Gate of Humility, ch. 10: he never met a man in whom he did not see something better than in himself; the preacher's cases (wiser, simpler, poorer) follow that chapter. The question, "Wherewith have you prolonged your days?", is that of b. Meg 27b–28a.
  • ⚠37 חזו גברא זוטרא דאחורי — Aramaic, read as "See the little man at the back"; חזו is taken as an imperative. The source of the wording is not found.
  • 38 ובא אשר לו הבית — The words of Lev 14:35, from the law of leprosy in a house, turned to Him whose House the Temple is.
  • 39 סי' רד"ך אזיוידו — רד"ך is ר' דוד כהן: R. David ha-Kohen Azevedo, whose son Daniel appears in the earlier Amsterdam entries.
  • 40 עצ"ב מזריע — A play on עשב מזריע זרע, "herb yielding seed" [Gen 1:11], with עצב, sorrow, for עשב; it also plays on Tazria (תזריע), the reading of the week. The scan has מזריע (the first pass read מוריע).
  • ⚠41 אברהם פלגאה הוא — The Ḥida's attendant Abraham. The scan has פלגאה (the first pass read פלנאה), taken as Aramaic for "at odds" (root פלג): he quarrelled with the landlady. The form is rare and the sense is a proposal.
  • 42 משוחר"ר במרה שחורה — The marks show a double sense: משוחרר is "set free" and, from שחור, "blackened" with melancholy; compare שחרחורת משוחרר on 3 Iyar.
  • ⚠43 אברהם די וויגא — Transliterated as de Veiga; not identified.
  • ⚠44 הפריזידינטי סי' יצחק קראסטו — De Crasto, a form of de Castro, is a known family of the Portuguese community of Amsterdam; this Isaac is not further identified.
  • 45 למשרי תיגראי — Freimann: b. Ned 62a, where a scholar may ask that his suit be settled first (שרו לי תיגראי ברישא). The first pass had "to settle the quarrels".
  • 46 הג' מהר"ש — R. Saul Löwenstamm, the Ashkenazi chief rabbi of Amsterdam, who pleads for the Ḥida at the Ashkenazi council on 16 Iyar.
  • 47 האבו"ת ... תולדו"ת — האבו"ת, the "fathers", are the two padrinos (padrino, godfather, from padre), Capadose and Suasso: "fathers" (avot) that bring forth no "offspring" (toladot), the pair of terms of b. BK 2a, and "the two of them" that do not couple.
  • ⚠48 על רגע"י ארץ — The words of Ps 35:20, רגעי ארץ, with marks in רגע"י: read as a play on רגלי, "on my feet on the ground", that is on foot and not by carriage; so he missed Capadose, who came by carriage. The reading is a proposal.
  • ⚠49 סי' אד"ו — Probably Abraham de Veiga (אברהם די וויגא), Capadose's clerk, named in the entry of 29 Nisan; the initials fit.
  • ⚠50 הו' — So printed; read as ו', Friday, which the date confirms.
  • ⚠51 אחל"י — Abbreviation not resolved; left as initials.
  • 52 השלחן נהפך לנחש ומשתה למשטה — A chain of word plays by changed or moved letters: שלחן and לנחש, משתה and משטה (with tet: mockery, folly), כרס and קרץ, בטן and פתן, ששון and אישון, צחיקה and צעקה, שירים and צירים, עשור and חסור, נבל and הבל.
  • ⚠53 סאליאה — Read as a sedan chair (silla); uncertain.
  • ⚠54 בוטראמאם — Read as Dutch boterhammen, slices of bread and butter.
  • 55 ס' ברית יצחק הקצר — Freimann: printed in Amsterdam in 5489 (1729).
  • ⚠56 מפראיישדורף — Place transliterated from the print; not identified.
  • 57 ושאלוהו שלא יניחוהו — Literally "that they would not let him": what if the soldiers do not allow it. The end of the story shows the sense.
  • ⚠58 ר' ליב מצלוסק — Freimann: perhaps Slutsk (מסלוצק), with a question mark.
  • 59 מהאבו"ת — As on 3 Iyar: the "fathers" are the two padrinos, Capadose and Suasso.
  • 60 ר' צבי באלחוב — The R. Hirsch of Bolechów of 25 Nisan. Freimann takes him to be the author of a book printed in 5528 (1768).
  • 61 ח' פסוקים שהם ר"ת ישעיה בא"ל — Eight verses of Ruth do not begin with vav: 1:9, 1:12, 2:9, 2:12, 1:13, 1:17, 1:21 and 3:13. Their first letters, in this order, spell ישעיה באל. Freimann lists the verses (three of his verse numbers are one too low).
  • 62 גרוש בויינא — Freimann: the expulsion from Vienna under Emperor Leopold I, which he dates 5431 (1671).
  • 63 שכמ"ה — שכרם כפול מן השמים, "may their reward be double from heaven"; Freimann expands it in the same way.
  • ⚠64 שא' ה' שישקול השער — Freimann: 2 Sam 14:26, where Absalom's hair is weighed "by the king's stone" (באבן המלך); that is the link with the stone. The ה' is perhaps for הכתוב, "the verse".
  • 65 והנושא ... והמאמר — A Sephardi sermon opens with a verse, the נושא (theme), and a rabbinic saying, the מאמר.
  • ⚠66 כלב האבן — "Like the heart of stone" (ke-lev ha-even): the words of Ezek 36:26, from which b. Suk 52a learns that the evil inclination is called a stone. The same letters give kelev, a dog, that returns [Prov 26:11]; this second sense is a proposal.
  • 67 ר' דוד מיזה — Freimann: David Meza; he refers to Kayserling, Biblioteca Española-Portugueza-Judaica, p. 71.
  • 68 הגאון אב"ד דאשכנזים — Freimann: R. Saul, son of R. Aryeh Leib (Löwenstamm); he died in Amsterdam in 5550 (1790).
  • ⚠69 (ברור מובר"י מדינה) — Parenthesis as printed; not resolved. מדינה may be the Medina family, but ברור מובר"י is unclear.
  • ⚠70 ובמכות פרעה הוכה בצפרדעים ... ויסר מעלי רק את המות הזה — So printed: the verse Exod 10:17 belongs to the plague of locusts, not of frogs. The preacher (or the Ḥida's summary) joins the croaking frogs and the locust; translated as printed.
  • 71 וזה ט' — ט' is טעם, "the reason". Freimann refers to b. Men 86b on these windows. The point is that such a window lets one look out and not in, as a man sees the faults of others and not his own. The Ḥida refers to his own notebook.
  • ⚠72 ממ"ז — Abbreviation not resolved; from the context it means forbidden food (perhaps ממאכל זר or ממזון). Best reading: "from forbidden food".
  • ⚠73 וייחום די לה נאסון — So printed (ייחום for ייחוס): Hebrew yiḥus, "pedigree", with Spanish de la Nación: the leading families of the Portuguese "Nation" of Amsterdam.
  • 74 מסיבת ת"ה — ת"ה is תשמיש המטה, sexual intercourse.
  • ⚠75 לתת להם חינות — Literally "to give them graces": probably a calque of Spanish dar las gracias, "to give thanks"; a farewell visit.
  • ⚠76 ה' שלם — ה' read as הרב, "Rabbi"; the 1885 printing has R. Shalem.
  • 77 הג' מהר"ש — R. Saul Löwenstamm, chief rabbi of the Ashkenazim of Amsterdam (the 1885 printing has "R. Saul").
  • ⚠78 והיינו בלילה וביום — Literally "and we were by night and by day": the boat left on Monday evening and they traveled through the night; the sense is not fully clear.
  • ⚠79 ושינתה בחרוז מח' לכ' ... וקוי ה' יחליפ"ו כ"ח — In the Ashkenazi pronunciation ḥet and kaf sound alike, so she rhymed a word ending in ח with one ending in כ. She answered with Isa 40:31: yaḥalifu koaḥ, literally "shall exchange כ-ח".
  • ⚠80 משה די פינא — So printed here; elsewhere in these pages the name is פינטו, Pinto (the 1885 printing has Moses de Pinto).
  • 81 החזן ר' שלמה סרוקו — Freimann: Selomoh Saruco, son of Yshak Hasan, rabbi of the congregations Ḥonen Dal and Beit Yaakov in The Hague from 5512 to 5544 (1752–1784).
  • 82 מהר"ר שאול סגל — Freimann: R. Saul, son of R. Isaac ha-Levi, born in 5472 (1712), rabbi of the Ashkenazi congregation of The Hague from 5508 to 5545 (1748–1785). His wife was Dinah, daughter of R. Aryeh Leib, head of the court of Amsterdam; she died on 18 Av 5548 [21 August 1788].
  • 83 ר' טוביה בועז — Freimann: born in 5456 (1696), died in 5542 (1782).
  • ⚠84 ר' שמעון קיץ בית — So printed here and on 11 Sivan. The 1885 printing has ר' שמעון זימיר in this circle. זימיר is Dutch zomer, "summer", and קיץ בית is "summer house" in Hebrew, so the family name is probably Zomerhuis or Sommerhausen, put into Hebrew. A proposal.
  • 85 ר' משה לירון — Freimann: Lehren; he died on 22 Tevet 5575 [4 January 1815]. His teacher is R. Samuel Hilman, rabbi of Metz.
  • ⚠86 ר' חד"א — Probably the Ḥida's travelling companion David Ḥayyim Ariani (Signor David, דח"א on p. 154); the letters are in a different order. Not certain.
  • ⚠87 אברהם — Abraham Gabbai, the Ḥida's attendant on this journey (as in the 1885 printing), not his son.
  • ⚠88 עד שאמרתי שילך סי' דוד — Literally "until I said that Signor David should go": apparently that Signor David, not Abraham, was to come with them. Sense not fully clear.
  • ⚠89 ובבקר — "In the morning": the Torah is not read on Sunday, so this is probably the Sabbath morning service, told after the afternoon; the entry is not in strict order.
  • ⚠90 שמואל פולאק בן הרב יהושע פלאק — So printed. The Baal Shem of London is Samuel Jacob Ḥayyim Falk (about 1710–1782), son of Joshua Falk; the Ḥida writes the name as Polak. Freimann refers to Adler's study "The Baal-Shem of London".
  • ⚠91 ויוסף די לה ריינא ור' יהודה החסיד נתעסקו במעשיו — Literally "occupied themselves with his deeds": probably that Falk claimed the same practical kabbalah as Joseph della Reina and Judah the Pious; sense uncertain.
  • ⚠92 גרגירים — The OCR has נרגירים; read by the image and the sense as גרגירים, literally "grains, berries": small extracts.
  • ⚠93 ובורו מבהיק — בורו read as Spanish/French buró, bureau, "a writing desk"; uncertain.
  • 94 ר' אליעזר בן ר' בער קאן — Freimann refers to van Zuiden, De Hoogduitsche Joden in 's Gravenhage (The Hague 1923), pp. 22, 36–37.
