Sefer Ma'agal Tov II

ספר מעגל טוב II.

49.2

It is good to give thanks to the Lord [Ps 92:2] for all the kindnesses and good things, all His bounties [Ps 116:12]. He was with me in the way [Gen 35:3], on my distant roads, in how many hardships, narrow straits, and empty, barren places, so that a man's countenance changes [Dan 5:6] ⚠1. And the kindness of the Lord from of old was gracious ⚠2 in distress and brought me out to a broad place [Ps 18:20]: His signs, how great, and His wonders [Dan 3:33]. This was my lot: this thin reed ruled lines and wrote one in ten of the account of what passed over me and over my neck, yet only the stump of its roots [Dan 4:12]. Truly they said: the one for whom a miracle is done does not recognize his miracle [Niddah 31a]; and the miracles after their kind, a hundredfold [Gen 26:12, Onkelos] ⚠3. And I will fill my mouth with praises to God who answers, the light of the clouds ⚠4, great are His words. Rivers, streams of kindness upon kindness: may His name be blessed and exalted above every blessing, the blessing above His blessings. Says the servant, I, the small and least of his brothers: Ḥida, the pure Sephardi ⚠5. 1885 printing: ix, x

טוב להודות לה' על כל החסדים והטובות כל תגמולוהי. ויהי עמדי בארחי רחיקי ובכמה הרפתקי ושנוי"י דחיקי ובוקי סריקי שאין האיש מנוקה זיווהי שנוהי. וחסד ה' מעולם חניג"ה בצער ויוצא למרחב אותי אתוהי כמה רברבין ותמוהי. זאת היתה לי דק קנה זה שרטט וכתב אחד מעשרה מפרשת העבור עלי ועל צוארי ברם עקר שרשוהי. באמת אמרו אין בעל הנס מכיר ואת הנ"ס למינהו על חד מאה בדשערוהי. ופי אמלא תהלות לאל העונה נהור ענג"י סגיאן מלוהי. נהרי נחלי חסדי חסדים יהי שמו מבורך ומרומם על כל ברכה בר"ך על ברכוה"י. אמר יאמר העבד אנא זעירא זוטרא דאחוהי. חיד"א ס"ט.

49.3

5533 [1772], Ḥeshvan

תקל"ג חשון.

49.4

Ḥeshvan [Monday 26 October 1772]: On the eve of Rosh Ḥodesh Ḥeshvan, Monday of the portion Noaḥ, we set out from Hebron with the sage R. Isaac Alshekh ⚠6 and the gevir, the sage R. Y. Naḥmias, and a servant; and Abraham Gabbai was our servant. We were in distress all that day. The sheikh of Yatta and Samu ⚠7 made trouble for us and wanted a large sum as ghafar (protection money), and our soul was very weary [Num 21:5] for fear of the enemy. We settled for some zolotas, besides the toil of the way and of the camels, which was very great.

חשון. ערב ר"ח חשון יום ב' פ' נח נסענו מחברון עם החר"י אלישר והגביר חר"י נאחמיאש ומשרת ואברהם גבאי משרת לנו. והיינו בצער כל אותו היום. והתעולל בנו שיך יאטה וסמוע והיה רוצה סך גדול לגאפאר ונפשנו קצה מאד מפחד אויב ונתפשרנו בכמה זולוטאס מלבד טורח הדרך והגמלים הרבה מאד.

49.5

1 Ḥeshvan [Tuesday 27 October 1772 ⚠8]: Tuesday, Rosh Ḥodesh Ḥeshvan. We reached the caravan that Abu Dhahab, the ruler of Egypt, had sent to bring the son of Sheikh al-Ṣalīṭ ⚠9, the ruler over thousands of Arabs, that he should come with a thousand camels and Arabs; for he wished to make war on Ali Bey, who was encamped near Jaffa, since so the Sultan had decreed. These things came about because the Sultan had sent him an official with a sword and a gold chain, because he had driven Ali Bey out of Egypt, and he had commanded him to pursue him and kill him. In the presence of the official he did this, sending to bring these Arabs, but his heart was not right with him [Ps 78:37]: he was false to him. But this was a miracle and a wonder for us, for the roads were in disorder, and in so great a caravan, with the sheikh, the son of al-Ṣalīṭ, we were able to come by way of the Sultan's desert road; and it was a miracle.

1. יום ג' ר"ח חשון הגענו השיירא שאבו דהב מושל מצרים שלח להביא בן שיך היצליט המושל באלפי ערביים שיבא עם אלף גמלים וערביים שרצונו לערוך מלחמה עם עאלי ביי החונה אצל יפו כי כן גזר המלך. והיו הדברים האלה יען המלך שלח לו שר א' עם חרב ורביד זהב על אשר גרש עאלי ביי ממצרים והוא צוה p. 50 לרודפו ולהמיתו. ובפני השר עשה זה לשלוח להביא הערביים האלה ולבו לא נכון עמו כחש לו. אבל זה היה נס ופלא לנו כי הדרכים היו משובשים ובשיירא גדולה כזו עם השיך בן הצליט יכולנו לבא דרך המדבר סולטאני ויהי לנס.

50.1

But great fear fell upon us, because the communal funds of the holy city Hebron owed Sheikh al-Ṣalīṭ five thousand zolotas at interest. He was captured by the pasha of Damascus and gave Hebron's debt to his creditors ⚠10, and the pasha sent to collect it. They were in great distress and were forced to borrow at enormous interest and pay; but the bond remained in his hand. Although the matter was well known to the Arabs, it was not unlikely that they would arrest us and torture us until we paid, or until others paid for us; and all this distress was in our hearts like a burning fire [Jer 20:9]. Praises to God, blessed be He, who saved us from his hand. For the sheikh mentioned above, Bakir son of al-Ṣalīṭ, met the sage R. Isaac Alshekh and said to him: "You are from Hebron; how is Aṣlan ⚠11? I have a claim against him," and "days should speak" [Job 32:7]. The said sage answered him: "Aṣlan is a servant to his master, and what a servant acquires, his master acquires" [Pesaḥim 88b].

אבל חרדה גדולה נפלה עלינו בהיות כי השיך היסליט היו חייבים לו כוללות עה"ק חברון חמשה אלפים ז' ברבית ונלכד ביד הפאשה מדמשק ונתן לגירמ"י חוב חברון ושלח הפאשה לגבותו ונמצאו בצרה גדולה והוכרחו לקחת ברבית עצום ולפרוע והשט"ח נשאר בידו ואף שהדבר מפורסם לערביים לא היה רחוק לאסרנו ולענותנו עד אשר נפרע או יפרעו בעדינו וכל זה הצער היה בלבנו כאש בוערת. ותהלות לאל ית' אשר הצילנו מידו כי השיך הנז' באכיר בן הצליט פגע בחר"י אלישר וא"ל אתה מחברון איך ש' הצלאן אני יש לי תביעה עמו וימים ידברו והשיבו החר"י הנז' אצלאן הוא עבד לאדונו ומה שקנה עבד קנה רבו.

50.2

3 Ḥeshvan [Friday 30 October 1772]: On the sixth day ⚠12 we were near El-Arish, and we were very distressed about the Sabbath; in the end, by means of money ⚠13, we went about half a mile and kept the Sabbath in a valley with two Arabs. On Friday night we ate with a glad heart, and on the Sabbath day we dined a little well. At the time of the afternoon offering a sheikh came and made much trouble for us, demanding a large sum for the ghafar of El-Arish, and other pretexts. We gave him raisins and told him that it was the Sabbath day and we could not take money. He grew angry, and rode off on his horse to bring some Arabs who were nearby; and we, the hand of the poor ⚠14, were shaking and trembling and falling at his feet that he should wait until nightfall, while he heaped up threats, so that we had great distress. In our distress we prayed the afternoon prayer, ate the third meal, and took great pains until sunset. About a quarter of an hour later we prayed the evening prayer and made Havdalah, and we gave him some sum, as I recall six or seven sequins, and mounted the camels to reach the caravan, which had set out on the holy Sabbath day.

3. יום הו' היינו אצל העאריש והיינו מצטערים מאד על השבת וסוף דבר ע"י ממון הלכנו כחצי מיל ושבתנו בעמק א' עם ב' ערביים וליל שבת אכלנו בשמחת הלב ויום שבת סעדנו קצת בטוב. ובהעלות המנחה בא שיך אחד והתעולל בנו מאד והיה שואל ממון גדול בעד גאפאר עאריש ושאר עלילות. ונתנו לו צמוקים וא"ל שהוא יום שבת ולא נוכל ליקח מעות וחרה אפו ורכב על סוסו להביא כמה ערביים שהיו סמוכים ואנן יד עניי רועשים ורועדים ומתנפלים לרגליו ימתין עד הלילה והוא מרבה באיומים באופן שהי"ל צער גדול ובצערנו התפללנו מנחה וסעדנו סעודה ג' וטרחנו הרבה עד שקיעת החמה ואח"כ כרביע שעה התפללנו ערבית והבדלנו ונתנו לו סך מה כמדומה כששה שבעה סיקינוס ורכבנו על הגמלים להגיע לשיירה שנסעה יום ש"ק.

50.3

5 Ḥeshvan [the evening of Saturday 31 October 1772]: Sunday of the week of Lekh Lekha. On the night after the Sabbath mentioned above we had great trouble, for the world grew dark around us; there was gloom in the world, great fear, and rain. And I in particular had in my heart distress, grief, fear and trembling beyond measure, and I made several vows from the great fear. We went eight hours in the night and reached the great caravan as they were beginning to move off; we had no strength to go on, and we camped there from the great distress and toil. Afterwards we took heart to travel. As we set out we saw opposite us a small caravan coming from Egypt and going to Gaza. We travelled on and went about an hour, and could go no further; we camped and fell down there, and slept until the sun came up. We rose in haste and went all day, and on Monday night, four hours into it, we came to Qaṭya and found the caravan, and camped there. There we learned that on Sunday night, after we had travelled on from the caravan coming from Egypt, enemies fell upon them with seventy-two riding camels ⚠15, killed some of them and plundered them. The Arabs of that small caravan were near to our Arabs, so that had we not travelled on, God forbid, by the way of nature their wrath would have fallen on us too. I will give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. Blessed is He who bestows good things on the undeserving, who has bestowed every good on us.

5. א' לך לך. ליל מש"ק הנז' הי"ל צרה גדולה כי העולם חשך בעדינו והיה אופל בעולם ופחד גדול וגשמים ואני בפרט היה בלבי צרה ויגון ופחד ורעדה אשר לא ישוער והייתי נודר כמה נדרים מרוב הפחד. והלכנו ח' שעות בלילה והגענו לשיירא הגדולה שהיו מתחילים ליסע ולא היה בנו כח ללכת וחנינו שם מרוב הצער והטורח ואח"כ נשאנו לבנו ליסע. ובעת נסיעתנו ראינו שיירא מנגד קטנה הבאה ממצרים והולכת לעזה. ואנחנו נסענו והלכנו כשעה אחת ולא יכולנו ללכת עוד וחנינו ונפלנו שם ונרדמנו עד שיצא השמש וקמנו בבהלה והלכנו כל היום ובליל ב' ד' שעות שבאנו לקאתיי"א ומצאנו השיירא וחנינו שם וידענו שם שבליל א' אחר שנסענו מאצל השיירא הבאה ממצרים נפלו עליהם שונאים בע"ב גמלי פרחי והרגו מהם ושללום והערבים של אותה השיירא קטנה היו קרובים לערביים שלנו שאם לא היינו נוסעים ח"ו דרך טבע גם עלינו היה זעמם. אודה לה' כי טוב כל"ח ברוך הגומל לחיבים טובות שגמלנו כל טוב.

51.1

Halfway along the road a kashif ⚠16 came with twenty servants riding swift horses [Esth 8:10]; he was fleeing from Ali Bey and going over to Abu Dhahab. At night the gevir, the sage R. Y. Naḥmias, went and paid him his respects, and told him that we are under the shadow of his roof [Gen 19:8], and the like, in the Turkish language. The kashif mentioned above was a good man and a great lord, and he spoke kind words to him.

ובאמצע הדרך בא כאסיף אחד עם עשרים משרתים רוכבי הרכש שהיה בורח מעאלי ביי ובא לאבו דהב ובלילה הלך הגביר חר"י נאחמיאש ועשה לו כבוד וא"ל שאנחנו בצל קורתו וכיוצא בלשון טורקי והכאסיף הנז' היה טוב ושר גדול ודיבר לו דברים טובים.

51.2

9 [Ḥeshvan], Thursday [5 November 1772]: a spirit came upon the kashif and his men, and they went to Bilbeis; and these ⚠17 Arabs went another way, which is the way to go to Egypt, but we heard that they wanted to stop in the desert, and we were in great distress. The sage R. Y. Alishar wanted to go with the caravan, while I and the sage R. Y. Naḥmias wanted to go with the kashif. Because R. Y. A. was free in giving the Arabs our biscuits and fruit, they listened to his words, and he told the Arabs to lead the camels to go with the caravan. I became angry; we got down from the camels, rebuked the Arab who was leading us, and began to walk on foot to Bilbeis. R. Y. A. rebuked us sharply, as if we were common folk ⚠18, and we kept silent and went on foot; in the end the Arab came with the camels to take us there. I spoke much reproof to R. Y. A., and he said to me: granted that in Torah you are great, in the affairs of the world you know nothing … ⚠19

9. יום ה'. רוח לבשה לכאסיף ואנשיו והלכו לבילבים והערביים האלף הלכו דרך אחרת שהוא הדרך ללכת למצרים אך שמענו שהיו רוצים לשבות במדבר. והיינו בצער גדול. והחר"י אלישר היה רוצה ללכת עם השיירא. ואני וחר"י נאחמיאש רוצים ללכת עם הכאסיף והחרי"א יען היה ותרן לתת לערבים בוקסמאט ופירות שלנו לכך היו שומעים לדבריו והיה או' לערבים להנהיג הגמלים ללכת עם השיירא וחרה אפי וירדנו מהגמלים וגערנו בערבי המנהיגנו והתחלנו ללכת ברגלינו לבילבים. והחרי"א נזף בנו נזיפה כהדיוטות ושתקנו והלכנו ברגל וסוף דבר בא הערבי עם הגמלים להוליכנו שם. ודברתי להחרי"א טובא תוכחתי וא"ל נהי דבתורה אתה גדול בעניני העולם אינך מכיר כלום...

51.3

I kept Passover [April 1773] in joy with Signor Samuel mentioned above, and I ate kazira ⚠20, and I had honor with all the friends.

ועשיתי פסח בשמחה אצל מ' שמואל הנז' ואכלתי קצירה והי"ל כבוד עם כל האהובים.

51.4

Iyyar [April–May 1773]: all the scholars mentioned above, and more besides, except the sage R. A. D. N. ⚠21, set out on their way, relying on a miracle; but I was afraid to travel because of the Muscovite [the Russian fleet], which lay between Alexandria (Na Amon) and Livorno.

אייר. כל הת"ח הנז' ויותר מהמה חוץ מח"ר עד"ן נסעו לדרכם עפ"י הנס ואני נתפחדתי ליסע מהמוסקוף השרוי בין נא אמון לליוורנו.

51.5

14 [Iyyar; Friday 7 May 1773] ⚠22: That son of a smith ⚠23, Ali Bey, for evil a cloud over Jacob ⚠24, who did much to destroy, who cut down the tree [Dan 4:11] to make an end of my people in Egypt, to take the spoil and to plunder [Esth 3:13] by hundreds and by thousands; an adversary and an enemy [Esth 7:6], who killed the gevir, the official Joseph ha-Levi Sakandari, under the rod, plundered all his money and sold his lands and his goods, so that all his household were left naked and bare [Ezek 16:7]. He it was who despoiled the gevirim Signor Nissim, the sage R. Y. Provinzal and Signor Ḥayyim Sasson, sold all they had and set them down as an empty vessel [Jer 51:34]. He it was who multiplied his wounds [Job 9:17] on all the gevirim of Egypt, emptying them of silver and gold; even clothing, precious as cushions and bolsters, was sold in the market ⚠25, with word-plays on bundles, garments and coins ⚠26. He it was who destroyed all peoples and mighty men to seize their money; many were his slain, and all who held office in Egypt, many as they were, he wiped out, killed or banished, and set his slaves in their place. He it was who rebelled against the Turkish king, made war on the Sharif in Mecca and conquered them, and sent eighty thousand with Abu Dhahab to Gaza and Damascus and conquered them. All the precious and pleasant wealth [Prov 24:4] of the whole world around him he licked up as the ox licks up [Num 22:4]; he stole, robbed and murdered, and all his mind was to reign. And he lost all the money in wars and in gathering troops and weapons. But a voice fell from heaven: the kingdom has departed from you [Dan 4:28]. He sought to kill Abu Dhahab, who fled to Upper Egypt (the Sa'id), gathered an army and came against him to fight him in the land of Egypt. All of them trembled and quaked before Abu Dhahab, for he is a man of war and successful, and the whole land of Egypt turned after Abu Dhahab. Ali Bey, may his bones be ground, was forced to flee from Egypt. He set out with the army that was with him, and before him thirty-two camels laden with gold and pearls; and Abu Dhahab rose up in his place ⚠27 and ruled with great dominion. The son of the murderess, Ali Bey, the black cloud, fled bald-headed, fled and escaped, for [his fear was] strong as death ⚠28. From there he went to the shores of Acre: the starling went to the raven [Ḥullin 65a], to the rebel Sheikh Ẓahir ⚠29, who is among the thorns a pricking thorn and a malignant brier [Ezek 28:24] ⚠30. They took counsel together to rebel against the Turkish king, made war on Sidon and took it, and besieged Jaffa for some eight months. The roads were stopped: Aram from the east, the Muscovite at sea with flaming fire, and the Philistines behind [Isa 9:11], Ẓahir and Ali Bey. The commanders of the Muscovite conferred with the rebels mentioned above and they became one, to destroy the Turkish kingdom, and they sent letters to the Empress of Muscovy. The force of the wars made desolations in the earth [Ps 46:9], and there was a famine in the land [Gen 12:10]; and the terrors in Zion, the city of our solemnities [Isa 33:20]: many were swollen with hunger, suffering at its beginning and at its end, and many of Israel died. Some fled for their lives and died on the way; it was a famine of destruction, and it reached Hebron. The distress rose ⚠31, for the money was gone to keep alive even with barley bread, and it broke the teeth with gravel [Lam 3:16], with its mixture of plain dust and ash of the stove, and flour was not the main part. So the measure called suissa ⚠32, which in normal years was worth four paras, rose to a hundred paras. At the end of the summer everything was lost; there was no bread, herbs were sold at great price, and gentiles were found dead in the fields. The gentiles ate vermin and creeping things and cats, and whoever had the luck to catch a cat ate it. For our many sins this caused many of Israel who were already sick to pass away afterwards to the World to Come, far be it from those who hear ⚠33. Meanwhile the rebel Ali Bey, his iniquities shall ensnare him [Prov 5:22], prepared himself chariots and horsemen [1 Kgs 1:5] and set out to return to Egypt, to contend ⚠34 and fight against Abu Dhahab. The report reached Abu Dhahab, and he and all the beys and all the officers in Egypt knew that if Ali Bey returned, there is one law for them, to put them to death [Esth 4:11]. They set the battle in array. Abu Dhahab went out and brought forth the weapons of his indignation [Jer 50:25], as on the day of battle. Murad Bey went up first and met Ali Bey; the bear Murad met him [for the] kingdom and hastened to fulfil the commandment of pinching off the head, devouring and breaking in pieces [Dan 7:7]; they take the pestle (eli) ⚠35 like one of the base fellows [2 Sam 6:20]. His fellow Ismail Bey came, girded his sword like Ishmael and saved Ali Bey, for all of them were his slaves, slaves who break away [1 Sam 25:10]. The horsemen overtook him, as their custom is, with swords, and struck the wicked Ali Bey on the flesh of his arm; never before had there been such a fresh wound, a wound of the sword. They brought him before Abu Dhahab, and the wicked man spoke supplications [Prov 18:23]; and Abu Dhahab, since he was his brother-in-law and his slave, had mercy on him. He came to the land of Egypt and lay barely a day or two on his couch in Damascus [Amos 3:12] ⚠37, and the land of Egypt languished [Gen 47:13]: everyone able to gird a belt was stirred up; all of them answered and said, to the slaughter he is led [Jer 11:19]. So they called a skilled physician there; poison came to hand; and the physician who passes by his house, Samael, put a bandage on him to bind it [Ezek 30:21], and saddled his ass [Gen 22:3], since he is Samael ⚠38. On the night of the fourteenth of Iyyar the spirit of perverseness [Isa 19:14], the soul that touched … ⚠39, the leprosy broke out: as the cloud is consumed and vanishes [Job 7:9], may his bones be ground. His dog's head was cut off in secret and severed, according to law and rule, and sent to the place whose name is Kushta [Constantinople] ⚠40; when it came before the king and the officials, these rejoiced with joy and singing, as with the joy of harvest [Isa 9:2]. Lo, these are but the edges [Job 26:14], one in ten thousand, of the portion of plagues, the portion of the leper, after the death [Lev 13–16]. So let all Your enemies perish, O Lord [Judg 5:31].

53.2

After this the gevir Signor Samuel was very ill, and all his body trembled [Dan 5:6]. I prayed much for him, and made redemption offerings for him from my own money, besides what they did from his money. I spent many days of distress in this, until the Holy One, blessed be He, had mercy on us: He sent His word and healed him [Ps 107:20]. Meanwhile I was in distress and did not know where to turn. If I stayed, I no longer had strength to bear it, and I was already ashamed to stay several months with the gevir mentioned above. And if I thought of going to Livorno, I was afraid of the danger of capture, for the Muscovite had built and compassed with gall [Lam 3:5], and the sea was full of Muscovite corsairs and also wicked Greeks; my heart melted within me and became as water [Josh 7:5], and this constant distress day and night was beyond measure, until I heard that on the side of Tunis there were no Muscovites, and there was a ship going to Tunis and from there to Livorno. I decided to travel in it and stay aboard a day or two, until they unloaded the merchandise at Tunis, and then go on to Livorno. I took steps to obtain a certificate from the Imperial consul that I am a resident of Livorno: two Jews went and testified that my daughter is married in Livorno, and it came into my hands. But all this is worth nothing, for my dress and my language are themselves witnesses that I am a Levantine; still, it may help.

53.3

22 Tammuz, Tuesday of [the week of] Mattot [13 July 1773]: I set out in the ship mentioned above from Alexandria (Na Amon). I had no money, because I had spent on provisions, fruit, preserved fish ⚠42, water, all kinds of utensils, wine and beer, and the fare of the ship, over a hundred piastres from Alexandria. I said: how shall I go to the lazaretto of Livorno without money? Although my daughter, Signora Sarah, and my son-in-law are in Livorno, and my son-in-law, may his Rock keep him, is a gevir, I did not want to come empty-handed, lest, God forbid, they look away and I have to ask, and one cannot rely on such things. So I borrowed ten reales from the gevir Signor Jesse, and set out in peace. But the captain was a very wicked man, a Frenchman, proud and foolish. He did [great] wrongs to me: he ordered that they give me no water, and that when I wanted [anything] I should ask him. On Friday, when we were making two dishes for the Sabbath, he begrudged it, saying that I am no prince, to eat so much and to waste water as in town. And I had brought two barrels of water, and the sailors finished them in two weeks. I made him a gift of fine tobacco and several flasks of syrup, and it was worth nothing to him; he was in his wickedness from beginning to end. All the week I did not eat the measure [of bread] that requires Grace after Meals, because of the water for washing the hands, and I had much distress over this at the meals of the holy Sabbath. The wicked captain had a personal hatred for me, so that he did not want the sailors to speak with me. I gave them money and fruit so that they would talk a little, for I was weary of many troubles; in secret they talked a little, and said that they were afraid of the master. I was in great distress from several sides. First, I had no one to talk with. Second, the place where I was was dark by day and very narrow by night. Third, the constant fear of the Muscovite: at every ship seen from afar I was in distress until I knew that it was not Muscovite. Fourth, the storms of the sea: the weather was always right against us, and the ship went many miles against its will on a crooked course; it was carried towards Candia [Crete], where the Muscovite is, and the wicked captain did not want to go by the Barbary route, because the wind was against it. Fifth, the ship had a leak: all day and all night, or most of it, the sailors bailed out water; they grew tired of going down to caulk, and did not find the leak; the sailors rowed hard [Jonah 1:13] and could not. We had to pass a place called Cao Dukra ⚠43, and it was impossible to pass because of the wind, so that we were in great distress, tossed and confused, in fear and sorrow, and with no water. Almost every Sabbath there was a storm, waves of distress mounting up to the heavens [Ps 107:26]; on the Sunday the sea was calm, but even so the wind was against us, so that we were in great trouble; and on top of our pains, the wickedness of the captain. In all my distress I studied as time allowed: I read the whole of Parashat Derakhim ⚠44, and wrote some novellae, plain interpretations and topics, and sermons from the portion Vayikra to the portion Emor. We passed the Ninth of Av [Thursday 29 July 1773] very hard, in a storm at sea, and the fast was a very great trouble for me. On 22 Av [Wednesday 11 August 1773] we passed that wicked Cao Dukra. This is a little of what I remember of the many sorrows we had on this ship, from several sides, and great dangers.

6 Elul, Wednesday of [the week of] Ki Tetze [25 August 1773]: we reached the city of Sousse. They did not let us go ashore, for the custom is to send a runner to Tunis and obtain leave from the king [the Bey]. When we reached the harbor I gave thanks to God for what He had done for me, and for saving me from all evil, blessed be His name for ever, who bestows good things on the undeserving. I sent a note to the holy congregation asking them to send me some food, for I was faint; and for the Sabbath they sent fowls, bread, fruit and charcoal.

אלול 6. יום ד' תצא. הגענו לעיר סוסא. ולא הנחנו צאת כי המנהג לשלוח רץ לתונים וליקח רשות מהמלך. וכשהגענו לנמל נתתי הודאות לאל על אשר גמלני והצילני מכל רע יתברך שמו לעד הגומל לחייבים טובות. ושלחתי בילייטי לק"ק שישלחו לי איזה מאכל כי עיף אנכי. ולשבת שלחו עופות ולחם ופירות ופחמין.

54.2

10 [Elul], Sunday of [the week of] Ki Tavo [29 August 1773]: leave came from Tunis and we went ashore in peace. There I saw their sage, R. Solomon Uzan, who holds himself to be the rabbi. The sage R. Judah Franco of Tunis was there too. The sage wanted me to stay in his house; he brought me to his house and gave me a small dark room, so that even for my small self it was narrow and there was no space at all. His house is small and cramped, musty, dirty and in disorder, and he has young children and all kinds of utensils: their going out and coming in, their sitting down and rising up, I am their song [Lam 3:63]. They told me that there is a gevir named R. Moses Uzan, and that if he invited me to stay in his house I should not agree. I then said: if he calls me, I shall answer him with a just "yes" ⚠45, since I do not want to trouble your honor; and he answered me with words. I got up and went to R. Moses. He received me with honor, and his son said to me in a whisper, so that the sage would not hear, that if I wished I could stay there. I answered aloud that this is certainly my wish, so as not to trouble the sage; I stayed there, and the sage was offended with him and with me. There I began to "enjoy" the dishes of the north-west wind ⚠46: the overhanging remainder hangs over [Exod 26:12], without cleaning of spoon and shovel, whether one sees it in a dream ⚠47; and even the king stretched out his foot [cf. Esth 5:2] to eat, and every one heaps a full handful of fat meat, "dirt" is its name, and they have spices: "out of the north the evil shall break forth" [Jer 1:14] blows on them ⚠48. Bowls, cups and jugs on the table, and one broad and round ⚠49 bowl full of couscous, six or seven pounds for each person, and several whole onions, half cooked, on the rim of the bowl. In truth I loathed to eat. For my honor they went round the town to find a spoon. Still, it is in abundance and great plenty. The gevir and his son were troubled about R. Solomon, who was offended. I said to them: do not fear and do not be afraid [Deut 1:29]. I sent to call him, spoke kindly to him with great honor, seated him above me and gave him every honor. I debated a little with R. Judah Franco and saw that he is a scholar. But R. Solomon thinks himself a rabbi and is wise in his own eyes, and enough of that.

10. א' תבא. בא רשות מתונים ויצאנו לש'. ושם ראיתי החכם שלהם ר' שלמה ואזן מחזיק עצמו לרב. ושם היה הח"ר יאודה פראנקו מתונים והחכם היה רוצה שאשב בביתו והביאני בביתו והזמין לי חדר קטן חשוך שאני בקטני היה צר לי ולא היה מקום כלל וביתו קטנה וצרה מעופשת ומטונפת ומשובשת ויש לו ילדים רכים וכלים מכלים שונים. יציאתם וביאתם שבתם וקימתם אני מנגינתם. וא"ל שיש גביר אחד שמו ר' משה ואזן ושאם אומר לי שאשב בביתו שלא אודה לו אז אמרתי אם הוא יקראני אני אענהו הין צדק שאיני רוצה להטריח למעכ"ת והשיבני דברים הדברים. וקמתי והלכתי אצל ר' משה ועשה לי כבוד וא"ל בנו בלחש שלא ישמע החכם שאם ארצה אשב שם ועניתי בקול שודאי זהו רצוני שלא להטריח לחכם ונשארתי שם והחכם הקפיד עליו ועלי. ושם התחלתי להתבסם מתבשילי הרוח מערבית צפונית יהיה סרוח סרח העודף בלי נקיון כף ומגריפה אם יראה בחלום ואפי' p. 55 המלך פשט לו את הרגל לאכול וכל המרבה מלא קומצו בשר שמן לכלוכית שמה ותבלין יש להן מצפון תפתח יפיח בהם. וקערות וכוסות וקיתונות בשלחן וקערת קצף אחת ורחבה ונסבה מלאה כוסכו"סו לכל אחד ששה ושבעה ליטרין וכמה בצלים חצי בישול שלמים על פי הקערה. באמת הייתי קץ לאכול. ולכבודי סובבים בעיר למצוא כף אחת ומ"מ הוא בשפע ושבע גדול. והיו בצער הגביר ובנו על ר' שלמה שהקפיד א"ל אל תיראו ואל תערצו ושלחתי לקרותו ודברתי על לבו בכבוד גדול והושבתיו עלי ונתתי לו כל כבוד. ופלפלתי מעט עם ר' יהודה פראנקו וראיתי שהוא ת"ח. אבל ר' שלמה נראה בעיניו שהוא רב וחכם בעיניו ודי בזה.

55.2

19 [Elul], Wednesday ⚠51 [Tuesday 7 September 1773]: we came in peace to Tunis. There is a large open ground before the city, and the gentiles are wicked; they mocked us, and when the servant Abraham raised his voice a little they beat him badly. By a miracle a Jew happened by and took us to the place where all the emissaries go, called Dar al-Yafriḥa ⚠52. There was the sage Mordecai Levi of Jerusalem, son of the sage Aaron, may his soul rest in Eden, my friend. He knew me from Jerusalem, but I had left him a boy. Some years ago he went to Izmir, and they sent him as an emissary for the communities of Izmir that had burned; he had been there [in Tunis] some months and had a great name as a learned man with a good memory; he knew their language and found favor. When I came in, the sage R. Mordecai honored me and fell down to kiss my hand. I had not yet prayed, and I began to say the prayer. The sages came, R. Joseph Kohen, R. Nathan Borgel, R. Joseph Zarqa and his son-in-law R. Joshua, and waited until I finished my prayer, and I began to speak words of Torah. Meanwhile the gevir R. Moses, son of the qaid, the sage, the perfect R. Joshua Kohen Tanugi ⚠53, came and invited me in his father's name to his house. We went there; it is outside the ghetto, and he has a royal court of alabaster and marble stones. They gave me a fine room. Most of the people, great sages, came and said that they had thought of sending me a carriage, only [its animals] are mules; I brought them the words of the Yerushalmi on the mule and the words of the Tosafot Yom Tov ⚠54, and the difficulties I had in this matter. So I was busy with them in words of Torah, with what came to hand. The sage R. Mordecai was with me day and night for seven days in the house of the qaid. At midday the qaid came and showed me a bright face; we dined with him, and at table I said a homily of mine on the verse "And Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine, and he was priest" [Gen 14:18], following a responsum of the Ḥavvot Ya'ir, and it pleased him. In truth he did wonders, he and his son R. Moses, his son-in-law R. Judah Nataf and his daughter Signora Gazala; all of them showed very great kindness, with honor and gifts and very much money. The gevir the qaid wanted me not to leave his house even for prayer, except from Sabbath to Sabbath: on weekdays I would pray in a room in his courtyard, through the window that looks onto a synagogue where he used to pray, and on the Sabbath by day I would go with him to the congregation where he prayed. I had very great honor, so that several pregnant women came to the gevir's house wanting to see me; they stood at the windows and watched me from afar without my noticing. Once I was walking on the Sabbath in the courtyard there with the gevir, and I felt that the women were behind me, taking the hem of my garment from behind, quietly and lightly, to kiss it. The women never sat at table at all. But in the matter of food they kept their custom: all the tables full of dishes, their smell spreading, the overhanging remainder ⚠55; they eat with their hands and feet ⚠56, their palms full of fat. The gevir would lift a piece of fat, and while it was yet in his hand he would swallow it [Isa 28:4], and wipe his hand on the napkin on his knees, and the napkin became like a slaughterhouse. Men and women also go barefoot, with big feet. Every Sabbath after the afternoon prayer the court, the wardens, the notables of the city and rabbis come to the gevir the qaid; he sits at the head and takes his foot in his hand before them all, puts tobacco smoke in his mouth, and has a basin (tassa) black as a raven before him, into which he spits in front of them. I sat beside him and was distressed by this. At table too, when they set it, this comes up first: a loathsome vessel full of spittle. In some matters too "God led them not by the way of the land" [Exod 13:17], that is, of manners (derekh eretz). I used to tell them: manners are a coin not current in your land, and I am obliged not to use a thing that is not current among you; but what shall I do, for I am going to Livorno, to a people of strange language [Ps 114:1] who are all the tractate Derekh Eretz and politeness finer than fine. For in the little time I have been before you ⚠57, we were forsaken and forgotten [Isa 49:14], to live with you. But in truth, the honor of the Torah ….. ⚠58

Notes and uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 שאין האיש מנוקה זיווהי שנוהי — Read as: there is no man who comes out of them clear, and his countenance is changed (Dan 5:6, זיוהי שנוהי). Uncertain.
  • ⚠2 חניג"ה — Marked word, probably a play on a name or a word-count; read as חנינה, "was gracious". The print has ג, not נ.
  • ⚠3 על חד מאה בדשערוהי — Onkelos to Gen 26:12, "a hundredfold of what he had reckoned": the miracles were a hundred times more than he could record.
  • ⚠4 נהור ענג"י — So printed (marked word); perhaps read עננ"י, "light of the clouds", or a play on a word or number. Uncertain.
  • ⚠5 חיד"א ס"ט — ס"ט = ספרדי טהור, "pure Sephardi", the Ḥida's usual signature.
  • ⚠6 החר"י אלישר — R. Isaac Alshekh (Eliashar), whom Freimann identifies as the son of R. Ḥayyim Yeruḥam of Vilna, who came with his father to Hebron.
  • ⚠7 שיך יאטה וסמוע — Yatta (biblical Juttah, Josh 21:16) and Samu (Eshtemoa), villages south of Hebron, following Freimann's footnote.
  • ⚠8 1. יום ג' ר"ח חשון — Tuesday 27 October 1772 was 30 Tishrei 5533, the first day of Rosh Ḥodesh Ḥeshvan; 1 Ḥeshvan was Wednesday.
  • ⚠9 בן שיך היצליט — Spelled היצליט, הצליט and היסליט on these pages; a Bedouin chief, given as al-Ṣalīṭ. Not identified further.
  • ⚠10 ונתן לגירמ"י חוב חברון — גירמ"י read as Arabic gharīm, "creditor" (Freimann's note gives the Arabic word, explained as "payment"); he made over Hebron's debt to them. Uncertain.
  • ⚠11 איך ש' הצלאן — Aṣlan (also אצלאן): not identified; from the answer, a person of the Hebron community, perhaps its agent or treasurer, who had borrowed for it. ש' = שלום, "how is ...".
  • ⚠12 3. יום הו' — הו' = הששי, the sixth day, Friday: 3 Ḥeshvan 5533 was Friday 30 October 1772, and the worry about the Sabbath fits. The entries 5 and 9 also fall on their days of Ḥeshvan (Sunday 1 November, Thursday 5 November).
  • ⚠13 על השבת וסוף דבר ע"י ממון — In the NLI copy the end of the line is covered and reads only הש. ממון; the HebrewBooks copy of Freimann's edition (hebrewbooks.org 21838) has על השבת וסוף דבר ע"י ממון. The caravan went on over the Sabbath, so they paid to stop.
  • ⚠14 ואנן יד עניי — Aramaic-Hebrew, "and we, the hand of the poor": read as "we poor ones". Uncertain.
  • ⚠15 בע"ב גמלי פרחי — ע"ב = 72; גמלי פרחי read as fast riding camels (literally "flying camels"; cf. Arabic ḥajjān). Uncertain.
  • ⚠16 כאסיף — Arabic kāshif, a Mamluk governor of a district of Egypt (so Freimann).
  • ⚠17 והערביים האלף — So printed; read האלו, "these", as the sense requires.
  • ⚠18 נזף בנו נזיפה כהדיוטות — In the NLI copy a strip stuck on the page hides this line; the HebrewBooks copy of Freimann's edition (hebrewbooks.org 21838) reads נזף בנו נזיפה כהדיוטות.
  • ⚠19 אינך מכיר כלום... — The text breaks off here: two folios of the autograph are missing (Freimann). It resumes at Passover 1773 in Alexandria.
  • ⚠20 ואכלתי קצירה — Unclear word, read so in the image; perhaps the name of a dish. The house of Samuel is in Alexandria (Na Amon), as the next entries show.
  • ⚠21 ח"ר עד"ן — Name abbreviation, not resolved; left as initials.
  • ⚠22 14. — The printed date has no month or weekday. It stands under Iyyar 5533 and the entry speaks of the night of 14 Iyyar; 14 Iyyar 5533 = Friday 7 May 1773. Ali Bey died in Cairo in May 1773.
  • ⚠23 ההוא בר פחין — Aramaic, "that son of a smith" (Sanhedrin 96a, of Nebuchadnezzar); a term of contempt.
  • ⚠24 לרעו"ת ביעקב ע"ב הענן — Word-play on ע"ב = Ali Bey and עב, "cloud"; the rendering is free.
  • ⚠25 כיקר כרי"ם כסתות נמכרים באטלז — Read so; באטלז taken as the market. The rhetoric of this passage is dense; the rendering gives the sense.
  • ⚠26 עבידי דאתו צרורו"ת בשמלותם צררי דפשיטי לתת לאיש צורר זורה ומפרר טורה וסורר זה בורר — A chain of rhymes and Aramaic plays (bundles of coins, Exod 12:34) that cannot be rendered word for word; summarised.
  • ⚠27 ויקם על סלע תחת הנחשת אבו דהב — So printed; a play on Abu Dhahab, "father of gold", and "instead of brass" (Isa 60:17). Sense: Abu Dhahab took his place.
  • ⚠28 ובן הנרצחה ע"ב שחורה ברח"א קרחא ויברח וימלט כי עז"ה כמות — Obscure plays (בן הנרצחה; ע"ב = Ali Bey and "cloud"; עזה כמות, Song 8:6). Best reading given.
  • ⚠29 שיך דאיי"ר — Ẓāhir al-ʿUmar, the rebel ruler of Galilee and Acre, Ali Bey's ally.
  • ⚠30 כמדיי"ר בי דיירא — Play on the name Ẓahir (דאייר) and Aramaic דייר, "dweller"; not translated.
  • ⚠31 עלה הצער הבצר עודנו — Play on Hebron and בצר; sense: the distress grew severe.
  • ⚠32 סוסייא — A local dry measure; not identified.
  • ⚠33 חל"ש — Read as חוץ לשומעים, "far be it from those who hear".
  • ⚠34 להלוק — So printed; perhaps for לחלוק, "to contend".
  • ⚠35 הן נוטלין את העלי — A Mishnah phrase: "one may take the pestle (עלי)"; a play on the name Ali.
  • ⚠36 עשו לו לימודים נשאו ראש ולענה דרך הנס"ר ומעלה הלוחי"ת — Obscure; לימודים perhaps for a litter (cf. מטה). הנס"ר not identified. Rendering uncertain.
  • ⚠37 שכב בדמשק ערש — Amos 3:12, used for lying on a bed; Ali Bey was brought to Cairo, not Damascus.
  • ⚠38 חליף ס"ם שם לו חתול לחובשה ויחבוש את חמורו באשר הוא ס"ם — Plays on סם (poison) and ס"ם (Samael), and on חתול (bandage, Ezek 30:21, also "cat"): the physician poisoned the wound.
  • ⚠39 הנפש הנוגעת פרח"ה הצרעת — Plays on Lev 13 (the leprosy portions read in this season); sense: he died.
  • ⚠40 חלוטה וחתורה — Read so in the print; sense: cut off.
  • ⚠41 ציר וצוחה תוציא שמח"ה — Rhymed phrase; שמח"ה with marks, perhaps a play; rendered by sense.
  • ⚠42 מולייתא — Aramaic "stuffed (food)"; taken as preserved or stuffed fish.
  • ⚠43 קאו דוק"רא — A cape (Ital. capo) on the way from Egypt westward; not identified.
  • ⚠44 פרשת דרכים — Taken as the book Parashat Derakhim by Judah Rosanes (homilies), not a weekly portion.
  • ⚠45 אני אענהו הין צדק — Play on Lev 19:36 ("a just hin", read as "a just yes"); sense: I will accept.
  • ⚠46 להתבסם מתבשילי הרוח מערבית צפונית — Ironic: the food of the Maghreb (north-west). The following phrases are a chain of mocking allusions; rendered by sense.
  • ⚠47 אם יראה בחלום — Unclear allusion (perhaps to the Talmud on dreams); not resolved.
  • ⚠48 מצפון תפתח יפיח בהם — Play on Jer 1:14 and on the smell of the spices; sense uncertain.
  • ⚠49 ורחבה ונסבה — Ezek 41:7, "broad and winding round"; taken as a large round bowl.
  • ⚠50 לרגל אלקארו — A halting place on the road from Sousse to Tunis; not identified.
  • ⚠51 19. ד' — Printed ד' (Wednesday), but 19 Elul 5533 was a Tuesday (18 Elul is given as Monday). Perhaps ג' was meant, or 20 Elul.
  • ⚠52 דאר היפריחא — The lodging house for emissaries in Tunis; the name is given as printed, not identified.
  • ⚠53 ר' משה בן הקאייד החכם הש' ר' יהושע כהן טנוגי — Joshua Kohen Tanugi is the qaid: his son Moses invites the Ḥida "in his father's name", and the host's son Moses, son-in-law Judah Nataf and daughter Gazala follow. The 1885 text has a meal on another day with the qaid Solomon Nataf, a second holder of the title.
  • ⚠54 לשון ירושלמי בפרדא ולשון התי"ט — On the mule as kilayim: Yerushalmi Kilayim 8 and the Tosafot Yom Tov there (Freimann's reference).
  • ⚠55 סרח העודף — Exod 26:12, used mockingly for the excess of food, as above.
  • ⚠56 ואוכלים בידיהם ורגליהם — So printed; perhaps an exaggeration.
  • ⚠57 כי מעט אשר היה לפני היינו עזובה היינו שכוחה — Gen 30:30 and Berakhot 32b on Isa 49:14; the sense of the sentence is unclear.
  • ⚠58 ואיברא כי כבוד התורה..... — The sentence breaks off with dots in Freimann's print.
