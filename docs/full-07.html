56.1

The year 5534 [1773–74].

שנת תקל"ד.

56.2

Tishrei. On the day of Rosh Hashanah [Saturday 18 September 1773], after the afternoon prayer, all the wardens and the rabbis came, and a number of sages and keen scholars, far more than on other Sabbaths. I told them my interpretations of the saying "They slaughtered the bullock, and brought the child to Eli" [1 Sam 1:25], that he gave a legal ruling, etc., and other matters and questions by the way, and all of them were eager to hear my words; and in truth they are scholars and students of depth. The gevir the qaid himself is very keen and a deep student. He was a disciple of the rabbi R. Abraham Tayyib, who was a great student of depth and composed glosses and novellae on the order Kodashim before Birkat ha-Zevaḥ was published; and the said rabbi [is called] Baba Sidi. They told that when the book Peri Ḥadash came out, he tried hard to throw all its fortresses to the ground; and one night he dreamed of the rabbi of the Peri Ḥadash, who complained of him, and from that day on he would not raise objections against him. The said rabbi Abraham Tayyib was absorbed in study even on the road, and he had a number of disciples, rabbis and sages. The rabbi R. Isaac Lombroso was his disciple, the rabbi who wrote Zera Yitzḥak, which was printed in Tunis. Although R. Isaac was one of the Livornese, he was rabbi to the Tunisians too, and spread Torah in Israel. My teacher the rabbi, may his memory be for the life of the world to come, when he was in Tunis in the year 5498 [1737–38], was much esteemed, and the said R. Isaac, who was the rabbi of Tunis at that time, honored him greatly. They told that my teacher the rabbi used to say to the said rabbi about me, the youngest, that I had been with him in the chapter Or le-Arba'ah Asar at fourteen years of age ⚠1, and out of his goodness he spoke well of me. The said R. Isaac merited, by his diligence, his humility and his perfection according to his qualities, "for they are lively" [Exod 1:19] ⚠2, to raise up many disciples. Among them is the rabbi R. Joseph Zarqa, a sage and a scribe, pious, pure and righteous. He has about sixty disciples, among them weighty students; and among the greatest of his disciples are his son-in-law R. Joshua, R. Abraham Katan the warden, and R. Jacob Ma'arek. All of them were dear to me, and I studied with them; and so with very many scholars, perhaps about a hundred scholars, with whom I studied and debated, and I found favor in their eyes. 1885 printing: xiii

תשרי. יום ראש השנה אחר מנחה באו כל הפרנסים והרבנים וכמה חכמים וחריפים יותר ויותר משאר שבתות ואמרתי להם דרכים שלי במאמר וישחטו את הפר ויביאו את הנער אל עלי שהורה הלכה וכו' וגם שאר ענינים וקושיות באגב והיו כלם תאבים לשמוע דברי ובאמת שהם ת"ח ומעיינים. והגביר הקאייד בעצמו הוא חריף גדול ומעיין והיה תלמיד הרב ר' אברהם טייב שהיה מעיין גדול וחיבר הגהות וחידושים על סדר קדשים קודם שיצא לאור ברכת הזבח והרב הנז' באבא סידי. p. 57וסיפרו כי כשיצא ס' פרי חדש הוא היה משתדל להפיל כל מבצריו ארצה ולילה א' חלם עם הרב פרי חדש והיה מתלונן עליו ומאותו היום לא היה רוצה להקשות עליו. והרב אברהם טייב הנז' היה מתבודד בעיון אפי' בדרך והיה לו כמה תלמידים רבנים וחכמים. והרב יצחק לומברוזו היה תלמידו הרב המחבר זרע יצחק שנדפס בתונים. והרב יצחק אף שהיה ליגורניז היה רב גם לתוניסינום והרביץ תורה בישראל. ומורי הרב זלה"ה כשהיה בתונים ש' תצ"ח היה חשוב מאד והרב יצחק הנז' כיבדו הרבה והוא היה רב תונים בעת ההיא. וסיפרו שמורי הרב זלה"ה היה אומר להרב הנז' עלי אני הצעיר שאני הייתי בו בפרק אור לארבעה עשר שנה ומטיבותיה אומרת עלי טובות. והרב יצחק הנז' זכה בשקידתו וענותנותו ושלמותו על פי מדותיו כי חיות הנה להעמיד תלמידים הרבה. ומהם הרב יוסף זרקא חכם וסופר וחסיד ונקי וצדיק. והוא יש לו כששים תלמידים ומהם חשובי המעיינים ומגדולי תלמידיו חתנו ר' יאושע ור' אברהם קטן הפרנס ור' יעקב מעריך וכלם היו אהובים לי ולמדתי עמהם וכן עם כמה וכמה ת"ח אפשר כמאה ת"ח שלמדתי ונשאתי ונתתי עמהם ומצאתי חן בעיניהם.

57.1

Let us return to the matter of the rabbi R. Abraham Tayyib. He was the great man of his generation, and a disciple of the rabbis R. Abraham ha-Kohen, whom they call Baba Rabbi, and R. Zemaḥ Tzarfati; the rabbi Abraham Tayyib studied with them. Before them they did not study the Tosafot in Tunis, only the Gemara and Rashi alone. The said rabbis were wonderful in counsel [Isa 28:29] in the study of the Tosafot, the Maharshal and the Maharsha, with intense study. They went very deep into the words of the Maharshal and loved his words. Such was their love of the Torah that one eve of Yom Kippur they debated a passage of the Maharshal for so long that the time of the last meal before the fast came, and they had forgotten to buy anything to eat, so busy were they with study. They told me great things about the said rabbis; among them, that the rabbi Zemaḥ Tzarfati was ill for several months, lying in bed ⚠3, and Elijah, of blessed memory, revealed himself to him and healed him; the story is awesome, and I do not remember it. So the said rabbis spread Torah, and the kids became goats. From what I heard, there are today nearly three hundred scholars in Tunis. I also saw boys of fourteen who were students of depth and keen, who could sling at a hair [Judg 20:16]. But all their occupation is with the Talmud and the Maharsha, and the novellae and methods of the Ashkenazi rabbis, and a little with the wording of the Rambam. But they are not so well versed in the codes and the responsa, and they have no memory; so much so that I, for my sins, who have no memory, was counted by them a man of great memory. The gevirim esteem the Torah, and all their desire is to have sons who are scholars; they are very charitable, and honor the Torah and those who study it very, very much. 1885 printing: xiii

ונחזור לענין הרב אברהם טאייב שהוא היה גדול הדור ותלמיד הרבנים הרב אברהם הכהן שקורין לו באבא רבי, והרב צמח צרפתי, ועמהם למד הרב אברהם טייב. וקודם להם לא היו לומדים תוספות בתונים, רק גמרא ורש"י לבד. והרבנים הנז' הפליאו עצה בעיון התוס' ומהרש"ל ומהרש"א בעיון נמרץ. ומאד היו מעמיקים בדברי מהרש"ל ומחבבים דבריו. ולרוב חשקם בתירה ערב כיפור אחת היו נושאים ונותנים כל כך זמן בדבור מהרש"ל עד שהגיע זמן סעודה המפסקת ושכחו לקנות שום דבר לאכול מרוב טרדתם בלמוד. וסיפרו לי גדולות על הרבנים הנז'. ובכלל שהרב צמח צרפתי היה חולה כמה חדשים שוכב במטה הנט"ה ונגלה אליו אליהו ז"ל ורפאהו והענין הוא נורא ואיני זוכר. באופן שהרבנים הנז' הרביצו תורה וגדיים נעשו תישים. ולפי מה ששמעתי היום יש קרוב לשלש מאות ת"ח בתונים. וגם ראיתי נערים בני י"ד שנה מעיינים וחריפים קולעים אל השערה. אך כל עסקם הוא בש"ס ומהרש"א וחידושי ושיטות רבני אשכנז וקצת בלשונות הרמב"ם. אבל אינם בקיאים כל כך בפוסקים ובעלי תשובה ואין להם זכירה. עד שאני בעוניי שאין לי זכירה, היו מחשיבים אותו לזכרן גדול. והגבירים מחשיבים התורה וכל חשקם לזכות לבנים ת"ח והם בעלי צדקות מאד ומכבדי התורה ולומדיה מאד מאד.

14. ערב החג סייעתי לסכך הסוכה בעצמי.

58.1

15 Tishrei [the evening of Friday 1 October 1773]: On the night of Sukkot I wished to sleep in the sukkah, and so through the whole festival, and R. Judah Ḥayyim Raphael Nataf, may his Rock keep him, joined me. He is a son of great men, the husband of the modest and righteous woman Gazala, daughter of the gevir the qaid. All the festival I went to the synagogue and made the wavings [of the lulav] according to the intentions of the Ari, of blessed memory, and I put a small paper of [holy] Names in the case ⚠5, and they did not notice. When I first came, the band of kabbalists came to visit me, and I told them that I am not versed in kabbalah; in these words I told them: there are no kabbalot here. This was a miracle, for the gevir the qaid was quarreling with them: he had put their leaders in prison, and sent to their houses and taken all their books of kabbalah, on the claim that they practiced it out of conceit and not for its own sake. Afterwards they tried to find out whether I knew, and I fled from them. One day the qaid's brother, R. Abraham, who holds himself to be a wonderful rabbi, a great preacher and a kabbalist, and from time to time acts the madman and says he is a prophet, came to me when the house was full of scholars, and began to say great things that he interprets according to the secret; and he expounded "Remember, O Lord, what has come upon us" [Lam 5:1] as the Ari, may the memory of the righteous be for a blessing, wrote. I could not bear it, and I said to him: this that you pride yourself on is the words of the Ari, of blessed memory; and at once he got up and went. Afterwards he came back and said to me: Did you not say that you had not studied the words of the Ari? Where did you know it from? I said: it is written in Sha'arei Tziyyon, and from there I knew it. Also, one day the kabbalists came to me, and I knew that that week the Gate of the Sayings of the Rabbis (Sha'ar Ma'amarei Razal) of the Ari had newly come to them, and I remembered that at its beginning is the secret of "Let not the blessing of a common man be light in your eyes" [Megillah 15a], that in numerical value [hedyot] is dal and a Name ⚠6 ... etc. A child came for me to bless him, and before them I said "Let not the blessing of a common man ..." and blessed him. They answered: this is a great secret. I said to them: dal mi-hakha (leave that aside), I did not know a secret. They understood that I had hinted at the secret by saying dal mi-hakha. They pressed me, and I said: my meaning was that we should not speak of secrets that I do not know, and that is dal mi-hakha. It pleased them very much that they knew and saw that I had aimed at the secret. 1885 printing: xiv

15. ליל סוכות רציתי לישן בסוכה וכן כל החג ונלוה עמי ר' יאודה חיים רפאל נטף יצ"ו בן גדולים בעל האשה הצנועה והצדקת גאזאלא בת הגביר הקאייד. וכל החג הייתי הולך לבית הכנסת ועושה הנענועים עפ"י כוונות האר"י ז"ל ומשים בתיבה נייר קטן של שמות ולא היו מרגישים. ובתחילת ביאתי באו כת המקובלים לבקרני ואמרתי להם שאיני בקי בקבלה, ובלשון הזה אמרתי להם אין כאן קבלות. וזה היה נס כי הגביר הקאייד היה מתקוטט עמהם ושם בבית הסוהר לגדוליהם ושלח לביתם ולקח כל ספרי קבלה שלהם והיה זה בטענה שהם עושים ליוהרא ושלא לשמה. ואח"כ היו משתדלים לחוקרני אם הייתי יודע ואני בורח מהם. ויום אחד אחי הקאייד הוא ר' אברהם המחזיק עצמו לרב מופלא ודרשן גדול ומקובל וכפעם בפעם משתטה ואומר שהוא נביא בא אצלי והבית מלא מת"ח והתחיל לומר גדולות שהוא מפרש עפ"י הסוד ופירש זכור ה' מה היה לנו כמ"ש האר"י זצ"ל. ואני לא יכולתי לסבול ואמרתי לו זה שאתה משתבח בו הם דברי האר"י זצ"ל ותכף קם והלך. ואח"כ חזר וא"ל והלא אתה אמרת שלא למדת דברי האר"י זצ"ל ומהיכן ידעת אמרתי זה כתוב בשערי ציון ומשם ידעתי. גם יום אחד באו המקובלים אצלי וידעתי שבאותה שבוע בא להם מחדש שער מאמרי רז"ל מהאר"י זצ"ל וזכרתי שבתחילתו סוד אל תהי ברכת הדיוט שנימט' דל שם... וכו'. ובא ילד אחד שאברכהו ואמרתי בפניהם אל תהי ברכת הדיוט ובירכתיו וענו ז"ס גדול אמרתי להם ד"ל מהכא אני לא ידעתי סוד והבינו שרמזתי הסוד באומרי ד"ל מהכא. והיו מפצירים בי ואמרתי כונתי שלא נדבר בסודות שאני איני יודע וזהו ד"ל מהכא והיה ערב להם הרבה שידעו וראו שכיוונתי הסוד.

58.2

Kislev [5534, November–December 1773]. My coming to Tunis was against my will, for a reason: the wicked captain. It was Rosh Hashanah, and I was afraid that he would not let me blow the shofar and the like; also because of Yom Kippur and the festival; and because the ship was rickety. After the festival I began to prepare myself, for I wished to travel at once to Livorno; but the gevir and his son put me off day after day and held me back this long time. Several times I was really on the point of setting out, and the gevir's son swore a solemn oath that I should stay. The port is twelve hours away, and one needs permission from the king, and he swore that he would work to hold me back. I was in great distress, and Joseph could not refrain himself [Gen 45:1]. But their kindness was great toward me [Ps 117:2], both in the donations they made with honor, without a sermon or anything and without asking, for I did not go out of the house. But they behaved freely with my honor ⚠7: they wanted to see all my writings and my books, and above all to find out whether I had books of kabbalah. I had in my hands 2 manuscript books, whose story I will now tell. In the year 5524 [1764], when I came to Egypt with the rabbis our teacher R. Abraham ibn Asher, of blessed memory, our teacher R. Yom Tov Algazi, may his light shine, and the sage R. S. Alḥadif, of blessed memory, to go to Constantinople, we lodged in the house of the gevir the sage R. Y. Menasheh, of blessed memory. The said gevir took a liking to me and held me back, and for seventy days I was perplexed whether to send to bring my household. Meanwhile I did not go to the house of study at all, but studied at home; and I went to Signor Israel Sullam to see the writings of our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital, may the memory of the righteous be for a blessing. He had taken them as a pledge from the sage Joseph Bilaydo for a debt of seven ⚠8 reals; the sage R. Joseph Bilaydo went bankrupt and became poor, and he collected them for his debt. They are the autograph of our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital himself. I went there and saw all the Gates written in the pure holy hand of R. Ḥayyim Vital, and the arrangement of his son, our teacher R. Samuel Vital, in the hand of his son R. Moses Vital ⚠9, of blessed memory. Among them I saw a wonderful book in the hand of R. Ḥayyim Vital on practical kabbalah and the science of letter combination, and other matters from the Tannaim. I took this book on loan with other books, and went and secluded myself with the said book; I almost began to understand a little by way of attainment, and I had a great fervor. While I was studying it I dozed off and saw R. Ḥayyim Vital; I asked him a question and he answered me, and gave me a cup of wine to drink. I woke up and was very glad. At night I thought: I am not free there; all night I am obliged to recline at table with the gevir the sage R. Y. Menasheh, of blessed memory, where they speak improperly, jest and mockery and the like, so that every night until nearly midnight I am idle, listening and silent and so on; and I am also not in my own home. If I keep on studying the said book, "I will pursue, I will overtake" [Exod 15:9] some thing ⚠10, and since I am not prepared, perhaps, God forbid, I will come into danger. I held firm to this reasoning, so that in the morning after prayers I took the said book back to the said Signor Israel, and I gave up hope of them. After some months my household came from the holy city, and I spent several years burdened with the yoke of the community, with several illnesses in my household, and with news, so that the matter of the said book never entered my mind at all. I went to the holy city of Hebron and stayed three years and four months, and set out on this mission. When I came to No-Amon [Alexandria] they told me that Signor Menaḥem Mir had two manuscript books of kabbalah. I asked for them and he brought them to me: one of them is the said book of R. Ḥayyim Vital itself, and the other an ancient manuscript book of astronomy, with several matters. I was astonished at the sight [Dan 8:27], for the said Signor Israel is still in his strength, and he knows these books, and he is a scholar and rich: from where, and how, did the book come here? I looked at it, examined it and searched it, and remembered it, and behold, it was the very same. I said to the said Signor Menaḥem: know that this book is not to be found; but take care not to lend it to anyone, and let it be hidden with you like a pearl. He answered: I do not know how to read anything, and what shall I do with it? If you want it, take it, and it will be a pleasure to me; take both of them, for I bought them in the market in Egypt, and let them be yours. And I will tell you that Israel Sullam knew that I had bought these books, and sent to say that they were his and that he wanted to buy them; and I answered: I bought them in Egypt from the estate of the sage Naphtali, a traveler who came there, in public, in the middle of the street. These are the words of Signor Menaḥem. I was amazed to see how the book had rolled about and come into my hands after several years, and I did not know the meaning of the matter. I gave thanks to His name, who granted me this book. I had a box of books, and when I said that I did not know kabbalah, I took this book out and put it privately among my clothes. It was as a miracle: they came and searched the books and did not find this book among them, for surely it would have been lost, as Moses [the qaid's son] did, who took the second book, the one on astronomy, against my will. For there no argument helps, and however much I pleaded that he return it, he would not listen, and it was lost to me. They always wanted to open the small box in which my writings were, and I kept the keys. Once they forced me and took the keys, and wanted to take a book of my writings to have as a keepsake, and I began to cry out with bans until they returned the keys to me. One day I forgot a part of Birkei Yosef that I was writing, and many scholars were there (many); while I was talking with them they took my book. Afterwards I looked for it and did not find it; I asked them and no one answered. I was very distressed and told the matter to the qaid, and he rebuked them, and they brought it. But all this was out of love, and because they were mistaken about me, thinking that I am a great man in everything. Since in Egypt I had several [copies of] Sha'ar Yosef and had brought them with me, the qaid told me to distribute them there. I had about sixty of them, some 130 ⚠11 volumes. Furthermore, in the synagogue of the gevir, three times those called up to the Torah made a donation for me; and the qaid sent to some villages that they should make a donation, and they did. But at once it came to my mind that if I told of it, they would give me a wife against my will, and none could save me from their hand, for they were attached to me beyond all measure, and no arguments help with them; truly, they would have forced me to it by order of the king. Blessed be His glorious name, who showed me wondrous kindness in the moment of this great trouble, that this came to my mind. At once I took a candle and went to the synagogue, and there I wept about an hour; then I came out and washed my face. In my room I found R. Moses and R. Judah, my two brothers-in-law, and the young man R. Moses ben Meir, who was there with the qaid, and other people too, and I sat in sorrow with them until midnight. When they had gone, I sat on the ground to say the Midnight Lament, took off my shoes and stockings, and kept mourning as the law requires for news that comes late: for, for our many sins, she had died on 20 Sivan 5533, on a Friday [11 June 1773], may her soul be bound in the bond of life. 1 Nisan, Sunday of Vayikra [the evening of Saturday 12 March 1774]: it is the custom that on the night of the New Moon of Nisan the great sages gather at the qaid's house and argue Torah. Two houses were filled, and the Holy One, blessed be He, gave me strength and good understanding to argue with them with force and keenness. Among other things they read a doubt raised by the author of the Mishneh la-Melekh on the laws of sacrifices, and at once I began to bring proofs and to weigh the doubt, and I argued with vigor. I was sorry, for they would say that I had stolen the rabbi's words, since they would think that I had read the whole book and had a great memory. In an instant I answered and said: what the rabbi said is what he said; but I see that one can argue the opposite of all that the rabbi said. I began to argue the opposite of everything said, with the method turned round, and all the listeners were amazed that the Holy One, blessed be He, had poured His goodness on the lowest of the low: I was the whole night in breadth of heart, with great depth and keenness and vigor, all the night through, from one matter to another, until the dawn actually rose. The gevir's servant Raḥamim took the vessel of the matzot and the obligatory vegetables and passed it over the head of each of the men three times. He went to the women, who were sitting at the side, and passed it over them too, and asked whether the women also need three times. I said to the gevir's son: tell your father that his brother R. Abraham spoke ill of me because I did not agree with what he said in matters of Torah, when he wanted to say of the commentators that they did not know, and that he understood the sugyot in their truth. For the honor of your father I kept silent; but let him know that if he comes to Livorno and behaves like this, it is in my power to have him put in prison, and then your father should not take offense, because if he disgraces me there it harms the mission; and I trust that the community leaders of Livorno will do so, for he will want to speak wrongly of them as is his habit. He went to his father and spoke to him, and he answered that he would try to keep him from going, but he wanted to know who had told me about his brother. I answered: tell your father that I will not tell him, for hidden reasons, and what is in his power let him do to him. In truth I was distressed, for I knew the affairs of the city: he had already sent for R. He said that he had explained a verse in its plain sense, concerning Elkanah, where it is written “when he came to his house” ⚠26: that is, that a guest may not have marital relations, etc. I answered: certainly it is very good, but these are the words of our sages, of blessed memory, in the chapter Af al pi [Ketubbot ch. 5], and they derive it from there. I reproved them that they do not study the Beit Yosef and the decisors, and all their days they are in the Talmud and the Maharsha. One night I was studying with my beloved friend, the accomplished sage, keen and incisive, our teacher R. Joseph Bashmut, may his light shine, a plain comment of the Tosafot. I said to him: what do you make of this comment? He said to me: it is plain; there is not half a word to say about it. I said to him: “Wait a little for me, and I will show you” [Job 36:2]: there is much to remark on. And I told him four or five ways, and it was a marvel. I know my lack and my lowness in analysis and in every branch of wisdom, truly, not from modesty, for I am truly brutish [Prov 30:2]; but I used to plead before the Lord, who has mercy on all, that for the honor of the Land of Israel He would grant me wisdom and breadth of heart. One Sabbath the gevir raised objections against R. Abraham Kawwat ⚠27, an old sage and a famous judge, who was preaching on the sugya of Ketubbot with the Tosafot and the Maharsha; and I told him that his objection was nothing, and that I would explain at home. After I came home he said to me: so that you may know, all the questions are nothing, but one must not speak during a sermon and shame his face, God forbid. I began to answer all the questions except one of them, which was not broken: it was that he raised an objection against the Yad Malakhi, and it was truly a valid objection ⚠28. He agreed with my words; so that many beyond number were the matters and kindnesses in which the kindness of the Blessed Name prevailed over me [Ps 117:2]. His disciple told me the cause of his death: he had learned the science of the combination of letters and made use of it. I said to him: does he have no writing of this science? He answered that he had a small booklet, but all the books were put in a cupboard and the key was in the hand of his widow. I said to him: go and tell her that, if she is willing, she should lend me two or three books that are not to be found, and if she allows you, take this booklet too. The widow answered that I should take any book I wanted, and it would be a satisfaction to her. He brought me books of later authorities, and among them this booklet, and I copied it. I have already said that I found favor in the eyes of all, and I had a great name [of honor] there. There was one who longed for me to go to his house and offered a hundred reals, and one who offered, if I would go to his house, to have fine clothes made. But the qaid did not let me go out, for he said that it was not an honor for me, and I did not depart from his words. One day I wanted to go to the holy congregation of the Livornese, and he did not want it; I urged him, and he gave leave. This was not in his son's presence, and I did not tell his son. I went on the eighth day of Passover [Sunday 3 April 1774], and they made a donation; afterwards his son was displeased, for he had not wanted me to go. But with his leave I went one Sabbath to pray in the holy congregation where the gevir Abraham Sadboun prays, and they made a large and great donation. Also with leave I went to the house of Abraham Sadboun one day, and I dined one day with the qaid Solomon Nataf, and the rabbis were there. Blessed be the Lord, I discussed Torah with them at length, and I had an opening to question and to answer, from one matter to another. The things they said, I argued against them, and all with great ease, with the help of the Almighty ⚠30. And what shall I tell? Many beyond counting were the matters, in every detail and in general. Only this I will tell: one night after midnight, when I had gone to bed and a curtain was drawn before it, as is their custom, I looked, and behold, a man came in to me. I was astounded. He said to me: I am Moses, son of the qaid; my father says to you that there are some Livornese here who are of the sect of the franc-maçons: is it permitted to kill them? This is in great secrecy; answer me now. When I heard it I stood up trembling and said to him: you are asking me a case of life and death, and I am lying in bed! Wait, and I will tell you. And they say that it is not against the religion of Moses and Israel, and that it is like a comedy. I certainly agree that even a comedy is forbidden, and this all the more; but what reason could there be to pursue them to the point of killing, God forbid? 24 Nisan, Tuesday of the week of Shemini [5 April 1774]: after prayers I was ready to travel to Bizerte, and they made me a farewell. I began to say some new interpretation, and there was a delay of about two hours. The whole courtyard of the gevir was full to all its banks [Josh 3:15] with great and middling people, sages, gevirim, community leaders, close to four hundred people. From the moment I began to speak words of Torah I did not stop, from one matter to another: plain meanings, new interpretations, questions and answers. And on that day too, at the time of leaving, I gave interpretations according to the hidden meaning, in the introductions of the Ari, of blessed memory, and they were glad to see that they had judged rightly in thinking that I knew something of it. In the midst of the great crowd came the sage R. Bekhor Almosnino, emissary of Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt, and began to shout that Hebron owed him money and that he wanted to collect it from the share of the shekels due to Hebron. He had already come to me several times, and I had appeased him, and he had gone back on his words. One day he came to tell me that my father, the crown of my head ⚠31, of blessed memory, had appeared to him in a dream and told him to tell me that I should pay, and I paid, etc. I answered: there is no dream without some idle words [Berakhot 55a]; all is true, only what you dreamed, that I paid, is false. That day he came with shouting. I said: “Hear, all you peoples” [Mic 1:2]: I decree by a decree of the Torah on all the officials and community leaders that they give him not a small coin ⚠32 from Hebron's money; and if he has a claim against them, let him claim it in Constantinople or in Hebron. They removed him from there with disgrace. Afterwards they asked me whether the master of the house where he lodges should throw him out. I said: God forbid; he keeps his honor as before, only his hand must not touch the holy funds of Hebron; and a strong warning to all the officials and community leaders. I set out in peace, and about fifteen men on horseback came with me as far as Bizerte. On the way the son-in-law of the nagid, R. Judah, fainted and could not go on. I was in great distress, for it was in the middle of the wilderness and there was no place to rest. I got down and sat there with him about an hour, until with great difficulty he could ride, with two men walking with him. Blessed be He, we reached Bizerte in peace, and R. Judah too was well and healthy. We all lodged with R. Samuel Sadboun, son of the qaid Abraham, and he did wonders, every day a great feast: although I did not eat meat, he slaughtered several oxen and several geese, and many wines and strong drink, with royal bounty [Esth 1:7]. Among those who came with me were R. Moses, son of the nagid, and his son-in-law R. Judah mentioned above; R. Judah Kohen Tanuji, the bridegroom mentioned above; R. Moses ben Meir mentioned above; the accomplished sage R. Uziel Ḥaik; the rabbi Shalom Ma'arek; and other gevirim. 1885 printing: 5

29 [Sivan], Wednesday of Koraḥ [8 June 1774]: my son-in-law came with a calash and one horse and took me into the city to his house, and I stayed there. At once I asked the parnassim for leave to print the books Shem ha-Gedolim and Birkei Yosef; they gave leave, and I did not want the approbation of the sages. By day I studied in the room of Signori Aaron, Judah and David Acciaioli, and at night I slept at my daughter's; I also ate the midday meal with her. Meanwhile the council (ma'amad) met and pledged 250 ⚠38 pieces. The officials for the Land of Israel came and called me. I went and did not speak to them at all. The officials for the Land of Israel hinted that I should give gratuities ⚠39, and I, like a deaf man, did not hear [Ps 38:14], until they got up and said: the emissary gives them gratuities. They went out as if stealing away, for they saw that I was not pleased. After two hours I went on foot to the garden of Signor Jacob Bonfil, who is a parnas and an official for the Land of Israel. He said to me: why have you come on foot? I said to him: as befits this day ⚠40. He said to me: you have no leave to take the pledge of the council, and you have no leave to send it. I told him my arguments. He said to me: tell me, what is the remedy? I said to him: the remedy is to go back to Alexandria. He was weary of my talking to him, and I went out in hot anger and came to my room. Some friends gave me advice, and I would not accept it. R. Ḥ. B. ⚠41, may God keep him, came and said to me: this is a desecration of God's name. I answered in a loud voice: here are the Talmud and the decisors, and here with us are books of kabbalah and ethics; show us, please, sir, from what source you learn that it is a desecration of God's name. I explained the matter to him, and he was silent. By day the officials for the Land of Israel and the gevir, our friend, the elder Signor Michael Pereira, came and tried to appease me. I spoke to them pleasantly and with arguments. They brought out to me a paper signed by individuals: Signor Michael 25 pieces, Signor Leon 20, Signor Agib 20. They told me that the elder Signor Franco had exerted himself and gone to Signor Joseph Leon and Signor Ḥ. Y. Recanati and told them not to sign. Signor Leon paid no attention and signed, but Signor Recanati would not. They asked me whether I was willing to go with them to some gevirim so that they would sign. I said that they should go, but I would go to Signor Recanati alone. I got up and went to him, found him alone, threw myself down before him and pressed him hard until he signed; but he was really shaking, trembling and quaking. After he had signed I went to Signor Franco and said to him: Signor Leon and Signor Ḥ. Y. Recanati have signed; you sign too. He answered with empty words. I got up, went and handed the paper to Signor Bonfil, and they went and signed up to nearly two hundred pieces. It was a miracle. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. Whoever knows Livorno and the lordliness of its people will see that a miracle and wonder was done for me by the merit of the fathers. Blessed be God the Savior. The servant Ḥayyim Aryeh I dismissed on my arrival in Livorno, because I saw that he had no fear of God. With my daughter, my beloved, may she be blessed among women in the tent, I suffered great sorrow, as she asked about her mother; I strove to leave her in doubt, and though she understood a little, she found no ground to decide. And I began to print Shem ha-Gedolim and Birkei Yosef. 1885 printing: 9

11 Kislev, Monday of Vayishlaḥ [14 November 1774]: I went to the library and saw there some tractates of Gemara written on parchment in the year 4937 [1177]; a Pesikta, Genesis and Exodus, in manuscript. Since I was somewhat ill, the cold had grown strong, and one has to cross the mountains of Bologna; and also our friend R. A. 69.3

69.4

24 Kislev, Sunday of Miketz [27 November 1774]: after prayers a letter came to me from my daughter Sarah, may she be blessed, that she wished to come, she and her husband. כסלו 25. ב' הייתי בצער על כתיבותי שהוליכו לפיזא והניחום ולא ידעו איה וביום שלחתי איש לפיזא ומצאם באושפיזא והביאם לי תלי"ת.

69.8

7 Tevet, the Sabbath day [10 December 1774]: I went to pray in the yeshivah of R. David Rakaḥ, of blessed memory. Although there is a strict ordinance to pray in the synagogue, except for the scholars who are fixed members of the yeshivot, they said they would give me some stipend in that yeshivah.

69.9

Yom Tov and his companions acted and succeeded, and I had satisfaction from R. Yom Tov and his companions in reviving our friendship, praise God. R. Yakar picked a quarrel with me over the debt of Hebron to Damesek Eliezer ⚠45, and to avoid a desecration of God's name I came to a compromise with him. Also Ḥakham Ephraim Navon, son of my teacher the rabbi, of blessed memory, came from Turin about the debt of his mother-in-law, and I was forced to give him some money. I slept in the house of Signor Acciaioli and ate there; on Friday night with Signor Michael, and on the Sabbath day with my daughter, may she be blessed. 1885 printing: 13

Notes and uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 שאני הייתי בו בפרק אור לארבעה עשר שנה — Read: the Ḥida studied the first chapter of Pesaḥim (Or le-Arba'ah Asar) with this teacher at the age of fourteen; born 5484, he was fourteen in 5498. בו may also be read 'in it'.
  • ⚠2 כי חיות הנה — A phrase from Exod 1:19 ('for they are lively'), used loosely: his vigor enabled him to raise many disciples. The construction of the sentence is unclear.
  • ⚠3 שוכב במטה הנט"ה — So printed; the abbreviation הנט"ה is not resolved (perhaps 'the bed inclined [toward death]'). Rendered 'lying in bed'.
  • ⚠4 ניסו — Printed ניסו or גיסו (ג and נ look alike in this type). Nisso is a Tunisian name; read גיסו, it would be 'his brother-in-law Joshua Nataf'.
  • ⚠5 ומשים בתיבה — תיבה may be the case of the lulav or the synagogue's reading desk (teva); rendered 'the case'.
  • ⚠6 שנימט' דל שם — הדיוט has the numerical value 34, that of דל; what שם refers to (a divine Name?) is not clear. The dots are printed.
  • ⚠7 אבל היו מתנהגים בכבודי — Unclear: perhaps 'they made free with my honor (my things)', as what follows suggests, or simply 'they treated me with honor'.
  • ⚠8 ז' ריאלים — The numeral is printed like ז' (or ן'). Seven reals is a small debt for Vital's autographs; a figure may be missing.
  • ⚠9 וסדר בנו מהר"ש ויטאל מכ"י בנו הר"ם ויטאל — Construction unclear: read as Samuel Vital's arrangement, written in the hand of his son Moses Vital.
  • ⚠10 ארדוף אשי"ג איזה דבר — Exod 15:9, printed with an abbreviation mark. Read: by pursuing this study he would reach some (practical) result, for which he was not prepared.
  • ⚠11 כמו ק"ל מחבורים — ק"ל read as 130; what מחבורים means here (volumes, quires?) is not clear.
  • ⚠12 שחר"מ הלוי — The sage R. M. ha-Levi, given by initials; probably R. Mordecai ha-Levi, son of Aaron, who appears earlier in the diary.
  • ⚠13 דברים מראים תועה ותחתיהם ערמה — Unclear phrase; perhaps 'words that seemed (friendly) warnings of error, with cunning beneath them'.
  • ⚠14 סו"ך — Probably for כך וכך, 'such-and-such'.
  • ⚠15 שאני הייתי סיבה שהפסדתי לאיז' — Unclear: perhaps 'that I had caused him loss in Izmir'.
  • ⚠16 עד שלא נתחברו כמעט — Read: he subscribed in advance, almost before the books were written.
  • ⚠17 פליגו — Italian plico, a packet or bundle of letters.
  • ⚠18 הקאייד יאודה הכהן יאונתן — Name as printed; not identified. The 1885 printing calls him the gevir's father-in-law; מחותנו is a wider term for a relation by marriage.
  • ⚠19 ואינו בפניו אף שיש סברא דמיקרי בפניו — A eulogy is allowed on the New Moon only in the presence of the dead; the Ḥida held that the body was not "present" in the other house, though some would count it so. Read in that sense.
  • ⚠20 שהתריס כנגדי — The print reads שהתרים; read שהתריס, "defied".
  • ⚠21 ונהפך אל הרודף והיה רוצה להרכיבו עלי — Unclear: probably the gevir took the servant's side and wanted to force him on the Ḥida.
  • ⚠22 חיים 'ן רבי — 'ן = בן; a name or family name "Rabbi"; not identified.
  • ⚠23 מס' בירך יצחק — So printed; a book title, not identified.
  • ⚠24 והיה ע"ה — Read as עם הארץ, an unlearned man; perhaps עני, poor.
  • ⚠25 שמחזיק עצמו אפשר בעיון — So printed; אפשר ("perhaps") is odd here and may be a misprint. Rendered literally.
  • ⚠26 כאשר בא אל ביתו — So printed; the verse on Elkanah is 1 Sam 1:19, "and they came to their house". The Talmud passage is Ketubbot 65b (chapter Af al pi).
  • ⚠27 ר' אברהם כאוואט — Family name as printed; transliteration uncertain.
  • ⚠28 והיתה השגה באמת — The print reads השנה; read השגה, a critical objection, with the usual ג/נ confusion.
  • ⚠29 ר' ח' לומברוזו זלה"ה נבק"י — ח' is an unresolved initial (Ḥayyim?). נבק"י is unclear; perhaps a misprint for a formula such as "may his soul rest among the holy".
  • ⚠30 בעזר משדי — So printed; read as "with the help of the Almighty" (Shaddai), the extra mem not explained.
  • ⚠31 שעט"ר — Read as שעטרת ראשי, "that the crown of my head (my father)".
  • ⚠32 פ"ק — Read as פרוטה קטנה, "a small coin".
  • ⚠33 עושות זאגלוטאם — Unclear word; the last letter may be ס. Probably the North African women's trilling cry of joy (Arabic zaghrūṭa).
  • ⚠34 מהגורנאדה של תונס — Unclear; perhaps Italian carovana, a merchant convoy, or a word for a regular sailing.
  • ⚠35 לאיספייאנא של ליוורנו — Italian spiaggia, the beach or open roadstead outside the port, where ships waited for quarantine.
  • ⚠36 בנ"א — The print may read כנ"א; read בנ"א, in No-Amon, the usual name for Alexandria.
  • ⚠37 באיספייאנא — A place at Livorno where he waited before entering the lazaretto; perhaps the Spianata (an open esplanade) or the spiaggia (shore). Not identified.
  • ⚠38 רנ"ת פייס — The letters as printed are ר"נ"ת, an unusual order; read here as 250 (רנ). As a number the three letters would be 650.
  • ⚠39 שאתן חינות — חינות: read as gifts or gratuities for the officials (from חן, favor). The word is not common.
  • ⚠40 כמו ערך היום הזה — Literally "as the value of this day"; perhaps he means that on foot suits the humble state the community has put him in. Uncertain.
  • ⚠41 הרח"ב — Unresolved abbreviation of a Livorno sage's name (R. Ḥ. B.); left as initials.
  • ⚠42 ועשו וע"ג — וע"ג is read as ועד גדול, "the great council", as in the next entry (הוע"ג); the sense of the short clause is uncertain.
  • ⚠43 ובא שם פולאקו — פולאקו: read as "a Polack", a Polish Jew; it could also be a man named Polacco.
  • ⚠44 מהרא"י ני' — Probably R. Abraham Israel, rabbi of Ancona (called מהר"א ישראל on p. 71); not certain.
  • ⚠45 חוב חברון לדמשק אליעזר — "Damesek Eliezer" (Gen 15:2) seems to be a nickname for a person to whom Hebron owed money; Freimann refers back to p. 50. Not resolved here.
  • ⚠46 ניסן 10. אחרי ב' — 10 Nisan 5535 was Monday 10 April 1775. The Sabbath before (8 Nisan) read Metzora; the next two Sabbaths had Passover readings, so the next ordinary portion was Aḥarei Mot (29 Nisan). The heading names the coming portion across the festival, as in the entry of 15 Nisan 5537 (p. 93).
  • ⚠47 להביא כתיבותי לעוברם — לעוברם: perhaps "to pass them [through customs]" or "to send them on"; uncertain.
