156.1

14 Sivan [Tuesday 9 June 1778]: after the afternoon prayer we went by carriage to Baron Suasso, and several gevirim, relatives, were there, and they honored us greatly with a table of all kinds of delicacies. There I saw a small Torah scroll, the size of a double sit 1 [m. Shabbat 13:4], in perfect writing on gleaming parchment, a fine thing, and its ark is small, and "the taste of its wood" ⚠2 is worth money, a fine thing. I saw a Megillah in perfect writing, less than a thumb-joint long. We came back home by carriage in peace. And all these days Abraham provokes me: his feet do not stay at home [Prov 7:11], and day and night he does not cease [Gen 8:22] "to hold sway over the trees" 3 [Judg 9:9]. 1885 printing: 103, 104

156.2

15 Sivan, Wednesday [10 June 1778]: in the morning R. Simeon Kann, grandson of R. Moses Kann of Mainz 9, sent a carriage for me to go to his house with the rabbi R. Abraham Nancy, teacher in the house of the notable 4 R. Tobias Boas. I went to visit, and found R. Tobias Boas there; he did me great honor. His young man R. Joseph asked me about a tree that was uprooted with its earth and replanted in another place, whether it is subject to orlah; they reckoned that it could have lived with its earth for two months. I answered that the Rashba in a responsum, which Maran quotes in section 294 [Shulḥan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah], is in doubt about it, and the P.M.A. ⚠5, section 75 of part 2, agreed to forbid it. He honored me greatly and sent us home by carriage. We went to R. Isaac Saruk, son of the cantor R. Solomon, and Signor Aaron de Moses de Pinto, the gabbai, was there. We went to the cabinet (gabinete) of the Prince, and there are all kinds of creeping things, birds, fish and beasts, snakes and scorpions, and parrots (papagayos) in plenty, all embalmed; some of them are put in aqua fortis. Also large animals and deer, with the flesh taken off and their skins filled with straw, and they look as if they were speaking. Also all kinds of worms and kinds ⚠6 of locusts, and a model of a palace of China, and many scientific instruments, and ores of gold, silver and copper, and a very large magnet. Many kinds of sea creatures, and stones that the sea casts up, of many different kinds. Also two embalmed men who have become mummies; many kinds of herbs, and so on; weapons. A wooden hand that was made for a man who lacked a hand, and he used it like his own hand for everything; and the shoes of a giant. Precious stones, some of them large: diamond, ruby, sapphire, emerald and so on, more than thirty kinds of precious stones; and a topaz the size of twelve litre ⚠7, made into an ornament, and a thing like a small pomegranate worked from it, worth several ⚠8. A box with many kinds of spices. Models of several things from China, and a bearded man from China who seems to be speaking. Several sets of eggs, each with a model of its nest. Several sea creatures. Birds as large as sheep, of many kinds, all embalmed. Many kinds of stones whose color and pattern are beautiful, and that is their nature: so they grew. A picture (quadro) from a stone that was found so in nature (natural). Of birds and fish there are many; creeping things and worms by thousands; many herbs, large and small; many medicinal herbs from other kingdoms. The man of the mummy mentioned above is one thousand nine hundred years old. Of these things there are about six rooms, large and middle-sized. Several animals with the flesh taken off, so that their form remains in their bones. Things that the sea casts up, of many different kinds. In the middle of some rooms there is a large table, and on it many kinds of things, different and strange. All of them are wonderful things; among them there is a kind of wood that has become stone. In short, all are wonderful things, arranged in cases with glass over them and the doors closed, and everything in order. See the end of the matter below. 1885 printing: 105

157.1

R. David Meldola came here to The Hague to marry off his daughter, and he came to visit me, and his mouth was full of talk against Shalem ⚠10. He told me that when the Peri Ḥadash was in Amsterdam, the rabbi Isaac Aboab was a very old man, and the congregation asked him [the Peri Ḥadash] to be rabbi: the old rabbi would keep his honor, and when he died everything would pass to him. He said that he wanted them to give him no gifts at all, and therefore they must give generously from the holy congregation; and also that he did not want to be under the men of the Mahamad in any matter of law. They said they accepted that in a matter of Torah law the Mahamad could not go against him, and as to the salary, they would add to it, but only double the amount and no more. He said it could be no less than a thousand ducatoni. The men of the Mahamad discussed it, and in the end they agreed to give him the thousand ducatoni, all as he had asked, and they told him so gladly; and he answered that he would still think it over. On Sunday he went to the Mahamad and told them that he had thought it over and that it was not possible for him, and they began to argue with him. He answered: "If so, tomorrow I will send to all the members to tell them that they have accepted me; on Tuesday I will go and visit; on the Sabbath I will preach; and on Sunday I will go and tell them that my reign has already begun. I will tell the members of the holy congregation that they are not careful in several things, and that they must turn from their evil way. First I will tell the first parnas that he has two gentile women in his house and is suspected with them, that he must send them away; and I will tell the second parnas such and such, and so to all of them; for I want to save my soul, and they are sunk in the deep mire [Ps 69:3; 40:3] of open sins. This is the thing I asked: that I may do Torah justice, to refine and purify the congregation from all iniquity, and rebuke the transgressors and curse them [Neh 13:25], "and say: Destroy!" 11 [Deut 33:27] And I think that then you will join together against me, and the conspiracy will be strong [2 Sam 15:12] to drive me out of the land, and who knows what a day may bring [Prov 27:1]. But if it is in your heart to remove the impurity from the land, and you will truly uphold all the words of the Sages, then 'renew 12 a right spirit within me' [Ps 51:12] to merit this great commandment; and I could also study Torah as I wish, in peace and quiet and security [Isa 32:17], and from this they would see and do the same in many holy communities, and the merit of the many depends on you. But I see that you want me only to have a name for yourselves, and not for the sake of Heaven, and you want to walk in the stubbornness of your heart with no one to protest at all. Therefore, although you have offered a thousand ducatoni a year, nevertheless, in the matter I asked, that I may judge Torah law and none can stay my hand [Dan 4:32] in it, I see that you have not understood my intention." When the parnasim heard these holy words they said to the rabbi, the Peri Ḥadash, of blessed memory: "Justice is with you, and you have aimed at the truth: you cannot be rabbi in our congregation." So he traveled away in peace. Thus far R. David mentioned above told me at length. 1885 printing: 105

ור"ד מילדולא בא להשיא בתו פה האייא ובא לבקרני ופיו מילא לדבר על ה' שלם. וסיפר כי כאשר היה הפר"ח באמשט' היה הרב יצחק אבוהב זקן מופלא וביקשו הקהל ממנו להיות רב והרב הזקן יהיה בכבודו וכשיפטר ישאר הכל לו. והוא אמר שרצונו שלא יתנו לו שום מתנה ולכן צריך שיתנו מהק"ק בשופע. וגם שאינו רוצה להיות תחת אנשי המעמד בדבר הנוגע לדין ואמרו שמקבלים שבדבר הנוגע לדין תורה המעמד לא יוכלו לבא נגדו ולענין הפרס יוסיפו לו אך שיהיה כפל הענין ותו לא והוא אמר שאין פחות מאלף טוקטוני והיו אנשי המעמד נושאים ונותנים וסוף דבר הסכימו לתת לו האלף טוקאטוני הכל כמו ששאל ויגידו לו בשמחה והוא השיב שעדין הוא יחשוב. וביום א' הלך למעמד וא"ל שחשב שלא אפשר ליה והתחילו לחלוק עמו. והשיב א"כ מחר אשלח לכל היחידים להודיעם שקבלוני ויום ג' אלך לבקר ושבת אדרוש ויום א' אלך לו' להם שכבר התחילה מלכותי ואני אומר ליחידי הק"ק שאינם נזהרים בכמה דברים וישובו מדרכם הרעה ובתחילה אני או' לפרנס הראשון שיש לו שתי גויות בבית והוא חשוד עליהם שיוציאים ואומר לפרנס ב' כך וכך וכן לכלם שאני רוצה להציל נפשי והם מוטבעים במצולות טיט היון מעבירות מפורסמות והוא הדבר אשר שאלתי שאוכל לעשות דין תורה לזקק ולטהר הקהל מכל עון ואת פושעים אוכיחם ואקללם ויאמר השמד. ואני סובר שאז תתחברו כנגדי ויהי הקשר אמיץ לגרשני מן הארץ ומי יודע מה יולד יום ואי יש את לבכם לבער הטומאה מהארץ ותהיו מקימים כל דברי חז"ל על נכון אז רוח נכון חד"ש בקרבי לזכות למצוה רבה זו וגם אוכל לעסוק בתורה כרצוני בישוב והשקט בטח ומזה יראו וכן יעשו בכמה קהלות קדושות וזכות הרבים תלוי בכם אך אני רואה שאתם אינכם חפצים בי כי אם להיות לכם שם ולא לשם שמים ואתם רוצים ללכת בשרירות לבכם מבלי מוחה כלל ולכן אף שאתם התנדבתם אלף טוקטני לשנה מ"מ בענין שאלתי שאוכל לדון דין תורה ולית דימחא בידי בזה אני רואה שלא ירדתם לסוף דעתי וכשמוע הפרנסים אמרי קדוש אמרו להרב פר"ח ז"ל הדין עמך וכיונת אל האמת אשר לא תוכל להיות רב בקהלתנו ובכן נסע לש' עד כאן סיפר לי ר' דוד הנז' בהרחבה.

157.2

In truth it pleased me greatly to see the pleasantness of the words of the rabbi, the Peri Ḥadash. Happy is he, for he did not turn to imagined honor, and his eye saw the faults of others who do not keep away from sins, and he merited to rebuke them with open rebuke to their face. May his merit protect us.

ובאמת הנאני מאד לחזות בנועם דברי הרב פר"ח ואשריו כי לא פנה לכבוד המדומה ועינו רואה בשל אחרים שאינם פורשים מהעבירות וזכה להוכיחם תוכחת מגולה על פניהם זכותו יגן עלינו.

157.3

Let us return to the matter of the Prince's cabinet, for in truth it is a wonder of wonders, and everything is arranged in order, fair and pleasant and lovely, an astonishing thing; I have written the little that is left in my memory. On every thing, small or large, even small creeping things and worms, on each one there is a very small paper with the number (numero) written on it; so that beside this, the room at Padua is like an ape before a man [b. Bava Batra 58a], and what likeness can compare to it [Isa 40:18]? Signor Aaron de Moses de Pinto, the gabbai, paid two or three florins to the doorkeepers and the servants there. From there I went to R. Tobias, and we dined there with great honor, and afterwards I went to the rabbi. After the afternoon prayer we sat at table with the gabbai mentioned above, Aaron de Moses, and his brothers Signor Isaac, Signor Samuel and Signor Moses, and some others; he set a fine table with all kinds of sweets, and we ate something there. When I went to the rabbi's house on Tuesday, he was asleep in his room; the maid woke him, and he came down and fell on the stairs and cried out loudly. I was distressed, and blessed be the Lord that no harm came to him. And I had more sorrow this day: I received a letter from Amsterdam that on Shavuot Shalem was saying to everyone who came to him: "You have seen So-and-so: because he did not take my advice, he has not succeeded very much." He also spoke much about a legal point that I had stated, and some other things against me in [the learning of] the Torah 13. I was bewildered at what I heard [Isa 21:3]. In what he said against me the truth is with him, and I am brutish and do not know [Ps 73:22]; but in what he said, that I did not follow his advice, he did not speak rightly, for he did not advise me, nor was there any advice in the matter, as will be clear from what I wrote above. I was also grieved that I lost the money I had put in the lottery. And also a letter came to me that London would not give a farthing 14, and I was grieved. May the Merciful have mercy on me and make my end good, amen, may it be His will. 1885 printing: 106

158.1

16 Sivan, Thursday [11 June 1778]: as I came out from the afternoon prayer I spoke to R. S[olomon] Saruk about his slackness in the matter of the mission. He answered me angrily that he does not go to speak to individuals, for it is beneath his dignity and not his place, for he is a man of standing and no reed-cutter [b. Sanhedrin 33a]; they have already done much, and he cannot come with a sword and kill them to make them give, and so on; all with a sullen face and gestures. And the Lord in His great mercy helped me, and I showed him that I had not sinned in my words and had not touched his honor, God forbid, for I knew that he had taken trouble, and so on, at great length ⚠15, with much sweetness of tongue. From there we went to the house of Signor Isaac de Aaron Suasso, the parnas, and all the Signori Pinto and the Signori Suasso joined us, and also Signor Joseph de Pinto, who had come from Amsterdam, and Signor Raphael de Abraham Mendes da Costa and Signor Costa Athias. These two had come because Madame Patron of this place, who had a million, a widow with no children, wanted to deal in shares (acciones) and lost all her money, and more than that, a hundred and eighty thousand, most of it [owed] to gevirim of Amsterdam. They said that this was a great gentile scholar who was in this city six hundred years ago ⚠23; he was a great fool until he was thirty, so that he could not learn or write even one letter, and suddenly his heart was opened and he became learned, with a great memory, and a great scholar. Because of his greatness and importance his statue was made of iron. The name of this scholar is Erasmus. He was the head of a new faith of Holland, and he worked to establish it. Therefore they collected from all the gentiles of the towns of Holland, and they gave silver, gold and copper in plenty, and melted it all together and made his statue. Tammuz, Thursday, the day of the New Moon ⚠33 [30 Sivan, Thursday 25 June 1778]: in the evening we came to the edge of Zeeland. It is one of the provinces of Holland, for the Republic rules over seven provinces, and they are: Holland, Zeeland, West Friesland, Utrecht, East Friesland, Overijssel, Gelderland ⚠34. This village we came to is called Lillo, and it is the edge of the Republic on one side, and close by there are houses that belong to Brabant, under the rule of the Queen.

תמוז ה'. יום ר"ח בערב באנו לקצה זיי לאנד. והוא אחת מהפרובי[נ]סייאם של הולאנדה שהריפובליקה מושלת על שבע פרובינסייאם והם הולאנד. זיי לאנד. p. 161 וישט פרישלאנד אוטרייך הושט פרישלאנד הובריסיל גילידיר לאנד. וכפר זה שבאנו שמו לילו והוא קצה הריפובליקה מצד אחד ושם סמוך יש בתים השייכים לבראואנט ממשלת המלכה.

161.1

1 Tammuz [Friday 26 June 1778]: the windows of heaven were opened [Gen 7:11] and there was rain in a storm. Blessed be the Lord, it lasted about an hour and a half, and then it stopped. We came to Pelipo ⚠35, the beginning of Brabant, and hired a wagon without a cover, and we came two hours before midday to the city of Antwerp, and we had no rain on the way. Blessed be the Lord, our way was a straight way, for I had no trouble at all, except on Wednesday, the eve of the new moon of Tammuz [24 June 1778], when we crossed a little sea by which one goes to Middelburg, which is in Zeeland: we had some upset in our bodies, and at once we were settled again. Blessed is He who has bestowed every good on us. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever.

תמוז 1. יום הששי ארובות השמים נפתחו והיו גשמים בזעף וברוך ה' כמו שעה וחצי היו ואח"כ שבתו ובאנו לפליפו תחלת בראואנט ושכרנו עגלה בלי כיסוי ובאנו שתי שעות קודם חצות לעיר אנויריש ולא היה לנו גשמים בדרך. וב"ה שדרכנו היה דרך ישרה כי לא הי"ל שום צער רק ביום ד' ער"ח תמוז שעברנו מעט ים שהולכים בה למידילבורג שהוא בזיי לאנד היה לנו קצת שינוי בגופנו ותכף נתישבנו ברוך שגמלנו כל טוב הלכ"ט כל"ח.

161.2

2 Tammuz, the Sabbath of Koraḥ [27 June 1778]: we spent the Sabbath in Antwerp with R. Zelig, beside still waters [Ps 23:2]. Near midday I learned that there were Jewish travelers there, and I gathered them, and with us there was a minyan; I said some words of Torah and the Kaddish. We also prayed the afternoon and evening prayers with a minyan. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good.

תמוז 2. ש"ק קרח שבתנו באנויריש עם ר' זעלג על מי מנוחות וקרוב לחצות ידעתי שיש אורחים שם מישראל וקבצתים ועמנו היה מנין ואמרתי קצת ד"ת וקדיש. גם התפללנו מנחה וערבית במנין הלכ"ט.

161.3

3 Tammuz, Sunday of Ḥukkat [28 June 1778]: we traveled to Brussels with R. Zelig and arrived safely while it was still broad day. R. Lipman bar Nathan 38, the notable who lives there, went and hired places for us in the diligence. This day Abraham made his folly great. In the middle of the way, when we were at an inn on their feast day, and Abraham was going back and forth, R. Zelig told him to be quiet and not to go here and there. He grew angry, struck blows on the table and said to R. Zelig: "The exile there is comes from the Jews ⚠36, for you are haters of Israel." He grew angry and wanted to turn back, and in the end he would not get into the carriage and stayed outside. Because of this, when we came to the large town of Mechelen, gentiles gathered by the hundreds, laughing and shouting, and he enjoyed it, while to me it was like the thrusts of a sword [Prov 12:18]. When we came to Brussels there was a gentile who knew some Hebrew, and he [Abraham] argued with him and said to him, "Sir, [you are] a great ass." ⚠39 There was danger, and blessed be the Lord that he fell silent. After that I rebuked him with a soft tongue, that it is an unclean place ⚠37 and there is danger, and for his insolence and his anger. Before I had finished rebuking him, R. Lipman and R. Zelig went to see the diligence, and he wanted to go with them, and they did not want it, for some hundreds would gather to their disgrace. I got up and went out into the market to bring him back, and he swore that he would go; before the crowd it was a disgrace for me to hold him, and he slipped away, until I laid a decree on him. After that R. Lipman sent a carriage so that I should come to his house because of the gentiles, and he [Abraham] came on foot behind us to provoke us, and many gentiles joined him; R. Lipman was very angry. We came to the house at night, and he said that he did not want to sleep inside, and R. Zelig wanted to shut the door, and I had trouble, until my friend Signor Ariani took pains and entreated him to come.

תמוז 3. א' חקת נסענו לברוסילאם עם ר' זעלג ובאנו לש' עוד היום גדול ור' ליפמאן בר נתן הקצין שדר שם הלך והשכיר לנו מקומות בדיליחינסיא. וזה היום אברהם הגדיל שטותו שבאמצע הדרך בהיותנו באושפיזא יום חגא שלהם ואברהם הולך ובא א"ל ר' זעלג שישתוק ולא ילך מכאן לכאן ונתכעס והכה מכות על השלחן וא"ל לר' זעלג הגלות שיש הוא מהיאודים כי אתם שונאי ישראל ונתכעס והיה רוצה לחזור וסוף דבר לא רצה ליכנס בקארוסה ונשאר בחוץ וזה סיבה שכאשר באנו לעיר גדולה מיכלא נאספו גויים למאות צוחקים וצועקים והוא משתעשע בזה ולי היה כמדקרות חרב. ובבואנו לברוסילאם היה גוי יודע לה"ק קצת ונתוכח עמו וא"ל אדוני חמור גדול והיה סכנה וברוך ה' שנאלם. ואחר זה בשפה רפה הוכחתיו שהוא מקום טו' ויש סכנה ועל זדונו וכעסו. ועד שלא סיימתי להוכיחו הלכו ר' ליפמאן ור' זעלג לראות הדיליחינסיא ורצה לילך עמהם ולא רצו כי יאספו לקלונו כמה מאות וקמתי ויצאתי בשוק להחזירו ונשבע שילך ובפני המון העם הייתי חרפה לתופסו והוא נשמט עד שגזרתי עליו. אח"ך שלח ר' ליפמאן קארוסה שאבא לביתו מפני הגוים, והוא ברגליו אחרינו להכעיסנו ונלוו גויים רבים ור' ליפמאן היטב חרה לו. ובאנו לבית בלילה ואמר שאינו רוצה לישן בפנים ור' זעלג היה רוצה לסגור הפתח והי"ל צער עד שטרח ידידי סי' ארייאני וחילה פניו שיבא.

161.4

4 Tammuz, Monday [29 June 1778]: at four hours in the morning we set out in the diligence with eight gentile men and women, and among them one who spoke Spanish, and I talked with him. At midday we came to Soignies, and he [Abraham] had a quarrel with a gentile, and in a great stammering tongue [Isa 32:4] he said to him: "I am a great merchant and deal in all kinds of cloth." Signor David told him to be quiet, and he shouted at him. After that he came to pour water on my hands, and I told him that I did not want him to serve me, and from my great distress and anger I gave him "the mention of Fathers and Mighty Deeds" [m. Rosh Hashanah 4:5] and every name he has 40. He wanted to leave, and I laid a decree on him under ban. After that he beat himself with great blows on his covered head, in the way of the wicked mohashqom ⚠41, and kept saying that I was taking vengeance on him, that whoever is a Jew does not do this, that I am very cruel to him and am always killing him; all with great shouts, and on the side he reviled Signor D. Ḥ. A. So it was a very hard day. I humbled myself and was distressed, for there was a great crowd of gentiles there, and what he did was almost a danger; thanks to God they were busy, and the matter stayed between us. I gave in to him. Signor D. Ḥ. A. did not eat bread from distress.

תמוז 4. ב' בד' שעות מהבקר נסענו בדיליחינסיא עם ח' גויים וגויות וביניהם א' מדבר ספרדי והייתי מדבר עמו. בחצות באנו לסוונס והיה לו ויכוח עם גוי ובלשון עלגים גדול א"ל אני סוחר גדול ונושא ונותן בכל מיני בגדים וא"ל סי' דוד שישתוק והוא צעק עליו. ואחר זה בא ליצוק מים על ידי ואני אמרתי לו שאיני רוצה שישמשני ומרוב שיחי וכעסי עשיתי לו הזכרת אבות p. 162וגבורות וכל שום שיש לו. והוא היה רוצה לילך וגזרתי עליו בחרם ואח"ז היה מטפח במכות גדולות על ראשו חפוי כדרך המוהשקום הרעים והיה או' שאני עושה בו משפטי נקמות ומי שהוא יאודי אינו עושה זה ושאני מתאכזר מאד עליו ואני ממית אותו תמיד והכל בצעקות גדולות ומן הצד חירף לסי' דח"א. באופן שהיה יום קשה מאד. ואני נכנעתי ונצטערתי שהיו המון רב מגויים שם וכמעט הוא סכנה מה שעשה ות"ל שהיו טרודים ונשאר הדבר בינינו. ואני נכנעתי לו. וסי' דח"א לא אכל לחם מצערו.

162.1

After that we came to the beginning of France, and the guards mocked us and opened my boxes and searched our clothes; blessed be the Lord, we got away in peace. Then we came to the customs house of Valenciennes, and they emptied the wagon in which we had come from Brussels. The lock of my large trunk was broken, and they began to make false claims about old, washed handkerchiefs, that they were new, and there was no breath left in me [Dan 10:17]. After that I gave two livres to the inspector, and he said to me, "Give to my colleague"; they opened the second trunk and saw almost nothing. We came to the inn in peace. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. May He in His mercy complete it for good; so may it be His will.

אח"ך באנו לתחילת צרפת והשומרים התעללו בנו ופתחו הקאשיטאם שלי וחיפשו בבגדינו וברוך ה' נפטרנו לש'. ואח"כ באנו לדיוואנה של וואלאנסינה והריקו העגלה שבאנו מברוסילאם והבהול גדול שלי נשבר המנעול והתחילו להעליל על מטפחות ישנות ומכובסות שהם חדשות ולא נותרה בי נשמה ואח"ז נתתי ב' ליטרין לרואה וא"ל תתן לחבר שלי ופתחו הבהול שני וכמעט לא ראו דבר. ובאנו לאוש' לש' הודו לה' כי טוב כי לעולם חסדו. הוא ברחמיו יגמור לטובה כי"ר.

162.2

5 Tammuz, Tuesday [30 June 1778]: at night we slept in Valenciennes, and today before midday we came to the city of Cambrai. I was very worried about the customs of Péronne, for all bear witness to their wickedness and strictness. We came there while it was still broad day, and they took us to the customs house. The customs officers recognized me, for I had passed there in the winter; they opened the trunks, saw a little, closed them again and looked no further. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever, and blessed be His glorious name for ever and ever.

תמוז 5. ג' בלילה ישננו בואלינסינא והיום קודם חצות באנו לעיר קמברי ואני הייתי מצטער מאד על מכס פירוני שכלם יעידן ברשעם ודקדוקם ובאנו שם בעוד היום גדול והוליכונו לדיוואנה והמשרתים של מכס הכירו אותי שעברתי מימות החורף ופתחו הבהולים וראו מעט וחזרו וסתמו ולא ראו עוד הודו לה' כי טוב כי לעולם חסדו וברוך שם כבודו לעולם ועד.

162.3

6 Tammuz [the evening of Tuesday 30 June 1778, and Wednesday 1 July]: on the night of Wednesday, two hours before midnight, we set out in the diligence, and we went on all night and all day until seven hours after midday, when we arrived safely in Paris, except that at midday we stopped in the town of Pont-Sainte-Maxence for about an hour. In the evening they took us to the customs house, and there was great confusion from several diligences coming from the cities of France, and the customs men were very busy. When the time came to examine our things I was very afraid and trembling, and blessed be the Lord, they opened the trunks and gave them a brief look for a moment, and no more. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. I took a carriage and we came to the house of Signor David Naquet; he was not at home until night, and we went to an inn in peace. 1885 printing: 109

תמוז 6. אור לד' שתי שעות קודם חצות נסענו בדיליחינסיא והיינו הולכים כל הלילה וכל היום עד ז' שעות אחר חצות שהגענו לש' בפאריץ לבד שישבנו בחצות בעיר פונס כשעה אחת והוליכונו בערב לדיוואנה והיה ערבוביא גדולה מכמה דיליחינסיאם הבאות מערי צרפת והיו טרודים המוכסים מאד וכשהגיע העת לבדוק חפצינו הייתי ירא וחרד מאד וברוך ה' שפתחו הבהולים וראו ראיה מועטת כרגע ותו לא הודו לה' כטכל"ח. ולקחתי קארוסה ובאנו לבית סי' דוד נאקיט ולא היה בבית עד הלילה והלכנו לאושפיזא לש'.

162.4

7 Tammuz, Thursday [2 July 1778]: Abraham forgot the blanket (ḥiram) ⚠42, and in the morning after prayers I sent him, and they found it inside the coach, blessed be the Lord. Monsieur Fabri came and took us to the house of his relative, and he kept me four hours; he and his relatives showed me a very friendly face. They wanted me to eat there with them, and I said that I eat only eggs roasted by our own hands, and they agreed. I asked that gentile to tell me what he believes, and he told me: in the Lord, the God of Israel. I examined him on this, and it seemed to me that it is so. I told him: if so, morning and evening say the verse Shema Yisrael, keep the seven commandments [of the sons of Noah], and beware in every way of associating [another with God], but only the complete unity of the Lord, the God of Israel. He accepted, and said that he would pray only to the Lord. After this I went to Signor Pereira. 1885 printing: 109

תמוז 7. ה'. שכח אברהם החי"ראם ובבקר אחר תפלה שלחתיו ומצאו בתוך העגלה ב"ה. ובא מוסו פאברי והוליכנו לבית קרובתו ועכבני ד' שעות והראה לי הוא וקרובותיו פנים יפות מאד ורצו שאוכל שם עמהם ואני אמרתי איני אוכל כי אם ביצים צלויים על ידינו ונתרצו. ואני אמרתי להגוי הנז' שיא' לי במה מאמין וא"ל בה' א"י וחקרתי בזה ונראה בעיני שכך הוא. וא"ל א"כ בבקר ובערב תאמר פ' שמע ישראל. ותקיים ז' מצות ותזהר משיתוף בכל אופן. כי אם יחוד גמור לה' א"י וקבל וא' שלא יתפלל כי אם לה' ואח"ז הלכתי לסי' פירירה.

162.5

8 Tammuz, Friday [3 July 1778]: from the toil of the journey I was bruised and crushed [Lev 22:24]. At once, on Wednesday, I found a letter from the excellent and dear Signor Raphael, may his Rock keep him, and I was delighted [Prov 8:30]; also on the eve of Friday ⚠43 another letter, and the first was full of strong love and honor. From the great hardship of the way the large trunk broke; also the repeater watch: its chain broke ⚠44. In my small box there were some pencils 45; they broke and were ground up, and several writings and several things were soiled. The small white inkwell also broke, was blackened and spoiled. I had to buy a trunk. I gave the things mentioned to a craftsman, and I was sullen and angry [1 Kings 20:43]. And Abraham, in his folly, did not want to sleep at night and rebelled against me. Today too, about the washerwoman, he spoke insolently. It is like grief to my soul to bear his folly and the force of his insolence. 1885 printing: 109

תמוז 8. הו' בטורח הדרך הייתי מעוך וכתות ותכף ביום ד' מצאתי כתב מהמוצלח החביב סי' רפאל יצ"ו ואהיה שעשועים גם אור הו' ס"ל כתב אחר והראשון היה בתוקף אהבה וכבוד. ומרוב תלאת הדרך נשבר הבהול הגדול גם p. 163שעה ריפיטיסיון נשבר החבל. ובתיבה הקטנה שלי היו כמה אפים ונתקו ונטחנו ונתלכלכו כמה כתבים וכמה חפצים. וגם הקסת קטנה לבנה נשברה ונתפחמה ונתקלקלה. והוצרכתי לקנות בהול. והחפצים הנז' נתתים לאומן והייתי סר וזעף. ואברהם בשטותו שלא רצה לישן בלילה והתרים נגדי. גם היום בענין המכבסת הטיח דברים וכמו צער בנפשי לסבול שטותו ותוקף העזות שלו.

163.1

9 Tammuz, the Sabbath [4 July 1778]: we were with my beloved friend ⚠46 Signor David Naquet, and Signor Abraham Vidal and Signor Ventura came and ate with us. Signor David and the lady his wife did wonderfully in making great meals, with great honor and much love. Although there is now a synagogue near my inn, in the house of Signor Ḥananel de Milliav, I had to take great trouble and go to the synagogue of the Avignonese, since I receive kindness from them, and my host Signor David prays there; and I had great honor. Above all because, when I came the first time, Signor Abraham Vidal asked me to pray for the queen that she should conceive, and it is now four months that she has conceived; and Monsieur Fabri said to me, "See, your prayer has borne fruit." I answered: not by my righteousness, but by the merit of my fathers, of blessed memory. 1885 printing: 109

תמוז 9. ש"ק היינו אצל יר"א סי' דוד נאקיט ובאו סי' אברהם וידאל וסי' וינטורה וסעדו עמנו והפליא סי' דוד והגברת אשתו לעשות סעודות גדולות בכבוד גדול ואהבה רבה. ואעפ"י שעתה יש בה"כ סמוך לאושפיזא שלי בבית סי' חננאל דמילייאב. הוצרכתי לטרוח מאד וללכת לבה"כ של האב'יניונים להיות שאני גומל חסד מהם והבעה"ב סי' דוד שם מתפלל והי"ל כבוד גדול. ובפרט שבבואי פעם א' א"ל סי' אברהם וידאל שאתפלל על המלכה שתתעבר וזה ד' חדשים שנתעברה וא"ל מוסו פאברי ראה שתפלתך עשתה פירות והשבתי לא בצדקתי אלא בזכות אבותי ז"ל.

163.2

10 Tammuz, Sunday of Balak [5 July 1778]: at the end of the Sabbath, after the evening prayer, Abraham Léon came with me from the synagogue. He is a young man from Bayonne who used to serve me constantly there, and now he is in Paris. I told him to buy charcoal, and Abraham said: why do I tell him to buy it, when he [Abraham] takes great trouble in my service and does not want another to buy? I was very angry, and I told Abraham Léon not to buy it, and I remained without coffee, to see the insolence of Abraham Gabbai and that he wants to rule over me. On Sunday and Monday I did not speak to him, and he did not cook and did not make coffee; I ate only bread and cheese. I had distress, for all day and until midnight Gabbai was not seen, and he is always going about the markets and streets.

There is one very large and fierce dog, and the guards do not let a strange dog enter. This large dog goes out at will to the market to walk about, and comes back, and the guard knows him. One day the large dog came and brought with him a strange dog, poor and very thin, and he was hiding him under himself. The guard did not want the thin dog to come in, and the large dog rose up against him and barked, and was bold, and took the thin one and brought him in with him, and the guard looked the other way. The large one took the thin one and settled him in the kitchen for eight days, and he ate and drank until he grew fat. Then the large one took the one who had been thin and brought him out. In the evening Gabbai came and brought the basket in which he kept his clothes, torn and cut, and said that he wanted to buy a sack for five livres. I said to him, "Go," and gave him six livres. He came and said, "I bought it for ten." I told him that he was wasting money and should return the sack. He answered that he had bought it for three, and owed three, and the like; and in the end he had mocked me, for it seems that he had bought the sack and then went and tore the basket. He told so many lies and grieved me with his nonsense; and besides, the sack was very small and he had to bring the basket too. I had another grief: Signor Abraham Gradis in Bordeaux asked me whether one may divorce his wife against her will, and I answered that it is forbidden. He asked me to give it to him in writing, and so as not to refuse I wrote that a first wife who is modest and without blemish, and he has children by her, it is forbidden to divorce her. The matter reached Samuel Peixotto, who is in a lawsuit here, for he wants to divorce his wife and she does not want the divorce. His advocate made a book of pleadings and among other things brought my words, and wrote: this man is an emissary to collect money for the poor of the Land of Israel, and they sent him because he is trustworthy, but not because of his wisdom; what he wrote was for money, or else he knows nothing of matters of law; what he says, he cites no source, and it is nowhere in the Talmud, except that they said in an aggadah that for one who divorces his first wife the altar sheds tears [b. Gittin 90b], etc., and one does not learn law from aggadah. He went on at length about this, printed it all and distributed it to the whole Parlement. All this was on the advice of Lipman Calmer, Baron de Picquigny, Vidame d'Amiens, who professes an abomination ⚠48 and goes there and kneels and sits at Mass; and Raba of Bordeaux joined them. That is PKR 49: the initials of Peixotto, Calmer, Raba. I was very grieved that it was written and printed, and that it will go to Amsterdam, London, Bordeaux: the initials of evel, mourning. 25 Tammuz [Monday 20 July 1778]: ten hours after evening we came to the city of Mâcon. Because of the great confusion of the arrivals, for they were many, I had trouble. I got in first and took a room with three beds "and the lying down" ⚠57; and since the custom is that a servant does not pay as much as his master, I told the maidservant that for the servant I would give less. The servant was there, and he grew angry with me: why do I say that he is a servant, so that they despise him? He went out in hot anger [Exod 11:8], as is his way, going on and talking [2 Kings 2:11], saying that he would pay ⚠58, for he does not want to say that he is a servant. I kept "answer not" [Prov 26:4] and thought that surely he would come back to sleep in his bed, all the more since he had not eaten, and the bread and cheese were lying on the table. In any case, in the great crowding, sorrow, confusion and weakness, it was a great trouble to me to see his insolence, and that there is not even one day when he does not grieve me. Midnight passed and I slept; within two hours I woke and saw that the fire had caught most of the tallow candle. I got up and set it right, and went out at the door of the house to look for Abraham. I found him asleep on a round table behind it and called him to come and sleep; he answered that he already lives a life of sorrow in everything, and did not listen to me. As for the candle, since there were no snuffers to trim the wick, the wick burned and fell into the tallow; I got up to set it right and it went out. I called Abraham to look for a candle; he got up in a rage and talked as is his way. I did not turn my ear to all his insolent words; I only heard that he said among his words that he does not rest and does not let me rest. I was like a man who does not hear [Ps 38:15]. I waited for daylight so as to pray before we went to the boat, because of the tzitzit and the tefillin and standing among the gentiles in a great crush. With all that passed, after prayers I could not bear it: the sorrow rose in strength and melancholy overcame me, and I went up on the bed to rest. In the morning I got up to go to the boat in a black mood 59, and my face was no longer mine [1 Sam 1:18]. In truth, if I came to write the affairs of Abraham, a large volume would be needed for Abraham alone; but I spare the loss of time, and also so as not to write all his disgrace, and God knows that I am troubled even by this. But this I do to ease my sorrow, for "anxiety in a man's heart, let him speak it out" [Prov 12:25; b. Yoma 75a]. Therefore "with my staff I passed over" [Gen 32:11] "when Doeg came" 60 [Ps 52:2]; and may the good Lord pardon [2 Chr 30:18], may it be His will.

תמוז 25. ב' אחר עשרה שעות מהערב באנו לעיר מאקון. ומרוב בלבול הבאים כי רבים המה הי"ל צער ואני קדמתי ולקחתי בית א' בג' מיטות ואת רב"ע ויען המנהג כי המשרת אינו פורע כאדונו הייתי או' למשרתת שבעד המשרת אתן לו פחות. והיה שם המשרת וחרה אפו בי למה אני או' שהוא משרת שיבזוהו ויצא כמנהגו בחורי אף הלוך ודבר שהוא פ"א יפרע שאין רוצה לומר שהוא משרת. ואני קיימתי אל תען וסברתי כי ודאי בא יבא לשכב על מטתו ומה גם שלא אכל ועל השלחן היה מונח הפת וגבינה. ומ"מ לרוב הדחק והצער והבלבול והחולשא הי"ל צרה גדולה לראות חוצפתו ושאין אפי' יום א' שאינו מצער אותי ועבר חצות ואני ישנתי ובתוך ב' שעות נתעוררתי וראיתי נר של חלב אחז האור ברובו וקמתי ותקנתיו ויצאתי בפתח הבית לבקש אברהם ומצאתיו ישן ע"ג שלחן עגול מאחוריו וקראתיו שיבא לישן הוא ענה שכבר הוא חי חיים של צער בכל ולא שמע לקולי. והנר יען לא היה מספריים למחוט הפתילה הודלקה ונפלה על החלב וקמתי לתקנה ונכבית. וקראתי לאברהם שיבקש נר וקם בחרון והיה מדבר כמנהגו ולא הטיתי אזני לכל דברי חצפתו רק שמעתי שהיה או' בכלל דבריו הוא אינו נח ואינו נותן לי מנוחה ואהי כאיש אשר לא שומע והמתנתי שיהא יום להתפלל טרם נלך לספינה משום הציצית והתפילין ועמידה בתוך הגויים וערבוביא גדולה. ומכל העובר אחר תפלה לא יכולתי לסבול ובעוז עלה הצער והשחורה גברה עלי ועליתי על המטה לנוח ואקום בבקר ללכת לספינה משוחר"ר ופני לא היו לי. ובאמת אם באתי לכתוב עניני אברהם היה צריך קובץ גדול אצל אברהם ואני חס על אבוד זמן וגם שלא לכתוב כל גנותו וה' יודע כי צר לי אפי' בזה אבל זאת אעשה לפכוחי צערין ודאגה בלב איש ישיחנה לכן במקלי עברתי בבא דוא"ג וה' הטוב יכפר בעד[י] כי"ר.

166.2

In the day, while we were on the boat, many of the gentiles rose against me, for they had seen him sleeping on the ground outside, and such a thing is not done; also that he was eating bread and onions below. In truth I was ashamed, and I answered them that it was very hot and he could not lie inside, and the like.

וביום בהיותנו בספינה רבים קמים עלי מהגויים שראוהו ישן בארץ בחוץ וכן לא יעשה גם שהיה אוכל למטה פת ובצלים ובאמת בושתי והשבתים שהיה עושה חום גדול ולא יכול לשכב לפנים וכיוצא.

166.3

In the day we had a meal in the village of Reyssouze, and after that we came to the boat. Today it happened to us that the horses that draw the boat, with a rider on them: as we came to a post stage, the bank gave way from the force of the river's current, and the horses sank. The rider too was in danger, and the boat was about to sink. People came and saved the man and one horse, and the second horse drowned; they put on other horses, and we were saved with God's help. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His kindness endures forever ⚠61; may the Lord complete it for good, may it be His will. In the evening at five hours we came to the port of Lyon. Gentile women bring small boats 62 to take the people off. And behold, Signor Benjamin Naquet 63, Manasseh Bizes and Reuben Moses came in a barque that they had brought from the city; they came to the boat and received us with honor, and took us and our things in the barque. As we crossed, the customs officers came, and by the kindness of the Most High they opened the large trunk, a mere opening, and looked at nothing more. We came to the lodging that they had taken for us. Blessed be God the Savior, and His name is exalted above all blessing and praise [Neh 9:5] for ever and to all eternity. May He help me for the sake of His name always, may it be His will, and let us say amen.

וביום סעדנו בכפר ריאותיי ואח"ז באנו לספינה. והיום אירע לנו שהסוסים המנהיגים הספינה ורוכב עליהם כבואנו לפוסטא אחת נפתחה הארץ מתוקף מרוצת הנהר ונטבעו הסוסים וגם הרוכב היה מסוכן והספינה חשבה לטבוע ובאו אנשים והצילו האדם וסוס אחד וסוס ב' נטבע ונתנו סוסים אחרים וניצולנו בעה"י הלכ"ט כל"ה היל"ט כי"ר, ובערב בה' שעות באנו לנמל ליאון וגוייות מביאות ביצות קטנות לקחת הבני אדם והנה באו סי' בינימין נאקיט מנשה ביזיז ראובן משה בבארקו שהביאו מהעיר ובאו לספינה וקבלונו בכבוד ולקחונו לנו ולחפצים בבארקו ובעוברנו באו המוכסים ובחסד עליון פתחו הבהול גדול פתיחה בעלמא ולא ראו עוד שום דבר ובאנו למלון שלקחו לנו ברוך האל המושיע ומרומם שמו על p. 167כל ברכה ותהלה לעד ולנצח נצחים הוא יעדכ"ש תמיד כי"ר ונאמר אמן.

תמוז 26. ג' סי' אליא פירפינייאן וראו' משה ומנשה הנז' הביאו קארוסה והלכתי עמהם לסוחרי הגויים הגדולים שהבאתי להם ריקו' וקבלוני כלם בסבר פנים יפות וכבוד גדול ת"ל.

167.2

27 Tammuz [Wednesday 22 July 1778]: in the morning Signor Abraham Perpignan sent me a carriage, and I went to his house. His brother Signor Elia was there. I drank chocolate there; they honored me and invited me for the Sabbath, and I declined, for the place is far from my lodging.

תמוז 27. ד' בבקר שלח לי סי' אברהם פירפינייאן קארוסה והלכתי לביתו והיו שם אחיו סי' אליאו ושתיתי שם ג'יקולאטי ועשו לי כבוד והזמינוני לשבת וסרבתי כי המקום רחוק מבית אושפיזא שלי.

167.3

At midday a young man from Turin came to me, called by his surname Guastalla. The lad is a lad [1 Sam 1:24] who walks erect, with bared arm [Ezek 4:7], in embroidered clothes, his hair curled like a wig and dusted with powder [2 Sam 16:13], in the manner of the gentry; little Judaism and much lewdness, stingy for commandments and lavish for sins, scornful of those who study Torah, and with all his heart honoring women for sin; he gives his strength to women [Prov 31:3] and his money to gambling. The hour smiles on him [b. Berakhot 7b]. He came to me and told me: lately I was in Turin and they told me that you are coming, and that it would be better to send a small sum and not come, for the community is in utter poverty; and they answered that you would not agree. After that I asked about the customs, and he answered that it is a great risk: they search the person and his baggage and the coach in holes and cracks, and there is also a great risk for the books. He went away, and when I heard his words trembling seized me, for although I have neither a Gemara nor a Zohar with me, still I have many books. Many thoughts were knotted and tangled and caught in my brain; I was like a man without strength [Ps 88:5], melancholy rose very greatly, and I was very perplexed. May the Lord set me right with good counsel and save me from all loss and all sorrow; in Him my heart trusts and I am helped [Ps 28:7]. May He in His mercy have mercy on me according to all His righteousness, may it be His will.

ובחצות בא אצלי נער מטורין דקרו ליה בחניכתו וואצטאלא הנער נער הלך ילך בקומה זקופה וזרוע חשופה בבגדי רקמה מסולסל בשערו כמין פרוקה ועפר בעפר כמשפט השררות מעט יהדות ורוב זנות קמצן למצות ותרן לעבירות מזלזל לומדי תורה ובכל לבו מכבד הנשים לעבירה נותן לנשים אונו ולשחוק ממונו, שעה משחקת לו בא אצלי וא"ל מקרוב הייתי בטורין וא"ל שאתה בא וטוב היה לשלוח דבר מועט ולא תבא כי הקהילה בתכלית העוני והשיבו שלא תתרצה. ואח"ך שאלתי על המכס והשיב שהוא אחריות גדול ובודקין בגופו ובמשאו ובקרון בחורין ובסדקין וגם אחריות גדול על הס' והלך ואני כשמעי דבריו רעדה אחזתני כי הגם שלא יש בידי לא גמ' ולא זוהר מ"מ יש לי הרבה ספרים. וכמה מחשבות השתרגו ונסבכו ונאחזו במוחי הייתי כגבר אין איל ועלתה השחורה מאד מאד ואני נבוך מאד ה' יתקנני בעצה טובה ויצילני מכל הפסד ומכל צער בו בטח לבי ונעזרתי הוא ברחמיו ירחם עלי ככל צדקותיו כי"ר.

167.4

29 Tammuz [Friday 24 July 1778]: because of the great worry about the books, and the reports of the severity of the search and the scrutiny at the Turin customs, I said it is good to stay until the Ninth of Av has passed, and meanwhile we shall consider what to do. All the more since these days are hard for Israel, and the road is hard, a high mountain, and changes of kingdoms, from France to Savoy and from Savoy to Piedmont; and every delay is for the good. Since the slaughterers are suspected of drinking gentile wine and of their daughters, I did not want to eat meat on the Sabbath, and I bought fish. Reuben Moses invited me to eat at his house, for it is near; since he is a great miser I did not want to go, and I kept my Sabbath in the recesses of my house.

תמוז 29. הו' מרוב הצער של הספרים ושמועות מתוקף החיפוש והדקדוק במכס טורין אמרתי טוב להתעכב עד יעבר ט"ב וביני ביני נשכיל אשר נעשה ומה גם כי ימים אלו הם קשים לישראל והדרך קשה וגבה טורא וחילופי מלכיות מצרפת לסאבויא ומסאבויא לפיימונטי וכל עכבה לטובה. ויען כי השוחטים הם חשודים על סתם יינן ועל בנותיהן לא רציתי לאכול בשר בשבת וקניתי דגים. וראובן משה הזמינני לאכול בביתו כי קרוב הוא. ויען שהוא כילי גדול לא רציתי ללכת ושבת שלי בירכתי ביתי.

167.5

1 Av, Saturday [the Sabbath, 25 July 1778]: I went to the synagogue that was set up lately here in Lyon. There was no reading desk, only a bench on top of a large bench, and it vexed me; but since I do not speak in the synagogue, without vow, I said nothing. On the Sabbath and New Moon I was almost without food, only bread and vegetables, for Abraham in his folly spoiled and ruined the fish, both the boiled and the roasted. Only at the third meal did I eat a few anchovies (anchovas) ⚠65, and they were good. In the day the young man Signor Guastalla came and was with me alone for about two hours. I saw a change for the better in him, not as I had thought; he has good manners and spoke to me with great respect. He told me that he is going to Geneva and will come back in the middle of the week.

אב א' ז' יום ש"ק הלכתי לבית הכנסת שנקבע מקרוב פה ליאון ולא היה תיבה כי אם ספסל ע"ג ספסל גדול וחרה לי אך משום שאיני מדבר בבה"כ בל"נ לא דברתי מאומה. ויום ש"ק ור"ח כמעט הייתי בלא אכילה רק לחם וירק כי אברהם היה בשטותו וקלקל והשחית הדגים בין המבושלים בין הצלויים רק בס"ג אכלתי מעט אינ'ג'וב'אם והיו טובות, ביום בא הבחור סי' וואצטאלא והיה עמי ביחוד כמו שתי שעות והכרתי בו שינוי לשבח ולא כמו שעלתה על דעתי רק יש בו דרך ארץ ודיבר לי בכבוד גדול. וא"ל שהולך לג'יניברא ויבא באמצע השבוע.

167.6

4 Av, Tuesday of Devarim [28 July 1778]: there were with me the brothers Signor Perpignan, Aaron Reuel, Benjamin de Montilitz ⚠66 and his son-in-law Azriel Carmi, the husk of his brothers R. Solomon and R. Mordecai of Carpentras; for he cares only for the vanities of this world: gambling, "the order of Women", and "the order of Festivals" kept as weekdays in impurity. Lately he lost six thousand francs at play. His father-in-law came, who is very rich; his only daughter, an heiress and modest, is the wife of Azriel. I spoke to them about the reading desk, that it is not an honor for the Torah scroll, and I told the cantor, Signor Azariah Vidal 68: say a Mi she-Berakh that I donate six francs for the reading desk. After this those present made donations, and I told the cantor to bless Signor Manasseh de Bizes and Signor Reuben Moses, twelve francs each; so that about 120 francs were collected in a moment. In these days a spirit came over Abraham [Judg 6:34]; he was insolent to me and ran off as is his way, and I was worried lest he be lost, or lest it be heard of ⚠67 in these lands [Gen 26:3]. After they brought him back in the day, at night he did not sleep with us and went to another place. I was worried, thinking: may no mishap occur. I forced my inclination to bear it at least until we come to Turin. In the morning he came; I spoke to him and gave him six francs as a gift.

אב 4. ג' דברים. היו אצלי האחים סי' פירפינייאן ואהרן ראויל ובנימין דמונטיליץ וחתנו עזריאל כרמי קליפת אחיו ר' שלמה ור' מרדכי אשר בקארפינטראץ p. 168כי לא חפץ כי אם בהבלי העה"ז שחוק וסדר נשים וסדר מועד חולין בטומאה ומקרוב הפסיד ששת אלפים פראנקו בשחוק ובא חמיו שהוא עשיר גדול ובתו יחידה בת יורשת וצנועה היא אשת עזריאל. ואמרתי להם על התיבה שאינו כבוד לס"ת ואמרתי לחזן סי' עזריה וידאל תאמר מי שברך שאני מתנדב לתיבה ששה פראנקום ואח"ז התנדבו מי שהיו ואמרתי לחזן תברך לסי' מנשה די ביזיז וסי' ראובן משה כל א' י"ב פראנקום באופן שנתקבצו כמו ק"ך פראנקום ברגע. ובאלו הימים רוח לבשה את אברהם והעיז פניו נגדי וברח כמנהגו והייתי בצער שלא יאבד או לא ישמע בארצות האל. ואחר שהביאוהו ביום. הלילה לא לן אצלנו והלך למקום אחר. והייתי בצער לחשוב שלא תארע תקלה וכפיתי את יצרי לסבול עד שנבא לטורין לפחות ובבקר בא ודברתי לו ונתתי לו במתנה ששה פראנקום.

168.1

5 Av, Wednesday of Devarim [29 July 1778]: I wrote out from the ready material for Shem ha-Gedolim, part two. In the evening I felt myself hot, with much itching on my body. The brothers Signor Perpignan and Signor Manasseh de Bizes came and took me in a carriage for an outing. We crossed by the bridge 69 that was built a few years ago, for there is a fine place for walking, and people used to have to cross the river in small boats. A great gentile came, laid out the costs and made a fine bridge, and it was fixed for him that he takes two pennies from everyone who crosses, going and coming back. This comes to a sum of sixty thousand francs a year, because of the many who pass over it, and the gentile made a great profit. They took me there and we sat about two hours, and they gave me sorbet worth half a livre; may their reward be doubled from Heaven. When I came back I was unwell, and at night large spots came out all over my body, and above all the trouble of the itching, and I hardly slept at night.

6 Av, Thursday [30 July 1778]: the doctor came, a gentile, Monsieur Rast, the greatest of the doctors and of pleasant temper ⚠70. He told me that I have discomfort but there is nothing to worry about, for I have no fever and no headache; and he prescribed water of herb tisane for me to drink constantly.

7 Av, Friday [31 July 1778]: the doctor came and ordered a bloodletting, for the pain of the spots and itching had grown. I was bled, and after the bloodletting I was a little better, but after three hours the spots and itching came back. אב 7. הו' בא הרופא וסידר להקיז דם כי גדל הכאב בבהרות וחיכוך והקזתי דם ואחר ההקזה הייתי מעט טוב אבל אחר ג' שעות חזרו הבהרות והחיכוך. ונתחזקתי והלכתי ערבית לבה"כ גם ביום ש"ק ובמנחה אבל הייתי בצער. ולבי ירא וחרד על ט"ב בתוקף חום הזמן וחום גופי שאיני סובל מלבוש וכיסוי ביום ובלילה. ועכ"ז גמרתי להתענות כי ודאי לא היה סכנה רק צער.

9 Av, Sunday of Va-ethanan [2 August 1778]: I did not go to the synagogue, neither at night nor in the day, but lay on the bed; and blessed be the Lord that I did not suffer so much. 168.5

10 Av, Monday [3 August 1778]: I went with the bridegroom Azariah Vidal, the cantor, to the baths on the doctor's advice. There is a large building, all in rooms, and in each room something like one large cradle of tin ⚠71. They put a sheet over it, and a person sits reclining with legs stretched out. Above it are two taps, one of hot water and one of cold; they fill it, and he sits naked up to his neck in the lukewarm water, each according to his nature, and they stay there so for an hour or more. When the water has cooled, the vessel has a tap at its bottom to let out the water, and they pour in fresh water, hot or cold as he wishes. There one can call with a bell, and the servant comes; they warm sheets, clothes and towels for him, and he comes out and dries himself, all with cleanliness and speed. The water comes from the spring, and in another building they heat the water, and it comes to each vessel through the tap. There are about twelve places, all in rooms with windows. There is a small window between one room and the next, closed from both sides, so that if those sitting are friends, each opens from his side and they talk from room to room while they are in the water, and all with decency. For each time the fee is 30 sous, and six sous for the servant. When they come out there is a coffee house ready, and each one sends to bring coffee or lemonade and the like as he wishes; everything is arranged in order, cleanliness and completeness. I went morning and evening, and blessed be the Lord, I saw at once a change for the better; may the Lord complete it for good.

אב 10. ב' הלכתי עם הארום עזריה וידאל החזן לבאניוס ע"פ רופא ויש בית גדול חדרים חדרים ובכל חדר כמו עריסה אחת גדולה של טיניקי ונותנים עליה סדין ויושב האדם מוטה בפישוט רגלים. ועליו ב' ברזות א' מים חמים וא' צונן וממלאים אותה ויושב ערום עד צוארו בתוך המים פושרין כל א' לפי טבעו p. 169ונשארים שם כך שעה או יותר. וכשנתקררו המים יש בכלי בתחתיתו ברזא לשפוך מים ומטילים מים חדשים חמים או צונן כחפצו. ושם יש לקרות בקאנפאנא ובא המשרת ומחממין לו סדינין ובגדים ומטפחות ויוצא ומנגב עצמו והכל בנקיות וזריזות והמים באים מהמעין ובבית אחר מחממין המים ובא על כל כלי דרך הברזא. ויש כמו י"ב מקומות הכל בחדרים וחלונות. ויש חלון קטן בין חדר לחדר סגור מב' צדדין שאם הם אהובים היושבים פותחין כל א' מצידו ומדברים מחדר לחדר בעודם במים והכל בכבוד. ועל כל פעם שכרו ל' סולדם ולמשרת ששה סולדום ובצאתם יש בית קאפי מוכנת וכל אחד שולח להביא קאפי או לימונאדה וכיוצא כרצונו והכל מתוקן בסדר ונקיות ושלימות ואני הלכתי בבקר וערב. וברוך ה' ראיתי תכף שינוי לטובה ה' יגמור לטובה.

12 Av, Wednesday [5 August 1778]: on this day too I went to the baths morning and evening. But today I was weak and my constitution was not even, and I had to go to bed for some hours. May the Lord in His great mercy heal me completely for His service, blessed be He, may it be His will.

אב 12. ד' גם ביום זה הלכתי בבקר וערב לבאניוס. אך היום הזה הייתי חלוש ואין מזגי שוה והוצרכתי ללכת על המטה כמה שעות ה' ברוב רחמיו ירפאני רפואה שלימה לעבודתו יתברך כי"ר.

169.3

13 Av, Thursday [6 August 1778]: I went for a short walk in the square they call Bellecour, that is, "fine court". In the middle there is a large monument, and on it the figure of Louis XIV in metal, riding a horse. Below, on one side, a male figure that stands for the river Rhône, which is on that side, and opposite it on the other side a female figure, all in metal, standing for the river Saône. For so they call le Rhône in the masculine and la Saône in the feminine; the reason is that the Rhône is strong and mighty, while the Saône is gentle, and in the end it is swallowed by the Rhône. The Rhône runs to Avignon and the sea of Marseille and the sea of Toulon. Its beginning is from Geneva, coming out about one cubit wide; after that it is swallowed into the ground and not seen at all, and some miles before Lyon it comes out in strength and grows as it goes. The Saône runs as far as Paris ⚠72. In Bellecour there are two pools of water facing each other, and over them a monument and a bronze figure, and it spouts water very high. At the end of Bellecour is the river Rhône. Lately a philosopher named Perrache, by his skill and at his expense, drove the Rhône back four cubits and more along its whole length, and it became dry land, and houses are being built on it over the whole area; for he widened the place greatly, and in time it will be added like a city joined to Lyon.

אב 13. ה' הלכתי לטייל מעט ברחוב שקורין ביל קור ר"ל חצר טוב ובאמצע יש מצבה גדולה ועליו צורת לואים י"ד ממתכת רוכב על סוס ולמטה מצד א' צורת זכר הרומז על נהר רוזא שהוא מאותו הצד וכנגדו מצד אחר דמות נקבה הכל ממתכת רומז לנהר שונא. וכך קורין לי רוזא בלשון זכר. לה שונא בלשון נקבה. והטעם שרוזא הוא חזק ומתגבר ושונא הוא נוח וסופו נבלע ברוזא. ורוזא הולך עד אב'יניון וים מארסיליא ולים טולון ותחילתו מג'יניברא יוצא כמו אמה אחת ואח"כ נבלע בארץ ולא ניכר כלל וקודם ליאון איזה מיל יוצא בתגבורת ומתגבר והולך ושונא הולך עד פאריץ. ויש בביל קור שתי בריכות מים זו כנגד זו ועליו מצבה ודמות נחשת ומקלח מים גבוהה גבוהה. ובסוף ביל קור הוא נהר רוזא ומקרוב פלוסוף א' שמו פיראשו בחכמתו והוצאתו הבריח רוזא רחוק ד' אמות ויותר בכל האורך ונעשה יבשה ועליו נבנים בתים בכל השטח כי הרחיב המקום הרבה ולימים יתוסף כעיר א' שחוברה לליאון.

No one [else] was there; they asked in French, and I answered partly in French and partly in Italian, so that we understood each other; and the priest was pleased with my answers, as I heard. 1885 printing: 113

אב 14. יום הו' הידיד סי' ארייאני היה בקדחת שלישית והייתי בצער גדול וביום ה' הנז' בא הרופא מוסו ראסט והביא גלח גדול עמו והיה רשימה בידו כמו ד' שאלות לשאול אותי ולא נמצא שום אדם והיו שואלין בלשון צרפת ואני משיב קצת בצרפת וקצת בטאליאנו באופן שהיינו מבינים זה את זה והגלח היה לו נחת מתשובותי כפי הנשמע.

169.5

16 Av, Sunday of Ekev [9 August 1778]: we set out in peace in a carriage, and with us were Azariah Vidal and Elijah Moses as far as the village of La Verpillière. After five hours they went back with the carriage. I was distressed by the stinginess of Reuben Moses and Manasseh de Bizes: they and their companions had said that they would pay the doctor and the medicines, and afterwards they asked Signor Samson Franquet ⚠75, Moses S. ⚠76 and Moses de Alpuget to help them. It vexed me greatly, and I said I would pay everything myself and they should give each one back his money. Afterwards, since I had already left the city, I had to pass over it; but I spoke wonders with the young men mentioned above. In truth it did me harm, for after my escort mentioned above went off in peace I was melancholy and weeping from the illness. I prayed the afternoon prayer in the village of La Tour-du-Pin.

אב 16. יום א' עקב נסענו לש' בקארוסה והיו עמנו עזריה וידאל ואליהו משה עד כפר לה וירפיליירה. ואחר ה' שעות חזרו עם הקארוסה והי"ל צער מסיבת כיליות ראובן משה ומנשה די ביזיז שהם ודעמיהו אמרו שיפרעו הם לרופא ורפואות ואח"ך שאלו מסי' שמשון פראנקיטי ומשה ש' ומשה די אלפוג'יט שיסייעום וחרה לי מאד ואמרתי לפרוע אני הכל ויחזרו לכל אחד מעותיו. ואח"ך יען שכבר יצאתי מהעיר הוכרחתי להעביר על מדותי אך דברתי פלאות עם הבחורים הנז'. p. 170ובאמת עשה לי נזק שאחר שהלכו לש' בני לויה הנז' הייתי משוחרר ובוכה מהחולי. והתפללתי מנחה בכפר לה טורי די פאן.

17 Av, Monday [10 August 1778]: we passed Pont-de-Beauvoisin, where we had slept the night; it is the edge of France. In Lyon they said that they are very strict at the customs there; but blessed be the Lord, they said nothing to us except a mere question whether we had any merchandise. We answered no, and they let us pass. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His kindness endures forever. That same day, as we were climbing the mountain, a man and a boy from Turin met us and told me that my grandson had been left by my son in Turin ⚠77. I was very distressed, both over the matter of money and because I cannot bring him with me, and many thoughts troubled my mind. Also our friend Signor Ariani, may his Rock keep him, had the fever, and he is in the calèche, and I was very sorry; may the Lord heal him completely, may it be His will. About two hours [after midday] ⚠78 we came to the city of Chambéry, which is of Savoy. There Signor Ariani, may his Rock keep him, lay in bed to sweat; may the Lord heal him completely, may it be His will. In the morning we drank coffee in the town of Les Échelles ⚠79, the beginning of Savoy.

אב 17. ב'. עברנו פון בון ווזאן ששם ישננו בלילה והוא קצה צרפת והיו או' בליאון שהם מדקדקים מאד במכס וב"ה לא דברו אלינו כי אם שאלה בעלמא אם יש לנו איזה סחורה והשבנו לא והניחונו לעבור. הודו לה' כ"ט כל"ח. בו ביום ואנו עולים בהר פגעו בנו איש ונער מטורין וא"ל שנכדי הניחו בני בטורין והי"ל צער גדול אם על ענין מעות ואם שאין אני יכול להביאו עמי ורבות מחשבות עכרו דעתי. גם הידיד סי' ארייאני יצ"ו הי"ל הקדחת והוא בקאליסו וצר לי מאד ה' ירפאהו רפואה שלימה כי"ר. וקרוב לשתי שעות באנו לעיר ג'אמבארי והיא מסאבויא. ושם שכב על המטה סי' ארייאני יצ"ו להזיע ה' ירפאהו ר"ש כי"ר. ובבקר שתינו קאפי בעיר היגילא תחילת סאבויא.

170.2

I will tell what I heard in Lyon. The king of France is styled "King of France and Navarre". Navarre is a small, low duchy, and the kingdom of France is great and wide; so why do they mention Navarre more than the province of Languedoc and the like? But the matter is that the duke of Navarre had no sons, and he left the duchy to the king of France on condition that he mention Navarre. So too the duke of Dauphiné, which is near Lyon, a small duchy: its duke left it to the king of France on condition that his firstborn son be called Dauphin, so that his place would be remembered. So it is to this day: the heir to the throne, the eldest son of the king of France, is called Dauphin. Now "Monsieur" without a name is the elder brother of King Louis XVI, that is the Comte de Provence; his wife is "Madame" without a name, and his daughter ⚠80 "Mademoiselle" without a name.

ואגיד אשר שמעתי בליאון שמלך צרפת מתארים אותו מלך צרפת ונאוארה ונאווארה הוא דוקאטו קטן ושפל ומלכות צרפת הוא גדול ורחב ומדוע יזכירו נאווארה יותר מפרובינצא לאנגדוק וכיוצא. אך הענין שדוקי נאוארה לא הי"ל בנים והניח הדוקאטו למלך צרפת ע"מ שיזכיר נאוארה. וכן דוקי דופינה שהוא אצל ליאון הוא דוקאטו קטן אך דוקי שלו הניחו למלך צרפת על מנת שבן הבכור שלו יקראו דופין כדי שיזכר מקומו וכן הוא עד היום כי יורש עצר הבן הגדול של מלך צרפת קורין אותו דופין. ועתה מוסיו סתם הוא אח הגדול של המלך לואים 16 שהוא קונדי די פרובינצא ואשתו מאדאמה סתם ובתו מאדאמא זילא סתם.

170.5

20 Av, Thursday [13 August 1778]: we slept in the village of Lanslebourg, and in the morning we began to climb the mountain, I in a hand chair carried by two men and their relief. We were an hour on the climb, and two hours mostly level on top of the mountain, where there are houses, and two hours on crooked and twisting descents; after a little descent there is a bridge, which is the end of Savoy and the beginning of Piedmont. Savoy has more than a hundred villages, but almost all of them poor, and the villages ruined and desolate. There is much water, springs and very many rivers. The king sends thirty-six thousand bales of salt from Turin to Savoy, each of 175 pounds, for it has nearly half a million [people]. I sat calm and quiet in the chair with the umbrella in my hand, and Signor David and Abraham rode on the mules. We came to Novalesa, a village at the foot of the mountain. Abraham lost a trout 84 that I had bought for fifteen sous, and because I spoke to him and mocked him that he is always talking and forgets the world, he began to shout. I scolded him, and he went off, wanting to go back over the mountain. I do not know the measure of his folly, for whoever has not reached his level cannot know it; and I rebuked him with a ban that he come back. After this I went to the customs, and they did me great honor: they opened only the trunk, a mere opening, and closed it, and did me honor. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His kindness endures forever.

Notes and uncertain renderings

  • 1 מלא היסט כפול — The measure of m. Shabbat 13:4, מלא הסיט כפול, "the full width of a double sit" (the sit is the span between two spread fingers): a very small scroll. The Ḥida writes היסט for הסיט.
  • ⚠2 וטעם עצו — A play on b. Sukkah 35a, the tree "whose wood and fruit taste the same" (עץ שטעם עצו ופריו שוה), which is the etrog: here the wood "is equal to money". The ark was of a costly wood, perhaps citron wood.
  • 3 לנוע על העצים — The refrain of Jotham's parable, והלכתי לנוע על העצים: Abraham is always out and about, and gives himself airs. The trees need not be real ones.
  • 4 בבית הק' רט"ב — הק' = הקצין, the notable. Freimann: רט"ב = R. Tobias Boas, who is named in full in the next line.
  • ⚠5 והפמ"א סי' ע"ה מח"ב — *Peraḥ Mateh Aharon*, the responsa of R. Aaron ha-Kohen Peraḥiah of Salonika, part 2, no. 75. The title is read from the initials; the responsum was not checked.
  • ⚠6 ועיני ארבה — So printed ("eyes of locusts"); probably for ומיני ארבה, "kinds of locusts".
  • ⚠7 כמות י"ב ליטרין — Literally "the size of 12 litre (pounds)"; the measure is not clear.
  • ⚠8 שוים כמה מונילים — מונילים not identified; perhaps "millions" (מיליונים) or a coin. Left as "several".
  • 9 ר' משה קאן אשר במגנצא — Freimann: on R. Simeon Kann see van Zuiden's history of the Jews of The Hague, p. 37; on R. Moses Kann of Mainz see the Ḥida's *Penei David* (Livorno 1792), f. 13b.
  • ⚠10 ופיו מילא לדבר על ה' שלם — ה' שלם read as Ḥakham (or ha-Rav) Shalem, i.e. Solomon Shalem of Amsterdam, who is spoken of again on p. 158.
  • 11 ויאמר השמד — The end of Deut 33:27, ויגרש מפניך אויב ויאמר השמד: he will drive the sinners out as an enemy is driven out. The word is hashmed, "destroy", not shemad, persecution.
  • 12 רוח נכון חד"ש בקרבי — The marks on חד"ש point to the speaker himself: the Peri Ḥadash, R. Ḥezekiah da Silva, whose book has this word in its title.
  • 13 עלי (כתובה) [בתורה] — Freimann corrects the manuscript's כתובה to [בתורה]: Shalem also belittled the Ḥida's learning. The Ḥida answers in the next words that in this Shalem is right.
  • 14 פ"ק — Freimann: פרוטה קטנה, a small coin: the London congregation gave nothing.
  • ⚠15 רחב רחב — The word is doubled for force: "broadly, broadly", at great length.
  • 16 רוחו"ת מספרות — The two spirits that a pious man heard conversing in the graveyard (שתי רוחות שמספרות זו לזו). The marks show the second sense of רוחות, puffs of wind: the company puffed smoke and chattered.
  • ⚠17 הב"ח — Read as הבחור החשוב, "the worthy young man", as with Isaac Selig on p. 160.
  • ⚠18 זימירוז — Freimann points to p. 159, where the name is written out as זימיר הוזין; זימירוז here is the short form.
  • ⚠19 שני ריזאם משי — ריזאם is not identified, and Freimann has no note. Perhaps lengths of silk (cf. Spanish resma), or Indian quilts (Hindi razāī).
  • ⚠20 שמעון זימיר הוזין — The same man as זימירוז on p. 158; the second name is uncertain.
  • ⚠21 גירזוני — Name as printed; not identified.
  • 22 ר' חיים ברעסלא — Freimann: the father of the author of *Penei Aryeh* (R. Aryeh Leib Breslau, later rabbi of Rotterdam).
  • ⚠23 זה ת"ר שנה — So printed (600 years). Erasmus died in 1536, about 240 years before; the account the Ḥida heard is inaccurate also on his "new faith" and on the metal of the statue (bronze).
  • 24 ואמרתי מא' — מא' = מאמר, the saying. Freimann: Numbers Rabbah 17:2 and Kohelet Rabbah on Eccl 9:7, where the verse is applied to the portion of ḥallah.
  • 25 הרב — Freimann: R. Abraham son of R. Judah, rabbi of Rotterdam from 1754 to 1779; he cites Italie's history of the Rotterdam community, p. 37.
  • ⚠26 ר' יעקב דפריץ — דפריץ: read as de Vries (de Fries). The Ḥida writes a final s as ץ, as in פאריץ for Paris. Freimann has no note.
  • ⚠27 חתן נוח"ה — Unresolved abbreviation of the father-in-law's name.
  • 28 ומשם חזר להאג — "There" is Delft: the party escorted R. Moses Lehren as far as Delft and prayed there, and he went home to The Hague.
  • 29 בלו"ט — Freimann: apparently lotto (Italian) or lotería (Spanish).
  • 30 החלמיא"ת — Freimann: the first letters of Ps 138:8, ה' חסדך לעולם מעשי ידיך אל תרף.
  • ⚠31 מכים הק"ק — Printed מכים; read מכיס, "from the purse".
  • ⚠32 ר' משה פרענער — So printed; perhaps the same man as the parnas R. Moses Springer (שפריגיר / שפרינר) just mentioned.
  • ⚠33 תמוז ה'. יום ר"ח — The day of the month is not given. The first day of Rosh Ḥodesh Tammuz 5538 was 30 Sivan, a Thursday (ה'), and the next entry is 1 Tammuz, Friday; so ה' is the weekday.
  • ⚠34 וישט פרישלאנד ... הושט פרישלאנד ... גילידיר לאנד — The Ḥida's list of the seven provinces. Freimann: West Friesland, Utrecht, East Friesland, Overijssel, Gelderland. "West Friesland" is Friesland, and "East Friesland" stands for Groningen (Stad en Lande). OCR נילידיר corrected to גילידיר; the print is faint.
  • ⚠35 לפליפו תחלת בראואנט — Perhaps Fort Saint-Philippe (Sint-Filips) on the Scheldt below Antwerp, or a landing named after it; not certain.
  • ⚠36 הגלות שיש הוא מהיאודים כי אתם שונאי ישראל — Abraham's outburst is rendered literally; the sense is obscure.
  • ⚠37 מקום טו' — Freimann reads טו' as טומאה, an unclean place (a Christian city); the Ḥida's point is that such talk was dangerous there.
  • 38 ר' ליפמאן בר נתן הקצין — Freimann: Philipp Nathan, who in 1783 worked to obtain a burial ground for the Jews of Brussels; he cites the *Revue des études juives*, p. 269 ff.
  • ⚠39 אדוני חמור גדול — Read as an insult to the gentile, which explains the danger. The words can also be read "my master is a great ass", said of the Ḥida.
  • 40 עשיתי לו הזכרת אבות וגבורות וכל שום שיש לו — A play on the order of the blessings in m. Rosh Hashanah 4:5, אבות וגבורות וקדושת השם: the Ḥida abused him, his fathers and his feats, and called him every name.
  • ⚠41 כדרך המוהשקום הרעים — So printed, and the scan is clear. The word is not identified, and Freimann has no note; the ending is the Spanish plural. Transliterated.
  • ⚠42 החי"ראם — Freimann: jiram. Read as the Arabic ḥirām (iḥrām), a woollen blanket or wrap, a word that Judeo-Spanish also uses; the marks show a foreign word.
  • ⚠43 גם אור הו' ס"ל כתב אחר — ס"ל is not resolved; the sense seems to be that another letter came on Thursday night (the eve of Friday).
  • ⚠44 שעה ריפיטיסיון נשבר החבל — Freimann: שעון repeticion, a repeater watch. חבל, literally "rope", is read as its chain or cord.
  • 45 כמה אפים — Freimann: lápiz, a pencil. The print has אפים, for לאפים.
  • ⚠46 יר"א — An abbreviation of affection, read as ידידי ורעי אהובי or similar, "my beloved friend"; not certain.
  • 47 נימוקי רי"ד על החומש — Freimann: the Ḥida later printed these glosses of R. Isaiah di Trani in his *Penei David* (Livorno 1792).
  • ⚠48 שהוא פרופיסור מתועבה א' — Literally "who is a professor of an abomination": the Ḥida accuses Calmer of professing Christianity, going to church and attending Mass. Rendered literally.
  • 49 והוא פק"ר — פק"ר, from the first letters of the three names, is the root of הפקר and אפיקורוס: lawless men. In the same way the first letters of Amsterdam, London and Bordeaux give אבל, mourning.
  • ⚠50 ועשה מקח על הבגדים — בגדים, literally "clothes", is read here and in 18 Tammuz as the baggage (the trunks of clothes) to be carried by the diligence.
  • ⚠51 באביניון פישון — פישון is not explained: perhaps the wife's name, or a place or family name. Transliterated.
  • ⚠52 צרה שמה — Literally "her name is Tzarah": either her real name (Sarah, spelled as pronounced) or the Ḥida's pun on צרה, a rival wife.
  • 53 וידאל פיר אי פילס — Freimann: Vidal père et fils was apparently the name of the trading house, and the Ḥida took it for the man's own name.
  • ⚠54 פונטי סוברי ליאון — So printed: the Ḥida wrote ליאון, Lyon, where the river Yonne is meant. Freimann: Pont-sur-Yonne.
  • ⚠55 אוברי — Freimann: Houvrai. No place of that name lies on the road; Rouvray, the post stage between Vermenton and Arnay-le-Duc, fits.
  • 56 היינו טוב נפשות — Freimann: טו"ב is the number 17, seventeen souls. The word is printed without marks.
  • ⚠57 ואת רב"ע — The marks point to a verse: ומספר את רבע ישראל [Num 23:10], where Rashi explains רבע as lying down, and ארחי ורבעי [Ps 139:3]. Read as the lodging for the night; the sense is not certain.
  • ⚠58 שהוא פ"א יפרע — פ"א not resolved; perhaps פעם אחת ("for once" he would pay as a full guest) or a sum.
  • 59 משוחר"ר — The marks show two senses. משוחרר is the word for a freed slave; here it is "blackened", in the black mood (השחורה) of the line before. The servant plays the master, and the master is the "freedman". The word comes again, without marks, on p. 170.
  • 60 בבא דוא"ג — בבא דוא"ג is the heading of Ps 52, בבוא דואג האדומי. The marks show the two senses of דואג: Doeg the slanderer, for the Ḥida fears to speak evil of Abraham, and "one who worries", after the דאגה of the verse just quoted. So he passes over the matter quickly.
  • ⚠61 הלכ"ט כל"ה — Read as הודו לה' כי טוב כי לעולם חסדו; כל"ה is printed for the usual כל"ח (as on p. 170).
  • 62 ביצות קטנות — Freimann: small boats, as ביצית in b. Bava Batra 73a.
  • 63 בינימין נאקיט — Freimann: the syndic (shtadlan) of the Lyon community; he cites the *Jewish Encyclopedia*, vol. 8, p. 230.
  • ⚠64 ריקו' — Read as ריקומנדציוני, Italian raccomandazioni, letters of recommendation.
  • ⚠65 אינ'ג'וב'אם — אינ'ג'וב'אם: read as Spanish anchovas (Portuguese enchovas), anchovies, with the Spanish plural ending. Freimann has no note.
  • ⚠66 דמונטיליץ — De Montélis, an Avignon surname also written Montel and Monteux; Freimann gives de Monteaux. Benjamin's daughter Léa Montel married Azariel Crémieux at Carpentras on 23 March 1776 (J.-C. Cohen, Les communautés juives d'Avignon et du Comtat Venaissin, genealogy, p. 179): this is the son-in-law Azriel Carmi named here.
  • ⚠67 או לא ישמע — Read as או [ש]לא ישמע: lest the scandal be heard of in foreign parts.
  • 68 עזריה וידאל — Freimann: he was put to death in 1793 by the tribunal of the French Revolution; he cites the *Jewish Encyclopedia*, vol. 8, p. 230.
  • 69 בפונטי אשר עשו בקרב שנים — The wooden toll bridge over the Rhône that the architect Jean-Antoine Morand built in 1774, which led to the walks of Les Brotteaux.
  • ⚠70 ומזגו יפה — ג/נ unclear in the print (ומזנו); read as ומזגו, "his temper is pleasant".
  • ⚠71 עריסה אחת גדולה של טיניקי — The scan has טיניקי, not מיניקי: Turkish teneke, Judeo-Spanish tenekí, tin plate. A bathtub of tin. Freimann has no note.
  • ⚠72 ושונא הולך עד פאריץ — So in the text; in fact the Saône does not reach Paris.
  • ⚠73 וביום ה' הנז' — Read as "on the Thursday mentioned above" (13 Av), told under Friday's entry.
  • ⚠74 כמו ד' שאלות — So Freimann (ד'); the 1885 printing has כ', twenty.
  • ⚠75 שמשון פראנקיטי — Name given as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠76 ומשה ש' — Family name abbreviated; not resolved.
  • ⚠77 שנכדי הניחו בני בטורין — Read as "my son left my grandson in Turin"; the order of the words allows also "that my son's grandson...". Uncertain who is meant.
  • ⚠78 וקרוב לשתי שעות — "About two hours" with no reference point; probably two hours after midday.
  • ⚠79 היגילא — Read as [Les] Échelles, the first town of Savoy after Pont-de-Beauvoisin, where they drank coffee in the morning, before Chambéry; ג stands for the French ch, as in ג'אמבארי. Freimann: Aiguebelle; but that town lies beyond Chambéry, and it is the דיגיביל of 18 Av.
  • ⚠80 ובתו מאדאמא זילא סתם — So in the text; the Comte de Provence had no daughter. "Mademoiselle" was then the title of the king's eldest daughter.
  • ⚠81 ליל ג' — The night before Tuesday 18 Av (Tuesday 11 August 1778).
  • ⚠82 לאניזא — Given as printed; Freimann has no note. Perhaps Planaise, by the post stage of Maltaverne between Montmélian and Aiguebelle.
  • 83 דיגיביל — D'Aiguebelle. Freimann puts his note "Aiguebelle" on היגילא in 17 Av.
  • 84 טרוטא — Italian trota, a trout.
