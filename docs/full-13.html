116.1

18 Ḥeshvan [Tuesday 18 November 1777]: in the morning R. David Attias came to visit and said to me tasteless words, and I was looking for a pretext to speak up against him ⚠1. Signor Israel mentioned above came, and I spoke harshly; he stepped in to plead for the gevir mentioned above, and my anger was kindled that day. I said to him: what business have you to get into this? and other words that followed. Signor Israel was angry enough to die [Jonah 4:9] and wanted to leave; I rebuked him so that he stayed. R. David went away, and afterwards I told Signor Israel that I am his friend and that I had done it only "as in former times in Israel" [Ruth 4:7] ⚠2, and I appeased him. After this R. David sent a large almond cake, and I sent it back.

חשון 18. ג' בבקר בא לבקר ר' דוד עטיאס וא"ל דברים מחוסרי תבלין והייתי מבקש עילה להתרים נגדו ובא סי' ישראל הנז' ודברתי קשות והוא נכנס להליץ בעד הגביר הנז' וחרה אפי ביום ההוא. וא"ל מה אתה ליכנס בזה וכיוצא דברים הנמשכים. וסי' ישראל חרה לו עד מות והיה רוצה ללכת וגערתיו שישב והלך ר' דוד ואח"כ אמרתי לסי' ישר' כי אני איש שלומו ולא עשיתי אלא לפנים בישראל ופייסתיו. אח"ז ר' דוד שלח עוגה משקדים גדולה והחזרתיה לאחור.

116.2

19 Ḥeshvan [Wednesday 19 November 1777]: the brother-in-law of Abraham Gradis, Mosinio ⚠3, spoke to Signor Alexandre and the leaders of the council, that they should do my wish in the name of the gevir his brother-in-law; and I knew that several of them agreed. But the scoundrel, the warden Solomon Lopes, whose wife is unclean and does not immerse ⚠4, and he is busy with the chapter "Eight Creeping Things", children of a menstruant ⚠5, so that he is liable to several thousand penalties of karet for every time he lies with the menstruant, continually; and he does not believe in the words of the Sages ⚠6 at all. He answered that a thing that has been written down and made a deliberation (deliberación) cannot be reversed. He is also a scoundrel, son of a scoundrel, for his father is a villain; and if villainy were forgotten from the world, he would restore it by his foolishness [cf. BM 85b]. All of Wednesday and the night of Thursday I remained in very great, writhing distress.

חשון 19. ד' גיסו של אברהם גראדיץ מוסיניאו דיבר לסי' אליחאנדרו וגדולי הועד שיעשו רצוני בשם הגביר גיסו וידעתי שכמה מהם הודו והבליעל הגבאי שלמה לופיס אשר טימיה אשתו לא טבלה והוא עסוק בפרק שמונה שרצים בני נדה ונמצא שהוא חייב כמה אלפים כריתות על כל ביאה שבא על הנדה תדיר וגם אינו מאמין ברו"ל כלל. הוא ענה שדבר שנכתב ונעשה דיליביראסיון אין להשיב. וגם הוא בן בליעל אביו שהוא נבל ואי משתכח הנבלות מן העולם הוא מחזירו בטפשותו ונשארתי כל יום ד' וליל ה' בצער מתחולל מאד מאד.

116.3

20 Ḥeshvan [Thursday 20 November 1777]: on the night of Thursday [the evening of 19 November], in my distress, I made a dream question about this matter, and on Thursday morning I rose with the verse "For the Lord shall be your confidence, and shall keep your foot from being caught" [Prov 3:26]. May it be His will. In the day Isaac Lange came, and I showed him a very angry face; he sat a little and left. R. David came with the warden Peixotto and brought two livres from the charity box, because Ḥ. Bagaio had taken a large advance from the share of the rest of the Land of Israel, and this is what was left, and that I should sign for it. I would neither take it nor sign.

חשון 20. ליל ה' בצערי עשיתי שאלת חלום בענין זה ויום ה' בבקר קמתי בפ' כי ה' יהיה בכסלך ושמר רגלך מלכד. כי"ר. ביום בא יצחק לאנג'ה והראיתי לו פנים זועפות מאד וישב מעט והלך. ובא ר' דוד עם גבאי פינ'וטו והביא שני ליטרין מהקופה כי ח' באגאייו הוא לקח מוקדם הרבה מחלק שאר א"י וזהו הנשאר ושאני אחתום בזה ולא רציתי לקחת ולא לחתום.

116.4

23 Ḥeshvan, Sunday of Toledot [23 November 1777]: Signor Israel came and told what people are saying: that if I do not take the sum they pledged, they will make an agreement not to give any more to any sage, and the like. Also in a letter from Signor Shealtiel came to me what he had heard from a certain gevir, etc. I was very distressed, and I accepted it all as atonement for my sins and agreed to give in to them for the honor of God. The ḥakham R. M. Tamah ⚠7 and Signor Israel got up and went to Signor Rodrigues to tell him that I agree. He came to me, and I spoke harshly to him by hints, and in the end I told him that I agree to whatever he does. On Saturday night a letter came to me from my beloved Signor Raphael, may his Rock keep him, saying that inside the letter of 5 Tishri he had received a billet (billete) from the fruits of Rosh Hashanah, and ⚠8 wrote two lines there. These are hard and bitter words, for I sent the letter by the post, and he had the effrontery to say there was a billet in it. And what have the fruits of Rosh Hashanah to do with it? See how far his insolence, wickedness and folly go! May the Holy One, blessed be He, grant me wisdom and knowledge and keep me from those who do harm, for truly I suffered much from every side. May He in His mercy have pity on me in His great mercy; may it be His will. During the week I visited some Sephardi gevirim and the wardens and spoke gently, and they received me with a friendly face; they came to me, and the warden too, several times.

חשון 23. א' תולדות בא סי' ישר' וסיפר מה שאומרים שאם אני לא אקח הסך שהתנדבו שיעשו הסכמה שלא יתנו עוד לשום חכם וכיוצא וגם במכתב מסי' שאלתיאל בא לי מה ששמע מגביר א' וכו'. ואני הייתי מצר מאד וקבלתי הכל לכפרת עונותי והסכמתי להכנע להם לכבוד ה'. וקמו הרמת"י וסי' ישר' והלכו לסי' רודריגיז לו' לו שאני מתרצה ובא הנז' אצלי ודברתי לו קשות ברמז וסוף דבר א"ל שאני מתרצה בכל מה שיעשה. ובליל מש"ק בא לי כתב מחמודי סי' רפאל יצ"ו שאומר שבתוך הכתב מה' תשרי קבל בלייטו מפירות ר"ה ואנ"י כתב שתי שורות שם: והם דברים קשים ומרים כי אני שלחתי הכתב לפוסטא והוא העיז פניו לשים שם בלייטי. ומה ענין פירות ר"ה. ותא חזי עד היכן עזותו ורשעתו ושטותו. הקב"ה יחונני חכמה ודעת וישמרני מן המזיקים כי באמת מכל הצדדין נצטערתי מאד. הוא ברחמיו ירחם עלי ברב רחמיו כי"ר. ובמשך השבוע ביקרתי לאיזה גבירים ספרדים ולפרנסים והייתי מדבר רכות וקבלוני בסבר פנים יפות והם באו אצלי וגם הגבאי פעמים.

116.5

26 Ḥeshvan [Wednesday 26 November 1777]: at midday I was at a meal in the house of my beloved Signor Israel Astruc, and from there we went to the seven blessings of the son of David Lamira ⚠9 with the stepdaughter of Samuel Pereyra. Most of the guests were gentile men and women of the great ones of the city.

חשון 26. ד' הייתי בחצות לסעוד בבית אהובי סי' ישראל אסתרוק ומשם p. 117 נסענו לז' ברכות של בן דוד לאמירה עם חורגת שמואל פירירא ורוב הנועדים היו גויים וגוייות מגדולי העיר.

117.1

27 Ḥeshvan [Thursday 27 November 1777]: I went to the exchange (bolsa) with the ḥakham R. M. Tamah and saw shops decorated with many kinds of fine things. In the evening I went with Solomon Foa to the son of Jacob Raphael, who had lain with a noble (noble) gentile woman, and she bore this son; they sent him to Amsterdam and circumcised him. He is a great philosopher and asked me many things on matters of religion and many philosophical questions, and I answered everything.

חשון 27. ה' הלכתי לבולסה עם חרמת"י וראיתי חנויות מעוטרות בכמה מיני ענינים יפים. ובערב הלכתי עם שלמה פואה לבן יעקב רפאל שבא על גוייה נוב'לי וילדה בן זה ושלחו לאמשט' ומלוהו והוא פילוסוף גדול והיה שואלני כמה דברים מענייני הדת וכמה חקירות והייתי משיב על הכל.

117.2

28 Ḥeshvan, Friday [28 November 1777]: in the morning after prayers I went to the warden and took the money they had pledged. From there I went to Samuel Astruc and took a hundred escudos that he held from the yeshivah ⚠10. After that I went to the Peixottos, and there were Signor da Vina and Signor Baiz ⚠11, the officers of the yeshivah mentioned above, and I took the money they held. In the evening the ḥakham R. M. Tamah came and told me that Signor David Ariani had made up his mind to go back home. I said many good words to him until he agreed; the sheet is too short to hold this matter. May God give me good understanding and set me right in everything with good counsel. Afterwards Signor Isaac Alexandre came and brought me a recommendation (recomendación) and three louis as a gift.

חשון 28. יום הו' בבקר אחר תפלה הלכתי לגבאי ולקחתי המעות שהתנדבו ומשם הלכתי לשמואל אסתרוק ולקחתי ק' איש' שהיו בידו מישי' כ"י ואח"ך הלכתי אצל הפינוטוס ושם סי' דא ויינא וסי' באיסה פקידי הישיבה הנז' ולקחתי המעות שהיו בידם. ובערב בא חרמת"י וא"ל שסי' דוד אריאני גמר בדעתו לחזור לביתו. ודברתי לו כמה דברים טובים עד שנתרצה וקצרה היריעה מהכיל ענין זה. ה' יתן לי שכל טוב ויתקנני בכל בעצה טובה. ואח"כ בא סי' יצחק אליחאנדרו והביא לי ריקו' וג' לואים נדבה.

117.3

29 Ḥeshvan, Sabbath [29 November 1777]: on the night of the holy Sabbath [the evening of 28 November] I prayed the evening prayer in the holy congregation Ateret Zeqenim, called Kahal de Payim ⚠12. In the day, after prayers, the Avignonese came to entreat me to speak to Signor Shalom, who had been warden and had withdrawn six months ago because of a grievance; and Moses and Solomon Petit kept it up ⚠13. In the evening he came to me, and although I was melancholy and somewhat ill, I restrained myself and spoke to him until he agreed to go back to being warden, and everyone rejoiced.

חשון 29. ז' ליל שבת קדש התפללתי ערבית בק"ק עטרת זקנים הנק' קהל די פאיים. וביום אחר תפלה באו האביניוניזים לחלות שאדבר לסי' שלום שהיה גבאי וסילק עצמו זה ו' חדשים משום קפידא והיו מחזיקים משה ושלמה פיטיט ובערב בא אצלי והגם שהייתי בשחורה וקצת חולה התאפקתי ודברתי לו עד שנתרצה לחזור להיות גבאי ושמחו הכל.

117.4

Kislev, Rosh Ḥodesh, Sunday of Vayetze [30 Ḥeshvan, 30 November 1777]: in the morning I drank chocolate at the house of Signor Judah and Ḥayyim, sons of Mordecai, called Lange; at midday I ate with our beloved, the ḥakham R. M. Tamah, may his light shine, who has shown wonderful kindness and has taken trouble for me with his person and his money.

כסלו ר"ח. א' ויצא. בבקר שתיתי ג'יקאלאטי אצל סי' יאודה וחיים בר מרדכי הנק' לאנג", ובחצות סעדתי אצל אהובינו חר"ם תמ"ה נר"ו אשר הפליא חסדיו וטרח עמי בגופו וממונו.

117.5

1 Kislev, Rosh Ḥodesh, Monday [1 December 1777]: at night [the evening of 30 November] I went to Isaac Lange and found him saying grace after meals, "whose gift is small and whose shame is great" ⚠14. I went in; he put out his hand and gave me two escudos, which are 12 lire, and I gave them back. He told me he wanted to sign for the subscriptions of the yeshivah, and got up to take a pen. I said to him: how much will you sign? He said: 6 livres a year. I did not accept, because he had formerly signed for 50 livres a year. His wife began to say that her husband is not rich, and the like. I got up to go; he brought me back and said he would sign for ten. I did not accept; I got up and came home. He came after me, and I told him that I do not want him to sign, and that he and his wife had slighted me. In the end he signed for twenty, and I made him warden for the yeshivah, and with him Signor Judah Segre, Signor Caleb Fonseca and Signor David Astruc. Signor Caleb brought his wife for me to make for her an annulment of curses, and so I did.

כסלו 1. ר"ח ב'. בלילה הלכתי אצל יצחק לאנג'ה ומצאתיו שהיה אומר ברכת מזון שמתנתם מעוטה וחרפתם מרובה ונכנסתי ופשט ידו ונתן לי שני אס' שהם 12 ליראס והחזרתים וא"ל שרוצה לחתום בחתימות הישיבה וקם ליקח קנה א"ל כמה תחתום א"ל 6 ליטרין לשנה ולא רציתי כי הוא חתם מקדם 50 ליטרין לשנה. ואשתו התחילה לומר שבעלה אינו עשיר וכיוצא וקמתי ללכת והחזירני ואמר שיחתום עשרה ולא רציתי וקמתי ובאתי לבית ובא אחרי וא"ל שאיני רוצה שיחתום ושהוא ואשתו זלזלו בי וסוף דבר חתם עשרים ועשיתיו גבאי לישיבה ועמו סי' יאודה סגרי וסי' כלב פונסיקא וסי' דוד אסטרוג. וסי' כלב הביא אשתו שאעשה לה התרת קללות וכן עשיתי.

117.6

2 Kislev [Tuesday 2 December 1777]: on the night of Tuesday [the evening of 1 December] Signor Solomon and Moses Petit came, and I began to speak to them about peace. Signor Solomon agreed to come to the synagogue, and I asked his forgiveness on behalf of Jacob Naquet; he did me honor and forgave him. I was ready to set out in the morning, but all night the rains were heavy. Moreover, later that night Signor Israel brought me a piece of canvas (canavaccio) from India as a gift, and that is a danger at the customs. After prayers, since it was a hard day of rain, the master of the house, the gevir Signor Daniel, told me not to travel on so hard a day, and because of the cloth mentioned above he told me to stay until next Sunday, may it come upon us for peace. I trusted that it would be for my good, for every delay is for the good. May God Almighty bless us with His blessings and give us success in Torah and in the commandments; wherever I turn may I prosper, and whatever I do may I succeed; may it be His will.

כסלו 2. ליל ג' בא סי' שלמה ומשה פטיט והתחלתי לדבר להם על השלום וקבל סי' שלמה לבא לבה"כ ושאלתי ממנו מחילה בעד יעקב נאקיט ועשה לי כבוד ומחל לו. והייתי מוכן ליסע בבקר אך כל הלילה רבו הגשמים. ועוד שאח"כ בלילה הביא לי סי' ישראל פייסא קאנאבאסו מהינדיא מתנה והוא סכנה במכס. ואחר תפלה שהיה יום קשה מגשמים א"ל הבעה"ב הגביר סי' דניאל שלא אסע ביום קשה כזה ומפני הבגד הנז' א"ל לישב עד יום א' הבא עלינו לש' בטחתי שיהיה לטובתי כי כל עכבה לטובה ואל שדי יברכנו מברכותיו ויצליחנו בתורה p. 118 ובמצות וכל אפנה אשכיל וכל אשר אעשה אצליח כי"ר.

118.1

6 Kislev, Sabbath [6 December 1777]: on the holy Sabbath in the morning Signor Solomon and Moses Petit came. I went up to the reader's desk with Signor Jacob Naquet and said the words of apology that he was to say; after that I blessed the whole holy congregation. After the afternoon prayer, in the yeshivah of Signor Daniel, I preached for about half an hour on peace, with words of grace for the gevir Signor Daniel, and I spoke well of the ḥakham R. M. Tamah.

כסלו 6. ז'. יום ש"ק בבקר באו סי' שלמה ומשה פיטיט ועליתי לתיבה עם סי' יעקב נאקיט ואמרתי לשון המחילה שיאמר, ואח"ך בירכתי לכל הק"ק ואחר המנחה בישיבת סי' דניאל דרשתי כחצי שעה על הש' וחינות לגביר סי' דניאל ודברתי טוב על ההרמת"י.

118.2

7 Kislev, Sunday of Vayishlaḥ [7 December 1777]: I set out in peace, and all the Avignonese, nearly every one of them, escorted me. We crossed one river, and they came to the inn, where most of them stayed. After that there escorted me until midday the ḥakham R. M. Tamah, may his Rock keep him, Signor Israel Astruc, David and Elijah, four more Astruc young men, Signor Moses Carcassonne, three Lange sons, Jacob Naquet, two Segres, Benjamin Ab. ⚠15 and Signor Todros: seventeen men in all. At midday we ate at a gentile inn in the village of Carbon-Blanc.

כסלו 7. א' וישלח נסעתי לש' וליווני כל האביניוניזים רובם ככלם ועברנו נהר א' ובאו לאושפיזא אשר שם רובם ואח"כ ליווני עד חצות חר"ם תמה יצ"ו וסי' ישר' אסתרוק ודוד ואליה ועוד ד' בחורים אסתרוק וסי' משה קרקשונה וג' בני לאנג'ה ויעקב נאקיט וב' סגרי ובנימין אב' וסי' טודרוס ס"ה טו"ב אנשים ואכלנו בחצות באש"ג בכפר קארבון בלאן.

118.3

After that Signor Israel, Carcassonne, the four Astruc young men, one Lange and one Segre went back, and the rest stayed. We crossed the second river; blessed be the Lord that it was a fine day and the rivers were calm, and we crossed at ease, seated, in a very short time. After that we came to the village of Saint-André-de-Cubzac; the gentile inn was good and wide, and they took ten beds, and they ate and drank in love. There Signor Segre took it upon himself not to drink gentile wine, and Signor David and Elijah Astruc and Signor Lange took it upon themselves to keep the three Sabbath meals. We rejoiced with them. They spent a great deal; they also bought shoes for Abraham. May the Lord repay their deeds.

ואח"ך הלך סי' ישר' וקרקשונא וד' בחורי אסתרוק וא' לאנג'ה וא' סגרי והשאר נשארו ועברנו הנהר שני וברוך ה' שהיה יום טוב והנהרות שקטים ועברנו בנחת בישוב כמעט קט זמן ואח"כ באנו לכפר סאנט אנדרי די קוזאק והאש"ג טובה ורחבה ולקחו עשרה מטות ואכלו ושתו באהבה. ושם קבל סי' סגרי שלא לשתות סתם יינם וסי' דוד ואליה אסתרוק וסי' לאנג'א קבלו לעשות ג' סעודות ושמחנו עמהם והוציאו הרבה גם קנו מנעלים לאברהם ישלם ה' פעלם.

118.4

8 Kislev [Monday 8 December 1777]: there, at the gentile inn, we slept in joy and love; in the morning we went on our way in peace. At midday we were at the gentile inn of Pelatzut ⚠16, a small village, and they were wicked.

כסלו 8. ב' שם באש"ג ישננו בשמחה ואהבה ובבקר נסענו לדרכנו לש' ובחצות היינו באש"ג פלצות כפר קטן והיו רשעים.

118.5

118.7

11 Kislev [Thursday 11 December 1777]: at night [the evening of 10 December] in the town of Ruffec. We set out before daybreak. In this town there is a customs post, and, blessed be God, we came in in peace and went out in peace, and no one said a word. But the day was a hard day of ice and great cold, and I suffered great distress almost all day. At midday, in the town of Chaunay, the chain of my watch (orologio) broke, and I was distressed. May it be His will that it be an atonement for my sins; may it be His will.

כסלו 11. ה. בלילה בעיר רופיק. ונסענו קודם היום. ובעיר זו יש מכס וב"ה נכנסנו לש' ויצאנו לש' ואין דובר דבר. אך היום היה יום קשה בקרח וקור גדול וסבלתי צער גדול כמעט כל היום ובחצות בעיר שוניי נשברה חבל הרילוג' שלי והי"ל צער יה"ר שיהיה לכפרת עונותי כי"ר.

118.8

12 Kislev [Friday 12 December 1777]: at night [the evening of 11 December] we were in the town of Vivonne, and by day, at midday, we came to the city, great and old, of which it is written in their books that Alexander of Macedon came to it and conquered it. They call it Poitiers, and in ancient times they called it Gula. We came to a large gentile inn. The wicked mistress of the inn, with pride and contempt, asked 26 lire. I began to speak gently, and she grew angry and told me to go. I said to the coachman (vetturino): let us go to another inn. He answered that he knows they are all wicked and want a lot of money. In the end, after much pleading, she agreed to 14 lire. An hour later two Jews came, Signor Abraham Ashkenazi and Signor Reuben, who had been with me in Toulouse. I was glad; they told me that had they known, they would have taken us to their rooms, but now nothing can be done after the fact. Some more boys came, and while we were at the end of the evening prayer two boys arrived, and I said Kaddish and Barekhu.

כסלו 12. ו' בלילה היינו בעיר ביבון וביום בחצות באנו לעיר הגדולה וישנה שכתוב בספריהם שבא אליה אלכסנדרוס מוקדון וכבשה. וקורין אותה פותיי ומקדם היו קורין אותה גולא. ובאנו לאש"ג גדולה. ובעלת האוש' המרשעת בגאוה ובוז שאלה 26 ליט' והתחלתי לדבר רכות ונתכעסה ואמרה שאלך. אמרתי לויטוריני נלכה נא לאוש' אחרת והשיב כי מכיר שכלם רשעים ורוצים הרבה מעות וסוף דבר בהפצרה נתרצית בליט' 14. לאחר שעה באו ב' יאודים סי' אב' אשכנזי וסי' ראובן שנמצאו עמי בטולוסא ושמחתי וא"ל שאם היו יודעים היו מוליכים אותנו לחדרם ועתה אין אחר מעשה כלום ועוד באו איזה נערים ובעוד שהיינו בסוף ערבית היו באים ב' נערים ואמרתי קדיש ברכו.

We prayed the afternoon prayer and the evening prayer of Saturday night with a minyan, blessed be God.

14 Kislev, Sunday of Vayeshev [14 December 1777]: today we set out, and at midday we came to a gentile inn in the town of Châtellerault. There are factories (fábricas) of iron goods there, and when a traveler passes, many gentile girls stand before him, one with a razor and one with scissors and the like, in case he wants to buy.

15 Kislev [Monday 15 December 1777]: at night [the evening of 14 December] we were at the gentile inn of Port-de-Piles. There is a customs post there, and, praise God, they did not search us. The innkeeper, an evil-doer, spoke harshly to us and took a lot. By day we were at a gentile inn in the town of Dibona ⚠17. The season is one of fierce cold, and I had distress on the road, for one must start early and stop late, since the day is short; it is the height of winter, and in these towns the cold is great. Praise God, on most days the sun shone, and with the light ⚠18 [it was bearable]; but the great cold prevails over the sun.

18 Kislev [Thursday 18 December 1777]: at night [the evening of 17 December], until three hours [into the night], when we reached the great and ancient city called Orléans. In former times there were Jews in it, and one of our masters the Tosafists, R. Y. of Orléans. In its streets there are lanterns, and they can be seen two hours' distance away. We arrived there tired and weary. The innkeeper fell upon us and abused us, asking a lot, since all the merchants pay so; I begged him until he took off a little from what he asked. When we left the inn in the morning, we rode in the carriage and went along a straight road for nearly an hour inside the city, and that day we traveled without a halt.

119.6

19 Kislev, Friday [19 December 1777]: at nightfall [the evening of 18 December] we reached the town of Toury, and there too the wicked mistress of the inn fell upon us and took more than the inn at Orléans. We rode out before daybreak; the world was full of snow and the cold was very great indeed. The iron shoes ⚠19 of our mules' hooves broke, and smiths happened to be there and mended them. We were impatient with the cold, and how great the distress for the servant Abraham, who was going on foot! One hour after midday we reached the town of Étampes and lodged there in a large inn where the people of the diligence (diligenza) lodge.

כסלו 19. הו' הגענו בתחילת הלילה בעיר טורי וגם שם בעלת האושפיזא המרשעת התגוללה ולקחה יותר מאושפיזת אורליאנס. ורכבנו קודם היום והעולם מלא שלג וקור גדול יתר מאד ונשברו פרזלי פרסות פרדינו ונזדמנו שם חרשי ברזל ותיקנום, ותקצר נפשנו בקור וכמו הצער על המשרת אברהם שהיה הולך ברגליו. ושעה אחת אחר חצות הגענו לעיר איתאמפא וחנינו שם באושפיזא גדולה אשר חונים אנשי הדילחינסיא.

119.8

21 Kislev, Sunday of Miqetz [21 December 1777]: we set out after prayers and reached in the evening the town of Longjumeau. There was a fair in the town, and at two inns they would not take us; and at the third, with pride and contempt, he gave us ⚠20 a small, ugly room and asked a high price, and we were forced to give what he asked.

כסלו 21. א' מקץ נסענו אחר תפלה והגענו בערב לעיר לוגומו והיה יריד בעיר ובשתי אושפיזות לא רצו לקבלנו ואל השלשה בגאוה ובוז נתן לנו בית קטן ומכוער ושאל מחיר רב והוכרחנו לתת שאלתו.

119.9

22 Kislev [Monday 22 December 1777]: in the morning we set out for Paris, and my heart feared and trembled for the clothes, for there ⚠21 they search and pry, from a thread to a shoelace [Gen 14:23], lest, God forbid, they fall upon us; and even if it were only the clothes thrown into disorder, small things are apt to be stolen or to be scattered ⚠22, and the like. We came at midday. The guards did us great honor; then he took the key and opened a few of the cases ⚠23: a mere look over the top of everything. The large trunk ⚠24 was not opened. Afterwards I gave him money, and he would not take it. We took a carriage (carrozza) and came to the house of the noble Signor David Naquet. He was not at home, and his wife, a woman of valor, received us with joy; afterwards Signor David came, and I went with him to two rooms that he had rented for me. After that the gevirim came to visit. May the Lord repay their deeds with sons who are a support and with long life, wealth and honor, for ever, selah, and ever. This city of Paris is the metropolis of France, a great city; they say it is 46 ⚠25 miles round. The markets and streets are wide, so that two carriages pass with room to spare while there are people at the sides. The river Seine is in it, and it draws its water from it. There is a wide and long bridge they call Pont-Neuf, that is, Ponte Nuovo, and feet never cease from this bridge all day and all night; and there is the clock (orologio) of the Samaritaine, around which is the water. They say that there is not a moment in the twenty-four hours of day and night when there is not on this bridge a white horse, a priest and a harlot. The city is beautiful and everything is found in it, only everything is dear, but harlotry is cheap and open: they say there are thirty thousand registered prostitutes written in a ledger, besides tens of thousands who are not open to every passer-by. There are many academies in it and factories of every kind. The Jews live in quiet; there are many Ashkenazim and many Portuguese from Bordeaux and Bayonne, and many Avignonese. There are congregations that pray every Sabbath, but there is no [established] community in it; most of them are guests, merchants there. Nor are the synagogues by privilege (privilegio), only by a miracle ⚠26. 1885 printing: 55

9 Tevet, the night of Thursday [the evening of Wednesday 7 January 1778]: the Marquis de Thomé came with the Marquise de Croix. She sat with me and asked me to pray for her. Then she said that she studies the Bible, and that she has seen angels and spirits, and they speak to her, and when they are from the Other Side she drives them away. She gave a louis for Hebron, and she mentioned the Baal Shem of London, and said that a Jew had given her a book of kabbalah; and she said several more things, a wonder. I answered her according to her measure. Afterwards they told me that she is a very important noblewoman, that she saved several Jews in Avignon from the Inquisitor, that she is a marquis's daughter, and that her husband was a marquis. All these are the idle words of the gentile woman; and how much among the gentiles ⚠48 was caused by the one called Baal Shem, who in his pride and wickedness revealed practical kabbalah and adjurations to many noblemen and noblewomen in order to boast. Many asked me about him, and I answered them. In the day [Thursday 8 January 1778] I went to the library of manuscripts and copied a little from the notes of R. Isaiah [di Trani] on the Pentateuch. I went round the whole of it: there are several rooms full of writings in every language, of every branch of knowledge and every religion. Among the writings of China there is a long book of broad palm leaves, written in a clear, even and beautiful hand. They said there are about seven thousand volumes of manuscripts, and more. The library of printed books I did not see now; but twenty-two years ago I went round the whole of it, and it is a wonder, as befits a king. They said that this library of Paris is the most remarkable and the largest in the world. 1885 printing: 62

124.6

14 Tevet, Tuesday [13 January 1778]: we lodged in the town of Roye, at a gentile inn, and the Lord helped us to say the blessing of the moon; for until now it had been hidden, with the snow falling, and this was the right moment to bless it. In the day, at midday, we were in the town of Péronne, and our hearts were afraid because of the customs and the spoiling of the clothes in the trunks (bauli), which had needed several hours to put right and arrange. I had a recommendation to the governor. After we came to the inn, which is at the end of the town, as is the custom of the vetturini, as soon as I reached the inn I showed the letter ⚠50, and I went on foot a long way with the snow falling on me and the world full of snow; I suffered going and coming back. I came to the governor, and the snow was by him on the table ⚠51. He read the letter and gave me 24 sous, and I gave them back to him. I said to him in Spanish, for I perceived that he knew a little of it: no, my lord; the recommendation I brought is from the Marshal Comte de Maillebois, of Paris, and it is that you should give orders on my behalf to some town, or at the customs. He answered: I have no one to write to; and he said to the gentile who came with me: say at the customs that they are not to trouble him. I went out from there in dismay. In Paris the gentile Monsieur Fabri had told me that he knew that even if the trunks were searched in Paris and he sealed them, in the town of Péronne their custom is to break the seal and search through and through; and so I did not seal the trunks in Paris. I came back to the inn tired and weary and could not eat. Just before we set out, a man from the customs came who wanted to see the clothes. I handed him the keys, and gave into his hand the passport (passaporto) from the King of France. He took off ⚠52 his hat and began to read, and when he came to my name he took off his hat and said to me: you are honored by the king; there is no need to see the clothes; and he went. As we left the town, about seven gentiles from the customs house stood at the door of the customs and made a reverence with their hats in their hands, and I passed through in peace. Uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 להתרים נגדו — Read as "to raise [my voice] against him"; the verb is unusual here.
  • ⚠2 לא עשיתי אלא לפנים בישראל — A play on Ruth 4:7; read as "I did it only for form's sake".
  • ⚠3 גיסו של אברהם גראדיץ מוסיניאו — מוסיניאו is read as the brother-in-law's name; not identified.
  • ⚠4 אשר טימיה אשתו לא טבלה — טימיה read as טמאה, "unclean"; it could also be the wife's name.
  • ⚠5 בפרק שמונה שרצים בני נדה — A pun on the Mishnah chapter "Eight Creeping Things" (Shabbat 14); he seems to have had eight sons, conceived (as the Ḥida says) in impurity.
  • ⚠6 ברו"ל — So printed; read as ברז"ל, "in the words of the Sages".
  • ⚠7 הרמת"י — The abbreviation appears as הרמת"י, חרמת"י, חר"ם תמ"ה and חר"ם תמה יצ"ו: the ḥakham R. Mordecai Tama (the final י of the short form is the blessing יצ"ו). Probably Mordecai son of Isaac Tama of Hebron, who published Pe'er ha-Dor (Amsterdam 1765) and later lived in France.
  • ⚠8 ואנ"י כתב שתי שורות — ואנ"י is not resolved: a name in abbreviation, or "and I wrote two lines there".
  • ⚠9 בן דוד לאמירה — Read as the son of David Lamira (or: of David, at Lamira); not identified.
  • ⚠10 ק' איש' שהיו בידו מישי' כ"י — איש' read as escudos; מישי' כ"י read as "from the yeshivah", with כ"י not resolved.
  • ⚠11 סי' דא ויינא וסי' באיסה — Family names as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠12 קהל די פאיים — The name of the congregation as printed; not identified.
  • ⚠13 והיו מחזיקים משה ושלמה פיטיט — Read as "Moses and Solomon Petit kept up the quarrel"; perhaps "supported him".
  • ⚠14 שמתנתם מעוטה וחרפתם מרובה — A phrase of the grace after meals (of human givers, "whose gift is small and whose shame is great"); the Ḥida notes the irony.
  • ⚠15 ובנימין אב' — אב' is not resolved (a family name, or Abraham).
  • ⚠16 באש"ג פלצות — פלצות is a biblical word for horror (Job 21:6, Ps 55:6). Perhaps an exclamation, "a gentile inn, horrors!", which suits "and they were wicked"; or a place name (Pleine-Selve lies on this road). Not decided.
  • ⚠17 בעיר דיבונה — Not identified; the road from Port-de-Piles to Tours suggests Sainte-Maure or Montbazon.
  • ⚠18 ומתוך האור אך רוב הקרירות גובר מלמעלה החרס — The sentence seems incomplete; read as "with the light [it was easier], but the cold prevails over the sun".
  • ⚠19 פרזלי — The print may read פרולי; read as פרזלי, "irons", the horseshoes.
  • ⚠20 נתן לנו — The end of the line is torn away in the NLI copy; the HebrewBooks copy of Freimann's edition (hebrewbooks.org 21838) reads נתן לנו.
  • ⚠21 ששם מחפשים — The ends of this line and the next three are torn away in the NLI copy; restored from the HebrewBooks copy of Freimann's edition (hebrewbooks.org 21838): ששם, ולא יהא אלא, וכיוצא ובאנו, ופתח קצת מהסגרים. Marx and Liber, REJ 65 (1913), p. 249, who collated the autograph, have the same words. The 1885 printing does not have this passage.
  • ⚠22 ומילי קטנ"י עבידי דמגנבי או אבדורי אבדור — Aramaic in the style of the Talmud: "small things are apt to be stolen or scattered".
  • ⚠23 ופתח קצת מהסגרים ... וראיה בעלמא לעילא מכל סגיא — So Freimann; Marx and Liber, REJ 65 (1913), p. 249, read ופתח קצת מהבגדים, "opened some of the clothes". Either way the officer only glanced at the top of the luggage.
  • ⚠24 והכהול הגדול — Freimann's own note corrects to והבהול, the large trunk (Italian baule), as Marx and Liber, REJ 65 (1913) also print.
  • ⚠25 מ"ו מילין — So printed (46). The 1885 printing has ט"ו (15), and so does Marx and Liber, REJ 65 (1913), p. 249, for a passage they collated with the autograph; 15 is the likelier figure.
  • ⚠26 רק על פי הנס — Read as "only by a miracle", that is, tolerated without legal right; the last letter could also be read ם.
  • ⚠27 וסי' מרדכי חירף וגידף אותו — Which Mordecai answered him is not said; perhaps Mordecai Ventura (below).
  • ⚠28 מהקאדימיא. די לא סיסיניאס — So printed, for de la Sciences; "Fabri" not identified.
  • ⚠29 שם ... ה' פעלו — The starts of these lines are torn away in the NLI copy; the HebrewBooks copy of Freimann's edition (hebrewbooks.org 21838) reads שם and ה' פעלו, as does the 1885 printing.
  • ⚠30 ושם באו הפרנסים — The starts of lines 33-36 of p. 120 are torn away in the NLI copy; restored from the HebrewBooks copy of Freimann's edition (hebrewbooks.org 21838): ושם באו הפרנסים, ואח"ך סעדנו שם עם, ועשה הבעה"ב כבוד גדול, לכבוד חברת גמילות חסדים. Marx and Liber, REJ 65 (1913), p. 250, have the same words from the autograph. The 1885 printing does not have this entry.
  • ⚠31 ושמו את שמי לראש — They put his name first, probably on the list of the new society; no hymn or acrostic is named. That evening he preached in praise of the same society.
  • ⚠32 ועדיין הם מטין — מטין is not clear; read as "still at odds".
  • ⚠33 ר' דוד נאקיט — Printed נאקיט (here and below); read as the Comtadin family name Naquet. Not identified further.
  • ⚠34 בני אבניון וקק"ק וכו' — Probably the Jews of Avignon and the other holy congregations of the Comtat Venaissin (Carpentras, Cavaillon, L'Isle), the "four communities" named next; the list then gives six cities for "seven", the four communities apparently counted as one.
  • ⚠35 סי' פינ'וטו — Printed פינ'וטו here and פינ'יטו on p. 122; read as Pinto, a member of the Portuguese Jewish family of that name. Which Pinto lived in Paris in 1777 is not identified.
  • ⚠36 לרוצאה — So printed; read להוצאה, "for expenses", as Freimann's own note suggests.
  • ⚠37 חרמ"ת — Initials; Freimann reads חכם רבי מרדכי תמה, R. Mordecai Tama, and takes "he" in what follows to be Tama, not Lopes Laguna.
  • ⚠38 והפליא לדבר — Literally "he made his speech wonderful"; read as "he spoke out strongly" (the young man, when questioned).
  • ⚠39 חננאל דמילייאב — Family name as printed (דמילייאב); the reading de Milliau (Milhaud) is a guess. Not identified.
  • ⚠40 שבע מאות וטו"ב — Printed וטו"ב, 717 (1778 − 1061). The 1885 printing has the same, and so has Marx and Liber, REJ 65 (1913), p. 263; Liber (n. 2) thought it was a Latin Bible.
  • ⚠41 ובן ר' יצחק ישר' — ובן read as "and (the books) of"; Isaac Israeli, a disciple of the Rosh, wrote Yesod Olam at his request.
  • ⚠42 וחתי רש משוה אותו לטועה — Garbled in the autograph (so Freimann). Freimann suggests וחתי[מת] ר"ש or וחר"ש (חכם ר"ש); the man R. Sh. is not identified, and the sense of the clause is uncertain.
  • ⚠43 ואחר חתם בכם — So printed, with a question mark in Freimann's note; בכם is unexplained. Translated "and another signed".
  • ⚠44 והכתו בפרנק — Unclear; Freimann suggests reading והכתו' בפסק, "and the writings in the ruling". The sentence remains broken.
  • ⚠45 סי' יעקב פירירה — Probably Jacob Rodrigues Pereira (1715–1780), the teacher of the deaf and agent of the Portuguese Jews in Paris.
  • ⚠46 כתב חמודי — Literally "a letter of my precious one(s)": perhaps a letter from his family; or "a delightful letter".
  • ⚠47 רואי די ניש וירסאליאם שיווא רושו — French in Hebrew letters. The 1885 printing has ויש for ניש: "rue de Vieux Versailles", the rue du Vieux-Versailles; שיווא רושו is probably "Cheval Rouge", an inn sign. ניש is left untranslated.
  • ⚠48 וכמה גויים גרם — So printed ("how many gentiles he caused"); the sentence lacks an object. Perhaps a misreading of נזקים, "how much harm", as the 1885 translation renders it.
  • ⚠49 חאן לואים — Freimann identifies the town as Senlis, on the road north from Louvres, and suggests that the Ḥida had Saint Louis in mind; the form itself is unexplained.
  • ⚠50 וכבואי לאושפיזא הראתי הכתב — The order of events is not clear: he probably showed the letter at the inn and was then sent on foot to the governor.
  • ⚠51 והשלג אצלו על השלחן — So printed ("the snow was by him on the table"); the sense is unclear, perhaps a misreading in the autograph.
  • ⚠52 והוא הסיר הכובע והתחיל לקרות — הסיר ("took off") is printed twice; the first is probably an error (the 1885 printing has the abbreviation הסי'). Perhaps he put on or kept on his hat before reading.
  • ⚠53 לכפר פאיי — Freimann identifies the village as Épehy, between Péronne and Cambrai.
  • ⚠54 יש בה בכניסה ויציאה ויש פתחים חזקות — The sentence is broken (יש ... ויש); read as one statement about strong gates at the entry and exit.
  • ⚠55 וביום כצאתנו העיר — No date is given; the next entry (17 Tevet, the night of Friday) brings them to a village, and they reach Brussels on Friday at midday, so the departure from Valenciennes is probably Thursday afternoon (15 January) rather than Friday.
  • ⚠56 ופורטיזאס — Probably Italian fortezze or Spanish fortalezas, "fortresses"; possibly "portes", gates.
