On the New Moon of Tevet 5538 [30–31 December 1777] I went to Monsieur Fabri, the learned gentile mentioned above, and he showed me a book in French on divine kabbalah. It began with the Name of seventy-two letters, by which Moses our teacher, peace be on him, struck the plagues in Egypt, and it mixed things up with the constellations. I told him: know that this is a branch of practical kabbalah; the ten plagues were not done by this Name, and this has nothing to do with the constellations. The gentile did me great honor and gave us chocolate and pan di Spagna. Snow was falling, and he took us by carriage to the library; but since it was the end of their year, none of the keepers was there, and we came back. We went with R. Mordecai Ventura to the house of Elijah Perpignan, and I spoke at length to his wife with words of persuasion. Her husband came, and I made him give her in her own hand what they need for household expenses, so that she would be mistress; and I tried with all my strength to make peace between them.

On Friday [2 January 1778] we went with the gentile Signor Fabri to the library of manuscripts. Although it was not a day set for this, he is an important man; he got permission, and they opened it for us. There are several thousand manuscripts in it, in every branch of knowledge. I saw a Bible on parchment written in the year 1061 by their count, so that it was written seven hundred and sixteen years ago, and it looks new. There are several hundred of our books in manuscript. There is Radak on Psalms, with more than the printed text: on the verse "Kiss the son" [Ps 2:12] we saw about a large column, half a sheet. There are many books of natural science, philosophy, astronomy, calendar reckoning ⚠1 and early kabbalah; the books of R. Joseph ibn Kaspi, and of R. Isaac Israeli, who wrote the Yesod Olam, [a disciple] of the Rosh; and many books of the Semak and the like, of books already printed. There is the first part of the Shibbolei ha-Leket, and the complete Sefer Yere'im, which has 464 commandments; it says there that it followed the count of the commandments in the order of R. Yehudai Gaon, the Halakhot Gedolot, except that several times it counted two commandments as one.

On the night of Tuesday in the week of Vayeḥi, 4 Tevet ⚠2 [probably the evening of Monday 5 January 1778], the Marquis de Thomé ⚠3, a scholar, came to my house with great honor, with another distinguished gentile and an Italian abbé. They sat about two hours, and I answered their questions. At the end the marquis asked me to bless him, and I blessed him and the other gentile, which is a wonder. In the morning [Tuesday 6 January 1778] Signor Fabri sent a fine carriage to go to Versailles. We went there with Signor Ventura, and he sent a sword for Signor D. Ḥ. A. [David Ḥayyim Ariani] ⚠4. I put on good clothes and went to Versailles, au rue de Vieux Versailles, Cheval Rouge ⚠5 [probably: on the rue du Vieux-Versailles, at the sign of the Red Horse], where Signor Fabri was, at the house of his kinswoman. They received us with a friendly face, and we drank chocolate. Then we went to the royal court; the gentile went in, and we after him. We came to a beautiful and splendid hall with many gilded columns on both sides and great lamps on them, and the ceiling painted and adorned to look at: this is the Galerie, and noblemen stand guard there. We passed through many rooms, royal rooms, and went into the room where the council sits; above, there is a gilded baldachin, painted and adorned, as befits a king, where the king sits on his royal throne, with the ministers below in the room. We went on into inner rooms and stood at the head of the room. After a short time great noblemen passed, among them the king's brother, Monsieur the Comte de Provence, who is called simply "Monsieur", and the younger brother, Monsieur the Comte d'Artois; they stood near me for about five minutes. Then the king passed with great noblemen, and I said the blessing over a king. He was dressed in plain red, with the blue order (ordre), on which are his arms. As soon as the king had passed, a nobleman came and said to Monsieur Fabri, who was beside me, that the king asks from which place I am ambassador. He answered him that I am not an ambassador; I am from Egypt and came to see out of curiosity (curiosidad). Then we left, and all who stood there did us honor; and some of the ladies (dames) who passed made a reverence, as is their custom. We came to the house of Monsieur Fabri's kinswoman, and they did me great honor. He gave me a cup (fincan) and its porcelain saucer, which Madame the Comtesse d'Artois had given as a gift to Monsieur Fabri's kinswoman, and on the cup are the arms of King Louis. He also gave me a case (caja) ⚠6 to put paper in, like a memorandum book; the case is of glass and looks like cloth. She asked me what I would eat, and I told her: eggs roasted by my servant. They set the table and we sat; I ate bread and two roasted eggs, said the grace with the invitation and the grace after meals, and prayed the afternoon prayer. We drove back by carriage, and the world was full of snow. The gentile said that there are great beasts there, but because of the snow they are shut in a room within a room and cannot be seen; and the garden is twenty-one miles round, but now, in winter and snow, nothing can be seen. We came home in peace. The gentile wanted to pay for the carriage, a wonder of the grace that the Lord gave me in his eyes; I give thanks and praise for this, for His kindnesses, blessed be He. The gentile's kinswoman and her daughter asked me to bless them. I came a little closer to them and blessed them, but would not lay my hands on their heads. A wonder, praises to God, blessed be He, who has made my name great. I am too small for all the kindnesses [Gen 32:11], for His kindness has prevailed over us [Ps 117:2], blessed be He and exalted be His name for ever.

On Wednesday evening [7 January 1778] the Marquis de Thomé came with the Marquise de Croix ⚠7. She sat with me and asked me to pray for her. Then she said that she studies the Bible, that she has seen angels and spirits, and they speak to her, and when they are from the Other Side she drives them away. She gave a louis for Hebron, and she mentioned the Baal Shem of London ⚠8, and said that a Jew had given her a book of kabbalah; she said several more things, a wonder. I answered her according to her measure. Then she told me that she is a very important noblewoman, that she saved several Jews in Avignon from the Inquisitor, that she is a marquis's daughter and her husband was a marquis. All these are the idle words of the gentile woman; and how much harm was done by the one called Baal Shem, who in his pride and wickedness revealed practical kabbalah and adjurations to many noblemen and noblewomen in order to boast. Many asked me about him, and I answered them. In the day [Thursday 8 January 1778] I went to the library of manuscripts and copied a little from the notes of our master R. Isaiah [di Trani], of blessed memory, on the Pentateuch. I went round the whole of it: there are several rooms full of writings in every language, of every branch of knowledge and every religion. Among the writings of China ⚠9 there is a long book of broad palm leaves, written in a clear, even and beautiful hand. They said there are about seven thousand volumes of manuscripts, and more. The library of printed books I did not see now; but twenty-two years ago I went round the whole of it, and it is a wonder, as befits a king. They said that this library of Paris is the most remarkable and the largest in the world.

On Sunday of the week of Va'era [18 January 1778] we left Paris in peace, and some friends, may God keep them, accompanied us by carriage to outside Paris. Just before we set out, a man from the customs came who wanted to see the clothes. I handed him the keys and gave into his hand the passport from the King of France. He ⚠10 his hat and began to read, and when he came to my name he took off his hat and said to me: you are honored by the king; there is no need to see the clothes; and he went. As we left the city, about seven gentiles of the customs house stood at the door of the customs and made a reverence with their hats in their hands, and I passed through in peace. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. Blessed is God the Savior.

On Thursday [22 January 1778] we came to the city of Valenciennes. I had a letter from Monsieur the Comte de Maillebois, a marshal in Paris, to the commandant of the city. I sent it, and asked for a recommendation to the commandant of Brussels, and he sent it at once. The day we left the city they asked for a passport to leave, for such is the law, that they do not let anyone leave without a passport from the commandant. I showed them the letter of the commandant, and they let us go. After this others made trouble, and in the end they let us go in peace.

In the towns of Flanders we saw small carts, with five or six dogs pulling them.
