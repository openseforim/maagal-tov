When I came to Florence, the wardens came and told me what the holy congregation had pledged. I told them that I wished to collect from individuals, and they said that the council does not give permission. I spoke harshly to them and said that in any case they must give permission. At night the wardens came and said that all the members of the council had agreed that I should go to individuals; and blessed be the Lord, we came out of it in peace. There I found the pious rabbi, our teacher R. Jacob Ḥai Levi, may God keep him, who had been my friend of old in Venice, and he did me great honor. I went to the individuals, and they gave, each according to his blessing [Gen 49:28]. I went to the place of the animals and saw a lioness, a leopard and a bear. I went and saw the Duke's garden, celebrated and praised, great and important. I went to the library and saw there some tractates of the Gemara written on parchment in the year 4937 [1177], and a manuscript of the Pesikta on Genesis and Exodus; and they have books in every language, seven rooms, as befits a king [Esth 1:7].

In Shevat [January 1775] our teacher R. Yom Tov Algazi and the sage R. Y. Ḥazan ⚠1 came to Livorno. There was confusion in Livorno, and our teacher R. Yom Tov and his companions acted and succeeded; and I had satisfaction from our teacher R. Yom Tov and his companions, who stirred up our love, praise God.

In the year 5535, on 3 Nisan, Thursday of the week of Bemidbar ⚠2 [probably Thursday 1 June 1775], I was godfather (sandak) for the son of Signor Shalom Tzaddik, and they named him Joseph Ḥayyim after me, with a good sign.

In Tammuz [June to July 1775], on Saturday night, at the close of the Sabbath, I was in distress, and on Sunday I sent Abraham to Livorno, by a lot: the verse that came to me was "You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain" [Exod 20:7]. At once its meaning came to me: do not go there yourself, but send your servant at once. God will be your keeper and savior, amen. I sent him to bring my papers and to get them through ⚠3, with the help of Heaven.

In the town of Senigallia I was with the sage R. Moses Foa, the rabbinic judge of the holy congregation. After prayers, at midday, I set out for Ancona, and I had great trouble with the horses. Some miles before Ancona the servant fell, and the carriage fell on top of us, and the horses, and we were in danger; blessed be the Lord, we got up unhurt and came to Ancona, to the house of the gevir Signor Phinehas, son of the honored R. Abraham ha-Kohen, may his end be good.

In the year 5536, on Sunday of the week of Vayera, at the close of the Sabbath [Saturday night, 4 November 1775], letters came to me from my son Abraham, may his Rock keep him, and several things in them distressed me, and I could not sleep until seven o'clock. In my dream I saw the rabbi, our honored teacher R. Moses Mizraḥi, of blessed memory. He was bringing me a letter that he had written to Constantinople in his own hand, and I was giving him good reasons not to send it. After that we went to pray with him, and I woke. And I was glad, for when I see this rabbi it means salvation for me: "who raised up from the east [mizraḥ]" [Isa 41:2]; and all is well, with God's help.

The year 5536, Tevet [24 December 1775 to 21 January 1776]. I did many things in Ancona and dealt with them with great honor. Among them: they gave the title of maskil to R. Joel Ḥamitz in the face of most of the gevirim, who had to agree almost against their will; and R. Joel was appointed cantor in the Sephardi congregation against a great many of the heads of the congregation. In the month of Tevet we finished looking into the lawsuits of these gevirim according to the Torah, as we saw it. But we saw that ruin and great hatred would come of it, so we girded ourselves with strength to make a compromise between them. In truth it was an enormous and fearful labor for me, more than the mouth can tell; had it not been the Lord who was for us [Ps 124:1], who granted us knowledge and patience and expenses ⚠4, until we made a compromise between them in the matters of the lands and of damage by neighbors. These matters took several months. Blessed be God the Savior.

They wrote from Jerusalem that the sages had quarreled, and that our teacher R. Yom Tov A. ⚠5 and the sage R. Y. Meyuḥas had fled and gone to Safed; and it became known to the gevirim. In truth every hint of these matters makes the eyes fail and the soul languish [1 Sam 2:33], and profanes the name of God and the sages of the Land of Israel; so that, for our many sins, whoever is called an emissary receives the scorn of men [Ps 22:7]. All of it comes from a lack of good management. And as for the contempt: may God save us from contempt and from all shame, and enlighten our eyes to walk always in the ways of uprightness ⚠6, amen, may it be His will.

On Wednesday of the week of Nitzavim, in the year 5536 [4 September 1776], I left Padua and came to Fusina, and from there by gondola to Venice. Thank God, no sbirro came near, who make searches ⚠7 of the clothes; and it was a wonder.

On 28 Elul, Sunday of the week of Ha'azinu ⚠8 [September 1776], I went with our friend the accomplished ⚠9 Abraham Jona by gondola to the Piazza San Marco. I went into the Palace and saw some rooms and magistrates there; they said that there are seventy-two tribunals, an astonishing thing. There is the clock (orologio), and two black slaves strike it. There are the two columns of San Marco, and other great things, and shops adorned with silver and gold and precious stones and many wonderful pearls. There is a bridge (ponte), and on it, on each side, twelve shops on the bridge itself. In the evening we went to a very beautiful garden, with several rooms in it, and tables, and partitions ⚠10, all of them made of plants.

5537, Tishrei. In the Ten Days of Repentance [14 to 23 September 1776] I kept to myself in seclusion, praise God. At the close of the Day of Atonement [the evening of 23 September 1776] I went with Signor Daniel Tolentino of Ragusa by gondola to the Riva Alta, which they call Rialto, the merchants' place, and the Banco del Giro.

On the first of the intermediate days of Sukkot [Monday 30 September 1776] I went with Signor Menaḥem Ashkenazi of Constantinople to the Arsenal, and we went round inside it for three hours walking, and saw royal things beyond description. The rule is this: everything needed to make war at sea and on land is found there, every kind of weapon in plenty. Likewise everything a ship needs, to build it and to sail it, its tackle and ropes and masts and sails, covers ⚠11, destructive weapons, fire and sulphur, is found there. Two thousand four hundred and twenty craftsmen and workers are always there, eating the king's bread [Dan 1:5]; day and night they do not rest [Isa 62:6], each at his work. Many ships with all they need, and many kinds of cannon ready and prepared. There are four buildings of various firearms, clean and polished and shining, with many suits of armor of every kind; rooms full of timber of every kind for the ships; smiths making cannon, and many storehouses full of firearms and of everything. Many furnaces burn together for all their work. There is the Bucintoro, on which the Prince Doge rides every year to consecrate the sea with a ring, to say that she is still a virgin and no man has taken Venice. It is a gilded ship with several hundred pictures and carvings, devices and emblems, and there is a printed book that explains the devices and emblems gathered in this small ship. There are very large ships; we went into one and walked round it. In it is room for several water cisterns and stores and a place for the guard, and eight hundred hired soldiers with their officers go into it. Several other ships are ready, and there are fishponds. Everything is under a roof, so that the workers can work in summer and in winter; and there are roads and passages in the open air, so that it is a city in itself, with everything a man of war needs at sea and on land, ready and arranged in cleanliness, sorted and each thing kept apart from the next. To see only the general and coarse things we walked three hours without a break. Signor Menaḥem gave the man who went with us about twenty-six lire.

On Thursday of the week of Bereshit [10 October 1776] I went with Signor Daniel to the Piazza San Marco, and we climbed the campanile up to the place of the bells. All of Venice can be seen from there as it is. We went to the Zecca, and I saw ducats being struck. I also saw the library, which is very small; but there is a room there full of marble statues of men and women, and statues of birds of prey seizing a man and a child, and statues of warrior women: astonishing things. At the entrance of the Zecca are giant statues of marble, wonderful. In the Piazza is a statue of four horses in bronze; whoever sees them with his own eyes would think they speak. At its end are two columns. One is San Marco, who is one of the four founders of the Gospel; it is the figure of a lion with wings, and they say that he is the protector of Venice. On the other column is a figure of the fear of Genoa ⚠12: when they defeated the Genoese they made a figure of their fear facing San Marco, and San Marco turned his face away from it, to say that he counts it for nothing and rules over it.

On Wednesday of the week of Noaḥ [16 October 1776] I went to the Bragadin press, which Signor Gad son of Samuel Foa runs, and I saw several rooms. There they cast letters, square and Rashi of every kind, with many workers. The nobleman Bragadin takes two hundred ducats a year, and three hundred for the rent of the buildings; and Signor Gad has an outlay of 140 ducats every week for wages and workers.

On Thursday of the week of Noaḥ [17 October 1776] I went with Signor Daniel by land to the Piazza, and I saw streets, markets and roads full of shops, about an hour and a half of them, set out with every kind of merchandise: pearls, vessels of silver and gold, ironsmiths and fruit sellers, everything set out in good order. They said that this is one of a hundred of the markets of Venice, and all in proper order. Every craft and all the trade in the world is found there, and everything is arranged in settled order, quiet and good order.

Signor Daniel had a dispute with Gershon Bianchini of Ferrara over a purchase of wool, in which Signor Gershon, the buyer, had claims. I wrote two lines to Signor Solomon Pazini and his son R. Mordecai, may his Rock keep him, and at once they sent him the money in full.

On Wednesday of the week of Ḥayyei Sarah [6 November 1776] I went by gondola to great gentile merchants, to take them the letters of recommendation (raccomandazione) that I had, and they did me great honor.
