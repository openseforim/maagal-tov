I am grieved for the bag of dinars ⚠1 that went on the roads without enjoyment and with pain. May the Omnipresent have mercy on us in His great mercy, say "Enough" to our troubles, and pour out blessing for us [Mal 3:10] for His service and His fear; so may it be His will.

R. Eizik and his wife remembered me from twenty-two years ago, when I was there ⚠2. My soul revived, and I began: Blessed is God who saves from troubles upon troubles on this road. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever.

On Monday of the week of Bo, at daybreak [26 January 1778], we arrived in peace at Amsterdam, and we went into an inn of gentiles until the gates of the city should open. I found him whom my soul loves [Song 3:1], the sage R. Abraham Ze'evi, may his Rock keep him, emissary to Germany. Although it had occurred to me that it was not good for the said sage R. Abraham to be there, nevertheless, since he is humble and God-fearing and knows me, he behaved toward me with great honor. I too, my covenant was with him [Mal 2:5], and day and night we were together. I also wanted him to make some visits with me, so that I had satisfaction and benefit from him by his upright qualities, for they are lively ⚠3 [Exod 1:19].

On the night of Tuesday [the evening of Monday 26 January 1778] I went to Rabbi Solomon Shalem. He received me with great honor and a friendly face, and I was there about three hours, in worldly matters and in Torah. Afterwards I came home in a carriage ⚠4. In the day [Tuesday 27 January] the gevirim began to visit, and all the wardens, the gevirim and the heads of the academies kept coming to visit, until Sunday of the next week [1 February], by which time nearly all of them had come. The wardens of the Ashkenazim also sent their trustees in their name to greet me.

On the night of Wednesday [the evening of Tuesday 27 January 1778] I went to visit the gaon R. Saul. The sons of the said gaon R. Saul and his sons-in-law are rabbis, so that on every side there is a chain of lineage. He showed me a very friendly face and showed me two passages of the Magen Avraham: one that he questioned in his book, and the second, which was not printed; and I answered him then and there, and he did us great honor. In the day [Wednesday 28 January] there was a wedding with the seven blessings ⚠5, and we went to the house of the bridegroom; the wardens came and greeted us. Then they went to pray the afternoon prayer, and we went to the house of the bride, and we were in a single room. Rabbi Shalem came there, and he did not show me a friendly face. The bridegroom came from the synagogue, and the said rabbi rose and gave the honor to the cantor Gomez. He passed in front of me and showed me no courtesy at all ⚠6, and went in at once, without any delay and without honor. He said the blessing of betrothal, eight blessings, and they showed the ring, and in all this he did not look at me. Afterwards he passed right in front of me, behind him ⚠7, and did great honor to the wardens, and I was left behind. He went out at once, rode off in a carriage and went, and I remained full of shame, for it was almost a disgrace to me. Afterwards I came home on foot, and my anger burned in me [Esth 1:12]. In the evening he sent his son to tell me that he wished to come to me at night, and I told him that I was going to the gevir Signor Isaac Teixeira de Mattos, who is my friend. I went there after the afternoon prayer, for he was ill ⚠8.

On Thursday [29 January 1778] the gaon R. Saul came to visit, and asked me to send him the Birkei Yosef; I had great honor from him.

On the night after the holy Sabbath, the night of Sunday of the order of Beshallaḥ [the evening of Saturday 31 January 1778], I went to Rabbi Shalem, and we were there in honor. I ordered a carriage to come, and went home in it.

On Sunday [1 February 1778] Rabbi Shalem came to visit me and showed me love, and I did him great honor. At night, before I went to the said rabbi, I went to the president ⚠9 and handed him the letter of the mission. On Monday [2 February 1778] I began to visit the wardens. I went in a carriage, and all of them did me great honor. They asked me about the marquise from Paris, and I told them the affairs with the gentiles that had happened to me in Paris, and it was a wonder in their eyes.

On the night after the Sabbath, the night of Sunday of Yitro [the evening of Saturday 7 February 1778], I went to the house of Rabbi Shalem with the sage R. Abraham Ze'evi, may God keep him, and we were there about two hours and more, in words of Torah and stories. On the night of Monday [the evening of Sunday 8 February] Rabbi Shalem came to me and sat about three hours. On the night of Tuesday [the evening of Monday 9 February] I went to the house of R. Moses Alvares, brother of Signor Solomon of Bayonne, and to my friend his son R. Joseph, son-in-law of Rabbi Shalem, and they did me great honor. From there I went to the house of Rabbi Shalem. Before them was the passage of Ha-Mani'aḥ, that a man may take the law into his own hands [b. Bava Kamma 27b]. As soon as I sat down I told them the words of the Mishneh la-Melekh, the Baḥ, our teacher R. Moses di Trani, part 1, the responsa of the Rashba, and so on, and it was a wonder.

On 13 Adar [Thursday 12 March 1778] I fasted the Fast of Esther, but I was crushed and broken [Lev 22:24]. On the day of Purim [Friday 13 March 1778] Rabbi Shalem sent a very large sponge cake ⚠10, oranges and lemons, and I sent him choice kinds of sweets. Signor Medina and Signor Mesquita sent dairy food, wine and sweets, and Signor R. David ha-Kohen sent a Pentateuch in five parts, handsomely bound, newly printed, called Ezrat Sofer ⚠11.

On the holy Sabbath, 15 Adar [14 March 1778], I went to the house of the rabbi, our teacher R. Saul, may God keep him, who had invited me to the third meal, and the rabbi, his son and all his household received me with great honor. They went to pray the afternoon prayer in the synagogue that he has there, and I went with them, and he called me up to the Torah; afterwards we ate in his house. The rabbanit, his second wife, and his daughter-in-law, the wife of R. Leib, may God keep him, were at the table. Several scholars were there, and also R. Leib Hirsch. I said the grace after meals, and the said rabbi kept me there. After the evening prayer we came into his house and found a table set, and on it a structure about two cubits high: the likeness of the king's court, with Mordecai sitting in the king's gate in Persian dress; a canopy and a palace, and in it Esther on the couch, and Ahasuerus, and Haman falling [Esth 7:8]. Outside this palace, on another side, in the street, Haman hanging; and all round the structure, soldiers surrounding it; and many fine figures, beautiful to look at, in different colors. All this was made of sugar alone, with great skill, and for the amount of work it is worth about twenty sequins. I think it was a gift that some notables had sent to the rabbi. The whole table was full of delicacies: fruits as in nature, a cheese with "kosher and for Passover" written on it, and the likeness of cucumbers in vinegar, all of sugar.

So it was a table of kings. Scholars and notables began to come, and the rabbi spoke words of Torah; and I, the young one, also spoke what God had graciously given His servant [Gen 33:5]. Musicians came, and in the middle of this one of the empty fellows stood up and made a kind of mock sermon, and said things in the holy tongue in filthy rhyme. After some hours the rabbi went upstairs with us, and we had a great meal there. But I was careful, for I could not eat at all, and with difficulty I ate a little bread and fowl. The rabbi gave the grace after meals to Signor Ḥada ⚠12, and he said: "With the permission of the wondrous rabbi, the famous gaon, the master of the house, and with the permission of the perfect sage ⚠13, the emissary." After the grace we went downstairs, and other fine musicians came. The rabbi sent for a carriage and had it brought, and we came home near midnight. We found many men and women there with music, and among them a troupe of masked people dancing and carousing; and more kinds came in masks, cripples and Turks and many kinds. At once I got up and went upstairs to sleep. I had pain in the stomach, for although I had been careful, I still suffered a little, and I fell asleep long after midnight. In the day I had a bad stomach, and for some days I was not well.

On the night of Tuesday of Vayakhel, 18 Adar [the evening of Monday 16 March 1778], I was at Rabbi Shalem's. I went against my will, for I was weak in constitution.

On Wednesday [18 March 1778] I heard a groaning voice, and the city was in confusion because of the fall of the funds ⚠14 in London. They said that the gevirim had lost heavily in this. There were also two bankruptcies, of a gentile and of a Jew, to the sum of three hundred thousand florins, and the whole world suffered loss from them. Signor Aaron Capadose was struck twice over. Because of this confusion nothing was done for me in the matter of the mission. Also some were hesitating and complaining: some because I had not visited him ⚠15, and some because years ago, at the death of the sage Isaac ben Dana de Brito ⚠16, they had sent for me through the Officials of Constantinople, by courier to the holy city of Jerusalem, and I did not want to come. After this they invited the rabbi, our teacher R. Ḥayyim Abulafia, of blessed memory, when he was in Salonika; and after he had given them his word, he went to Izmir. At that time Rabbi Shalem happened to be there, having come to print the book Shoneh Halakhot, and they took him; for it was a disgrace to them that we had refused to come to them. Now some petty fellow has lifted up his spear [2 Sam 23:18], with the claim that afterwards I was in Egypt. And some did not like the words of the printed letter, and said that it praises me, the letter that I distributed ⚠17, and that it is not right for it to contain praise; above all, that they call me "divino", which means "divine", God save us, and that it is not proper; and such things and such. So when I heard all this and saw this astonishing affair, my grief grew great, and the black sickness returned to its blackness, and I spent most of the week in grief, for several reasons.

On the night of Sunday of the week of Pekudei, after the Sabbath [the evening of Saturday 21 March 1778], all night they were making merry in the streets, with masks and follies, for Purim ⚠18, as if the city were theirs, very publicly, in the streets of the town. Most of them were Ashkenazim, for there are more than fifty thousand Ashkenazi souls in Amsterdam. The city is a city of liberty, and they take too much liberty, as if they were the rulers and the kings of the land; a wonder.

On 27 Adar [Thursday 26 March 1778] I went with Signor ben Dana to visit gentiles: Britano ⚠19, Ebelsman ⚠20 and Carlo Fernando ⚠21. They did me great honor, above all Ebelsman, and he gave me four pounds of chocolate ⚠22, made in his house.

On 29 Adar, the holy Sabbath [28 March 1778], I stayed at home; and on the night of the holy Sabbath [the evening of Friday 27 March 1778] I was very sullen and displeased [1 Kgs 21:4].
