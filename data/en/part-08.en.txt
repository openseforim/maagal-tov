On the New Moon of Sivan [Wednesday 27 May 1778], in the city of The Hague ⚠1, I went to the ḥazzan R. Solomon Saruk and handed him the letters. Then I went to the great and pious rabbi, our teacher R. Saul Segal ⚠2, brother-in-law of the gaon R. S. of Amsterdam: he is married to his sister, the daughter of the gaon our teacher R. Leib. They said that this woman is learned and very modest, and that she writes fine verse. Once she wrote a rhyme, and in the rhyme she put ḥet for kaf. Her brother, the gaon R. S., criticized it in writing, and she answered: "They that wait upon the Lord shall renew their koaḥ" [Isa 40:31] ⚠3. The pious rabbi received us with great honor. In the evening I went to visit Signor Moses de Pinto and the remarkable notable R. Tobias Boas ⚠4, who is eighty-three years old; his sons are great men of the city. The Lord has blessed him with children, life and great wealth; he is a ruler, a great man, and beloved at the court of the government. They showed me great kindness.

The whole city is beautiful; it is like one garden.

On 14 Sivan [Tuesday 9 June 1778], after the afternoon prayer, we went by carriage to Baron Suasso. Several gevirim, his relatives, were there, and they honored us greatly with a table full of delicacies. There I saw a small Torah scroll, the height of a double hesset ⚠5, in perfect writing on shining parchment, a fine thing; and the ark is small, and the fragrance of its wood is worth money, a fine thing. I also saw a Megillah in perfect writing, less than a thumb's joint long. We came home by carriage in peace.

All these days Abraham ⚠6 angers me. His feet do not stay at home [Prov 7:11], and day and night he does not stop going to "sway over the trees" [Judg 9:9].

On 15 Sivan [Wednesday 10 June 1778] I went to the cabinet (gabinetto) of the Prince ⚠7. There are all kinds of creeping things, birds, fish, beasts, snakes and scorpions, and a great many parrots (pappagalli), all embalmed; some of them have been put in strong water (agua fuerte) ⚠8. There are also large animals and deer, stripped of their flesh, with their skins filled with straw, and they look as if they could speak. Also all kinds of worms and kinds of locust; the likeness and model of a palace; many kinds of learning; ores of gold, silver and copper; a very large lodestone; many kinds of sea creatures, and stones of many different kinds that the sea casts up. Also two embalmed men, who have become mummy. Many kinds of plants, and the like; weapons of war; and a wooden hand made for a man who lacked a hand, and he used it like his own hand for everything; and the shoes of a giant. Precious stones, some of them large: diamonds, rubies, sapphire, emerald and the like, more than thirty kinds of precious stones. A topaz of about twelve pounds ⚠9, uncut; and a cut piece of it, like a small pomegranate, worth several "tons" ⚠10. A chest with many kinds of spices in it, and models of many things from China ⚠11; and a bearded man from China who looks as if he is speaking. Several arrangements of branches, each with the likeness of its nest ⚠12. Many sea creatures and large birds, preserved, of many kinds, all embalmed. Many kinds of stones whose color and pattern are beautiful, and this is their nature, that they were born so; and the likeness of a picture (quadro) in a stone, found so in nature (natural). Of the birds and fish there are very many, and of the creeping things and worms, thousands. Many plants, large and small, and many medicinal plants from other kingdoms. The man of the mummy mentioned above is one thousand nine hundred years old. Of these things there are about six rooms, large and middle-sized. Several animals stripped of their flesh, whose shape remains in their own bones; things of many different kinds that the sea casts up. In the middle of some of the rooms there is a large table with many different and strange things on it, and all of them are wonderful things; among them is a kind of wood that has turned to stone. So everything is a wonder, set out in cabinets like towers, with glass over them and the doors closed, all in order; truly it is a wonder of wonders. All is arranged in a beautiful, fine and lovely order, an astonishing thing; and I have written down the little that remains in my memory. On every thing, small or large, even the little creeping things and worms, there is a very small paper with its number (numero) written on it. So, compared with this, the room at Padua mentioned above ⚠13 is like an ape before a man [b. Bava Batra 58a], "and what likeness will you compare" to it [Isa 40:18].

On Tuesday of the week of Beha'alotekha ⚠14 [9 June 1778] I went to the rabbi's house. He was asleep in his room, and the maidservant woke him. He came down and fell on the stairs and cried out loudly. I was distressed, and blessed be the Lord, no harm came to him. I had more distress that day: I received a letter from Amsterdam that on the festival of Shavuot [1–2 June 1778] Rabbi Shalem was saying to everyone who came to him: "Have you seen So-and-so? Because he did not take my advice, he did not do so well." He also spoke at length about a point of law that I had stated, and about some other things against me in Torah. I was bowed down at hearing it [Isa 21:3]. As for what he said against me in Torah, the truth is with him, and "I am brutish and do not know" [Ps 73:22]; but in saying that I did not follow his advice he did not speak rightly, for he never advised me. I was also distressed that I lost the money I had put into the lottery; and also that a letter came to me from London that they did not want to give a penny ⚠15, and I was distressed. May the Merciful One have mercy on me and make my end good; so may it be His will.

On 21 Sivan [Tuesday 16 June 1778] I went to Rotterdam with R. Ezekiel, R. Moses Lehren and R. Simeon Zimir ⚠16.

On 26 Sivan [Sunday 21 June 1778] they made an outing for me, and they spent almost forty florins ⚠17 on this outing for my sake, all with great joy. It is a wonder and great miracles: in winter, when I was here, no one spoke to me, and "Depart!" they cried [Lam 4:15], and when I left they gave one ducat. Now they have done wonders in speaking ⚠18, spending and giving. R. Moses Springer also said that he won twelve thousand in the lottery ⚠19 through the blessing I gave him in winter. And in The Hague too, when I came, I was with R. Isaac Segal Leiden, and it came into my heart to bless his wife as I left. He told me that for fifteen years she had not conceived, and there had never been even a doubt of pregnancy; and since I blessed her, she may now be pregnant. This is what they said: "Let not the blessing of an ordinary man be light" [b. Megillah 15a]. These are "the wondrous works of Him who is perfect in knowledge" [Job 37:16]. "I will bless the Lord at all times; His praise shall continually be in my mouth" [Ps 34:2]. "I will cry to God Most High, to God who accomplishes for me" [Ps 57:3]. "The Lord will accomplish for me; Your kindness, O Lord, endures for ever; do not forsake the work of Your hands" [Ps 138:8].

On 6 Tammuz ⚠20 [Wednesday 1 July 1778], two hours before midday, we set out by the diligence, and we travelled all night and all the next day, until seven hours after midday [Thursday 2 July 1778], when we reached Paris in peace. Monsieur Fabri came, and we went to the house of his kinswoman, and he kept me four hours. He and his kinswomen showed me a very friendly face and wanted me to eat there with them. I said that I eat only eggs roasted by us, and they agreed. I asked the gentile to tell me what he believes in, and he told me: in the Lord, the God of Israel. I examined him on this, and it seemed to me that it is so. So I told him: if so, say the verse Shema Yisrael morning and evening, keep the seven Noahide commandments, and beware in every way of associating anything with God (shittuf); hold only to the complete unity of the Lord, the God of Israel. He accepted it and said that he would pray only to the Lord, the God of Israel. When I came the first time, Signor Abraham Vidal asked me to pray for the queen, that she might conceive; and it is now four months since she conceived ⚠21. Monsieur Fabri said to me: see, your prayer has borne fruit. I answered: not by my righteousness, but by the merit of my fathers, of blessed memory.

On 14 Tammuz [Thursday 9 July 1778] I went with Monsieur Fabri to visit the Comte de Maillebois. He gave permission and wrote for me to the keepers of the king's manuscripts in the library (bibliothèque), that they should give me a manuscript to copy. We went there, and I took the notes of R. Isaiah [di Trani] on the Pentateuch ⚠22. It is a wonder to take a book out of there, even a printed one, and all the more a manuscript from the king's books.

Later that same day I had distress. Signor Abraham Gradis in Bordeaux had asked me whether a man may put away his wife by a bill of divorce against her will. I answered that it is forbidden, and he asked me to give it to him in writing. So as not to refuse, I wrote that a first wife who is modest ⚠23 and without blemish, from whom he has children, may not be divorced. The matter concerns Samuel Peixotto ⚠24, who wanted to divorce his wife, and she does not want a divorce. His advocate ⚠25 set out arguments, and among them he brought my words and wrote: "This man is an emissary to collect money for the poor of the Land of Israel; they sent him because he is trustworthy, not for his learning. What he wrote, he wrote either for money, or because he knows nothing at all of matters of law. What he says cites no source and is nowhere in the whole Talmud; only, they said in an aggadah that over one who divorces his first wife the altar sheds tears [b. Gittin 90b], and laws are not learned from aggadah [y. Pe'ah 2:4]." He went on at length about this, printed it all and distributed it to the whole Parlement. All this was by the plot ⚠26 of Lipman Calmer, who is a "professor" of an abomination ⚠27. Raba of Bordeaux joined them ⚠28, and he is hefker, lawless: P-Q-R, the initials of Peixotto, Calmer, Raba. I was very distressed that it was written and printed, and it will go to Amsterdam, London, Bordeaux: their initials make avel, mourning. May it be His will that it be an atonement for my sins, and that "the children of wickedness afflict me no more" [2 Sam 7:10]. I was also in great distress to see the insolence of Abraham Gabbai; there is not even one day on which he does not distress me. In truth, if I set out to write about Abraham's doings, it would need a large volume on Abraham. But I spare the loss of time, and I do not want to write down all his disgrace, and the Lord knows that it grieves me even to write this much. But this I do to relieve my distress, for "care in a man's heart, let him tell it" [Prov 12:25]. Therefore "with my staff I passed over" [Gen 32:11] "when Doeg came" [Ps 52:2] ⚠29, and "the good Lord pardon" [2 Chr 30:18]; so may it be His will.

On 26 Tammuz [Tuesday 21 July 1778] Signor Elijah Perpignan came to me with others. They brought a carriage, and I went with them to the great gentile merchants to whom I had brought letters of recommendation (recommandation). They all received me with a friendly face and great honor, praise God.

On 14 Av [Friday 7 August 1778] my friend Signor Ariani ⚠30 had a tertian fever, and I was in great distress. On that day the physician Monsieur Rast came and brought a priest with him. He had in his hand a list of about twenty questions ⚠31 to ask me, and there was no one else there. They asked in French, and I answered partly in French and partly in Italian, so that we understood each other. The priest was pleased with my answers, as far as one could hear.
