# Ḥida, pure Sephardi

In the year 5524 [1763–64], when I came to Egypt, a wonderful book came into my hands, from the manuscript of our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital, of blessed memory, on practical kabbalah and the science of letter combinations, and other matters from the Tannaim. I took this book on loan together with other books, and went and secluded myself with it. Hardly had I begun to understand a little of it, by way of attainment, and I had a great fervor; and while I was studying it I dozed off, and I saw our teacher R. Ḥayyim Vital, of blessed memory. I asked him a question, and he answered me; and he gave me a cup of wine to drink, and I was very glad.

In Sivan 5533 [May to June 1773] the gevir R. Samuel was very ill, and his whole body trembled. I prayed much, and I made redemption-offerings for him, from my own money as well as those they made from his money ⚠28. Many days of distress I passed in this, until the Holy One, blessed be He, had mercy on us: He sent His word and healed him [Ps 107:20]. Meanwhile I was in distress and did not know where to turn. If I thought of going to Livorno, I was afraid of the danger of captivity, for the Muscovite had struck and raised his head ⚠29, and the sea was full of corsairs ⚠30, and my heart melted within me and became like water [Josh 7:5]; until I heard that on the side of Tunis there were no Muscovites. There was a ship going to Tunis and from there to Livorno, and I made up my mind to travel on it. On Tuesday of the week of Matot and Masei [13 July 1773] I set out on the said ship. But the captain was a very wicked man, proud and a fool. He did great injustices to me: he ordered that they give me no water, and that whenever I wanted anything I should ask him. I made him a present of fine tobacco and several flasks of syrup ⚠31, and it availed me nothing [Esth 5:13]; he was wicked from beginning to end. All the week I did not eat the measure of bread that requires the grace after meals, because of the water for washing the hands; and how much distress I had over this at the meals of the holy Sabbath. On Wednesday of the week of Ki Tetze [25 August 1773] we reached the city of Sousse. On Sunday of the week of Ki Tavo [29 August 1773] permission came ⚠32, and we went ashore in peace. On Wednesday of the week of Nitzavim [8 September 1773] we came to Tunis, and the master of the house did me very great honor. Several women came to the gevir's house, women who were pregnant, wishing to see me; they stood at the windows and looked at me from afar, and I did not notice. On the Sabbath I was walking in the courtyard there with the gevir, and I felt that the women were behind me, taking the hems of my garments from behind, gently and lightly, to kiss them; and the women never sat at the table at all. But in the matter of food they have no manners whatever, "for God did not lead them by the way [derekh eretz]" [Exod 13:17] ⚠33; and I used to tell them that manners are a coin not current in their land. There was the rabbi R. Abraham Tayyib, who was absorbed in study even on the road; he had a number of disciples, rabbis and sages. And the rabbi R. Joseph Zarqa, a sage and a scribe, pious and pure and righteous, had about sixty disciples. Among them were weighty students; and among the greatest of his disciples were his son-in-law R. Joshua, R. Abraham Katan ⚠34, the warden, and R. Jacob Ma'arek ⚠35. All of them were dear to me, and I studied with them; and so with very many scholars, perhaps about a hundred scholars, with whom I studied and debated, and I found favor in their eyes. From what I heard, there are today nearly three hundred scholars in Tunis. I also saw boys of fourteen who were studious and keen, who could sling at a hair [Judg 20:16].
