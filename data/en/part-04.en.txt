26 Tammuz 5537 [Thursday 31 July 1777], in the city of Avignon, one of the cities of France. This day was a hard day. After prayers, with the treasurer, Signor Isaac, who would not give from the charity box: he said that there was nothing in it, that they were owed money, and on what grounds should one take an advance? In truth, all this sorrow and turmoil, and the disgrace of the mission, which is treated as a common thing in a place where the emissary used to be like a king, were caused by the sage R. Bagaio ⚠1. He was excessively burdensome: after he had taken out what had been in the box for thirteen years, his eye was not satisfied, and he took a large sum as an advance, so that in ten years the hole he made was not filled. After that he burdened the individuals beyond measure, and after that he burdened them with subscriptions for the yeshivah, men apart, women apart and young men apart; and he stayed a long time in each city, going round from house to house, until the mission came to this state, that it seems to them a heavy yoke and they prepare themselves to resist. In truth I suffered much sorrow in several communities. And here ⚠2, where all of them had loved me since my first mission, and where I, the least of men, have a name, even so I did not have the honor I had at first in several things. I sent to call them all to the council. Elijah Reuel came and told me that the opinion of all of them was to give four louis d'or. I rebuked him and said to him: are you the whole of Avignon, or do you have authority from me? and other words of reproof. I sent that they should all come; they came and told me they would give seven louis d'or. I did not agree; I got up and went to another house, and they got up and left, and I was very distressed. I lifted my eyes to heaven [Dan 4:31] that He grant me wisdom, understanding and knowledge, good qualities and good conduct, for I was in great distress; and I trusted in His kindness that He would help me for the sake of the glory of His name and give me success in His service, blessed be He. At the time of the afternoon offering I went to the synagogue sullen and angry [1 Kings 20:43], and with tears of the heart I prayed to the Lord to save me from a quarrel. When I came out I took a roundabout way, so as not to pass before the gevirim. Afterwards R. Elijah came and told me that at midday the wife of Elijah Reuel, who had been the chief of those holding back, had taken to her bed with fever, fear and trembling. He himself went to each one of them to appease them, so that they would agree to give as I had asked of them. They agreed, and sent to him that they now accept my terms, and asked whether I would give them leave to come. I told him that leave is given; they came, and I showed them a friendly face, and all was at peace.

One day a priest came to say that a gentile had died, and in the hour of his death he had confessed (confesso) that he had stolen two thousand lire from a Jew named Abraham Gradis, and that they should be returned to him. That Jew answered and said that they should be divided among the poor; and his name became great.

In a village I stayed on the Sabbath of the week of Vayishlaḥ [probably 13 December 1777, on the way to Paris]. A minyan came, and I myself prayed as cantor and read the weekly portion from a printed Pentateuch, and at the end of the prayers I preached them a sermon of moral instruction, praise God.

14 Shevat, in the year 5537 [Wednesday 22 January 1777], I traveled by carriage with the son of R. Isaiah Senigaglia, and after half a mile I took Signor Jacob Cusin into the carriage, and we came by post to Trieste; I spent the night in his house.

15 Shevat [Thursday 23 January 1777], at first light in the morning, the wardens sent a sedan chair to carry me to the synagogue. After prayers Rabbi Mas'ud came with me to the house of the rabbi, our teacher R. Y. Formiggini, may his Rock keep him, who received me in his house and sat there until midday; so did the gevir, the head of the community, Signor Marco Levi, with his grandsons, Signor David Levi and Signor Jacob Cusin.

At night I went to visit Signor Marco Levi; he is very keen, and I answered him according to his keenness.

16 Shevat ⚠3, on Friday night [24 January 1777], the wardens came, Signor Marco, Signor Lapdon ⚠4 and Signor Treves; and Signor David mentioned above was there the whole meal to honor me. On the Sabbath they did me honor in the synagogue, and Signor Jacob and Signor David, may God keep them, came to the meal.

18 Shevat, Sunday of the week of Yitro [26 January 1777], I went with Signor David mentioned above by carriage to visit Signor Joachim Arsil ⚠4, Signor Jacob mentioned above and Signor Lapdon.

21 Shevat [Wednesday 29 January 1777] I went with the gevirim mentioned above to the twelve gevirim of the council, and all of them did wonderfully, more than the custom. The first, and the cause of it all, was Signor Marco, who sent a large vessel full of coffee and chocolate with nine sequins inside it, and through this they gave much. In truth I was greatly honored. I sent to the council to say that I ask only the charity box and some addition to the account of the box, and that they give leave to collect from individuals; and it pleased them. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever.

The week of Shelaḥ, on Friday [probably 27 June 1777 ⚠5], some of the great men of the city were with me to mediate peace between them ⚠6, and I was sullen and angry. In the morning I could not get up; I had vomiting and diarrhea and suffered all day. On the holy Sabbath I prayed the evening prayer at home and ate a little. On the Sabbath day, in great distress, I went to the synagogue, and with great difficulty I managed the two meals; the vomiting and diarrhea continued. On Sunday of the week of Koraḥ [29 June 1777] I suffered all day in my illness. On Wednesday [2 July 1777] the physician gave me rhubarb, and the flux stopped. I ate meat and toasted bread soaked in wine, for I saw that my strength was gone [Deut 32:36] and there was no strength in me ⚠7. Blessed is He who bestows good things on the undeserving, who has bestowed every good on me.

On Thursday [3 July 1777] I went to the synagogue and said the blessing of thanksgiving (ha-gomel). Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever.

In the year 5538, on 22 Kislev [Monday 22 December 1777], I reached Paris. This city of Paris is the metropolis of France, a great city; they say it is fifteen miles round ⚠8. The markets and streets are wide, so that two carriages pass with room to spare while there are people at the sides. The river Seine is in it, and it draws its water from it. There is a great and long bridge they call Pont-Neuf, that is, Ponte Nuovo, and feet never cease from this bridge all day and all night; and there is the clock (orologio) of the Samaritaine, around which is the water. There are many academies in it. The Jews live in great quiet: many Ashkenazim and many Portuguese.

On 26 Kislev [Friday 26 December 1777] Signor Fabri, a learned gentile of the Académie des Sciences ⚠9, came to visit me. He was an inquirer into speculations and into practical kabbalah, and I answered him. On Friday ⚠10 I went to pay my respects to this gentile and sat there two hours. He showed me a book in French in which are written the names of the angels and their seals and their letters, and questions put in dreams by adjurations, all in French. The gentile showed me a friendly face. I went there with Signor Mordecai Ventura, a grammarian, learned in languages, who took much trouble with me. May the Lord repay his deeds.

On 28 Kislev, Sunday of the week of Vayigash [28 December 1777], Signor Elijah Perpignan and his wife came. I gave the woman a Shema Yisrael to swallow, a segullah of R. Menaḥem Azariah [of Fano], of blessed memory, for they were afraid that she would change her religion, God forbid; and I spoke to them to make complete peace.

On 29 Kislev, the night of Monday in the week of Vayigash [the evening of 28 December 1777], Signor Abraham Vidal and Signor Moses Perpignan came and gave a gift for Hebron with honor. I will recount the kindnesses of the Lord ⚠11 [Isa 63:7]: I have a great name, and they think a hundred times more of me than what I know. I already tell them that I am ignorant [Prov 30:2], and they think it is from humility. Among the gentiles too, the scholars of Edom, my name has gone out, and they ask the Jews about me, which is a wonder; and what I say to them they think are words more precious than pearls. These are His mighty deeds: He raises up the needy from the dunghill, to seat him with princes [Ps 113:7–8]. And how very precious are the kindnesses of the people of Avignon! For in every place where there is even one of them, I received great honor and benefit. There are seven cities of France where I met them, and they were lights in the places where they live: the four communities, Nîmes, Montpellier, Pézenas, Narbonne ⚠12, Bordeaux and Paris. They restored my spirit and honored me greatly, and did me good with their persons and their money; they never stopped showing me affection and raising me up, a wonderful thing. And David blessed [1 Chr 29:10] them and all their house. May the Lord repay their deeds, and may their reward be wealth and honor and life and holy seed; may the Lord save them from all evil, and may the merit of the Land of Israel protect them and their seed; may they be full of sap and green [Ps 92:15] with abundance of everything. Amen, may it be His will, for ever and ever.
