We also heard from truthful tellers that when the Ḥida, of saintly blessed memory, was going about on his mission in the towns of France in the winter, they came to the bank of a flooding river. He was sitting in a carriage drawn by horses, and several carriages full of other people were standing there; they could not cross the river for fear, because it was at its full strength and force, a flood of many waters, and it was dangerous to go into it. When the Rabbi came in his carriage, his coachman also stopped, for he was afraid to go into the river lest they and the horses drown. At that moment the Rabbi, of blessed memory, called to mind what he knew ⚠1, and he said to the coachman: "Go on your way and go into the river, and do not be afraid." The coachman knew the Rabbi and his holiness; he relied on his words and went into the river, and they crossed the river from bank to bank with the horses and the carriage, calmly and without trouble. All the other carriages standing there "saw, and so they marveled" [Ps 48:6], and they used to tell of this wonder that the Ḥida, of saintly blessed memory, did. May his merit protect us, amen.

We also heard from truthful tellers that here in Livorno, may God protect it, he lived in the house of Signor Pereira ⚠2, may he rest in Eden. He had a maidservant in his house who used to bring him his coffee every morning to his room, the place where he studied. Sometimes gevirim or the like came to visit the Rabbi, of blessed memory, or for some other need, and she had standing orders to bring coffee for everyone sitting with him. So before she brought him the coffee she used to peep "through the lattices" [Song 2:9] into the Rabbi's room, to see whether some other man or men were with him, so as to bring them coffee by their number. One day she peeped as usual and saw three more men sitting with him. At once she went and brought four cups of coffee and came in to the Rabbi. The Rabbi lifted up his eyes and saw, and behold, four cups in her hand. He said to her: "What is this you have done? Why did you bring more?" She answered him: "My master, I peeped beforehand and saw three men sitting with you, so I brought for them too, as you ordered me." He said to her: "Happy are you, and it is well with you" [Ps 128:2], "that you were worthy to see." And they said that, as appeared from his deeds, Elijah used to come to him, and souls of the righteous, to tell him "the secrets of wisdom" [Job 11:6]. May his merit protect us, amen.

We also heard that once a friend pressed him to tell him three numbers that would come out in the lottery. The Rabbi did not want to, saying that he could not. When he pressed him hard, he said to him: "I will give you a sealed note, on condition that you do not open it until after the draw has come out." So he did, and gave him a sealed note. After the draw came out he opened the note and found the five numbers ⚠3 that had come out, all in their order. It seems that after he settled in Livorno he added to his holiness tenfold over what it had been before, when he was on his mission, "a wanderer and a fugitive" [Gen 4:12] in "the wandering of a man, which is hard" [b. Sanhedrin 26a], as appears from what the Rabbi himself wrote in the book Ma'agal Tov. May his merit protect us, amen.

We also heard that once a simple man asked the Rabbi, of blessed memory, to give him a letter of recommendation ⚠4 praising and extolling him, so that he could go round on a mission for himself. The Rabbi, of blessed memory, held back from giving it to him, because he knew and recognized that there was not a scent of Torah in him: how could he write about him what was not in him, so that people would rely on his words? But the man pressed him very much, and in his great humility he could not send him away empty-handed. So against his will he wrote him a letter with a number of praises. But in his wisdom the wise man considered "which is the way where light dwells" [Job 38:19], so that a wise man reading the letter would understand the hint that hints at the man ⚠5, to "know the man and his talk" [2 Kgs 9:11]. Among his praises he wrote: "So great is his worth that, had he lived in the days of Elisha, the oil would not have stopped" [2 Kgs 4:6]. The man took the letter from the Rabbi, of blessed memory, and went off in great joy. Everywhere he went he wrapped himself in the cloak of a scholar ⚠6 and showed them the Rabbi's letter, and for the sake of the Rabbi's honor they honored him without testing him. Until he came to a certain town whose sage was "a sage for whom a hint is enough" ⚠7. "The look on his face testified against him" [Isa 3:9], and the sage saw that there was not a scent of Torah in him. Yet he was very surprised to see in his hand the Ḥida's letter of recommendation, so full of his praises. So he went back over the text, to read the letter carefully and to weigh the Rabbi's language, and among his words he saw that "the words of a wise man's mouth are gracious" [Eccl 10:12], where he says: "had he lived in the days of Elisha, the oil would not have stopped", a praise that had never been given to any scholar. He studied it until he understood the Rabbi's intention: he was hinting there that this man is an empty vessel, of the kind that could not be found in the days of Elisha ⚠8. He laughed, and they gave him something, so as not to shame him altogether, and he went off to his own land. Thus far the story.

There was another story. When the Rabbi, of blessed memory, was in Livorno, an Ashkenazi emissary came there. The Rabbi, of blessed memory, heard that this emissary had a bad reputation ⚠9 and that people murmured about him behind his back, and so the Rabbi, of blessed memory, kept him at a distance and did not show him a friendly face. The emissary bore him a grudge in his heart, and he wrote an accusation against the Rabbi, of blessed memory, to the rabbis of Germany, saying that most of the Rabbi's study was in the book Ḥemdat Yamim ⚠10, which is hated among them. This angered them, and they wondered greatly at the Rabbi, of blessed memory. A rabbi from a town in Germany, a faithful friend of the Rabbi, of blessed memory, let him know of this accusation that the emissary had written about him. The Rabbi answered him that nothing of the kind had ever happened; "and even if it were so, know that if I loved Ḥemdat Yamim ['Delight of Days'], that emissary loved Ḥemdat Leilot ['Delight of Nights']" ⚠11. And that is enough said.

We also heard that when the Ḥida, of blessed memory, was in the town of Pisa, may God protect it, they brought before him a baby of eight months to bless him, for he was a little ill. The Rabbi looked at him and began to teach him the alphabet, aleph, bet, in order, and then in reverse, tav, shin, resh, kof, and so on; and so in this way three times, and the baby answered after him, like him. Then he said a verse to him, and the baby did not answer. The Rabbi, of blessed memory, struck his hand on his forehead and said to his mother: "Carry him and take him away" [cf. 2 Kgs 4:19]. That night at midnight the baby died. Some days later, at a circumcision feast to which they had invited the Rabbi, of blessed memory, the gevirim were arguing with the Rabbi, of blessed memory, about why the coming of our Messiah is delayed. The Rabbi, of blessed memory, said to them: "More than we want his coming, the Messiah longs for it himself; but our sins hold him back. A short time ago the Messiah was born here, and because of the sins of the generation he died as a little child." And they understood that it was the baby mentioned above.

We also heard an awesome and wonderful story: in his old age, when he was in the holy congregation of Livorno, an important man, a kohen, came to him with a complaint against his wife, saying that his wife had been secluded ⚠12 with a certain man. The gaon, the Rabbi mentioned above, was sitting there with the distinguished judges of the town, and the Rabbi said that according to the commandment of the Torah he must divorce her. The judges could not restrain themselves, especially as they were relatives of the woman and wanted to shield her. They said to the Rabbi: "Let our master teach us: where is his source for this ruling, to judge without witnesses? This is nothing but prophecy, and 'it is not in heaven' [Deut 30:12]. If we make law and judgment by the holy spirit, then, God forbid, 'judgment will come out crooked' [Hab 1:4]; for because of our many sins we have in this time neither priest nor prophet, only the words of our sages, of blessed memory, based on the words of the holy Torah." The gaon gave them no answer at all, but he said a second time to the man, the woman's husband: "Listen to my voice and divorce this woman." When they saw that he did not answer them a word, they went to the woman's relatives, who were among the notables of this town, and told them the whole matter and what the Rabbi, of blessed memory, had said, and they "made all the congregation murmur against him" [Num 14:36]. When the Rabbi, of blessed memory, heard these words of strife, the spirit of the Lord came upon him. He sent and called the woman, and she came to him to the upper room, where his study and his house of prayer were. When he saw her, he rose from his chair and went to the ark in which the Torah scrolls lay; he took a Torah scroll and read in her ears the portion of the sotah [Num 5:11–31] with the cantillation. And as she "turned her back to go" [1 Sam 10:9], the Rabbi, of blessed memory, called after her in a loud voice: "If you have not gone astray..." and "But if you have gone astray..." [Num 5:19–20]. As she put her foot on the first step, before she could go down, her face turned green and her eyes bulged [cf. m. Sotah 3:4], and everything written in the portion of the sotah came upon her. At the sound of her cry, for she cried out bitterly, the judges and many of the people of the town came to see this awesome sight. They were very frightened and called out: "Take this accursed woman out, and pull her away, so that she does not defile our master's house!" [cf. m. Sotah 3:4]. And they said to one another: "Now we know that he is a holy man of God, and the word of the Lord in his mouth is truth" [1 Kgs 17:24; 2 Kgs 4:9]. From that time "they were afraid to come near him" [Exod 34:30], "and his name was very dear" [1 Sam 18:30] as a man of wonders. May his merit protect us, amen.

Thus far our hand has reached: a very little from the manuscript of the Ḥida, of blessed memory, and, of what our ears have heard, one in a thousand of his praises, his wonders, his holiness and his ways. May it be His will that his merit protect us and all Israel our brothers, amen, may it be His will.

Finished and complete.
