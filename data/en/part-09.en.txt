On 2 Elul ⚠1 [Tuesday 25 August 1778], two hours after midday, I went to the house of the king's great minister for foreign affairs of the state ⚠2, and gave him the letter of Madame Adélaïde to her niece ⚠3, the sister of the King of France, Louis XVI, who is married to the son of the King of Turin, the Prince of Piedmont. She is in the country, several miles away. He received us with a friendly face and said that he would give it to her. As I went along I saw the streets and the markets of Turin, beautiful and well ordered. The palace (palazzo) of the minister is also lovely. On another side is the great minister of state, with all the offices of the kingdom around it, and the king has a passage from his palace to there. Through the windows I saw a little of the king's garden ⚠4, well kept and beautiful. On my way back, being crushed and battered [Lev 22:24] and a little ill, I was in pain, and I was tired and weary. In the night I was not well and could not get up for prayer. On this day the ambassador of France came to visit me with his servants, because of a recommendation from the Comte de Maillebois in Paris. It was a great honor for the Jews, for he stands in the place of the King of France, and he is very important here: either because of the greatness of the King of France, a mighty king, or because the daughters of the King of Sardinia, who is Prince of Piedmont and Duke of Savoy ⚠5, are married to the brothers of the King of France, Monsieur the Comte de Provence and Monsieur the Comte d'Artois; and the king's sister is his daughter-in-law, married to his son the Prince of Piedmont, the heir to the throne; and the king's wife too is of the royal house of France ⚠6. All the great merchants and the bankers also came to visit with great honor, although I had not gone to them, being a little ill. And I filled my mouth with reproofs [Job 23:4] for the sons of our people: for, by our many sins, the gentiles and the ministers honor me more than they do. I also set before them the order of the differences ⚠7 from when I was here on my first visit, when they did me great honor, with gifts from individuals and presents to me; and now no man remembered [Eccl 9:15]. They have made up several new chapters in the laws of taking a handful, and they flee from the emissary so as not to be under one tent with him ⚠8, woe to them. Of this and of that I spoke at length, two out of a hundred of the things in my heart.

On 10 Elul [Wednesday 2 September 1778] a runner came from the town of Moncalvo, and they had sent three sequins. I sent him back once more and wrote on a small paper in these words: "Individuals of Moncalvo. I was much grieved at your stoutness of heart: in the past you sent sixteen, and now, in the height of the troubles, you sent three. Under three the earth trembles [Prov 30:21]; and you shall know that there is a God in Israel; the three shall be yours ⚠9. But we will make mention of the name of the Lord our God [Ps 20:8]." Thus far the text of the small paper. The honored sages wrote to them very strongly, and they paid the runner and sent eight sequins, with apologies. This is to show what a difference there is between this time and the time past. Happy are they, Israel; the Shield of the fathers is their help and their shield [Ps 115:9].

On 14 Elul, at the going out of the Sabbath [the evening of Saturday 5 September 1778], I had toothache, and all the same I went to the synagogue. Abraham Gabbai came to the house so that I would take him with me, after he had gone his own way for several weeks and grieved me. In the end I would not see him or give him a letter, for I am afraid of his bad character; and he went.

In the year 5538, on 25 Elul [Thursday 17 September 1778], I reached Pisa from the mission and came to the house of my dear Signor Raphael ⚠10. He was alone, and I said the blessing "who has kept us alive". After some hours I gave him a fine Amsterdam prayer book; two double Venetians ⚠11, which are not to be found; a little flask ⚠12 with scented water; two silk handkerchiefs (fazzoletti); and, from my own things, a silver case with a thimble ⚠13 and its accessories; a white inkstand of bone from France; and other such things ⚠14.

On 28 Elul [Sunday 20 September 1778] I travelled in the morning from Pisa to Livorno with Signor A. Ḥ. L. ⚠15, may his Rock keep him. My friend the gevir, my son-in-law, came to meet me, and after him came the beloved Signor Ferridas ⚠16, Signor Miranda and Signor Abraham Judah Faro ⚠17, in light carriages (calessi), and I enjoyed myself with them. I dined at my daughter's. I visited the gevir Signor Michael, may his Rock keep him, and the gevirim the Signori Franco, may God keep them. At night I delighted in love [Prov 7:18] with my friends and lovers, my brothers and companions, men of kindness, Signor Gabriel and Signor Jacob Pereira, may their Rock keep them, and we were in delight all night. The pious lady their mother and the ladies her daughters-in-law sent gifts and roses to Signor Raphael, may his Rock keep him. The next day [Monday 21 September 1778], after prayer, I came to Pisa and brought the gifts and the earrings. My friend Signor Jacob also sent two rings, and the matter was made public, for good and for blessing.

The year "Great shall be the glory of this house" [Hag 2:9] ⚠18, which is the year 5539. On 7 Ḥeshvan, on Wednesday of the week of the portion "and you shall be a blessing" [Gen 12:2] [28 October 1778], I came to the time of taking in marriage the honored and modest maiden Mrs Rachel M. B. T. ⚠19, in the city of Pisa. May it be His will that it be in a good sign and with fortune rising ⚠20, for length of days and years of life [Prov 3:2], riches and honor, health and holy seed [Isa 6:13]; and may we be worthy to go up to the Land of Israel in quiet and security [Isa 32:17], with abundance of all things [Deut 28:47], for ever, selah, and always.

In the year 5543, on 12 Kislev [Monday 18 November 1782], a letter came to me from my dear younger son, may his Rock keep him, from Tripoli ⚠21. From it I learned that he had arrived in peace, and that a miracle had happened to him: as he left the ship he fell into the sea altogether, but they caught hold of him firmly by his clothes. Blessed is He who redeems and saves. In this year, in the middle third of the month of Elul [about 8–17 September 1783], my son, my dear one and the light of my eyes, the sage R. Abraham, may God keep him, came from Tunis, may God protect it, to the lazaretto in peace. On the Sabbath of Nitzavim [20 September 1783] I preached in the yeshiva of the gevir, the honored ⚠22 Signor Michael, may his Rock keep him.

In the year 5545, on the Great Sabbath [19 March 1785], in the morning, I had a chill, and I went to pray. I needed to pass water, but I held it back, since I had begun "Blessed is He who spoke". After the Kedushah I went and could not pass water, and I sat in pain until after the Kedushah of Musaf. I came home and was in pain; after this I went to preach, and I preached an hour and a half in pain. Afterwards, before the afternoon prayer, I came home in pain, and I prayed the afternoon and evening prayers in pain. On 9 Nisan, the night of Sunday [the evening of Saturday 19 March 1785], I hardly slept; I was in pain, with pain in passing water, drop by drop, all night. All day [Sunday 20 March 1785] I went through great distress, so that, for my many sins, I did not pray, except that with great difficulty I read the Shema with the tefillin of Rashi. But the Holy One, blessed be He, had mercy on me, and I was able to pray the afternoon prayer, Ashrei and the Amidah, with difficulty and in the haste of Egypt [Deut 16:3]. I was in pain all the week, but not as on Sunday. Water of the root of althaea (radice d'altea) was very good for me, for it took away the pain at once.

On the night when the feast is hallowed [Isa 30:29] [the evening of Friday 25 March 1785] the Signori Acciaioli ⚠23, may God keep them, gathered with their little ones to be with us, as is the custom. I said two or three berries [Isa 17:6], a few words, in the first passages of the Haggadah, and I did not have the strength to expound and to search out [Eccl 1:13] as is my custom. Blessed be the Lord, for, considering what was passing over me, it was plain mercy on the two nights of Passover. From the first day I let almost no one come in to me; even the very closest saw my face with difficulty and went. For my many sins, from the first day to the eighth day of Passover I did not hear Kaddish, Kedushah or Barekhu. On the Sabbath, the eighth day of Passover [2 April 1785], I forced myself and went to the yeshiva, carried on the shoulder ⚠24. Before the Torah reading I went out to pass water, and came back and opened the ark, and was called up to the Torah. Before the haftarah I went out, and I went [again] for the afternoon prayer, on the Sabbath, the second festival day ⚠25.

On 18 Nisan, the night of Tuesday, at the beginning of the night, at twilight [the evening of Monday 28 March 1785], my son the sage R. Abraham, may God keep him, came from Algiers (Argel). He was at the anchorage ⚠26 until ten in the morning. He had passed through distress at sea from a storm wind. At ten, as he went to the lazaretto, the captain's launch ⚠27 was overturned by the force of the wind, and they were all nearly killed, but only one of them drowned. And on the night of Thursday [the evening of Wednesday 30 March 1785] the anchor broke in the storm; but he was in the lazaretto. Blessed be the Lord for his rescue and for his arrival in peace. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever [Ps 136:1]; to the God of salvations, abundant thanks. In the year 5545, on 26 Nisan [Wednesday 6 April 1785], I said the Blessing of the Sun ⚠28, praise to God; and I had already said it here in Livorno in the year 5517 [1757]. In the year 5553, on 14 Av [Tuesday 23 July 1793], I fell very ill, and for forty days I did not pray in the yeshiva. I went through many troubles, and by a miracle I fasted on the Day of Atonement. Give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever [Ps 136:1].
