Kislev, Wednesday of the week of Toledot, at night [13 November 1776]: the sage R. Y. B. ⚠1 came, who was leaving before prayers. He had had my Birkei Yosef on Yoreh De'ah for some days, and when he returned it, at the very moment of leaving, he raised an objection against what I wrote in Yoreh De'ah, section 13, and I answered a little. But I was sorry that he had kept the matter until the moment of his departure. In the morning, at prayers, it came to my mind that his objection was no objection at all, and I was sorry, for surely he had already left, and what could I do? After prayers I found him going off on his way; I accompanied him and told him that his objection does not hold, and he left. I hinted at it in the last quire.

18 Kislev [29 November 1776]. I wrote to Verona to the wardens, of blessed memory, and I wrote to a certain man, and he answered words without savor, and he also ⚠2 to make light of me. On the Sabbath, 24 Shevat [1 February 1777], he went to give account to his Maker: he died suddenly, and the authorities ordered that his head be opened, God save us.

On Monday of the week of Vayetze, at night [18 November 1776], I went to the council ⚠3 and told of the distress of Hebron. I said that they had taken endowments that had been dedicated to Hebron from the Zecca for their own debts; and since they had gained by the endowment, now that Hebron is in danger they are bound to be generous with this benefit. And now, if they were in the Zecca, we would sell the income of several years at half profit. They told me: you have not found your hands and feet in the Zecca, only in the council, and they do not trust them; and they answered that they would look into the matter. I also spoke about this: that in the ghetto gentiles sell their wine, and people buy it from them for Kiddush and the four cups. Besides being a blessing in vain, it is a profanation of God's name. If a man sins in private, he bears his own sin; but in great publicity, in the Jewish street, it is a profanation of the religion. I have saved my soul [Ezek 3:19]. They answered that they would see to correcting it.

On Friday of the week of Vayeḥi [27 December 1776] I was godfather (sandak) for the child Abraham son of Kalonymus Joseph Ḥai, son of the gevir Abraham Franco Almeida, with great honor, praise God.

On the Sabbath [28 December 1776] I dined with the gevirim Almeida, who held the circumcision meal on the Sabbath.

Shemot, on the seventh day [Saturday 4 January 1777], I prayed in the Levantine holy congregation in honor of the child's father, the gevir Jacob Ḥayyim Bivante son of Judah. On Friday night I was at the villa, and on the day of the meal with Rabbi Calimani, and the circumciser was the great gevir Signor Isaac son of Menaḥem Treves. The meal began two hours before nightfall and ended two hours into the night. In Venice I stayed in my house and went to no gevir until they came to me, except some of the council ⚠3 and the exalted gevir Daniel son of Jacob Bonfil and the gevir Isaac Treves mentioned above. I ate out only on the first day of the festival ⚠4, with Signor David Uziel, whose wife is the sister of Signor Godica, and of Signor David and Isaac of Ancona; and on the first day of the intermediate days, with Signor Almeida mentioned above; and the two circumcision meals mentioned above. But from Ḥeshvan on, on Friday nights, I used to go to the gevir, our beloved Signor Ḥayyim ha-Levi of Bosnia, may God keep him, and he showed me wonderful kindness in his person and his money. The accomplished sage, our honored teacher R. Abraham Jona, may God keep him, came to me every day after prayers for about two hours, and we read the Birkei Yosef. The gevir, the sage R. Abraham son of the honored R. Menaḥem Curiel, may his Rock keep him, did me honor and never stopped showing me affection. The gevir Gad son of the honored R. Samuel Foa, may his Rock keep him, the printer, was also a great friend of mine. And the old scribe Signor Moia was my friend. Praise God, I conducted myself with great honor in all matters; give thanks to the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures for ever. The gevir Vidal Angel also did me kindness, and so the three great gevirim, Signor Treves, Signor Bonfil and Signor Bivante, showed me wonderful kindness with honor and love. May the Lord repay their deeds, amen, may it be His will.

On Monday of the week of Va'era [6 January 1777] I left Venice. From the house to the sea, our beloved Signor Ḥayyim Levi, Signor Menaḥem Vivante, the sage R. Abraham Jona, Signor Vidal Angel, may God keep them, and others accompanied us. On the way, in Friuli ⚠5, before Gradisca, there is a small settlement called Jerusalem.

11 Adar [probably Thursday 20 March 1777 ⚠6]: at the time of the afternoon prayer we reached Modena. On the Sabbath the rabbis and gevirim came to visit, and Rabbi Ishmael ha-Kohen dined there with us. In Modena I saw the rulings of R. Menaḥem ha-Meiri, six volumes; the book of kavvanot arranged by R. Nathan Shapira ⚠7, and his prayer book, and notes on the Shulḥan Arukh, Passover and terefot, which seem to be by R. Y. Valle ⚠8; and a wonderful book of practical kabbalah by the great rabbi, our teacher R. Ephraim son of Elijah of Ostrog, of blessed memory, who was rabbi there and worked great deeds. I saw several kabbalistic books in old prayer books, and a large prayer book in manuscript by Rabbi B[enjamin] K[ohen] of Reggio, of blessed memory, with prayers for unifications (yiḥudim) in Aramaic. Also a wonderful book of practical kabbalah by the keen rabbi, our teacher R. Moses Lipschitz of Chęciny ⚠9, and some of his writings; he too was rabbi ⚠10 and taught there in Modena.

On Wednesday of the week of Shemini [2 April 1777] I went with the gevir Signor Ḥayyim, the master of the house, and Signor Azariah son of Abraham Nethanel Sanguinetti to the library. It is two beautiful rooms with marble columns and statues and beauty, and all the books are well bound. There are sixteen volumes of the book Or Yakar by R. Moses Cordovero, of blessed memory, on the Zohar and the Tikkunim, the Idrot, the Sifra di-Tseniuta and the Sefer Yetsirah. They were written in Safed, may it be rebuilt, in the year 5342 [1582] for R. Menaḥem Azariah [of Fano], of blessed memory. This is what Rabbi Joseph Solomon [Delmedigo] wrote in Matsref la-Ḥokhmah, that R. Menaḥem Azariah sent a thousand sequins to the widow of R. Moses Cordovero, of blessed memory, to copy his books. I saw the Duke's palace and cabinet (gabinetto), a beautiful and splendid building with statues and rooms of pictures in majesty and splendor. I saw the garden (giardino), beautiful and well kept; and the street of the entrance to the palace and its ascent are pleasant to see. Signor Abraham Nethanel also copied for me several quires of the writings of R. Moses Zacuto, of blessed memory. I also saw there several books by the author of Ma'avar Yabbok, in his own hand: a commentary on the Tikkunim, Shemen Mishḥat Kodesh and Shetil Poreaḥ. The whole Sanguinetti family are God-fearing and learned, and most of them showed me wonderful kindness, as did the other gevirim. May the Lord repay their deeds.

On Friday of the week of Tazria [11 April 1777] I went to Reggio, and there I saw a treasure of glory that lights up the eyes: a manuscript of the Gemara on parchment; a Soncino print of Berakhot with the Mordekhai in place of the Rosh, without leaf numbers, about ninety leaves, printed in the year 5244 [1484]; an early Tur printed in the year 5235 [1475]; and a Pentateuch with the Targum and Rashi on fine parchment, printed in Lisbon in the year 5250 [1490].

I visited Rabbi Ishmael, and he read me a ruling on a split hair, and I refuted his argument. He brought a peacock to me; its wings are longer than three cubits, of many colors like embroidery, and on its head a crown of four upright feathers, and the feathers high, the work of an embroiderer. Blessed be His name.

I visited Rabbi Ishmael again and refuted his ruling on inoculation ⚠11. And I wrote a responsum, five leaves, on a ruling of his on the annulment of a betrothal.

In the city of Mantua, on Sunday of the week of Behar Sinai, in the year 5537 [18 May 1777], I immersed in the ritual bath and went by carriage to the cemetery, to the graves of R. Menaḥem Azariah and R. Moses Zacuto, of blessed memory. I prayed there, came back and immersed again, with the help of Heaven. When I was in the casino outside the ghetto, they gave me a letter from my son, the sage R. Isaiah, may his Rock keep him, saying that he wants to come. As I was ill, and the melancholy had also overcome me, the world almost grew dark for me.

On Sunday of the week of Beḥukkotai, on Saturday night [24 May 1777], I saw there a model of the whole Tabernacle and the garments of Aaron, a beautiful thing. On Lag ba-Omer [Sunday 25 May 1777] I was ill with melancholy and a very strong headache. The physicians told me that I must be bled, and I was bled; a little meat that I ate did me harm, and I slept and got up with the headache.

25 Tammuz 5537 [Wednesday 30 July 1777]: after prayers we came by carriage to Avignon with R. Mordecai Carmi and Rabbi Moses son of Joseph R. D. Sh. ⚠12. Almost the whole way we talked of words of Torah and of laws. After we had arrived and the carriage was going back on the highway, the horses stumbled and the carriage broke; blessed be the Lord that this did not happen while we were on the road.
